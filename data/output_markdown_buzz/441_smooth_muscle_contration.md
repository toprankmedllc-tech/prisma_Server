## Buzzword Question

A 41-year-old man is brought to the emergency department with severe abdominal pain caused by intestinal smooth muscle spasm after food poisoning. During a physiology lecture discussing smooth muscle contraction, a professor explains that activation of myosin light-chain kinase (MLCK) is a critical step in the contraction process. A student asks what directly happens after MLCK becomes activated inside smooth muscle cells.
Which of the following is the most immediate consequence of myosin light-chain kinase activation in smooth muscle?

## Buzzwords Used

consequence of myosin light-chain kinase activation

## Answer Options

A. Binding of calcium to troponin
B. Phosphorylation of myosin light chains
C. Release of acetylcholine from motor neurons
D. Depolarization of T tubules

## Correct Answer

B. Phosphorylation of myosin light chains

## Explanation

In smooth muscle, calcium binds to calmodulin, forming a Ca2+–calmodulin complex that activates myosin light-chain kinase (MLCK). Activated MLCK phosphorylates myosin light chains, which allows myosin heads to interact with actin and initiate contraction.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Smooth muscle + MLCK activation + myosin phosphorylation = smooth muscle contraction

## Wrong Options

### A. Binding of calcium to troponin

Troponin-mediated regulation occurs in skeletal and cardiac muscle, not smooth muscle.

**Buzzword Combo:** Troponin + skeletal muscle + thin filament regulation = striated muscle contraction

### C. Release of acetylcholine from motor neurons

Acetylcholine release occurs at neuromuscular junctions and is not the direct effect of MLCK activation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neuromuscular junction + acetylcholine release + motor neuron signaling = skeletal muscle stimulation

### D. Depolarization of T tubules

T tubules are important in skeletal and cardiac muscle excitation-contraction coupling and are not the primary mechanism in smooth muscle.

**Buzzword Combo:** T tubules + excitation-contraction coupling + sarcomere organization = skeletal/cardiac muscle physiology
