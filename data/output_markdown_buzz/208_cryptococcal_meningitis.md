## Buzzword Question

An AIDS patient with confirmed cryptococcal meningitis (positive CSF India ink and CrAg) is admitted for treatment. The most appropriate INDUCTION regimen is:

## Buzzwords Used

Confirmed cryptococcal meningitis in AIDS; induction therapy

## Answer Options

A) Fluconazole monotherapy
B) Acyclovir
C) Ceftriaxone plus vancomycin plus dexamethasone
D) Amphotericin B plus flucytosine for at least 2 weeks

## Correct Answer

D) Amphotericin B plus flucytosine for at least 2 weeks

## Explanation

Amphotericin B (binds ergosterol, forming membrane pores) combined with flucytosine (converted to 5 FU intracellularly, inhibiting fungal DNA synthesis) is the standard induction therapy. After at least 2 weeks, transition to high dose fluconazole consolidation, then maintenance until CD4 recovery.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Cryptococcal meningitis in AIDS + induction = amphotericin B + flucytosine

## Wrong Options

### Fluconazole monotherapy

Fluconazole alone is used for CONSOLIDATION and MAINTENANCE, not induction in symptomatic AIDS meningitis, where it has inferior fungal clearance compared with amphotericin plus flucytosine.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cryptococcal consolidation and maintenance therapy = high dose fluconazole

### Acyclovir

Acyclovir treats herpesvirus infections (HSV, VZV). It has no activity against Cryptococcus.

**Buzzword Combo:** HSV 1 encephalitis (temporal lobe + RBCs in CSF) = IV acyclovir

### Ceftriaxone plus vancomycin plus dexamethasone

This is empiric therapy for bacterial meningitis. It has no antifungal activity and does not treat cryptococcal disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Empiric bacterial meningitis = ceftriaxone + vancomycin + dexamethasone

Amphotericin B mechanism (binds ergosterol); Flucytosine mechanism (converted to 5 FU); Azole class mechanism (inhibit fungal lanosterol 14 alpha demethylase)
