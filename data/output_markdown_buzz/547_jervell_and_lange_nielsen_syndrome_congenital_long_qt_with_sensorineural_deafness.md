## Buzzword Question


A child with congenital sensorineural deafness has recurrent syncope and a corrected QT interval of 520 ms on ECG. Her parents are both unaffected. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Congenital sensorineural deafness; recurrent syncope; prolonged QT; unaffected parents

## Answer Options


A) Romano-Ward syndrome
B) Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome
C) Brugada syndrome
D) Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT)

## Correct Answer

B) Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome

## Explanation


Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome is an autosomal recessive form of congenital long QT syndrome caused by mutations in KCNQ1 or KCNE1 (slow delayed rectifier potassium channel, IKs). It is distinguished by the combination of prolonged QT and congenital sensorineural deafness — unaffected parents (both carriers) are consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Prolonged QT + sensorineural deafness + autosomal recessive = Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome (KCNQ1/KCNE1)

## Wrong Options

### A) Romano-Ward syndrome

Autosomal dominant congenital long QT with pure cardiac involvement — no deafness. The most common inherited LQTS.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prolonged QT + no deafness + autosomal dominant = Romano-Ward syndrome

### C) Brugada syndrome

A sodium channelopathy (SCN5A) presenting with coved ST elevation in V1–V2, nocturnal sudden cardiac death in Southeast Asian males — not QT prolongation or deafness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Coved ST elevation V1–V2 + nocturnal SCD + Southeast Asian male = Brugada syndrome

### 
D) Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT)

RyR2 mutations causing exertion-triggered syncope and bidirectional VT, but with a normal resting ECG (normal QT) and no deafness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Exertion-induced syncope + normal QT + bidirectional VT = CPVT (RyR2)
