## Buzzword Question

A 24-year-old woman schedules an appointment with her family physician to discuss contraceptive options before starting university abroad. During the visit, the physician explains that prescribing contraception conflicts with personal moral beliefs. The patient asks how she can still obtain appropriate care without delaying treatment.

Which of the following is the most appropriate physician response?

## Buzzwords Used

Contraception request ; Physician moral objection ; Referral obligation ; Access to care

## Answer Options

A) “I cannot discuss contraception in any form.”

B) “I will refer you to another clinician who can provide counseling and treatment.”

C) “You should avoid contraception because it is morally inappropriate.”

D) “I am required to prescribe contraception regardless of my personal beliefs.”

## Correct Answer

B) “I will refer you to another clinician who can provide counseling and treatment.”

## Explanation

Physicians may decline non-emergency services that conflict with personal beliefs, but they should still provide timely referral to ensure patient access to care.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Moral objection + non-emergency care + continued patient access = referral

## Wrong Options

### A) “I cannot discuss contraception in any form.”

Completely refusing discussion or referral risks patient abandonment.

**Buzzword Combo:** Moral objection + refusal of communication + blocked access = inappropriate response

### C) “You should avoid contraception because it is morally inappropriate.”

Physicians should not impose personal moral beliefs through coercive counseling.

**Buzzword Combo:** Personal beliefs + judgmental counseling + reduced autonomy = ethical violation

### D) “I am required to prescribe contraception regardless of my personal beliefs.”

Physicians are generally not required to personally provide non-emergency services conflicting with sincere beliefs if appropriate referral is arranged.

**Buzzword Combo:** Conscientious objection + forced participation + misunderstanding of ethics = incorrect obligation
