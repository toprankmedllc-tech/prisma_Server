## Buzzword Question

A teenager has recurrent infections with meningococci and gonococci despite normal antibody production and normal neutrophil oxidative killing. Which of the following immunodeficiencies best explains this pattern?

## Buzzwords Used

Recurrent infections with meningococci and gonococci; normal antibody production; normal neutrophil oxidative killing

## Answer Options

A) Terminal complement deficiency
B) C3 deficiency
C) Chronic granulomatous disease
D) Bruton agammaglobulinemia

## Correct Answer

A) Terminal complement deficiency


## Explanation

Terminal complement deficiency is caused by deficiency of C5-C9 proteins, preventing formation of the membrane attack complex. This causes recurrent Neisseria infections because the MAC normally allows direct bacterial membrane lysis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recurrent Neisseria infections + defective C5-C9 proteins + impaired direct bacterial lysis = terminal complement deficiency

## Wrong Options

### B) C3 deficiency

C3 deficiency causes impaired opsonization and recurrent pyogenic bacterial infections, not a selective pattern of recurrent Neisseria infections.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent pyogenic infections + impaired opsonization + low C3b = C3 deficiency

### C) Chronic granulomatous disease

Chronic granulomatous disease is due to NADPH oxidase deficiency, causing impaired respiratory burst and recurrent catalase-positive infections.

**Buzzword Combo:** Catalase-positive infections + impaired respiratory burst + NADPH oxidase deficiency = chronic granulomatous disease

### D) Bruton agammaglobulinemia

Bruton agammaglobulinemia is an X-linked B-cell maturation defect due to BTK mutation, causing recurrent bacterial and enteroviral infections after 6 months of age.

**Buzzword Combo:** Male infant + recurrent bacterial infections after 6 months + absent B cells = Bruton agammaglobulinemia
