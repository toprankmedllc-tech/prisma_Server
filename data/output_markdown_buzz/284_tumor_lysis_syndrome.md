## Buzzword Question

A patient with acute leukemia develops muscle weakness and rising creatinine shortly after starting chemotherapy. Labs show increased potassium, increased phosphate, increased uric acid, and decreased calcium. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Acute leukemia shortly after starting chemotherapy; muscle weakness and rising creatinine; increased potassium, increased phosphate, increased uric acid, and decreased calcium

## Answer Options

A) Refeeding syndrome
B) Tumor lysis syndrome
C) Syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion
D) Disseminated intravascular coagulation

## Correct Answer

B) Tumor lysis syndrome


## Explanation

Tumor lysis syndrome is caused by rapid tumor cell breakdown after treatment initiation, especially in leukemias and lymphomas. The classic lab pattern is increased potassium, increased phosphate, increased uric acid, and decreased calcium.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Leukemia after chemotherapy + increased K/PO4/uric acid + decreased calcium = tumor lysis syndrome

## Wrong Options

### A) Refeeding syndrome

Refeeding syndrome occurs after nutrition is restarted in a severely malnourished patient. It causes low phosphate, low potassium, and low magnesium rather than increased potassium, phosphate, and uric acid.

**Buzzword Combo:** Severe malnutrition + restarting nutrition + low phosphate = refeeding syndrome

### C) Syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion

SIADH causes euvolemic hyponatremia due to inappropriate ADH activity. It does not explain hyperuricemia, hyperphosphatemia, or crystal-related acute kidney injury.

**Buzzword Combo:** Euvolemia + hyponatremia + concentrated urine = SIADH

### D) Disseminated intravascular coagulation

DIC causes systemic coagulation activation with bleeding and thrombosis. It is associated with prolonged PT/PTT, low fibrinogen, and increased D-dimer rather than the electrolyte pattern in this question.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bleeding and thrombosis + prolonged PT/PTT + low fibrinogen = DIC

## Related Concepts

Hyperkalemia; hypocalcemia; acute kidney injury
