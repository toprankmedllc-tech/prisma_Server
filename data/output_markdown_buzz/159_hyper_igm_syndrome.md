## Buzzword Question

A 4-year-old boy has recurrent sinopulmonary infections. Laboratory studies show markedly increased IgM with decreased IgG, IgA, and IgE. Which of the following defects most likely explains these findings?

## Buzzwords Used

4-year-old boy; recurrent sinopulmonary infections; increased IgM with decreased IgG, IgA, and IgE

## Answer Options

A) Defective CD40 ligand on activated CD4+ T cells
B) Defective Bruton tyrosine kinase in B-cell maturation
C) Defective WASp in hematopoietic cytoskeleton signaling
D) Defective NADPH oxidase in phagocyte respiratory burst

## Correct Answer

A) Defective CD40 ligand on activated CD4+ T cells


## Explanation

Hyper-IgM syndrome is caused by defective CD40L on activated CD4+ T cells. This prevents CD40 activation on B cells, leading to impaired class switching and persistently elevated IgM.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Increased IgM + decreased IgG/IgA/IgE + defective CD40L = Hyper-IgM syndrome

## Wrong Options

### B) Defective Bruton tyrosine kinase in B-cell maturation

This describes X-linked agammaglobulinemia, which causes failed B-cell maturation and low levels of all antibody classes. Hyper-IgM syndrome has B cells that can make IgM but cannot class switch.

**Buzzword Combo:** Male infant after 6 months + absent tonsils + low all immunoglobulins = X-linked agammaglobulinemia

### C) Defective WASp in hematopoietic cytoskeleton signaling

This describes Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome. The classic pattern is recurrent infections, eczema, and thrombocytopenia with small platelets, not isolated class-switching failure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent infections + eczema + thrombocytopenia with small platelets = Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome

### D) Defective NADPH oxidase in phagocyte respiratory burst

This describes chronic granulomatous disease due to impaired respiratory burst. It affects phagocyte killing, not B-cell isotype switching.

**Buzzword Combo:** Catalase-positive infections + granulomas + abnormal oxidative burst = chronic granulomatous disease

## Related Concepts

X-linked agammaglobulinemia; Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome; chronic granulomatous disease
