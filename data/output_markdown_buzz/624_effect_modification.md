## Buzzword Question

Researchers investigate whether regular aspirin use reduces the risk of ischemic stroke. In the overall study population, aspirin use is associated with a reduction in stroke risk. The investigators then perform a subgroup analysis. In one subgroup, aspirin use is associated with a marked reduction in stroke risk. In another subgroup, aspirin use is associated with little to no reduction in stroke risk.

Which of the following best explains these findings?

## Buzzwords Used

Subgroup analysis ; Different treatment effects ; Stratification ; Stroke risk ; Epidemiology

## Answer Options

A) Confounding

B) Effect modification

C) Recall bias

D) Selection bias

## Correct Answer

B) Effect modification

## Explanation

Effect modification occurs when the strength of an association differs across subgroups. The association remains present after stratification, but its magnitude varies between groups.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Different subgroup results + association persists after stratification + varying effect size = effect modification

## Wrong Options

### A) Confounding

Confounding is suggested when an observed association disappears or substantially weakens after stratification. Here, the association remains but differs in magnitude.

**Buzzword Combo:** Stratification + persistent association + differing subgroup effects = not confounding

### C) Recall bias

Recall bias occurs when participants inaccurately remember prior exposures and does not explain differing effects across subgroups.

**Buzzword Combo:** Subgroup analysis + treatment effect differences + no exposure recall issue = not recall bias

### D) Selection bias

Selection bias results from nonrepresentative participant selection and does not explain this stratification pattern.

**Buzzword Combo:** Different subgroup effects + no enrollment problem = not selection bias
