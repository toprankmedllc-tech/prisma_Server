## Buzzword Question

A patient with long-standing mitral stenosis develops chronic hoarseness without preceding upper respiratory illness. Which of the following best explains this finding?

## Buzzwords Used

Long-standing mitral stenosis; chronic hoarseness; left atrial enlargement; recurrent laryngeal nerve compression; Ortner syndrome

## Answer Options

A) Compression of the left recurrent laryngeal nerve by an enlarged left atrium
B) Direct extension of valvular infection to the larynx
C) Reflux esophagitis from elevated left atrial pressure
D) Embolic ischemia of the laryngeal nerves

## Correct Answer


A) Compression of the left recurrent laryngeal nerve by an enlarged left atrium

## Explanation

Ortner syndrome — hoarseness from compression of the left recurrent laryngeal nerve by an enlarged left atrium — is a classic complication of long-standing mitral stenosis with severe LA enlargement.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Mitral stenosis + hoarseness + LA enlargement = Ortner syndrome (left recurrent laryngeal nerve compression)

## Wrong Options

### B) Direct extension of valvular infection to the larynx

Mitral stenosis is a chronic mechanical valvulopathy, not an infectious process spreading locally to the larynx.

**Buzzword Combo:** Valvular infection + spread = infective endocarditis with abscess (not Ortner)

### C) Reflux esophagitis from elevated left atrial pressure

LA enlargement can cause dysphagia by compressing the esophagus, but does not produce reflux esophagitis or hoarseness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mitral stenosis + dysphagia = esophageal compression by enlarged LA

### D) Embolic ischemia of the laryngeal nerves

LA thrombi in MS embolize to the systemic arterial circulation (most often causing stroke), not to the laryngeal nerves.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mitral stenosis + LA thrombus + embolic event = stroke (not Ortner)
