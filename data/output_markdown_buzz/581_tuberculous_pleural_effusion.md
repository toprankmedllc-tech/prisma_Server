## Buzzword Question

A 45-year-old man presents with chronic low-grade fever, night sweats, and weight loss for 1 month. Chest X-ray shows a unilateral pleural effusion. Thoracentesis reveals lymphocyte-predominant exudative fluid with elevated ADA levels.

## Buzzwords Used

Low-grade fever; night sweats; weight loss; unilateral pleural effusion; lymphocyte-predominant exudate; elevated ADA

## Answer Options

A) Congestive heart failure
B) Tuberculous pleural effusion
C) Pulmonary embolism
D) Hepatic hydrothorax

## Correct Answer

B) Tuberculous pleural effusion

## Explanation

Tuberculous pleural effusion is a lymphocyte-predominant exudative effusion with elevated ADA and constitutional symptoms like fever, night sweats, and weight loss. It results from a hypersensitivity reaction to Mycobacterium tuberculosis in the pleural space.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic symptoms; lymphocytic exudate; high ADA = TB pleural effusion

## Wrong Options

### A) Congestive heart failure

CHF causes a transudative effusion due to increased hydrostatic pressure and typically presents with bilateral pleural effusions without lymphocytosis or elevated ADA.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bilateral effusion; low protein; low LDH = transudate

### C) Pulmonary embolism

PE causes a sudden onset pleural effusion that is usually small, serosanguinous, and not lymphocyte-predominant with elevated ADA. It is primarily a vascular event rather than infectious.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden dyspnea; pleuritic pain; serosanguinous fluid = Pulmonary Embolism

### D) Hepatic hydrothorax

Hepatic hydrothorax is a transudative pleural effusion due to cirrhosis and portal hypertension, typically right-sided, without lymphocytic predominance or elevated ADA.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cirrhosis; ascites; right-sided transudate = hepatic hydrothorax
