## Buzzword Question

An 8-year-old girl is brought to the clinic by her stepmother for evaluation of recurrent, unexplained fevers, skin rashes, and persistent diarrhea over the past six months. Extensive medical workups at three different hospitals have failed to yield a definitive diagnosis. Review of the electronic medical records reveals that the child's symptoms consistently improve during hospitalizations when visitors are restricted, but rapidly flare up within 48 hours of discharge. Lab results during an active flare show unexplained trace amounts of an unprescribed laxative in her stool. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Recurrent unexplained illness; Improvement during hospitalization; Laxative in stool; Multiple hospital providers; Factitious disorder by proxy

## Answer Options

A) Factitious Disorder Imposed on Another
B) Factitious Disorder Imposed on Self
C) Malingering
D) Somatic Symptom Disorder

## Correct Answer

A) Factitious Disorder Imposed on Another

## Explanation

Factitious Disorder Imposed on Another (formerly Factitious Disorder by Proxy or Munchausen syndrome by proxy) is a form of child abuse where a caregiver deliberately falsifies, exaggerates, or induces physical illness in a person under their care. The classic clues include a complex medical history with multiple providers, symptoms that only occur in the caregiver's presence, and objective evidence of induction (e.g., exogenous laxatives)

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Caregiver-induced illness + Disappearance of symptoms when separated + Normal medical workup = Factitious Disorder Imposed on Another

## Wrong Options

### B) Factitious Disorder Imposed on Self

Factitious Disorder Imposed on Self involves an individual intentionally fabricating or inducing symptoms on their own body for primary psychological gain (the sick role). This is incorrect here because the 8-year-old child is the victim of the stepmother's external inductions.

**Buzzword Combo:** Self-induced illness + Deceptive behavior + Primary internal gain = Factitious Disorder Imposed on Self

### C) Malingering

Malingering involves the intentional production of false or grossly exaggerated physical or psychological symptoms, but it is explicitly motivated by external incentives (e.g., avoiding work, obtaining compensation, evading legal trouble), which is not the primary driver in factitious disorders.

**Buzzword Combo:** Feigned symptoms + Identifiable secondary gain + Avoidance of duty = Malingering

### D) Somatic Symptom Disorder

Somatic Symptom Disorder involves genuine, distressing somatic symptoms accompanied by disproportionate, excessive anxiety and thoughts about the illness; however, the symptoms are not intentionally or deceptively produced or induced.

**Buzzword Combo:** Genuine somatic complaints + Excessive health anxiety + No intentional deception = Somatic Symptom Disorder

## Related Concepts

Somatic symptom vs. Factitious disorders; Primary vs. Secondary gain; Legal obligations in suspected child abuse
