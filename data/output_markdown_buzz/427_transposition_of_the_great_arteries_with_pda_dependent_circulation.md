## Buzzword Question

A cyanotic newborn develops severe hypoxemia shortly after birth. Echocardiography demonstrates parallel circulation of the systemic and pulmonary outflow tracts. The infant’s oxygen saturation improves after infusion of prostaglandin E1. Which of the following interventions would most likely rapidly worsen this patient’s condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Parallel circulation ; Prostaglandin E1 ; Cyanotic newborn ; PDA-dependent circulation

## Answer Options

A) Administration of indomethacin
B) Balloon atrial septostomy
C) Continuous prostaglandin E1 infusion
D) Supplemental oxygen therapy

## Correct Answer

A) Administration of indomethacin

## Explanation

This infant has transposition of the great arteries with PDA-dependent circulation. Indomethacin closes the ductus arteriosus and would rapidly worsen hypoxemia by preventing mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Parallel circulation + PGE1 dependence + Cyanosis = Keep PDA open

## Wrong Options

### B) Balloon atrial septostomy

Balloon atrial septostomy improves mixing between circulations and is often lifesaving in severe transposition of the great arteries.

**Buzzword Combo:** Severe cyanosis + Poor mixing + Emergent stabilization = Balloon septostomy

### C) Continuous prostaglandin E1 infusion

Prostaglandin E1 maintains ductus arteriosus patency and improves systemic oxygenation in PDA-dependent congenital heart disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ductal-dependent lesion + Neonatal cyanosis + Need for blood mixing = PGE1 infusion

### D) Supplemental oxygen therapy

Oxygen alone is insufficient to correct the parallel circulatory defect but does not worsen the condition.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypoxemia + Supportive therapy + Neonatal distress = Oxygen administration
