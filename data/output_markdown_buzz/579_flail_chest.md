## Buzzword Question

A 33-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after a high-speed motor vehicle accident. He is conscious but in significant respiratory distress with tachypnea and hypoxemia. On examination, there is localized chest wall pain, and a segment of the chest moves inward during inspiration and outward during expiration. Chest imaging shows fractures of three or more adjacent ribs in two places. Which of the following is the most likely complication associated with this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Blunt chest trauma; High-speed motor vehicle accident; Multiple rib fractures; Paradoxical chest movement; Flail chest; Hypoxemia; Tachypnea; Respiratory distress; Chest wall instability

## Answer Options

A) Alveolar hemorrhage
B) Ventilatory failure
C) Pulmonary artery thrombosis
D) Bronchospasm

## Correct Answer

B) Ventilatory failure

## Explanation

Flail chest causes an unstable chest wall segment leading to paradoxical movement, impaired ventilation, and ineffective respiratory mechanics. This results in ventilatory failure due to decreased tidal volume and impaired gas exchange.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Multiple rib fractures + paradoxical movement + chest wall instability = ventilatory failure

## Wrong Options

### A) Alveolar hemorrhage

Alveolar hemorrhage is seen in pulmonary contusion or vasculitis, not mechanical chest wall instability.

**Buzzword Combo:** Blunt trauma + patchy infiltrates + hemoptysis = alveolar hemorrhage

### C) Pulmonary artery thrombosis

Pulmonary embolism presents with sudden dyspnea and risk factors like immobility, not paradoxical chest wall movement after trauma.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden dyspnea + pleuritic chest pain + DVT risk = pulmonary embolism

### D) Bronchospasm

Bronchospasm causes wheezing and prolonged expiration, not chest wall instability or rib fractures.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wheezing + reversible airflow obstruction + atopy = bronchospasm/asthma
