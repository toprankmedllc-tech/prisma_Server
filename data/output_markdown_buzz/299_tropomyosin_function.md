## Buzzword Question

A physiology student is reviewing the molecular components of skeletal muscle contraction during a laboratory session. The instructor explains that, in resting skeletal muscle, a regulatory protein prevents interaction between actin and myosin by covering specific binding sites on the thin filament. When calcium binds to troponin C during excitation-contraction coupling, this protein shifts position and allows cross-bridge formation to occur.
Which of the following structures is responsible for blocking the myosin-binding sites on actin in resting skeletal muscle?

## Buzzwords Used

 blocking the myosin-binding sites on actin in resting skeletal muscle

## Answer Options

A. Troponin C
B. Myosin head
C. Tropomyosin
D. Titin

## Correct Answer

C. Tropomyosin

## Explanation

Tropomyosin is a regulatory protein located along the actin thin filament in skeletal muscle. At rest, it covers the myosin-binding sites on actin, preventing cross-bridge formation and muscle contraction. When calcium binds to troponin C, tropomyosin moves away from these binding sites, allowing myosin to attach to actin and initiate contraction.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Actin-binding site blockade + thin filament regulation + calcium-mediated movement = tropomyosin
