## Buzzword Question

A 67-year-old man with type 2 diabetes and hypertension is brought to the ER after a 3-hour episode of septic shock requiring vasopressors. His blood pressure is now stabilized at 118/74 mmHg. Labs show creatinine 3.8 mg/dL (baseline 1.1), BUN 58 mg/dL, and urinalysis reveals muddy brown granular casts. Urine sodium is 65 mEq/L and FENa is 3.2%. Which region of the kidney sustained the most damage and why?


## Buzzwords Used

Septic shock ; raised creatinine (3.8); muddy brown granular casts 

## Answer Options

A. Glomerulus, because it receives the highest pressure blood flow from the afferent arteriole
B. Renal cortex, because it contains the majority of metabolically active proximal tubules
C. Renal medulla, because it receives disproportionately low blood flow making it most susceptible to ischemia
D. Ureteropelvic junction, because it is the narrowest point and most prone to pressure-related injury
E. Juxtaglomerular apparatus, because vasopressors directly suppress renin release causing cortical ischemia

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation


The muddy brown granular casts + elevated FENa (>2%) + rising creatinine after a hypotensive episode = acute tubular necrosis (ATN). The medulla is preferentially damaged because it receives significantly less blood flow than the cortex, making it exquisitely sensitive to ischemia. The straight portion of the proximal tubule (S3) and thick ascending limb in the outer medulla are the most vulnerable segments.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Septic shock + muddy brown casts + FENa >2% = ATN → medullary ischemia due to low baseline blood flow

## Wrong Options

### A. Glomerulus



Incorrect — While glomeruli can be injured in other conditions (eg, glomerulonephritis), they are cortical and receive robust blood flow; not the primary target in ischemic ATN.

**Buzzword Combo:** Glomerular injury + RBC casts + proteinuria + hypertension = glomerulonephritis (NOT ATN)

### B Renal cortex

The cortex receives approximately 90% of total renal blood flow and is relatively spared during ischemia compared to the vulnerable medulla.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cortical necrosis + bilateral + obstetric catastrophe (eg, abruptio placentae) = diffuse cortical ischemia (rare, NOT classic ATN)

### D. Ureteropelvic Junction

The UPJ is the most common site of ureteral obstruction causing hydronephrosis, not a site of ischemic tubular injury.

**Buzzword Combo:** UPJ obstruction + flank pain + hydronephrosis + young male = ureteropelvic junction obstruction (NOT ATN)
