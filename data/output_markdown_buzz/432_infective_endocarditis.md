## Buzzword Question

A patient with infective endocarditis is found to have painful, tender, raised nodules on the pads of his fingers and toes. Which of the following best describes the underlying mechanism?

## Buzzwords Used

Infective endocarditis; painful nodules; finger pads

## Answer Options

A) Immune complex deposition in dermal vessels
B) Septic microemboli to the skin
C) Direct bacterial invasion of cutaneous tissue
D) Microvascular thrombosis from disseminated intravascular coagulation

## Correct Answer

A) Immune complex deposition in dermal vessels

## Explanation


Osler nodes are painful, tender, raised nodules on the finger and toe pads caused by immune complex deposition in dermal vessels. The mnemonic: "Osler = Ouchy" — painful and immune-mediated. They are distinct from Janeway lesions, which are painless, flat, erythematous macules on the palms and soles caused by septic microemboli.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Painful nodules + finger/toe pads + immune complexes = Osler nodes ("Ouchy")

## Wrong Options

### B) Septic microemboli to the skin

This describes the mechanism of Janeway lesions — painless, flat erythematous macules on the palms and soles — not Osler nodes.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Painless macules + palms/soles + septic emboli = Janeway lesions

### C) Direct bacterial invasion of cutaneous tissue

Describes cellulitis or skin abscess, not the peripheral stigmata of endocarditis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Direct bacterial invasion of skin = cellulitis / abscess

### D) Microvascular thrombosis from disseminated intravascular coagulation

DIC causes purpura fulminans and widespread microthrombi but is not the mechanism behind the peripheral stigmata of endocarditis.

**Buzzword Combo:** DIC + purpura fulminans + microthrombi = consumptive coagulopathy
