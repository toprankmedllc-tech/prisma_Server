## Buzzword Question

A 39-year-old woman presents with palpitations, heat intolerance, and weight loss. Physical examination reveals a solitary thyroid nodule. Laboratory studies show decreased TSH and elevated free T4. Radioactive iodine uptake scan demonstrates a single “hot” nodule with suppression of the remaining thyroid tissue. Which of the following is the most likely underlying mechanism?

## Buzzwords Used

solitary thyroid nodule; decreased TSH; elevated free T4; hot nodule; suppression of remaining thyroid tissue

## Answer Options

A) Activating mutation of the TSH receptor causing autonomous hormone production

B) Autoantibodies stimulating diffuse thyroid follicular hyperplasia

C) Granulomatous destruction of thyroid follicles after viral infection

D) Postpartum lymphocytic thyroid destruction with release of preformed hormone

## Correct Answer

A) Activating mutation of the TSH receptor causing autonomous hormone production

## Explanation

A solitary hot thyroid nodule with suppression of surrounding tissue is classic for toxic adenoma. Toxic adenomas are caused by activating mutations in the TSH receptor or Gs alpha signaling pathway.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hot nodule + suppressed surrounding thyroid tissue + hyperthyroidism = toxic adenoma

## Wrong Options

### B) Autoantibodies stimulating diffuse thyroid follicular hyperplasia

This describes Graves disease, which causes diffuse radioactive iodine uptake rather than a solitary hot nodule.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diffuse goiter + ophthalmopathy + diffuse uptake = Graves disease

### C) Granulomatous destruction of thyroid follicles after viral infection

Subacute granulomatous thyroiditis presents with painful thyroid enlargement and decreased radioactive iodine uptake.

**Buzzword Combo:** Painful thyroid + viral illness + low uptake = de Quervain thyroiditis

### D) Postpartum lymphocytic thyroid destruction with release of preformed hormone

Postpartum thyroiditis causes transient hyperthyroidism with low radioactive iodine uptake due to follicular destruction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Postpartum woman + painless thyroiditis + low uptake = postpartum thyroiditis
