## Buzzword Question

An immunosuppressive drug used to prevent transplant rejection binds an intracellular FK506-binding protein and prevents activation of a phosphatase needed for T-cell cytokine transcription. Which downstream effect best explains this drug’s action?

## Buzzwords Used

Transplant rejection prevention; binds FK506-binding protein; prevents activation of a phosphatase needed for T-cell cytokine transcription

## Answer Options

A) Decreased IL-2 transcription
B) Blocked T-cell response to IL-2
C) Decreased guanine nucleotide synthesis
D) Inhibited pyrimidine synthesis

## Correct Answer

A) Decreased IL-2 transcription


## Explanation

Tacrolimus binds FK506-binding protein and inhibits calcineurin. This decreases IL-2 transcription and reduces T-cell activation and proliferation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

FK506-binding protein + decreased IL-2 transcription + transplant rejection prevention = tacrolimus

## Wrong Options

### B) Blocked T-cell response to IL-2

This describes sirolimus, an mTOR inhibitor. It blocks T-cell response to IL-2 rather than decreasing IL-2 transcription.

**Buzzword Combo:** mTOR inhibition + blocked response to IL-2 + transplant immunosuppression = sirolimus

### C) Decreased guanine nucleotide synthesis

This describes mycophenolate. Mycophenolate inhibits IMP dehydrogenase and decreases guanine nucleotide synthesis in lymphocytes.

**Buzzword Combo:** IMP dehydrogenase inhibition + decreased guanine nucleotides + lymphocyte suppression = mycophenolate

### D) Inhibited pyrimidine synthesis

This describes leflunomide. It inhibits pyrimidine synthesis and does not act through FK506-binding protein or calcineurin.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dihydroorotate dehydrogenase inhibition + decreased pyrimidine synthesis + rheumatoid arthritis therapy = leflunomide
