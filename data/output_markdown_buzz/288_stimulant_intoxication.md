## Buzzword Question

A 19-year-old male college student is brought to the emergency department by his roommate after becoming increasingly agitated and paranoid over the past 6 hours. He believes the government has implanted a tracking device in his molar. His roommate states he was at a party earlier and may have taken “something.” Vitals show HR 118, BP 145/92, T 37.8°C. On exam, pupils are dilated, and he is diaphoretic. Urine toxicology is positive. Which of the following substances is most consistent with this presentation?

## Buzzwords Used

Agitation; paranoia; dilated pupils; diaphoresis; tachycardia; hypertension; urine tox positive; party

## Answer Options

A) Opioid intoxication
B) Cannabis intoxication
C) Stimulant (cocaine/methamphetamine) intoxication
D) Sedative intoxication

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

Stimulant intoxication (cocaine/methamphetamine) classically presents with sympathomimetic findings: tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, mydriasis, diaphoresis, agitation, and paranoia/psychosis. The party setting and urine tox positivity further support this diagnosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Party setting + paranoia + dilated pupils + sympathomimetic vitals = Stimulant intoxication

## Wrong Options

### A) Opioid intoxication

Opioid intoxication causes CNS/respiratory depression, miosis (pinpoint pupils), and bradycardia — the opposite of the sympathomimetic picture seen here. A student picking opioids may latch onto “party drug” without analyzing the vital signs pattern.

**Buzzword Combo:** Party drug → opioid assumption (ignores mydriasis + hypertension = wrong direction)

### B) Cannabis intoxication

Cannabis can cause mild paranoia and tachycardia but does not cause the degree of hypertension, diaphoresis, or severe agitation seen here. Conjunctival injection, not mydriasis, is the classic ocular finding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Paranoia + tachycardia → cannabis (ignores diaphoresis + hypertension + marked agitation = incomplete picture)

### D) Sedative intoxication

Sedatives (e.g., benzodiazepines, barbiturates) cause CNS depression, hypotension, and respiratory depression — not agitation, hypertension, or mydriasis. This is the opposite toxidrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Party + took “something” → sedative assumption (ignores that sedatives suppress, not stimulate)
