## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old man with fatigue, numbness in his hands and feet, difficulty with balance, macrocytic anemia, and hypersegmented neutrophils has which most specific laboratory finding for vitamin B12 deficiency?

## Buzzwords Used

fatigue; numbness; balance difficulty; macrocytic anemia; hypersegmented neutrophils; neurologic symptoms

## Answer Options

A) Increased homocysteine only
B) Increased methylmalonic acid
C) Decreased ferritin
D) Increased PT

## Correct Answer

B. Increased methylmalonic acid

## Explanation

Vitamin B12 is required for methylmalonyl-CoA mutase activity, so deficiency leads to accumulation of methylmalonic acid. Elevated MMA helps distinguish B12 deficiency from folate deficiency, where MMA remains normal.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Elevated MMA + neurologic symptoms + macrocytosis = Vitamin B12 deficiency

## Wrong Options

### A. Increased homocysteine only

Homocysteine levels are elevated in both folate and vitamin B12 deficiency, making it less specific for B12 deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elevated homocysteine + macrocytosis = Folate or B12 deficiency

### C. Decreased ferritin

Low ferritin is characteristic of iron deficiency anemia, which causes microcytic anemia rather than macrocytic anemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low ferritin + microcytosis + high TIBC = Iron deficiency anemia

### D. Increased PT

Prolonged PT is associated with factor VII deficiency, vitamin K deficiency, or warfarin therapy and is unrelated to vitamin B12 deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prolonged PT + coagulation defect = Vitamin K deficiency
