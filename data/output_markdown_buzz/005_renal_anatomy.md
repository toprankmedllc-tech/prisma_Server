## Buzzword Question

A 45-year-old woman undergoes ligation of her left ovarian vessels during a gynecologic procedure for endometriosis. Postoperatively, she develops flank pain and imaging reveals hydronephrosis. Which anatomical relationship most likely explains the iatrogenic injury?

## Buzzwords Used


Water (ureters) flows over the iliacs and under
the bridge (uterine artery or vas deferens).

## Answer Options

A. The ureter passes over the gonadal artery then under the common iliac artery
B. The ureter passes under the gonadal artery then over the common iliac artery
C. The ureter passes lateral to the uterine artery at the level of the cervix
D. The ureter travels within the broad ligament alongside the uterine vessels

## Correct Answer

Option B -The ureter travels under the gonadal arteries, then over the common iliac artery, then under the uterine artery/vas deferens (retroperitoneal). Gynecologic procedures risk ureteral injury → obstruction or leak.

## Explanation

The ureter travels under the gonadal arteries, then over the common iliac artery, then under the uterine artery/vas deferens (retroperitoneal). Gynecologic procedures risk ureteral injury → obstruction or leak.

## Wrong Options

### Option A Over gonadal, under iliac


Incorrect — The ureter passes under the gonadal artery, not over.

**Buzzword Combo:** NA

### Option C-Lateral to uterine artery

Incorrect — The ureter passes under the uterine artery medially, making it vulnerable in hysterectomy.

**Buzzword Combo:** NA

### Option D writhing Broad ligament

Incorrect — The ureter is retroperitoneal and runs near but not within the broad ligament vasculature.

**Buzzword Combo:** NA
