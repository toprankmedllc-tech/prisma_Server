## Buzzword Question

A 74-year-old man with advanced Parkinson disease has severe resting tremor, bradykinesia, and rigidity despite maximal medical therapy with levodopa/carbidopa. He also has significant “on-off” fluctuations throughout the day. Neurologic evaluation recommends placement of electrodes into a deep brain structure to improve motor symptoms. Stimulation of which of the following structures is most commonly used in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Advanced Parkinson disease ; on-off fluctuations ; refractory symptoms ; deep brain stimulation ; resting tremor ; subthalamic nucleus

## Answer Options

A) Subthalamic nucleus
B) Caudate nucleus
C) Mammillary body
D) Nucleus accumbens

## Correct Answer

A) Subthalamic nucleus

## Explanation

Deep brain stimulation of the subthalamic nucleus (STN) or globus pallidus interna (GPi) is used in advanced Parkinson disease with refractory motor symptoms and on-off fluctuations despite optimal medical therapy.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Advanced Parkinson + refractory motor fluctuations + deep brain stimulation = Subthalamic nucleus

## Wrong Options

### B) Caudate nucleus

The caudate nucleus is involved in cognition and motor processing but is not the standard target for deep brain stimulation in Parkinson disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Basal ganglia structure + cognition role + not DBS target = Caudate nucleus

### C) Mammillary body

The mammillary bodies are associated with memory pathways and are classically affected in Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, not Parkinson disease therapy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Confabulation + thiamine deficiency + memory circuit = Mammillary body

### D) Nucleus accumbens

The nucleus accumbens is part of the mesolimbic reward pathway and is associated with addiction and reinforcement behavior.

**Buzzword Combo:** Reward pathway + dopamine + addiction = Nucleus accumbens
