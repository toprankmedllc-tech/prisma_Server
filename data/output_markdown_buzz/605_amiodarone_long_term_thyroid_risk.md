## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old man with sustained ventricular tachycardia unresponsive to other agents is started on an antiarrhythmic that works primarily by blocking cardiac potassium channels to prolong the action potential duration and effective refractory period. The drug also has sodium channel, beta-adrenergic, and calcium channel-blocking properties and has an extremely long half-life.
Which of the following is this patient most at risk for developing with long-term use?

A) Hypercalcemia 
B) Thyroid dysfunction 
C) Adrenal insufficiency 
D) Nephrotic syndrome


## Buzzwords Used

Blocking cardiac potassium channels; also Na channel, beta-adrenergic, and Ca channel-blocking properties

## Answer Options

A) Hypercalcemia 
B) Thyroid dysfunction 
C) Adrenal insufficiency 
D) Nephrotic syndrome

## Correct Answer

B) Thyroid dysfunction 

## Explanation

The drug described is amiodarone. Due to its high iodine content, long-term use is associated with both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism. Excess iodine can block thyroid hormone synthesis (Wolff-Chaikoff effect), causing hypothyroidism, or can drive autonomous hormone production (Jod-Basedow effect), causing hyperthyroidism.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Amiodarone + long-term use + high iodine content = Thyroid dysfunction 

## Wrong Options

### A) Hypercalcemia 

Amiodarone is not associated with hypercalcemia. The drug's recognized endocrine toxicity is thyroid-related, not parathyroid or calcium metabolism-related. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Stones + Bones + Abdominal Groans + Psychiatric Moans = Hypercalcemia

### C) Adrenal insufficiency 

Amiodarone does not affect adrenal function or cortisol synthesis. Adrenal insufficiency is associated with prolonged corticosteroid use, autoimmune adrenalitis, or adrenal metastases. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyperpigmentation + Hyponatremia + Hyperkalemia + Hypoglycemia + Hypotension = Primary Adrenal insufficiency 

### D) Nephrotic syndrome 

Amiodarone is not associated with nephrotic syndrome or glomerular disease. Its renal toxicity, when it occurs, is generally limited to mild elevation in creatinine from interference with tubular creatinine secretion rather than true GFR reduction. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Proteinuria + Hypoalbuminemia + Edema + Hyperlipidemia = Nephrotic Syndrome
