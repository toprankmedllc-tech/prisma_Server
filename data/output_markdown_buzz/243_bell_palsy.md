## Buzzword Question

A 35-year-old man develops sudden right-sided facial droop on awakening, with inability to wrinkle the right forehead or fully close the right eye, decreased taste over the anterior right tongue, and sounds seeming unusually loud in the right ear. The rest of the neurologic examination is normal. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Acute unilateral facial weakness; forehead involvement (LMN pattern); hyperacusis; taste loss on anterior two-thirds of tongue; otherwise normal exam

## Answer Options

A) Stroke 
B) Trigeminal neuralgia
C) Acoustic neuroma
D) Bell palsy

## Correct Answer

D) Bell palsy

## Explanation

Acute unilateral peripheral CN VII palsy with FOREHEAD INVOLVEMENT, hyperacusis, and taste loss on the anterior two-thirds of the tongue is the classic Bell palsy pattern, attributed to HSV-1 reactivation at the geniculate ganglion.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Acute unilateral facial weakness + forehead involvement + hyperacusis + taste loss on anterior tongue = Bell palsy

## Wrong Options

### Stroke

Central (UMN) facial palsy SPARES the forehead because of bilateral cortical input. Forehead involvement here rules out a central cause.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden unilateral lower face droop SPARING forehead + arm weakness + slurred speech = central facial palsy (stroke)

### Trigeminal neuralgia

Trigeminal neuralgia presents with brief lancinating face PAIN triggered by light touch or chewing, not facial weakness or taste loss.

**Buzzword Combo:** Brief electric-shock facial pain triggered by touch or chewing + normal exam = trigeminal neuralgia

### Acoustic neuroma

Acoustic neuroma (vestibular schwannoma) at the cerebellopontine angle presents with progressive unilateral sensorineural hearing loss and tinnitus, sometimes with later CN VII involvement, not isolated acute Bell-pattern palsy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Progressive unilateral sensorineural hearing loss + tinnitus + CPA mass = vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma)

## Related Concepts

Central facial palsy from MCA stroke (forehead spared); Ramsay Hunt syndrome (VZV + ear vesicles); Lyme disease as a cause of bilateral facial palsy
