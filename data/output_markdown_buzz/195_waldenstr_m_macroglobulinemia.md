## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man with progressive blurred vision, recurrent epistaxis, headaches, dilated tortuous retinal vessels on fundoscopy, and an IgM monoclonal spike on serum protein electrophoresis most likely has which condition?

## Buzzwords Used

blurred vision; epistaxis; headaches; dilated retinal vessels; IgM monoclonal spike; hyperviscosity symptoms

## Answer Options

A. Multiple myeloma
B. Waldenström macroglobulinemia
C. Chronic lymphocytic leukemia
D. Amyloidosis

## Correct Answer

B. Waldenström macroglobulinemia

## Explanation

Waldenström macroglobulinemia is a lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma that produces monoclonal IgM, leading to hyperviscosity syndrome. This causes visual disturbances, neurologic symptoms, and mucosal bleeding due to impaired microcirculation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

IgM spike + hyperviscosity + retinal vein engorgement = Waldenström macroglobulinemia

## Wrong Options

### A. Multiple myeloma

Multiple myeloma typically produces IgG or IgA M protein and presents with CRAB features (hypercalcemia, renal failure, anemia, bone lesions), not hyperviscosity from IgM.

**Buzzword Combo:** IgG/IgA spike + CRAB features + lytic bone lesions = Multiple myeloma

### C. Chronic lymphocytic leukemia

CLL presents with lymphocytosis, smudge cells, and CD5+/CD23+ B cells, without IgM hyperviscosity syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Smudge cells + lymphocytosis + CD5/CD23 = CLL

### D. Amyloidosis

AL amyloidosis involves light chain deposition with Congo red-positive amyloid and organ dysfunction, not IgM monoclonal gammopathy or hyperviscosity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Congo red positive deposits + nephrotic syndrome = AL amyloidosis
