## Buzzword Question


A young patient with recurrent paroxysmal palpitations is found to have a short PR interval, a delta wave, and a widened QRS on ECG. Which of the following anatomic structures is responsible for these findings?

## Buzzwords Used

Short PR interval; delta wave; widened QRS

## Answer Options

A) Accessory pathway connecting the atria to the ventricles (bundle of Kent)
B) Slow and fast pathways within the AV node
C) Re-entrant circuit around a scarred ventricular myocardium
D) Ectopic focus in the pulmonary veins

## Correct Answer

A) Accessory pathway connecting the atria to the ventricles (bundle of Kent)

## Explanation

The bundle of Kent is an accessory pathway that directly connects the atria to the ventricles, bypassing the AV node. Because it lacks the AV node's conduction delay, ventricular depolarization begins early, producing the characteristic short PR + delta wave + widened QRS of Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Short PR + delta wave + widened QRS = bundle of Kent (WPW accessory pathway)

## Wrong Options

### B) Slow and fast pathways within the AV node

Describes the substrate for AV nodal reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT), in which the re-entrant circuit lies entirely within the AV node. AVNRT has a normal baseline ECG between episodes — no delta wave.

**Buzzword Combo:** Slow/fast intranodal pathways + paroxysmal SVT + normal baseline ECG = AVNRT

### C) Re-entrant circuit around a scarred ventricular myocardium

Describes the substrate for scar-related ventricular tachycardia, typically following prior myocardial infarction. Produces wide-complex VT, not pre-excitation findings.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prior MI + scar reentry + monomorphic VT = scar-related VT

### D) Ectopic focus in the pulmonary veins

Pulmonary vein ectopic foci are the main trigger for atrial fibrillation, which is the target of pulmonary vein isolation during AF ablation — not the substrate for WPW.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pulmonary vein ectopy + irregularly irregular rhythm = atrial fibrillation
