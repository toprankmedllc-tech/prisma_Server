## Buzzword Question

A 44-year-old obese man presents to the clinic with burning pain and numbness over his right thigh for the past several months. He reports that the symptoms worsen after prolonged standing and while wearing a tight tool belt at work. He denies weakness, difficulty walking, or lower back pain. Physical examination reveals decreased sensation over the lateral aspect of the thigh with normal muscle strength and intact deep tendon reflexes.
Compression of which of the following nerves is most likely responsible for this patient’s symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

44y; man

## Answer Options

A. Nerve providing sensory innervation to the anterolateral thigh
B. Nerve supplying motor innervation to the quadriceps muscles
C. Nerve providing sensation to the medial leg and foot
D. Nerve innervating the hamstring muscles and lower leg

## Correct Answer

A. Nerve providing sensory innervation to the anterolateral thigh

## Explanation

This patient most likely has meralgia paresthetica, caused by compression of the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve beneath the inguinal ligament. Patients classically present with: Burning pain ,Tingling or numbness over the lateral thigh ,Normal strength and reflexes .

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Obesity + tight belt + isolated lateral thigh numbness = lateral femoral cutaneous nerve compression

## Wrong Options

### B. Nerve supplying motor innervation to the quadriceps muscles

Incorrect — This describes the femoral nerve, which also contributes to the patellar reflex.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak knee extension + decreased patellar reflex = femoral neuropathy

### C. Nerve providing sensation to the medial leg and foot

Incorrect — This sensory distribution is supplied mainly by the saphenous nerve, a branch of the femoral nerve.

**Buzzword Combo:** Medial leg sensory loss = saphenous nerve involvement

### D. Nerve innervating the hamstring muscles and lower leg

Incorrect — This describes the sciatic nerve.

**Buzzword Combo:** Posterior thigh weakness + foot drop = sciatic nerve injury
