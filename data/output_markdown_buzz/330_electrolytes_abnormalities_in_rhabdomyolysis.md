## Buzzword Question

34-year-old construction worker is brought to the emergency department after his lower extremities were pinned under a collapsed concrete wall for 4 hours. On arrival, he is alert but complains of severe leg pain. Physical examination reveals extensive bruising and tense swelling of both thighs. Laboratory studies show a serum creatinine of 1.9 mg/dL, a serum potassium level of 6.8 mEq/L, and a serum creatine kinase (CK) level of 45,000 U/L. An ECG shows peaked T waves and widening of the QRS complex. Which of the following cellular mechanisms is primarily responsible for the rapid development of this patient's hyperkalemia?

## Buzzwords Used

Pinned under concrete wall; severe leg pain; serum potassium 6.8 mEq/L; creatine kinase 45,000 U/L; peaked T waves and widening QRS

## Answer Options

A) Mechanical rupture of cell membranes releasing intracellular ion stores
B) Ischemia-induced upregulation of the basolateral Na +/K +-ATPase pump
C) Metabolic alkalosis driving a transcellular hydrogen-potassium exchange
D) Acute secondary hyperaldosteronism causing profound renal cortical necrosis

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The patient is experiencing rhabdomyolysis from a crush injury, which causes direct mechanical and ischemic trauma to skeletal muscle cells. Because potassium is the primary intracellular cation, the subsequent lysis of the sarcolemma causes a massive, rapid release of intracellular potassium directly into the extracellular fluid space, overwhelming renal clearance.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Trauma/Crush injury + Markedly elevated creatine kinase + Severe hyperkalemia = Transcellular shift via rhabdomyolysis

## Wrong Options

### B

Ischemia actually depletes cellular ATP, which inhibits the energy-dependent basolateral Na+/K +
 -ATPase pump. This inhibition prevents potassium from being pumped into cells, worsening the extracellular accumulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** ATP depletion + Failure to pump potassium inward + Cellular swelling = Na+/K +-ATPase pump failure

### C

Crush injuries and tissue ischemia lead to a severe metabolic acidosis (lactic acidosis), not alkalosis. Acidosis drives hydrogen ions into cells in exchange for potassium ions moving outward into the extracellular fluid, which exacerbates hyperkalemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Lactic acidosis + Extracellular hydrogen buffering + Transcellular shift out of cells = Acidosis-induced hyperkalemia

### D

Hyperaldosteronism would cause increased renal potassium excretion and hypokalemia, which directly contradicts this patient’s high serum potassium level.

**Buzzword Combo:** High aldosterone levels + Cortical collecting duct activation + Urinary potassium wasting = Hypokalemia
