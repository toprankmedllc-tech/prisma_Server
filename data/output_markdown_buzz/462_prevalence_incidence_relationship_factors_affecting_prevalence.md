## Buzzword Question

A new effective treatment for type 2 diabetes is introduced that significantly reduces mortality without affecting incidence. Over the next decade, which of the following would be expected in the population?

## Buzzwords Used

Effective treatment introduced; reduces mortality; does not affect incidence; type 2 diabetes; population-level effect


## Answer Options

A) Prevalence increases because disease duration increases (patients live longer with the disease)
 B) Prevalence decreases because the disease is being better controlled 
C) Incidence decreases because fewer new cases will develop once treatment is available D) Prevalence and incidence both remain unchanged because the treatment only affects mortality

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Prevalence = Incidence × average disease duration. A treatment that reduces mortality (without affecting incidence or cure rate) increases the average duration of disease — patients with diabetes survive longer while still having the condition. Because prevalence is the product of incidence and duration, and duration increases while incidence remains constant, prevalence rises. This is why improved treatments for chronic diseases like HIV, cancer, and diabetes have historically INCREASED their prevalence despite improvements in outcomes.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Prevalence = Incidence × Duration; ↓ mortality (not cure) → ↑ duration → ↑ prevalence; incidence unchanged

## Wrong Options

### B) Prevalence decreases because the disease is being better controlled


Better control reduces morbidity and mortality but does not reduce prevalence — it increases it. Prevalence decreases when: incidence falls, disease duration shortens (cure or faster natural resolution), or migration of cases out of the population. A treatment that prevents death but does not cure the disease increases prevalence by extending disease duration.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prevalence ↓ when: ↓ incidence OR ↓ duration (cure/shorter illness); treatment that prevents death without cure → ↑ duration → ↑ prevalence

### C) Incidence decreases because fewer new cases will develop once treatment is available

Incidence measures the RATE of new case development and is determined by risk factors, prevention programs, and exposure reduction not by treatment of existing cases. Treatment affects those already diagnosed and does not prevent new cases from developing unless it is used prophylactically. The question specifies that incidence is not affected.

**Buzzword Combo:** Incidence = new cases/at-risk population per time; ↓ by: prevention, vaccination, risk factor elimination; treatment of existing cases does NOT affect incidence

### D) Prevalence and incidence both remain unchanged because the treatment only affects mortality

While incidence is unchanged (as stated), prevalence is NOT unchanged. Prevalence depends on both incidence and duration. Reducing mortality extends disease duration, which directly increases prevalence even when incidence remains constant.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prevalence ≠ incidence; prevalence changes whenever incidence OR duration changes; ↓ mortality → ↑ duration → ↑ prevalence
