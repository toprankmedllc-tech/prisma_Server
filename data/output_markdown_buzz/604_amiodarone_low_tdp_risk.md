## Buzzword Question

A 57-year-old woman with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation and heart failure with reduced ejection fraction is seen in the clinic for follow-up. She was started on a new antiarrhythmic for rhythm control 4 weeks ago. An electrocardiogram performed today shows normal sinus rhythm with a marked QTc interval of 548 ms (baseline 410 ms). Despite the prolonged QT interval, the physician decides to continue the medication with routine monitoring, noting that this specific agent has a paradoxically low incidence of inducing polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (torsades de pointes) compared to other drugs in its class. Which of the following medications was most likely prescribed?

A) Amiodarone
B) Flecainide
C) Lidocaine
D) Sotalol


## Buzzwords Used

prolonged QT interval; low incidence of torsades de pointes

## Answer Options

A) Amiodarone
B) Flecainide
C) Lidocaine
D) Sotalol

## Correct Answer

A) Amiodarone

## Explanation

Amiodarone is a broad-spectrum antiarrhythmic that prolongs the QT interval. However, unlike other Class III antiarrhythmics, it carries a substantially lower risk of inducing torsades de pointes. This effect is thought to be due to multi-channel blockade which reduces the dispersion of repolarization across the myocardium, making it highly effective for rhythm control with minimal torsades risk.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Blockage of K + Na + Ca + beta receptors = Amiodarone (Low Tdp risk)

## Wrong Options

### B) Flecainide

Flecainide is a Class IC sodium channel blocker that widens the QRS complex without significantly prolonging the QT interval. It is strictly contraindicated in patients with structural heart disease because it can induce monomorphic ventricular tachycardia, not torsades de pointes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Class IC + Na+ blockade + QRS widening = Flecainide

### C) Lidocaine

Lidocaine is a Class IB sodium channel blocker that rapidly associates and dissociates, effectively shortening the action potential duration. It does not prolong the QT interval and is not associated with torsades de pointes. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Class IB + shortens APD + used for ischemic VT = Lidocaine

### D) Sotalol

Sotalol is a Class III antiarrhythmic that provides pure blockade of the rapid delayed rectifier potassium channel (IKr), along with non-selective beta-blockade. It carries a high risk of torsades de pointes

**Buzzword Combo:** pure IKr blockade + non-selective beta-blockade = Sotalol
