## Buzzword Question

A 12-year-old boy with known tuberous sclerosis complex develops new-onset morning headaches with vomiting over 4 weeks. MRI of the brain shows a contrast-enhancing intraventricular mass arising near the foramen of Monro, with associated dilation of both lateral ventricles. The most likely lesion is:

## Buzzwords Used

Known TSC; morning headaches with vomiting (raised ICP); contrast-enhancing intraventricular mass at the foramen of Monro; obstructive hydrocephalus

## Answer Options

A) Medulloblastoma
B) Ependymoma
C) Glioblastoma multiforme
D) Subependymal giant cell astrocytoma

## Correct Answer

D) Subependymal giant cell astrocytoma

## Explanation

SEGA is a benign WHO grade I astrocytoma that arises from subependymal nodules near the foramen of Monro in 10 to 15% of TSC patients. Growth can obstruct CSF flow and produce hydrocephalus. Treatment options are surgical resection or mTOR inhibitors (everolimus, sirolimus).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

TSC + intraventricular mass at foramen of Monro + obstructive hydrocephalus = subependymal giant cell astrocytoma (SEGA)

## Wrong Options

### Medulloblastoma

Medulloblastoma is a pediatric malignant tumor of the cerebellar vermis with Homer-Wright rosettes; it is not located at the foramen of Monro and is not associated with TSC.

**Buzzword Combo:** Child + cerebellar vermis mass + Homer-Wright rosettes + drop metastases = medulloblastoma

### Ependymoma

Ependymoma typically arises in the fourth ventricle in children with perivascular pseudorosettes; it is not the typical TSC-associated CNS neoplasm and is not located at the foramen of Monro.

**Buzzword Combo:** Child + fourth ventricle mass + perivascular pseudorosettes + hydrocephalus = ependymoma

### Glioblastoma multiforme

GBM is a WHO grade IV astrocytoma of older adults with butterfly cerebral hemisphere distribution, ring enhancement, and pseudopalisading necrosis; not a typical pediatric or TSC-associated tumor.

**Buzzword Combo:** Older adult + butterfly mass across corpus callosum + pseudopalisading necrosis + GFAP positive = glioblastoma multiforme

## Related Concepts

mTOR inhibitors (everolimus, sirolimus) shrink SEGAs and renal angiomyolipomas in TSC; Cardiac rhabdomyoma often regresses spontaneously; Pulmonary lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM) in adult women with TSC
