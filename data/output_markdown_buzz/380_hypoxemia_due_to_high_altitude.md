## Buzzword Question

A mountain climber develops dyspnea and hypoxemia shortly after ascending to high altitude. Arterial blood gas reveals low PaO2 with a normal A-a gradient. Which mechanism best explains this finding?

## Buzzwords Used

 High altitude ; low PaO2 ; normal A-a gradient


## Answer Options

 A) Decreased inspired oxygen tension
 B) Right-to-left shunting
 C) Increased physiologic dead space
 D) Diffusion impairment across alveoli


## Correct Answer

 A) Decreased inspired oxygen tension


## Explanation

At high altitude, decreased barometric pressure lowers inspired oxygen tension, leading to hypoxemia with a normal A-a gradient.



## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

High altitude + low barometric pressure + normal A-a gradient = Decreased inspired oxygen tension


## Wrong Options

### B) Right-to-left shunting

Shunting causes increased A-a gradient and hypoxemia that does not fully correct with oxygen therapy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Congenital heart disease + refractory hypoxemia + increased A-a gradient = Right-to-left shunt


### C) Increased physiologic dead space

Dead space ventilation occurs in pulmonary embolism and causes V/Q mismatch with elevated A-a gradient.


**Buzzword Combo:** Pulmonary embolism + tachycardia + elevated A-a gradient = Increased dead space


###  D) Diffusion impairment across alveoli


Diffusion impairment occurs in interstitial lung diseases and produces elevated A-a gradient hypoxemia.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Fibrosis + exertional dyspnea + elevated A-a gradient = Diffusion impairment
