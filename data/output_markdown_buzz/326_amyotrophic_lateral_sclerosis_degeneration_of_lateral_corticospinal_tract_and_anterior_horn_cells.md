## Buzzword Question

A 56-year-old man presents with progressive weakness in his hands and legs over the past year. Examination shows muscle atrophy, fasciculations, spasticity, hyperreflexia, and bilateral Babinski signs. Sensory examination is normal.

Degeneration of which spinal cord tract is responsible for this patient’s upper motor neuron findings?

## Buzzwords Used

Mixed UMN and LMN signs ; fasciculations ; muscle atrophy ; hyperreflexia ; preserved sensation

## Answer Options

A) Dorsal columns
B) Spinothalamic tract
C) Lateral corticospinal tract
D) Spinocerebellar tract

## Correct Answer

C) Lateral corticospinal tract

## Explanation

The lateral corticospinal tract carries descending upper motor neuron fibers responsible for voluntary movement. Degeneration causes spasticity, hyperreflexia, and Babinski signs, which are classic UMN findings seen in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Spasticity + hyperreflexia + Babinski sign = Lateral corticospinal tract degeneration

## Wrong Options

### A) Dorsal columns

Dorsal column lesions produce loss of vibration and proprioception with sensory ataxia, not UMN motor findings.

**Buzzword Combo:** Positive Romberg + vibration loss + sensory ataxia = Dorsal column lesion

### B) Spinothalamic tract

Spinothalamic tract lesions cause pain and temperature sensory deficits rather than spastic weakness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pain/temp loss + contralateral sensory deficit + cord lesion = Spinothalamic tract lesion

### D) Spinocerebellar tract

Spinocerebellar tract lesions lead to ataxia and impaired unconscious proprioception, not Babinski signs or spasticity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ataxia + dysmetria + unsteady gait = Spinocerebellar tract lesion
