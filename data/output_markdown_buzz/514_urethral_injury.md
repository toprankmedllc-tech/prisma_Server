## Buzzword Question

A patient is brought to the emergency department after a pelvic fracture from a motor vehicle collision. He is unable to urinate and has gross hematuria. Which of the following physical examination findings most strongly suggests posterior urethral injury?

## Buzzwords Used

Pelvic fracture; Anuria; Hematuria

## Answer Options

A) High-riding prostate on rectal examination
B) Costovertebral angle tenderness
C) Perineal ecchymosis
D) Suprapubic fullness with urinary retention

## Correct Answer

A) High-riding prostate on rectal examination

## Explanation

Posterior urethral injury commonly occurs after pelvic fractures and may cause urethral disruption with displacement of the prostate superiorly, producing a “high-riding” prostate on rectal examination. Patients often present with inability to void and gross hematuria

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pelvic fracture + high-riding prostate + inability to void = Posterior urethral injury

## Wrong Options

### Costovertebral angle tenderness

Costovertebral angle tenderness is more suggestive of renal pathology such as pyelonephritis or renal trauma rather than urethral disruption. It reflects inflammation or injury involving the kidneys.

**Buzzword Combo:** Flank tenderness + renal inflammation + pyelonephritis = Costovertebral Angle tenderness

###  Perineal ecchymosis

Perineal ecchymosis may occur after pelvic trauma and can suggest underlying genitourinary injury. However, it is less specific for posterior urethral disruption than a high-riding prostate.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pelvic trauma + Perineal ecchymosis + soft tissue injury = Urethral Injury

### Suprapubic fullness with urinary retention

Suprapubic fullness may occur in bladder outlet obstruction or urinary retention and can also be seen in bladder injury. It is less specific for posterior urethral disruption than a high-riding prostate

**Buzzword Combo:** Urinary retention + suprapubic distention= Geinatourinary injury
