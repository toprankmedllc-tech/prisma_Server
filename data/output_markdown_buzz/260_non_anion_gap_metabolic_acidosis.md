## Buzzword Question

A 55-year-old man with CKD stage 3 has a routine ABG: pH 7.34, PaCO₂ 30 mmHg, HCO₃ 16 mEq/L. He is asymptomatic. Serum Na 140, Cl 108. What is the primary acid-base disturbance?

## Buzzwords Used

CKD; Asymptomatic; pH 7.34; PaCO₂ 30; HCO₃ 16; Normal anion gap; Metabolic acidosis; Hyperchloremia

## Answer Options

A) Respiratory alkalosis with metabolic compensation
B) High anion gap metabolic acidosis from uremia
C) Normal anion gap metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation
D) Mixed respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

pH is acidemic and HCO₃ is low — metabolic acidosis. Anion gap = 140 − 108 − 16 = 16, which is normal (8–12 with some variation, borderline here but not elevated). In early-to-moderate CKD, the kidney loses the ability to excrete ammonium, causing a hyperchloremic (normal anion gap) metabolic acidosis — not a high anion gap acidosis, which occurs in ESRD. Low PaCO₂ is appropriate respiratory compensation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

CKD stage 3 + hyperchloremia + normal anion gap + low HCO₃ + acidemic pH = Normal anion gap metabolic acidosis with compensation

## Wrong Options

### B) High anion gap metabolic acidosis from uremia

High anion gap uremic acidosis occurs in advanced/end-stage CKD. In earlier CKD stages, impaired ammonium excretion causes a normal anion gap (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis. Calculating the anion gap is essential.

**Buzzword Combo:** CKD + metabolic acidosis = Premature high anion gap label from uremia association without calculating the actual anion gap

### A) Respiratory alkalosis with metabolic compensation

The pH is 7.34 — acidemic. A primary respiratory alkalosis produces an alkalemic pH. The low PaCO₂ here is compensatory hyperventilation in response to the metabolic acidosis, not a primary process.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low PaCO₂ noticed first + alkalosis association = Premature respiratory alkalosis label before checking the acidemic pH

### D) Mixed respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis

A true mixed disorder would require the PaCO₂ to be lower than expected for compensation alone. The PaCO₂ of 30 in the setting of HCO₃ of 16 is consistent with appropriate compensation — no independent respiratory alkalosis needs to be invoked.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two abnormal values + acidemia = Mixed disorder label when second value represents appropriate compensation
