## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old man develops sudden right-sided peripheral facial weakness, severe ear pain, sensorineural hearing loss, and a cluster of painful vesicles inside the right external auditory canal and on the right tympanic membrane. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Peripheral facial palsy; severe ear pain; sensorineural hearing loss; vesicular rash in the external auditory canal; geniculate ganglion involvement

## Answer Options

A) Bell palsy
B) Acute otitis externa
C) Ramsay Hunt syndrome
D) Acoustic neuroma

## Correct Answer

C) Ramsay Hunt syndrome

## Explanation

Ramsay Hunt syndrome (herpes zoster oticus) is VZV reactivation within the geniculate ganglion of CN VII. The triad of peripheral facial palsy plus ear pain with vesicles plus sensorineural hearing loss is diagnostic. Treatment is oral acyclovir or valacyclovir plus corticosteroids.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Peripheral facial palsy + ear pain with vesicles in the external auditory canal + sensorineural hearing loss = Ramsay Hunt syndrome (VZV in geniculate ganglion)

## Wrong Options

### Bell palsy

Bell palsy produces a similar peripheral facial palsy but WITHOUT ear vesicles or sensorineural hearing loss. The vesicles and hearing loss here implicate VZV in the geniculate ganglion.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acute peripheral facial palsy + forehead involvement + hyperacusis + taste loss + no ear vesicles = Bell palsy

### Acute otitis externa

Otitis externa is bacterial or fungal inflammation of the ear canal with discharge and tenderness on tragal pressure, not facial palsy with vesicles.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ear pain + tragal tenderness + ear canal discharge + recent swimming = otitis externa

### Acoustic neuroma

Acoustic neuroma is a slowly growing cerebellopontine angle schwannoma with progressive unilateral sensorineural hearing loss and tinnitus, not acute vesicular rash with facial palsy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Progressive unilateral sensorineural hearing loss + tinnitus + CPA mass = acoustic neuroma (vestibular schwannoma)
