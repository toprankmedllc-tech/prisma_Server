## Buzzword Question

A 74-year-old man is admitted after a hip fracture and undergoes surgery. On hospital day 8, he develops fever, new hypoxia, and purulent sputum while still hospitalized. Chest x-ray shows a new right lower lobe infiltrate. He has no recent travel or sick contacts. Sputum culture grows Gram-negative rods on MacConkey agar. Which of the following best classifies this pneumonia?

## Buzzwords Used

Hospital day 8; post-surgery admission; new fever in hospital; new infiltrate on chest X-ray; Gram-negative rods; hospitalized patient >48 hours

## Answer Options

A) Community-acquired pneumonia
B) Aspiration pneumonia
C) Hospital-acquired pneumonia
D) Atypical pneumonia

## Correct Answer

C) Hospital-acquired pneumonia

## Explanation




Pneumonia developing ≥48 hours after hospital admission (hospital day 8) defines hospital-acquired pneumonia, caused by hospital flora such as Gram-negative rods. Timing is the key distinguishing feature from other pneumonia types.



## Buzzword Combination (Correct)


Hospital stay >48 hours + new infiltrate + inpatient setting = hospital-acquired pneumonia

## Wrong Options

### A) Community-acquired pneumonia

CAP occurs at presentation or within 48 hours of admission, not after prolonged hospitalization. This patient is already in ICU on day 8, making CAP incorrect.

**Buzzword Combo:** Outpatient onset + early admission pneumonia + no hospital flora exposure = CAP mismatch

### B) Aspiration pneumonia

Aspiration pneumonia typically follows vomiting, decreased consciousness, or dysphagia and often involves anaerobes with foul-smelling sputum, which are not described here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Altered consciousness + foul odor sputum + anaerobes = aspiration pattern

### D) Atypical pneumonia

Atypical pneumonia presents with dry cough, interstitial infiltrates, and organisms like Mycoplasma or Legionella, not Gram-negative rods or hospital-onset infection.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dry cough + walking pneumonia + interstitial pattern = atypical infection
