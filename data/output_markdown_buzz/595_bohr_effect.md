## Buzzword Question

A 24-year-old athlete undergoing intense sprint training develops localized muscle hypoxia. The working skeletal muscles show increased CO₂ production and decreased pH, which facilitates oxygen unloading from hemoglobin. Which of the following best explains this physiological adaptation?

## Buzzwords Used

Exercise; skeletal muscle hypoxia; increased CO₂; decreased pH; increased metabolic activity; oxygen unloading; working muscles

## Answer Options

A) Increased CO₂ decreases hemoglobin affinity for oxygen
B) Increased CO₂ increases hemoglobin affinity for oxygen
C) Increased O₂ increases CO₂ binding to hemoglobin
D) Decreased CO₂ increases oxygen binding to hemoglobin

## Correct Answer

A) Increased CO₂ decreases hemoglobin affinity for oxygen

## Explanation

This describes the Bohr effect, where increased CO₂ and decreased pH in active tissues reduce hemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen. This promotes oxygen unloading in metabolically active skeletal muscle.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Exercise; ↑CO₂; ↓pH; Bohr effect; right shift; O₂ unloading

## Wrong Options

### B) Increased CO₂ increases hemoglobin affinity for oxygen

This is incorrect because increased CO₂ causes a right shift, decreasing oxygen affinity rather than increasing it. It facilitates oxygen release, not binding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Incorrect affinity direction; opposite of Bohr effect

### C) Increased O₂ increases CO₂ binding to hemoglobin

This is unrelated to the Bohr effect and describes oxygen-related changes in CO₂ transport (Haldane effect), not oxygen unloading in tissues.

**Buzzword Combo:** Haldane effect; oxygenated hemoglobin; CO₂ transport

### D) Decreased CO₂ increases oxygen binding to hemoglobin

Decreased CO₂ and increased pH cause a left shift, increasing oxygen affinity. However, this occurs in lungs, not active exercising tissues.

**Buzzword Combo:** Left shift; alkalosis; lungs; oxygen loading
