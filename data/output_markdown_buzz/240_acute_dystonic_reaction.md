## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old man brought in for a first psychotic episode received a single intramuscular dose of haloperidol 6 hours ago. He now presents with sudden sustained upward deviation of both eyes, painful tilting of the neck to one side, and protrusion of the tongue, with no fever, no tachycardia, and a normal mental status. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Recent single antipsychotic dose; sudden sustained dystonic eye deviation (oculogyric); painful neck twisting (torticollis); tongue protrusion; afebrile; preserved mental status; no autonomic instability

## Answer Options

A) Tardive dyskinesia
B) Neuroleptic malignant syndrome
C) Acute dystonic reaction
D) Drug-induced parkinsonism

## Correct Answer

C) Acute dystonic reaction

## Explanation

Acute dystonic reaction is an extrapyramidal side effect occurring within hours to a few days of starting a dopamine D2 antagonist (typical antipsychotic, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine). It produces sudden sustained dystonic postures (oculogyric crisis, torticollis, tongue protrusion). Treatment is IM diphenhydramine or benztropine.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recent D2 antagonist + sudden oculogyric / torticollis / tongue dystonia + afebrile + normal mental status = acute dystonic reaction

## Wrong Options

### Tardive dyskinesia

Tardive dyskinesia develops after months to years of D2 antagonist exposure and produces involuntary choreoathetoid movements of the face and tongue (lip smacking, grimacing), not the acute sustained dystonia seen here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic antipsychotic use + involuntary lip smacking and grimacing + choreoathetoid movements = tardive dyskinesia

### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

NMS develops over days to weeks of antipsychotic exposure and produces fever, lead-pipe rigidity, altered mental status, and markedly elevated CK. This patient is afebrile and lacks these features.

**Buzzword Combo:** Antipsychotic exposure + lead-pipe rigidity + fever + autonomic instability + CK greater than 1000 = neuroleptic malignant syndrome

### Drug-induced parkinsonism

Drug-induced parkinsonism develops over weeks to months of antipsychotic exposure and produces bradykinesia, rigidity, and tremor, not acute sustained dystonic posturing.

**Buzzword Combo:** Subacute antipsychotic exposure + symmetric bradykinesia, rigidity, and tremor = drug-induced parkinsonism

## Related Concepts

Tardive dyskinesia (late EPS, often irreversible); Akathisia (subjective restlessness, inability to stay still); Drug-induced parkinsonism (subacute symmetric parkinsonism, reverses with drug withdrawal)
