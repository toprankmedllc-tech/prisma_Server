## Buzzword Question

A thin elderly smoker has severe dyspnea, minimal cough, barrel chest, and pursed-lip breathing. What is the primary pathology?



## Buzzwords Used

minimal cough; barrel shaped chest; pursed-lip breathing

## Answer Options

A. Mucus plugging of airways
B. Loss of alveolar walls and elastic recoil
C. Bronchial smooth muscle hyperplasia
D. Dilatation  of bronchial tree

## Correct Answer

B. Loss of alveolar walls and elastic recoil

## Explanation

This patient has  Emphysema- subtype of COPD, where destruction of alveolar septa leads to loss of elastic recoil, air trapping, and hyperinflation (barrel chest). Patients are “pink puffers” with severe dyspnea but minimal cough.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Barrel- shaped Chest + chronic smoker + dyspnea+ pursed-lip breathing

## Wrong Options

### A. Mucus plugging of airways

This is seen in Chronic Bronchitis, which presents with chronic productive cough and cyanosis rather than marked dyspnea alone. It is due to the mucus gland hypertrophy and impaired mucociliary clearance, not alveolar destruction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic Smoker +Cyanosis + Dypsnea+ chronic productive cough = Chronic Bronchitis

### C. Bronchial smooth muscle hyperplasia

This is characteristic of Asthma, which causes reversible airway obstruction with episodic wheezing, often triggered by allergens or exercise. It does not cause progressive hyperinflation with alveolar destruction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Episodic wheezing + reversible airway obstruction+ Eospinophils + Curshmann spirals= Asthma

### D. Dilatation of bronchial airway


This suggests Bronchiectasis, which presents with chronic purulent sputum and recurrent infections due to permanent bronchial dilation. It is not characterized by barrel chest or loss of elastic recoil.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic purulent sputum + recurrent respiratory infections + Tram- Track sign on HRCT = bronchiectasis
“Tram-track” sign → parallel thickened bronchial walls due to dilated bronchi
