## Buzzword Question

A 6-year-old boy has recurrent urinary tract infections. Imaging demonstrates a duplicated ureter draining the upper pole of the right kidney. Which embryologic abnormality most likely caused this finding?

## Buzzwords Used

Duplicated ureter ; Recurrent urinary tract infections ; Ureteric bud abnormality

## Answer Options

A) Premature division of the ureteric bud
B) Failure of ascent of the metanephros
C) Persistence of the urachus
D) Failure of cloacal partitioning

## Correct Answer

A) Premature division of the ureteric bud

## Explanation

Duplicated ureters occur when the ureteric bud divides prematurely during embryogenesis. This can predispose patients to vesicoureteral reflux and recurrent urinary tract infections.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Duplicated ureter + Recurrent UTIs + Upper pole drainage abnormality = Premature ureteric bud division

## Wrong Options

### B) Failure of ascent of the metanephros

Failure of kidney ascent causes pelvic kidney or horseshoe kidney rather than duplicated ureters.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pelvic kidney + Horseshoe kidney + Inferior mesenteric artery trapping = Failed ascent

### C) Persistence of the urachus

Persistent urachal remnants cause urine leakage from the umbilicus or urachal cysts, not duplicated collecting systems.

**Buzzword Combo:** Umbilical urine leakage + Midline cyst + Urachal sinus = Persistent urachus

### D) Failure of cloacal partitioning

Improper cloacal division can lead to anorectal and urogenital malformations but does not produce duplicated ureters.

**Buzzword Combo:** Imperforate anus + Urogenital anomalies + Persistent cloaca = Cloacal partition defect
