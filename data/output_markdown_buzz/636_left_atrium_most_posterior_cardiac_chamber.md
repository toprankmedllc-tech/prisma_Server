## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old woman with known severe mitral stenosis reports progressive difficulty swallowing solid foods over the past year. Upper endoscopy is unremarkable. A barium swallow study shows posterior indentation and displacement of the esophagus. Which of the following cardiac chambers is most likely responsible?

A) Right ventricle 
B) Right atrium 
C) Left ventricle 
D) Left atrium


## Buzzwords Used

Mitral stenosis; Difficulty swallowing foods; Posterior indentation of esophagus.

## Answer Options

A) Right ventricle 
B) Right atrium 
C) Left ventricle 
D) Left atrium

## Correct Answer

D) Left atrium

## Explanation

The left atrium is the most posterior cardiac chambers and lies in direct contact with the anterior wall of the esophagus. Significant left atrial enlargement which occurs in severe mitral stenosis from chronic pressure and volume overload can compress and displace the esophagus posteriorly, causing dysphagia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Posterior esophageal displacement + mitral stenosis = Left atrium enlargement

## Wrong Options

### A) Right ventricle 

The right ventricle forms the anterior surface of the heart and faces the sternum. It has no anatomical relationship to the esophagus

**Buzzword Combo:** Anterior surface + inferior border = Right ventricle

### B) Right atrium 

The right atrium forms the right cardiac border and lies to the right of midline. It does not contact the esophagus. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Right atrium = right cardiac border

### C) Left ventricle 

The left ventricle forms the left lateral cardiac border and apex. Although it is a posterior structure relative to the RV, it does not lie in direct contact with the esophagus. The left atrium is more superior and posterior. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Left lateral border + apex = Left ventricle
