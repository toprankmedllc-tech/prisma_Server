## Buzzword Question

A 50-year-old man with a large right-sided acute epidural hematoma becomes progressively unresponsive. On examination, the right pupil is fixed and dilated, and the left arm and leg do not move to painful stimulation. The most likely herniation syndrome is:

## Buzzwords Used

Right-sided supratentorial mass; ipsilateral (right) fixed dilated pupil; contralateral (left) hemiparesis

## Answer Options

A) Subfalcine (cingulate) herniation under the falx cerebri
B) Central transtentorial herniation
C) Cerebellar tonsillar herniation through the foramen magnum
D) Uncal (transtentorial) herniation through the tentorial notch

## Correct Answer

D) Uncal (transtentorial) herniation through the tentorial notch

## Explanation

Standard uncal herniation produces an ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil (CN III compression) and contralateral hemiparesis (compression of the ipsilateral cerebral peduncle, with corticospinal decussation in the medulla). This is the classic pattern from a large supratentorial mass.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Supratentorial mass + ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil + contralateral hemiparesis = uncal herniation (transtentorial)

## Wrong Options

### Subfalcine (cingulate) herniation under the falx cerebri

Subfalcine herniation compresses the ACA and produces contralateral lower extremity weakness, not a fixed dilated pupil or hemiparesis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Frontal mass + ACA compression + contralateral leg weakness more than arm = subfalcine (cingulate) herniation

### Central transtentorial herniation

Central transtentorial herniation produces bilateral diencephalic and brainstem findings (small or midposition pupils, decorticate then decerebrate posturing, Cheyne-Stokes), not a unilateral fixed dilated pupil.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bilateral small / midposition pupils + decerebrate posturing + Cheyne-Stokes = central transtentorial herniation

### Cerebellar tonsillar herniation through the foramen magnum

Tonsillar herniation compresses the medulla and produces cardiorespiratory collapse and rapid death, not a fixed dilated pupil with contralateral hemiparesis from a supratentorial mass.

**Buzzword Combo:** Posterior fossa mass + cardiorespiratory collapse + loss of brainstem reflexes = cerebellar tonsillar herniation (foramen magnum)

## Related Concepts

Kernohan notch phenomenon (false localizing ipsilateral hemiparesis); Anatomic course of CN III over the tentorial edge; Corticospinal tract decussation in the medulla
