## Buzzword Question

A 64-year-old multiparous woman presents with a sensation of pelvic heaviness and “something coming out” of her vagina that worsens with prolonged standing. She has had 6 vaginal deliveries. Pelvic examination reveals descent of the cervix into the vaginal canal. Weakness of which of the following structures is the most likely cause of this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Multiparous woman; Pelvic heaviness; Cervix descending into vagina; Worsens with standing; Pelvic organ prolapse; Vaginal deliveries

## Answer Options

A) Round ligament
B) Broad ligament
C) Cardinal and uterosacral ligaments
D) Suspensory ligament of the ovary

## Correct Answer

C) Cardinal and uterosacral ligaments

## Explanation

The cardinal and uterosacral ligaments provide the primary support for the uterus and cervix. Weakness of these ligaments, often due to multiple vaginal deliveries, can lead to uterine prolapse.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Multiparity + Cervix protrusion + Pelvic heaviness = Uterine prolapse from cardinal/uterosacral ligament weakness

## Wrong Options

### A) Round ligament

The round ligament helps maintain the anteverted position of the uterus but is not the primary support preventing prolapse. Pain in this ligament is common during pregnancy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pregnancy + Groin pain + Maintains anteversion = Round ligament

### B) Broad ligament

The broad ligament is a peritoneal fold that supports pelvic structures but does not provide major structural support against uterine descent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Double peritoneal fold + Uterus attached to pelvic wall + Mesometrium = Broad ligament

### D) Suspensory ligament of the ovary

The suspensory ligament contains the ovarian vessels and is clinically important in ovarian torsion or surgical ligation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Contains ovarian vessels + Lateral pelvic wall + Ovarian torsion = Suspensory ligament of ovary
