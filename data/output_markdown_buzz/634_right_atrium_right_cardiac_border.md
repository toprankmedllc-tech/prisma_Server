## Buzzword Question

A radiologist reviewing a posteroanterior chest radiograph describes a bulging of the right cardiac border. An echocardiogram subsequently confirms dilation of the chamber responsible for this contour. Which of the following chambers forms the right cardiac border on a standard PA chest radiograph?

A) Right ventricle 
B) Left atrium 
C) Right atrium 
D) Left ventricle


## Buzzwords Used

right cardiac border

## Answer Options

A) Right ventricle 
B) Left atrium 
C) Right atrium 
D) Left ventricle

## Correct Answer

C) Right atrium 

## Explanation

The right atrium forms the right cardiac border on a posteroanterior chest radiograph. Conditions causing right atrial enlargement — such as tricuspid stenosis, tricuspid regurgitation, or right-sided heart failure — produce a prominent convex right heart border on imaging.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Right cardiac border on PA CXR + bulging right border = right atrial enlargement

## Wrong Options

### A) Right ventricle 

The right ventricle forms the anterior cardiac surface and the inferior border — it does not contribute to the right heart border on a PA projection. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Anterior surface + inferior border = Right ventricle. 

### B) Left atrium 

The left atrium forms the posterior cardiac surface and, when enlarged, causes straightening or convexity of the left mid-cardiac border (double density sign, splaying of the carina). It does not form the right cardiac border. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Posterior cardiac surface + esophagus compression = Left atrium enlargement 

### D) Left ventricle 

The left ventricle forms the left cardiac border and the cardiac apex. Left ventricular enlargement causes the apex to shift downward and laterally it does not affect the right cardiac border. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Left cardiac border + apex = Left ventricle

## Related Concepts

Left atrial enlargement; Cardiac tamponade; Right ventricular hypertrophy
