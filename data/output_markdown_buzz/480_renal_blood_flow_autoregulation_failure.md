## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old woman with severe osteoarthritis presents to the emergency department complaining of decreased urine output and generalized malaise for three days. She reports taking high doses of ibuprofen for the past two weeks to manage severe joint pain. Laboratory analysis reveals an acute elevation in serum creatinine to 2.8 mg/dL from her baseline of 1.0 mg/dL. Which of the following vascular changes represents the primary tubuloglomerular feedback response initiated specifically by macula densa cells sensing a decreased solute delivery?

## Buzzwords Used

High doses of ibuprofen; Acute elevation in serum creatinine; Tubuloglomerular feedback; Macula densa cells

## Answer Options

A) Vasoconstriction of the efferent arteriole via Angiotensin II
B) Vasodilation of the afferent arteriole via specialized paracrine signaling
C) Vasoconstriction of the afferent arteriole via myogenic stretch receptors
D) Vasodilation of the efferent arteriole via nitric oxide pathways

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

When renal perfusion pressure drops, GFR drops, slowing tubular fluid flow and leading to increased NaCl reabsorption in the proximal segments, which leaves less NaCl delivered to the macula densa. In response to sensing low NaCl, macula densa cells trigger a paracrine-mediated vasodilation of the afferent arteriole to restore renal blood flow and GFR back toward normal.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Decreased solute delivery + Macula densa cells + Autoregulatory response = Vasodilation of the afferent arteriole via specialized paracrine signaling

## Wrong Options

### A) Vasoconstriction of the efferent arteriole via Angiotensin II

While angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction of the efferent arteriole does happen during hypoperfusion to sustain GFR, this is a systemic neurohumoral/RAAS response. It is not the local tubuloglomerular feedback mechanism managed directly by the macula densa's signaling to the afferent system. This functions as a massive Premature Closure trap for students who see "NSAID/Ibuprofen block prostaglandins, so Angiotensin II runs wild" and pick it immediately.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ibuprofen use + Sustaining filtration fraction = Vasoconstriction of the efferent arteriole via Angiotensin II

### C) Vasoconstriction of the afferent arteriole via myogenic stretch receptors

The myogenic mechanism responds directly to high arterial pressures stretching smooth muscle walls, resulting in reflexive vasoconstriction. It is separate from the chemical-sensing tubuloglomerular feedback pathway of the macula densa and acts in the opposite directional pressure shift.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increased arterial pressure + Smooth muscle stretch = Vasoconstriction of the afferent arteriole via myogenic stretch receptors

### D) Vasodilation of the efferent arteriole via nitric oxide pathways

Vasodilation of the efferent arteriole would cause a dangerous plummeting of the glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure , drastically dropping GFR further during a state of baseline low perfusion.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low renal perfusion pressure + Decreased filtration fraction = Vasodilation of the efferent arteriole via nitric oxide pathways
