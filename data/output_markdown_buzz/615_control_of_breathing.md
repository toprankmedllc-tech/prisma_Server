## Buzzword Question

A patient with medullary damage loses automatic respiratory rhythm but retains intact carotid body function. Which function is most impaired?

## Buzzwords Used

Medullary damage; loss of automatic breathing; intact carotid bodies; brainstem respiratory center; central pattern generator; respiratory rhythm

## Answer Options

A) Oxygen sensing
B) CO₂-driven respiratory rhythm generation
C) Blood pressure regulation
D) Airway resistance control

## Correct Answer

B) CO₂-driven respiratory rhythm generation

## Explanation

The medulla contains the central pattern generator responsible for automatic respiratory rhythm generation, which is primarily driven by CO₂/H⁺ levels. Damage to the medulla abolishes this automatic drive even if peripheral chemoreceptors remain intact.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Medullary injury + loss of automatic breathing + intact carotid bodies → failure of CO₂-driven central respiratory rhythm

## Wrong Options

### A) Oxygen sensing

Oxygen sensing is performed by peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid and aortic bodies), not the medulla, and remains intact in this patient.

**Buzzword Combo:** Peripheral chemoreceptors + hypoxemia detection + intact function

### C) Blood pressure regulation

Blood pressure regulation is mediated by baroreceptors and autonomic centers in the medulla but is not the primary function lost in respiratory rhythm failure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Baroreflex + autonomic control + cardiovascular regulation

### D) Airway resistance control

Airway resistance is regulated by autonomic control of bronchial smooth muscle, not by the medullary respiratory rhythm generator.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bronchoconstriction + autonomic tone + peripheral airway regulation
