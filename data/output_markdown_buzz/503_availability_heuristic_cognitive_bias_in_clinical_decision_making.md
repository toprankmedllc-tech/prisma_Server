## Buzzword Question

A senior resident is on overnight call when she admits her third patient this week with pulmonary embolism. The following morning, a 44-year-old woman presents with pleuritic chest pain, mild dyspnea, and a heart rate of 102 bpm. The resident orders a CT pulmonary angiogram immediately, before completing a full history, without calculating a pre-test probability score. The history later reveals the patient recently returned from a 14-hour international flight and has no other risk factors for PE; however, the CT scan is negative and the patient is diagnosed with musculoskeletal chest pain. The cognitive error that most directly led to the immediate CT order is:

## Buzzwords Used

Three recent PE admissions; pleuritic chest pain; tachycardia; CT ordered before pre-test probability; negative CT; musculoskeletal chest pain

## Answer Options

A) Availability heuristic — recent repeated exposure to PE cases inflated the perceived probability of PE beyond what the clinical data justified
B) Anchoring bias — the resident anchored to tachycardia as the primary diagnostic clue and failed to consider musculoskeletal causes
C) Premature closure — the resident stopped the diagnostic workup once CT-PE was negative and did not pursue further investigation
D) Overdiagnosis bias — the resident knowingly over-tested to avoid medicolegal risk, not due to cognitive error


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The availability heuristic is a mental shortcut in which the probability of an event is estimated based on how easily similar examples come to mind. Because the resident had recently admitted three PE patients, making PE cognitively 'available',  she overestimated the pre-test probability of PE in this patient and bypassed structured pre-test probability scoring (e.g., Wells criteria). The clinical data actually supported a low pre-test probability: young woman, single risk factor (recent long-haul flight), pleuritic pain that could be musculoskeletal. Availability bias is specifically triggered by the salience of recent, vivid, or repeated experiences,  exactly the three recent PE admissions.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recent repeated similar diagnosis + overestimated probability + bypassed pre-test scoring = Availability heuristic


## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Anchoring bias

Anchoring requires a pre-formed initial diagnosis that is not revised. Here the resident did not anchor to a prior label, she formed her PE suspicion rapidly based on recent experience, not a documented prior impression. The error is about the source of probability estimation, not the failure to revise.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prior label in chart + discordant new findings + unchanged management = Anchoring bias

### 2: C) Premature closure

Premature closure describes stopping the diagnostic process after reaching a diagnosis. The resident did not stop after a diagnosis, she stopped before one (no pre-test probability was calculated) and the imaging was negative. The error occurred at the initiation of the workup, not at its termination.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diagnosis accepted without considering alternatives + workup terminated early = Premature closure

### 3: D) Overdiagnosis bias

Overdiagnosis bias is not a standard cognitive heuristic in behavioral science. The resident's decision was driven by cognitive availability, not calculated risk-aversion or medicolegal strategy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Conscious decision to over-test for liability purposes = Defensive medicine (not a cognitive bias)
