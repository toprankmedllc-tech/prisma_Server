## Buzzword Question

A 76-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after being found wandering in his neighborhood wearing pajamas in the middle of the day. His family reports that over the past 24 hours, he has become increasingly withdrawn, speaks very little, and has had repeated episodes of urinary incontinence.

On examination, he is awake but minimally responsive. He does not initiate speech unless directly questioned and answers in single words. When asked to lift his limbs, he demonstrates marked weakness of the right lower extremity with near-normal strength in the right upper extremity and face. Deep tendon reflexes are increased in the right leg, and there is an extensor plantar response. He has a grasp reflex in the right hand. Sensory examination shows decreased pain and temperature sensation in the right lower limb.

Noncontrast CT head shows no hemorrhage. CT angiography reveals occlusion of a cerebral artery supplying the medial surface of the frontal and parietal lobes.

Which of the following is the most likely additional finding?

## Buzzwords Used

Abulia ; mutism ; urinary incontinence ; frontal release signs ; grasp reflex ; contralateral leg weakness > arm ; medial frontal cortex ; ACA territory infarct ; apathy ; behavioral disinhibition

## Answer Options

A) Contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing
B) Contralateral face and arm weakness with aphasia
C) Ipsilateral cerebellar ataxia with intention tremor
D) Ipsilateral Horner syndrome with anhidrosis

## Correct Answer

A) Contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing

## Explanation

Anterior Cerebral Artery Stroke can extend posteriorly to involve medial parietal cortex and adjacent deep structures supplied by ACA branches. This may produce contralateral lower limb motor/sensory deficits along with involvement of adjacent visual pathways causing homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing in extensive ACA infarcts.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Abulia + urinary incontinence + leg weakness + medial cortical infarct = ACA stroke with visual pathway extension

## Wrong Options

### B) Contralateral face and arm weakness with aphasia

Middle Cerebral Artery Stroke affects lateral cerebral cortex causing face/arm > leg weakness and aphasia (dominant hemisphere), not isolated leg-predominant deficits or frontal release signs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Face/arm weakness + aphasia + gaze deviation = MCA stroke

### C) Ipsilateral cerebellar ataxia with intention tremor

Cerebellar Stroke causes ipsilateral coordination deficits, dysmetria, and intention tremor due to cerebellar hemisphere involvement, not cortical behavioral or motor syndromes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vertigo + dysmetria + nystagmus = cerebellar stroke

### D) Ipsilateral Horner syndrome with anhidrosis

Lateral Medullary Syndrome involves disruption of sympathetic pathways causing ipsilateral Horner syndrome, typically with vertigo, dysphagia, and contralateral pain loss—not cortical behavioral changes or isolated leg weakness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dysphagia + vertigo + ipsilateral Horner = lateral medullary syndrome
