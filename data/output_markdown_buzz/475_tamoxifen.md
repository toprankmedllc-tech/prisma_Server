## Buzzword Question

A 54-year-old postmenopausal woman with a history of estrogen receptor–positive breast cancer is started on a medication that acts as an estrogen antagonist in breast tissue but partial agonist in bone and uterine tissue. Several years later, she develops abnormal uterine bleeding and is diagnosed with endometrial carcinoma. Which of the following drugs was she most likely taking?

## Buzzwords Used

Postmenopausal woman ; ER-positive breast cancer ; SERM ; antagonist at breast ; agonist at uterus ; endometrial carcinoma ; abnormal uterine bleeding

## Answer Options

A) Raloxifene
B) Clomiphene
C) Tamoxifen
D) Anastrozole

## Correct Answer

C) Tamoxifen

## Explanation

Tamoxifen is a SERM that antagonizes estrogen receptors in breast tissue but acts as a partial agonist in the uterus and bone. Its uterine agonist activity increases the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial carcinoma.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

ER-positive breast cancer + SERM with uterine agonist activity + endometrial cancer = Tamoxifen

## Wrong Options

### A) Raloxifene

Raloxifene acts as an estrogen antagonist in both breast and uterine tissue. Unlike tamoxifen, it does not increase the risk of endometrial cancer.

**Buzzword Combo:** Osteoporosis prevention + SERM + no endometrial cancer risk = Raloxifene

### B) Clomiphene

Clomiphene blocks estrogen receptors in the hypothalamus to induce ovulation and is used for infertility due to anovulation, especially in PCOS.

**Buzzword Combo:** Infertility + PCOS + ↑ LH/FSH release = Clomiphene

### D) Anastrozole

Anastrozole inhibits peripheral conversion of androgens to estrogen and is used in postmenopausal ER-positive breast cancer. It is not associated with endometrial carcinoma.

**Buzzword Combo:** Postmenopausal breast cancer + aromatase inhibition + ↓ estrogen synthesis = Anastrozole
