## Buzzword Question

Which of the following congenital heart defects can lead to Eisenmenger syndrome if left uncorrected?

## Buzzwords Used

Eisenmenger syndrome; uncorrected congenital heart defect

## Answer Options

A) Uncorrected ventricular septal defect, atrial septal defect, or patent ductus arteriosus
B) Tetralogy of Fallot
C) Transposition of the great arteries
D) Tricuspid atresia

## Correct Answer

A) Uncorrected ventricular septal defect, atrial septal defect, or patent ductus arteriosus

## Explanation


Eisenmenger syndrome develops from any chronic uncorrected left-to-right shunt — most commonly VSD, ASD, or PDA — that drives pulmonary overcirculation, raising PVR until shunt reversal occurs.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Uncorrected VSD / ASD / PDA + chronic L→R shunt + pulmonary remodeling = Eisenmenger syndrome

## Wrong Options

### B) Tetralogy of Fallot


TOF is already a right-to-left cyanotic shunt from birth due to pulmonary stenosis. It does not progress through Eisenmenger physiology (which requires an initial L→R shunt that subsequently reverses).

**Buzzword Combo:** Pulmonary stenosis + RVH + overriding aorta + VSD = Tetralogy of Fallot (primary R→L)

### C) Transposition of the great arteries

TGA produces cyanosis from parallel circulations at birth and requires immediate intervention; it is not a left-to-right shunt and does not progress to Eisenmenger physiology.

**Buzzword Combo:** Egg-on-a-string CXR + neonatal cyanosis + parallel circulations = TGA

### D) Tricuspid atresia

Tricuspid atresia is a cyanotic congenital lesion with absent tricuspid valve and a hypoplastic right ventricle, not a left-to-right shunt that reverses.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cyanotic infant + left-axis deviation + small RV + ASD + VSD required for survival = tricuspid atresia
