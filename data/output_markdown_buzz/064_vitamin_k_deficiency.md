## Buzzword Question

A patient treated with broad-spectrum antibiotics for several weeks develops easy bleeding. Laboratory studies show normal platelet count, normal bleeding time, increased PT, and mildly increased PTT. The earliest abnormal coagulation test is best explained by decreased activity of which factor?

## Buzzwords Used

broad-spectrum antibiotics for several weeks; easy bleeding; normal platelet count; normal bleeding time; increased PT; mildly increased PTT

## Answer Options

A) Factor VII

B) Factor VIII

C) Factor XIII

D) von Willebrand factor


## Correct Answer

A) Factor VII


## Explanation

Broad-spectrum antibiotics can deplete normal gut flora, which normally synthesize vitamin K. Vitamin K deficiency decreases activation of factors II, VII, IX, and X. PT becomes prolonged first because factor VII has the shortest half-life and belongs to the extrinsic pathway.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Broad-spectrum antibiotics + increased PT first + normal bleeding time = vitamin K deficiency

## Wrong Options

### B. Factor VIII


Factor VIII deficiency causes hemophilia A, which presents with isolated increased PTT and deep bleeding such as hemarthroses.

**Buzzword Combo:** Boy + hemarthrosis + isolated increased PTT = hemophilia A

### C. Factor XIII


Factor XIII deficiency causes impaired fibrin cross-linking, but PT and PTT are usually normal.

**Buzzword Combo:** Delayed bleeding + normal PT/PTT + poor fibrin cross-linking = factor XIII deficiency

### D. von Willebrand factor


von Willebrand factor deficiency impairs platelet adhesion and may mildly increase PTT due to decreased factor VIII stability.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mucosal bleeding + abnormal ristocetin + increased bleeding time = von Willebrand disease
