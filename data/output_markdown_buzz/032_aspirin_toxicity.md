## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old man with chronic knee pain has been taking high doses of aspirin daily for several months. He now presents with fatigue, low-grade fever, and a diffuse skin rash. Laboratory studies show elevated serum creatinine and eosinophilia. Urinalysis demonstrates white blood cell casts and eosinophils.
Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?


## Buzzwords Used

 aspirin daily for several months; elevated serum creatinine ; eosinophilia ;white blood cell casts

## Answer Options

A. Acute tubular necrosis
B. Minimal change disease
C. Acute interstitial nephritis
D. Membranous nephropathy

## Correct Answer

c

## Explanation

Aspirin and other NSAIDs can cause acute interstitial nephritis (AIN), a hypersensitivity reaction involving inflammation of the renal interstitium.
Classic findings include:
Fever 
Rash 
Eosinophilia 
Increased creatinine 
White blood cell casts 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

NSAID use + fever + rash + eosinophils = acute interstitial nephritis

## Wrong Options

### A. Acute tubular necrosis

Incorrect — ATN typically follows ischemia or nephrotoxic injury and presents with muddy brown casts rather than eosinophils.

**Buzzword Combo:** Muddy brown casts+ toxic injury = ATN

### B. Minimal change disease

Incorrect — Minimal change disease presents with nephrotic syndrome and massive proteinuria, not eosinophilia and WBC casts.

**Buzzword Combo:** Selective albuminuria in children+ response to steroids = minimal change disease

### D. Membranous nephropathy

Incorrect — Membranous nephropathy causes nephrotic syndrome and is associated with immune complex deposition.

**Buzzword Combo:** Nephrotic syndrome + spike and dome = membranous nephropathy
