## Buzzword Question

A hemodynamically stable young patient with AVNRT does not convert to sinus rhythm despite trials of Valsalva and modified Valsalva maneuvers. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

## Buzzwords Used

 AVNRT; failed vagal maneuvers

## Answer Options


A) Intravenous adenosine
B) Intravenous procainamide
C) Synchronized electrical cardioversion
D) Long-term oral β-blocker therapy

## Correct Answer

A) Intravenous adenosine

## Explanation

IV adenosine is the second-line acute treatment for AVNRT when vagal maneuvers fail. It produces a brief but profound AV nodal block that terminates the re-entrant circuit. Dose: 6 mg rapid IV push, followed by 12 mg if needed.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)


Stable AVNRT + failed vagal maneuvers = IV adenosine (6 mg → 12 mg)

## Wrong Options

### 
B) Intravenous procainamide

Procainamide is first-line for WPW with atrial fibrillation (wide irregular tachycardia), not AVNRT.

**Buzzword Combo:** WPW + atrial fibrillation + wide irregular tachycardia = IV procainamide

### C) Synchronized electrical cardioversion

Cardioversion is reserved for hemodynamically unstable patients, not stable AVNRT.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Unstable narrow-complex tachycardia = synchronized cardioversion

### D) Long-term oral β-blocker therapy

Oral β-blockers are used for long-term prevention of recurrent SVT, not acute termination.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent SVT + long-term prevention = oral β-blocker

## Related Concepts


Vagal maneuvers; WPW with atrial fibrillation (procainamide); Synchronized cardioversion in unstable SVT
