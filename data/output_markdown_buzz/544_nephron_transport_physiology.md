## Buzzword Question

During an experimental seminar, students analyze the composition of fluid collected from the end of the proximal convoluted tubule (late PCT) right before it descends into the loop of Henle. They note that the concentration of chloride ions has increased relative to the concentration in the early Bowman's capsule ([TF/P]>1.0). Which of the following statements best explains this physiological phenomenon?

## Buzzwords Used

Late PCT; Bowman's capsule; [TF/P]>1.0; chloride ions; early PCT; preferential reabsorption

## Answer Options

A) The late proximal tubule is completely impermeable to chloride ions.
B) Active secretion of chloride by the organic anion transporters increases its luminal mass.
C) Sodium is preferentially reabsorbed with bicarbonate and organic solutes in the early PCT, leaving chloride behind to be concentrated.
D) Water reabsorption is completely halted in the early parts of the proximal tubule

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

In the early proximal convoluted tubule, sodium is preferentially reabsorbed alongside bicarbonate, glucose, and amino acids, while water follows osmotically. Because chloride is not co-transported with these solutes in the early segment, it is left behind in the lumen and becomes progressively concentrated, driving its [TF/P] ratio above 1.0 by the late PCT.



## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Early PCT sodium-solute cotransport + Isosmotic water reabsorption + Chloride retention = Late PCT [TF/P] > 1.0

## Wrong Options

### A) The late proximal tubule is completely impermeable to chloride ions.

The late proximal tubule is highly permeable to chloride ions. The high luminal concentration of chloride established in the early PCT creates a concentration gradient that drives passive, paracellular chloride reabsorption in the late PCT.

**Buzzword Combo:** High chloride concentration + Paracellular diffusion + Late PCT = Passive chloride reabsorption

### B) Active secretion of chloride by the organic anion transporters increases its luminal mass.

Chloride ions are not handled by organic anion transporters (OATs). OATs are responsible for the active secretion of endogenous waste products and exogenous compounds like para-aminohippurate (PAH) and penicillin.

**Buzzword Combo:** Organic anion transporters + Active secretion + Exogenous compounds = PAH/Penicillin clearance

### D) Water reabsorption is completely halted in the early parts of the proximal tubule

Water reabsorption is highly active in the early proximal tubule, occurring isosmotically via aquaporin-1 channels following solute reabsorption. Halting water reabsorption would prevent the concentration of chloride ions in the tubule lumen.

**Buzzword Combo:** Aquaporin-1 channels + Isosmotic fluid movement + Solute tracking = Early PCT water reabsorption
