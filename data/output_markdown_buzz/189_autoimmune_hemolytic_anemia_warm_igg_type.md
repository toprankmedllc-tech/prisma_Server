## Buzzword Question

A woman with SLE, fatigue, jaundice, dark urine, mild splenomegaly, normocytic anemia, elevated LDH, elevated indirect bilirubin, and a positive direct Coombs test most likely has which condition?

## Buzzwords Used

SLE; fatigue; jaundice; dark urine; splenomegaly; normocytic anemia; elevated LDH; elevated indirect bilirubin; positive direct Coombs test

## Answer Options

A. G6PD deficiency
B. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia
C. Hereditary spherocytosis
D. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria

## Correct Answer

B. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Explanation

Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia is commonly associated with SLE and is caused by IgG autoantibodies against RBCs, leading to extravascular hemolysis. It is characterized by a positive direct Coombs test, spherocytes, and splenomegaly.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

SLE + positive direct Coombs + hemolysis + spherocytes = Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Wrong Options

### A. G6PD deficiency

G6PD deficiency causes episodic oxidative hemolysis with bite cells and Heinz bodies and is Coombs-negative, without association with SLE.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bite cells + Heinz bodies + oxidative stress + Coombs negative = G6PD deficiency

### C. Hereditary spherocytosis

Hereditary spherocytosis causes spherocytes due to membrane defects with a negative Coombs test and is inherited, not autoimmune or SLE-related.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spherocytes + negative Coombs + osmotic fragility = Hereditary spherocytosis

### D. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria

PNH is due to PIGA mutation leading to CD55/CD59 deficiency, causing complement-mediated intravascular hemolysis, thrombosis, and a negative Coombs test.

**Buzzword Combo:** CD55/CD59 deficiency + hemoglobinuria + thrombosis = PNH
