## Buzzword Question

A 72-year-old man with a history of hypertension and severe bilateral renal artery stenosis undergoes a routine screening colonoscopy and is found to have a large villous adenoma. Prior to the procedure, his serum creatinine was 1.1 mg/dL. Two weeks after starting a new antihypertensive medication to help control his stubborn blood pressure, he presents to the urgent care clinic complaining of severe generalized muscle weakness and fatigue. Laboratory investigations reveal an acute rise in serum creatinine to 2.4 mg/dL and a serum potassium level of 6.2 mEq/L. An electrocardiogram shows peaked T waves. Which of the following medications is most likely responsible for this presentation?

## Buzzwords Used

Bilateral renal artery stenosis; Muscle weakness; Acute rise in serum creatinine; Potassium level of 6.2; Peaked T waves

## Answer Options

A) Hydrochlorothiazide
B) Furosemide
C) Lisinopril
D) Amlodipine

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

In patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis, renal perfusion pressure is significantly reduced, making glomerular filtration heavily dependent on Angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction of the efferent arteriole. Blocking this via an ACE inhibitor like lisinopril drops efferent tone, drastically plunging the GFR (causing acute kidney injury/creatinine spike) and reducing aldosterone production, leading to severe hyperkalemia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Bilateral renal artery stenosis + Creatinine spike + Hyperkalemia = ACE inhibitor toxicity → Lisinopril.

## Wrong Options

### A) Hydrochlorothiazide

Hydrochlorothiazide is a thiazide diuretic that typically causes a reduction in serum potassium levels (hypokalemia) due to increased distal delivery of sodium, which directly conflicts with this patient's hyperkalemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bilateral renal artery stenosis + Creatinine spike + Hyperkalemia = Selecting a potassium-wasting diuretic instead of a drug causing retention → Hydrochlorothiazide.

### B) Furosemide

Furosemide is a loop diuretic that is frequently used to manage fluid volume and can lower potassium levels; it does not typically cause severe hyperkalemia or acute renal failure unless severe dehydration occurs, and even then, hypokalemia dominates.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bilateral renal artery stenosis + Creatinine spike + Hyperkalemia = Confusing loop-induced prerenal azotemia with drug-induced loss of GFR auto-regulation → Furosemide.

### D) Amlodipine

Amlodipine is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that works through peripheral vasodilation; it does not disrupt intrarenal hemodynamic feedback mechanisms governed by the renin-angiotensin system and does not cause severe hyperkalemia or acute renal failure in bilateral renal artery stenosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bilateral renal artery stenosis + Creatinine spike + Hyperkalemia = Selecting a calcium channel blocker with no effect on efferent arteriole tone → Amlodipine.
