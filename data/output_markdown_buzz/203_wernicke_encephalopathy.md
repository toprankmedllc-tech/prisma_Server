## Buzzword Question

A 55-year-old chronic alcoholic presents with acute confusion, impaired eye abduction with horizontal nystagmus, and a wide-based unsteady gait. The deficient nutrient most directly responsible is:

## Buzzwords Used

Chronic alcoholism; acute confusion; ophthalmoplegia; ataxia

## Answer Options

A) Thiamine (vitamin B1)
B) Cobalamin (vitamin B12)
C) Pyridoxine (vitamin B6)
D) Niacin (vitamin B3)

## Correct Answer

A) Thiamine (vitamin B1)

## Explanation

Thiamine is the cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and transketolase. Deficiency in chronic alcoholics produces the Wernicke triad: confusion, ophthalmoplegia, and ataxia, with selective injury to the mammillary bodies, periaqueductal gray, and medial thalami.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic alcoholic + confusion + ophthalmoplegia + ataxia = thiamine (B1) deficiency → Wernicke encephalopathy

## Wrong Options

### Cobalamin (vitamin B12)

B12 deficiency causes subacute combined degeneration with loss of vibration/proprioception, spastic weakness, and megaloblastic anemia, not the acute confusion/ophthalmoplegia/ataxia triad.

**Buzzword Combo:** Loss of vibration/proprioception + spastic weakness + megaloblastic anemia + low B12 = subacute combined degeneration

### Pyridoxine (vitamin B6)

B6 deficiency (often from isoniazid use) causes peripheral neuropathy, sideroblastic anemia, and seizures, not the Wernicke triad.

**Buzzword Combo:** Isoniazid use + peripheral neuropathy + sideroblastic anemia = B6 (pyridoxine) deficiency

### Niacin (vitamin B3)

Niacin deficiency causes pellagra: the "3 Ds" of dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia, not the Wernicke triad.

**Buzzword Combo:** Photosensitive dermatitis + diarrhea + dementia = niacin (B3) deficiency → pellagra

## Related Concepts

B12 deficiency / subacute combined degeneration; B6 deficiency (isoniazid); Pellagra (B3 deficiency)
