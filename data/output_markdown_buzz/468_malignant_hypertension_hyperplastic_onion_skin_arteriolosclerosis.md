## Buzzword Question

Microscopic examination of the renal arterioles of a patient with chronic severe hypertension reveals concentric, laminated thickening of the smooth muscle layers, resembling the layers of an onion. This finding is most consistent with:

## Buzzwords Used


Chronic severe hypertension; concentric laminated thickening; onion-skin appearance; hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis

## Answer Options

A) Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis
B) Hyaline arteriolosclerosis
C) Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles
D) Granulomatous inflammation with giant cell

## Correct Answer

A) Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis

## Explanation


Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis is the histologic finding of malignant hypertension, characterized by concentric, laminated thickening of the arteriolar smooth muscle layers — resembling onion skin. It often coexists with fibrinoid necrosis in the same vessels.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Malignant HTN + concentric laminated smooth muscle thickening (onion-skin) = hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis

## Wrong Options

### B) Hyaline arteriolosclerosis

Pink, homogeneous (not laminated) thickening of arteriolar walls in benign HTN and diabetes.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Benign HTN / diabetes + pink homogeneous arteriolar thickening = hyaline arteriolosclerosis

### 
C) Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles


A separate finding of malignant HTN involving plasma protein deposition and necrosis of the vessel wall — bright pink and glassy rather than laminated.

**Buzzword Combo:** Malignant HTN + bright pink glassy necrotic arteriolar wall = fibrinoid necrosis

### D) Granulomatous inflammation with giant cells


The histology of large/medium-vessel vasculitides such as giant cell arteritis and Takayasu — not hypertensive arteriolar disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Granulomatous arteritis + giant cells + elderly with jaw claudication = GCA
