## Buzzword Question

A healthy upright adult undergoes pulmonary physiology testing. Imaging-based modeling shows higher ventilation and perfusion in lower lung zones, but oxygen tension is paradoxically highest at the apex. Which of the following best explains the V/Q ratio distribution from apex to base of lungs?

## Buzzwords Used

Upright posture; apex to base gradient; ventilation perfusion mismatch; gravity dependent perfusion; alveolar oxygen tension; regional lung physiology

## Answer Options

A) V/Q ratio is lowest at apex due to maximal perfusion
B) V/Q ratio is constant throughout the lung
C) V/Q ratio is highest at apex because perfusion increases more than ventilation toward base
D) V/Q ratio is highest at base due to increased ventilation

## Correct Answer

C) V/Q ratio is highest at apex because perfusion increases more than ventilation toward base

## Explanation

In upright lungs, both ventilation and perfusion increase from apex to base, but perfusion increases more steeply due to gravity. This results in a high V/Q ratio at the apex and low V/Q ratio at the base.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Upright lung; gravity dependent perfusion; perfusion > ventilation gradient

## Wrong Options

### A) V/Q ratio is lowest at apex due to maximal perfusion

Perfusion is actually lowest at the apex due to gravity, not highest. This leads to a high V/Q ratio, not low, at the apex.

**Buzzword Combo:** Apex; low perfusion; gravity effect

### B) V/Q ratio is constant throughout the lung

Ventilation and perfusion are both gravity dependent but change disproportionately, so V/Q is not uniform across lung zones.

**Buzzword Combo:** Uniform gas exchange; no gravity effect; equal distribution

### D) V/Q ratio is highest at base due to increased ventilation

Although ventilation increases toward the base, perfusion increases even more, resulting in a lower V/Q

**Buzzword Combo:** Base lung; high perfusion; shunt-like physiology
