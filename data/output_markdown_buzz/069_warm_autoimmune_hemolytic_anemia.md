## Buzzword Question

A 6-year-old boy develops fatigue and jaundice after a recent viral infection. Laboratory studies show low hemoglobin, increased indirect bilirubin, and increased reticulocyte count. Peripheral smear shows spherocytes. Direct Coombs test is positive. The anemia is most likely caused by:

## Buzzwords Used

Recent viral infection + spherocytes + positive Coombs = warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Answer Options

A) Splenic macrophage removal of IgG-coated RBCs

B) Defective spectrin in the RBC membrane

C) Decreased β-globin chain synthesis

D) Impaired DNA synthesis in erythroid precursors


## Correct Answer

A. Splenic macrophage removal of IgG-coated RBCs


## Explanation

Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia is caused by IgG antibodies binding to RBCs. These IgG-coated RBCs are removed by splenic macrophages, causing extravascular hemolysis. Spherocytes can be seen because partial membrane removal by macrophages makes RBCs smaller and rounder.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recent viral infection + spherocytes + positive Coombs = warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Wrong Options

### B. Defective spectrin in the RBC membrane


This describes hereditary spherocytosis. It also causes spherocytes, but the Coombs test is negative because there are no antibodies against RBCs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spherocytes + negative Coombs + family history = hereditary spherocytosis

### C. Decreased β-globin chain synthesis


This describes β-thalassemia, which causes microcytic anemia with target cells due to defective globin production.

**Buzzword Combo:** Microcytosis + target cells + decreased β-globin = β-thalassemia

### D. Impaired DNA synthesis in erythroid precursors


This describes megaloblastic anemia due to vitamin B12 or folate deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** High MCV + macro-ovalocytes + hypersegmented neutrophils = megaloblastic anemia
