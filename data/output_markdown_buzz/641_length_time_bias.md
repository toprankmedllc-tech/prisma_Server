## Buzzword Question

A public health agency is evaluating whether a new screening program for ovarian cancer truly reduces deaths from the disease. Investigators are concerned that screening may preferentially detect indolent tumors with better prognoses, making survival statistics difficult to interpret.

Which of the following study designs would best determine whether the screening program is beneficial?

## Buzzwords Used

Screening effectiveness ; Ovarian cancer ; Indolent tumors ; Mortality outcomes ; Study design

## Answer Options

A) Case-control study comparing screened and unscreened patients

B) Cross-sectional study measuring cancer prevalence

C) Randomized controlled trial comparing disease-specific mortality in screened and unscreened groups

D) Cohort study comparing survival after diagnosis among screened patients only

## Correct Answer

C) Randomized controlled trial comparing disease-specific mortality in screened and unscreened groups

## Explanation

Length-time bias can make survival appear longer because screening preferentially detects less aggressive disease. Comparing mortality outcomes in randomized screened and unscreened groups is the best method to determine whether screening truly improves outcomes.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Screening effectiveness + concern for indolent disease detection + mortality comparison = randomized controlled trial

## Wrong Options

### A) Case-control study comparing screened and unscreened patients

Case-control studies are useful for identifying associations but are not the strongest design for determining whether screening reduces mortality.

**Buzzword Combo:** Association study + screening benefit question = suboptimal design

### B) Cross-sectional study measuring cancer prevalence

Cross-sectional studies assess prevalence at a single point in time and cannot determine mortality benefit.

**Buzzword Combo:** Single time point + mortality outcome question = inappropriate design

### D) Cohort study comparing survival after diagnosis among screened patients only

Survival after diagnosis is vulnerable to screening biases and does not directly assess whether screening reduces deaths.

**Buzzword Combo:** Post-diagnosis survival + screening bias concern = misleading outcome measure
