## Buzzword Question

A newborn boy develops excessive bleeding after circumcision. Laboratory studies show normal platelet count, normal bleeding time, normal PT, and isolated increased PTT. He is diagnosed with hemophilia A and receives factor VIII replacement, but his PTT remains persistently elevated. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

## Buzzwords Used

newborn boy; excessive bleeding after circumcision; normal platelet count; normal bleeding time; normal PT; isolated increased PTT; hemophilia A; factor VIII replacement; persistently elevated PTT

## Answer Options

A. Check for antibodies against factor VIII
B. Measure von Willebrand factor activity
C. Give platelet transfusion
D. Check factor VII activity

## Correct Answer

A. Check for antibodies against factor VIII


## Explanation

Patients with hemophilia A can develop inhibitors, or antibodies, against exogenous factor VIII. If PTT remains elevated despite factor VIII replacement, the next step is to check for a factor VIII inhibitor.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hemophilia A + factor VIII replacement + persistently increased PTT = factor VIII inhibitor

## Wrong Options

### B. Measure von Willebrand factor activity


This would be useful if von Willebrand disease were suspected, especially with mucosal bleeding, abnormal ristocetin test, and increased bleeding time.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mucosal bleeding + increased bleeding time + abnormal ristocetin = von Willebrand disease

### C. Give platelet transfusion


Hemophilia is a secondary hemostasis problem, not a platelet problem. Platelet count and bleeding time are normal.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal platelets + normal bleeding time + increased PTT = coagulation factor defect

### D. Check factor VII activity


Factor VII belongs to the extrinsic pathway. A factor VII problem would increase PT, not isolated PTT.

**Buzzword Combo:** Isolated increased PT + factor VII = extrinsic pathway defect
