## Buzzword Question

31-year-old tennis player presents to the clinic with progressive weakness in his right hand several weeks . He reports pain along the medial aspect of the wrist and increasing difficulty gripping objects firmly. Physical examination reveals weakness with finger abduction and adduction. When asked to hold a sheet of paper between the thumb and index finger, he compensates by flexing the thumb at the interphalangeal joint.
Which of the following muscles is most likely weakened in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

 weakness with finger abduction and adduction; when asked to hold a sheet of paper between the thumb and index finger, he compensates by flexing the thumb at the interphalangeal joint

## Answer Options

A. Adductor pollicis
B. Abductor pollicis brevis
C. Opponens pollicis
D. Flexor pollicis longus 

## Correct Answer

A. Adductor pollicis

## Explanation

This patient most likely has an ulnar nerve injury. The adductor pollicis muscle is innervated by the ulnar nerve and is responsible for thumb adduction. The patient also has Froment sign: Inability to maintain thumb adduction while gripping paper ,Compensation by flexing the thumb at the interphalangeal joint using flexor pollicis longus (median nerve) 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

 positive Froment sign + weak thumb adduction = ulnar nerve injury

## Wrong Options

### B. Abductor pollicis brevis

Incorrect — This muscle is innervated by the median nerve and abducts the thumb.


**Buzzword Combo:** Thenar atrophy + nighttime paresthesias + weak thumb abduction = median nerve compression

### C. Opponens pollicis

Incorrect — This median nerve–innervated muscle is responsible for thumb opposition.

**Buzzword Combo:** Difficulty touching thumb to fingertips + thenar weakness = recurrent median nerve injury

### D. Flexor pollicis longus

Incorrect — This muscle flexes the thumb and is innervated by the anterior interosseous branch of the median nerve. It produces the compensatory thumb flexion seen in Froment sign.

**Buzzword Combo:** Failure of thumb IP flexion + inability to make “OK” sign = anterior interosseous nerve lesion
