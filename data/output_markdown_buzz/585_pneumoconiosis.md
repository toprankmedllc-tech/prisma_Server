## Buzzword Question

A 64-year-old retired coal miner presents with progressive exertional dyspnea and a chronic dry cough for several years. Chest X-ray shows upper-lobe-predominant nodular opacities with progressive massive fibrosis. Which of the following best describes the pathogenesis of this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Coal miner; progressive dyspnea; chronic cough; upper-lobe nodular opacities; progressive massive fibrosis; coal dust exposure

## Answer Options

A) NLRP3 activation
B) Carbon-laden macrophages
C) IgE mast cell
D) Immune complexes

## Correct Answer

B) Carbon-laden macrophages

## Explanation

Coal workers’ pneumoconiosis is caused by inhalation of coal dust leading to accumulation of carbon particles in alveolar macrophages, triggering chronic inflammation and fibrosis. This results in upper-lobe nodular opacities and possible progression to massive fibrosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Coal miner; coal dust; upper-lobe nodules = carbon-laden macrophages

## Wrong Options

### A) NLRP3 activation

NLRP3 inflammasome activation is classically associated with silicosis due to silica crystals causing macrophage injury and IL-1–mediated inflammation, not coal dust.

**Buzzword Combo:** Silica exposure; macrophage apoptosis; IL-1 inflammation = silicosis

### C) IgE mast cell

IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation is seen in type I hypersensitivity reactions such as asthma and allergic diseases, not pneumoconioses.

**Buzzword Combo:** Allergen exposure; bronchospasm; eosinophils = asthma

### D) Immune complexes

Immune complex deposition (type III hypersensitivity) is seen in conditions like hypersensitivity pneumonitis or vasculitis, not in coal dust–induced lung disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Antigen exposure; lymphocytic infiltrates; granulomas = hypersensitivity pneumonitis
