## Buzzword Question

A patient with opioid overdose develops hypoxemia, hypercapnia, and a normal A-a gradient due to shallow respirations. What is the underlying mechanism?


## Buzzwords Used

Opioid overdose ; hypercapnia ; normal A-a gradient ; shallow respirations


## Answer Options

 A) V/Q mismatch
 B) Right-to-left shunt
 C) Alveolar hypoventilation
 D) Diffusion limitation


## Correct Answer

 C) Alveolar hypoventilation


## Explanation

Hypoventilation decreases alveolar oxygen tension and increases PaCO2, producing hypoxemia with a normal A-a gradient.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Opioids + hypercapnia + normal A-a gradient = Alveolar hypoventilation


## Wrong Options

### A) V/Q mismatch

V/Q mismatch causes hypoxemia with an elevated A-a gradient and is commonly seen in pulmonary embolism or pneumonia.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Pulmonary embolism + elevated A-a gradient + hypoxemia = V/Q mismatch


###  B) Right-to-left shunt


Shunts cause refractory hypoxemia and increased A-a gradient because blood bypasses ventilated alveoli.


**Buzzword Combo:** Cyanotic congenital heart disease + refractory hypoxemia + increased A-a gradient = Right-to-left shunt


###  D) Diffusion limitation


Diffusion limitation occurs in interstitial lung disease and causes exertional hypoxemia with elevated A-a gradient.


**Buzzword Combo:** Pulmonary fibrosis + exertional dyspnea + elevated A-a gradient = Diffusion limitation
