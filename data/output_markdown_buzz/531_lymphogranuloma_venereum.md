## Buzzword Question

A 29-year-old man presents with painful unilateral inguinal lymphadenopathy and fever 2 weeks after a small painless genital papule that resolved spontaneously. He recently returned from Southeast Asia and reports unprotected sexual intercourse with multiple partners. Physical examination shows tender enlarged inguinal nodes with overlying erythema (“groove sign”). Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Painful inguinal lymphadenopathy; painless transient genital ulcer; groove sign; sexually active; Chlamydia serovars L1-L3

## Answer Options

A) Syphilis

B) Granuloma Inguinale

C) Chancroid

D) Lymphogranuloma Venereum

## Correct Answer

D) Lymphogranuloma Venereum

## Explanation

Lymphogranuloma Venereum is caused by invasive Chlamydia trachomatis serovars L1-L3. It classically presents with a transient painless genital lesion followed by painful inguinal lymphadenopathy and the “groove sign.”

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Painless transient genital lesion + painful inguinal buboes + groove sign = Lymphogranuloma Venereum

## Wrong Options

### A) Syphilis

Primary syphilis causes a painless chancre with nonpainful lymphadenopathy rather than painful buboes and groove sign.

**Buzzword Combo:** Painless chancre + painless lymph nodes + spirochete = Syphilis

### B) Granuloma Inguinale

Granuloma inguinale causes painless beefy-red ulcerative lesions that bleed easily and usually lacks painful inguinal lymphadenopathy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Beefy-red painless ulcers + Donovan bodies + bleeds easily = Granuloma Inguinale

### C) Chancroid

Chancroid presents with painful genital ulcers and painful lymphadenopathy, but does not produce the transient painless lesion followed by groove sign typical of LGV.

**Buzzword Combo:** Painful ragged genital ulcer + painful lymphadenopathy + Haemophilus ducreyi = Chancroid
