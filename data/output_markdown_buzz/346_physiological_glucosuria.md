## Buzzword Question

A 31-year-old woman at 28 weeks gestation presents for routine prenatal care. Urine dipstick shows 2+ glucose. Fasting plasma glucose is 82 mg/dL and a 75g OGTT shows a 1-hour value of 155 mg/dL and 2-hour value of 128 mg/dL. Serum electrolytes, phosphate, uric acid, and amino acids are all normal. Which of the following is the most likely mechanism for her glucosuria?

## Buzzwords Used

2+ glucosuria; normal OGTT; normal electrolytes; normal amino acids


## Answer Options

A) Defective SGLT2 transporter causing isolated renal glucosuria
B) Increased GFR reducing the plasma glucose concentration at which renal threshold is reached
C) Global PCT dysfunction impairing reabsorption of glucose and other solutes
D) Pancreatic beta-cell insufficiency causing intermittent postprandial hyperglycemia

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

Pregnancy physiologically increases GFR and renal plasma flow, increasing the filtered glucose load. This causes the renal threshold to be reached at lower plasma glucose concentrations, producing glucosuria despite completely normal glucose tolerance testing. Normal amino acids and electrolytes rule out global tubular dysfunction.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pregnancy + ↑GFR + normal OGTT + normal amino acids = physiologic glucosuria (lowered threshold)

## Wrong Options

### C) Global PCT dysfunction (Fanconi)

Fanconi syndrome causes glucosuria alongside phosphaturia, aminoaciduria, uricosuria, and renal tubular acidosis — a global reabsorption defect. This patient has isolated glucosuria with all other solutes normal, making Fanconi incorrect.


**Buzzword Combo:** Glucosuria + normal amino acids + normal phosphate = NOT Fanconi


### A) Defective SGLT2 (Isolated renal glucosuria)

A finding of isolated glucosuria at normal plasma glucose could suggest a congenital SGLT2 defect, but the clinical context of pregnancy with its known hemodynamic changes makes physiologic GFR elevation the far more likely explanation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Isolated glucosuria + normal glucose = SGLT2 defect (incorrect in pregnant patient without family history)

### D) Beta-cell insufficiency / GDM

Her OGTT values fall below diagnostic thresholds for GDM (1-hour <180, 2-hour <153 mg/dL by IADPSG criteria), ruling out beta-cell dysfunction as the mechanism. Glucosuria with a normal OGTT points away from hyperglycemia-driven spillage.

**Buzzword Combo:** Glucosuria + pregnancy = GDM (incorrect — OGTT values normal)
