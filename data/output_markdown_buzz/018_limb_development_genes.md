## Buzzword Question

A researcher is studying the molecular signaling pathways of limb development in a chick embryo model. In one experiment, a group of cells at the distal end of the limb bud is removed. This results in the complete arrest of proximal-to-distal limb outgrowth. The signaling center removed in this experiment normally produces which of the following substances to maintain the underlying undifferentiated mesoderm?

## Buzzwords Used

Distal end of limb bud; Arrest of outgrowth; Proximal-to-distal limb outgrowth.

## Answer Options

A) Wnt-7a
B) Homeobox (HOX) gene products
C) Fibroblast Growth Factor (FGF)
D) Sonic Hedgehog (Shh)

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

The Apical Ectodermal Ridge (AER) is the signaling center at the distal tip of the limb bud that governs proximal-to-distal growth by secreting FGF. Removal of the AER prevents further lengthening of the limb.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

AER + FGF = Proximal-to-Distal growth

## Wrong Options

### A) Wnt-7a

Wnt-7a is produced by the dorsal ectoderm and is responsible for dorsal-ventral (back of hand vs. palm) patterning.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dorsal ectoderm + Wnt-7a = Dorsal-Ventral patterning

### B) Homeobox (HOX) gene products

HOX genes are responsible for the segmental organization of the embryo and the specific identity of limb segments, rather than the mechanical growth stimulated by the AER.

**Buzzword Combo:** HOX genes + Segment identity = Appendicular skeleton layout

### D ) Sonic Hedgehog (Shh)

Shh is produced by the Zone of Polarizing Activity (ZPA) and regulates anterior-posterior (thumb-to-pinky) patterning, not lengthening.

**Buzzword Combo:** ZPA + Shh = Anterior-Posterior patterning
