## Buzzword Question

A 3-year-old girl has recurrent infections with encapsulated bacteria. Phagocytes are present in normal numbers, but bacteria are poorly coated for ingestion. Which of the following deficiencies best explains these findings?

## Buzzwords Used

3-year-old girl; recurrent infections with encapsulated bacteria; bacteria are poorly coated for ingestion

## Answer Options

A) C3
B) C5
C) C1 esterase inhibitor
D) CD55 and CD59 anchoring

## Correct Answer

A) C3


## Explanation

C3 deficiency causes decreased C3b, a major opsonin that coats bacteria and promotes phagocytosis. This leads to recurrent bacterial infections due to impaired microbial clearance.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recurrent bacterial infections + poor microbial coating + impaired phagocytosis = C3 deficiency

## Wrong Options

### B) C5

C5 deficiency impairs formation of the membrane attack complex. It classically predisposes to recurrent Neisseria infections, not broad defective bacterial opsonization.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent Neisseria infections + impaired C5b-C9 formation + decreased MAC = terminal complement deficiency

### C) C1 esterase inhibitor

C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency causes hereditary angioedema. It produces recurrent swelling due to excess bradykinin, not impaired opsonization.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent swelling + no urticaria + C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency = hereditary angioedema

### D) CD55 and CD59 anchoring

Loss of CD55 and CD59 anchoring causes paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria. RBCs become susceptible to complement-mediated lysis rather than bacteria becoming poorly opsonized.

**Buzzword Combo:** Complement-mediated RBC lysis + dark urine + loss of CD55/CD59 = paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria
