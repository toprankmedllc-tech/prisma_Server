## Buzzword Question

Investigators design a trial comparing a new antihypertensive to placebo. They use sealed opaque envelopes opened by an independent coordinator at enrollment. Neither patients nor outcome assessors know the treatment assignment; the prescribing physician does know. Which of the following terms best describes this study's blinding design?


## Buzzwords Used

Sealed opaque envelopes; independent coordinator; patients blinded; outcome assessors blinded; physician knows treatment

## Answer Options

A) Single-blind trial with allocation concealment 
B) Double-blind trial with allocation concealment 
C) Open-label trial without allocation concealment 
D) Triple-blind trial with allocation concealment

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Blinding refers to concealment of treatment assignment from study participants during the trial (after randomization). Single-blind = one party (usually the patient) is unaware of treatment assignment. Double-blind = two parties (usually patient AND outcome assessor/investigator) are unaware. Triple-blind = patient, assessor, AND data analysts are unaware. Here, the patient and outcome assessor are blinded but the prescribing physician knows — only one of the two key parties is blinded, making this single-blind. Allocation concealment (sealed opaque envelopes opened by independent coordinator) is a separate concept that prevents selection bias at the time of randomization, and is present here regardless of blinding status.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Patient blinded + outcome assessor blinded + physician knows = Single-blind; sealed envelopes before randomization = allocation concealment

## Wrong Options

### B) Double-blind trial with allocation concealment

A double-blind trial requires that BOTH the patient AND the treating clinician/outcome assessor are unaware of treatment assignment. Because the prescribing physician knows the treatment in this trial, the double-blind criterion is not fully met.


**Buzzword Combo:** Patient + treating physician/assessor BOTH blinded = Double-blind trial

### C) Open-label trial without allocation concealment

An open-label trial has no blinding  all parties know treatment assignment. This trial uses sealed envelopes (allocation concealment) and blinds both the patient and outcome assessor. It is neither open-label nor without allocation concealment.

**Buzzword Combo:** No blinding of any party = Open-label; allocation not concealed at randomization = selection bias risk

### D) Triple-blind trial with allocation concealment


Triple-blind additionally conceals treatment assignment from the data safety monitoring committee or statistical analyst. Here, the prescribing physician already knows the assignment, which disqualifies even double-blind classification. Triple-blind is never appropriate here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Patient + assessor + data analyst all blinded = Triple-blind trial
