## Buzzword Question

A national cardiology guideline committee is reviewing evidence for a new anti-anginal medication. Multiple large randomized controlled trials demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in exercise tolerance compared with standard therapy, with an average increase of 12 seconds on treadmill testing (p < 0.001). The medication also shows a slight reduction in minor side effects such as headache and flushing compared with existing agents. The committee must decide whether to recommend the drug for routine clinical use.

Which of the following concerns is most appropriate when making this recommendation?

## Buzzwords Used

Stable angina ; Randomized controlled trials ; Exercise tolerance ; Statistically significant improvement ; Guideline recommendation

## Answer Options

A) The trials are invalid because statistically significant results cannot be trusted in large sample sizes
B) The improvement in exercise tolerance is too small to be clinically meaningful despite statistical significance
C) The medication must be recommended because it shows statistically significant benefit in all measured outcomes
D) The medication cannot be recommended because randomized controlled trials are not suitable for clinical guidelines

## Correct Answer

B) The improvement in exercise tolerance is too small to be clinically meaningful despite statistical significance

## Explanation

Statistical significance does not necessarily imply clinical importance. Although the medication shows a statistically significant improvement in exercise tolerance, the absolute gain (12 seconds) is too small to meaningfully impact patient function or quality of life in routine practice.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Large RCT evidence + statistically significant p-value + minimal functional improvement = lack of clinical significance

## Wrong Options

### A) The trials are invalid because statistically significant results cannot be trusted in large sample sizes

Large sample sizes increase power to detect small but real differences; they do not invalidate statistical significance.

**Buzzword Combo:** Large sample size + misinterpretation of p-value = false rejection of valid evidence

### C) The medication must be recommended because it shows statistically significant benefit in all measured outcomes

Statistical significance alone does not justify clinical recommendation without considering effect size and clinical relevance.

**Buzzword Combo:** p-value significance + ignores effect magnitude = inappropriate guideline adoption

### D) The medication cannot be recommended because randomized controlled trials are not suitable for clinical guidelines

RCTs are the gold standard for evaluating interventions and are central to guideline development.

**Buzzword Combo:** RCT evidence + incorrect methodological rejection = misunderstanding evidence hierarchy
