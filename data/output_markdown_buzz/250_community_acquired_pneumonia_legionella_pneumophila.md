## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old man presents with 4 days of fever, dry cough, diarrhea, and confusion. He recently stayed at a hotel with a centralized air-conditioning system. Chest X-ray shows patchy interstitial infiltrates. Labs reveal hyponatremia and elevated liver enzymes. Which organism is most likely responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

Hotel exposure; air-conditioning system; diarrhea; confusion; hyponatremia; atypical pneumonia; interstitial infiltrates

## Answer Options

A) Streptococcus pneumoniae
B) Mycoplasma pneumoniae
C) Klebsiella pneumoniae
D) Legionella pneumophila

## Correct Answer

D) Legionella pneumophila

## Explanation

This is Legionella pneumophila, an intracellular Gram-negative organism associated with contaminated water systems such as hotel air-conditioning. It causes atypical pneumonia with GI symptoms (diarrhea), confusion, hyponatremia, and elevated LFTs, which are key distinguishing systemic features.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hotel exposure + air-conditioning + hyponatremia + diarrhea + confusion = Legionella pneumonia

## Wrong Options

### A) Streptococcus pneumoniae

S. pneumoniae causes typical lobar pneumonia with rust-colored sputum and pleuritic chest pain, without GI symptoms, hyponatremia, or water-system exposure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Rust sputum + lobar consolidation + lancet diplococci = pneumococcal CAP mismatch

### B) Mycoplasma pneumoniae

Mycoplasma causes mild “walking pneumonia” in young adults with dry cough and cold agglutinins, but does not cause hyponatremia, diarrhea, or severe systemic toxicity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Crowded dorm + dry cough + cold agglutinins = Mycoplasma mismatch

### C) Klebsiella pneumoniae

Klebsiella occurs in alcoholics and hospitalized patients with mucoid sputum and upper lobe cavitation, not water exposure, hyponatremia, or gastrointestinal symptoms.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alcoholic + mucoid sputum + cavitation = Klebsiella mismatch
