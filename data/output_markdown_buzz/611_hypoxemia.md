## Buzzword Question

A 64-year-old man with pulmonary fibrosis develops worsening dyspnea. During exercise, his oxygen saturation falls from 95% to 82%. Why is exercise particularly likely to worsen hypoxemia in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Pulmonary fibrosis; exertional dyspnea; oxygen saturation drops during exercise; diffusion impairment; hypoxemia; restrictive lung disease

## Answer Options

A) Increased alveolar ventilation
B) Shortened pulmonary capillary transit time
C) Increased inspired oxygen tension
D) Increased hemoglobin concentration

## Correct Answer

B) Shortened pulmonary capillary transit time

## Explanation

In pulmonary fibrosis, oxygen diffusion across the thickened alveolar-capillary membrane is delayed. During exercise, blood moves through pulmonary capillaries more rapidly, reducing transit time and preventing complete equilibration of oxygen between alveoli and blood, thereby worsening hypoxemia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pulmonary fibrosis + exercise + thickened alveolar membrane = shortened capillary transit time worsens hypoxemia

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased alveolar ventilation

Exercise normally increases alveolar ventilation, which helps improve oxygen delivery rather than causing worsening hypoxemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Exercise + increased ventilation + improved oxygen delivery = compensatory response

### C) Increased inspired oxygen tension

An increase in inspired oxygen tension would improve oxygen diffusion and alleviate hypoxemia rather than worsen it.

**Buzzword Combo:** Supplemental oxygen + increased diffusion gradient + improved oxygenation = improved hypoxemia

### D) Increased hemoglobin concentration

Increased hemoglobin concentration improves oxygen-carrying capacity and does not explain exercise-induced worsening of hypoxemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increased hemoglobin + greater oxygen carrying capacity + compensation for hypoxia = improved oxygen delivery
