## Buzzword Question

A patient develops fever, rash, joint pain, and renal inflammation after exposure to a soluble antigen. Tissue injury is caused by complement activation and neutrophil recruitment after antibodies bind antigens that are not fixed to cell surfaces. Which of the following mechanisms is responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

Fever, rash, and joint pain; renal inflammation after soluble antigen exposure; antibodies bind antigens not fixed to cell surfaces

## Answer Options

A) IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation
B) Circulating antigen-antibody complex deposition
C) Cytotoxic T-cell–mediated tissue injury
D) IgG binding to fixed cell-surface antigens

## Correct Answer

B) Circulating antigen-antibody complex deposition


## Explanation

This describes Type III hypersensitivity, in which circulating antigen-antibody complexes deposit in tissues and activate complement. This leads to neutrophil recruitment and systemic inflammation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Soluble antigen exposure + complement activation + neutrophil-mediated tissue inflammation = circulating immune complex deposition

## Wrong Options

### A) IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation

IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation describes Type I hypersensitivity, which causes immediate allergic reactions. It does not match tissue injury from circulating immune complex deposition.

**Buzzword Combo:** Allergen exposure + IgE on mast cells + histamine release = type I hypersensitivity

### C) Cytotoxic T-cell–mediated tissue injury

Cytotoxic T-cell–mediated tissue injury describes Type IV hypersensitivity, a delayed antibody-independent reaction. It is not mediated by soluble antigen-antibody complexes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Delayed inflammation + T cells + macrophage activation = type IV hypersensitivity

### D) IgG binding to fixed cell-surface antigens

IgG binding to fixed cell-surface antigens describes Type II hypersensitivity. In Type III hypersensitivity, antibodies bind soluble antigens and form circulating immune complexes.

**Buzzword Combo:** IgG/IgM against fixed antigen + direct cellular/receptor injury + complement activation = type II hypersensitivity
