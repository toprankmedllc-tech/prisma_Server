## Buzzword Question

A 45-year-old man with a history of alcohol use disorder is found vomiting repeatedly for 2 days. He appears volume depleted. ABG: pH 7.52, PaCO₂ 48 mmHg, HCO₃ 38 mEq/L. Serum Cl is 88 mEq/L. What is the primary acid-base disturbance?

## Buzzwords Used

Alcohol use disorder; Vomiting; Volume depletion; pH 7.52; PaCO₂ 48; HCO₃ 38; Hypochloremia;

## Answer Options

A) Respiratory acidosis with metabolic compensation
B) Primary metabolic alkalosis with appropriate respiratory compensation
C) Primary respiratory alkalosis from alcohol withdrawal
D) Mixed metabolic alkalosis and respiratory alkalosis

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

pH is alkalemic and HCO₃ is elevated — confirming primary metabolic alkalosis. Vomiting causes loss of HCl, raising HCO₃. The elevated PaCO₂ of 48 represents the expected hypoventilation compensation — retaining CO₂ to buffer the alkalosis. Hypochloremia supports chloride-responsive metabolic alkalosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Vomiting + HCl loss + high HCO₃ + alkalemic pH + elevated PaCO₂ (compensation) + hypochloremia = Metabolic alkalosis

## Wrong Options

### A) Respiratory acidosis with metabolic compensation

This is the premature closure trap — the elevated PaCO₂ of 48 is noticed and conclusion of  respiratory acidosis is made .However, the pH is 7.52 (alkalemic), which rules out a primary acidosis. The elevated PaCO₂ here is compensatory hypoventilation, not a primary respiratory problem. Always check pH first.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elevated PaCO₂ seen first + acidosis association = Premature respiratory acidosis label before confirming pH direction

### C) Primary respiratory alkalosis from alcohol withdrawal

Respiratory alkalosis would feature a low PaCO₂ as the primary abnormality. Here the PaCO₂ is elevated at 48 mmHg. While alcohol withdrawal can cause anxiety and hyperventilation, this ABG pattern does not support respiratory alkalosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alcohol history + alkalosis = Anchoring on alcohol withdrawal hyperventilation when PaCO₂ is actually elevated

### D) Mixed metabolic alkalosis and respiratory alkalosis

A superimposed respiratory alkalosis would require a PaCO₂ lower than expected for compensation. Here the PaCO₂ of 48 is an appropriate compensatory response to metabolic alkalosis — no evidence of an independent respiratory alkalosis exists.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two abnormalities seen + alkalemic pH = Mixed disorder label when second finding is simply appropriate compensation
