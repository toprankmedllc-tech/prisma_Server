## Buzzword Question

An 8-year-old boy is brought to the clinic because of a 3-day history of puffy eyes and swollen ankles. His mother notes that his urine has appeared dark and "tea-colored." He was treated for a "sore throat" 2 weeks ago. Physical examination reveals periorbital edema and 2+ pitting edema on the lower extremities. Blood pressure is 142/90 mmHg. Urinalysis shows dysmorphic red blood cells and red blood cell casts. The patient is diagnosed with poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis and initiated on a loop diuretic to manage his volume overload. This medication acts by inhibiting a cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle. Which of the following sets of electrolyte changes is most likely to be seen in this patient's urine?

## Buzzwords Used

Puffy eyes; Tea-colored urine; Sore throat 2 weeks ago; Red blood cell casts; Inhibiting a cotransporter in the thick ascending limb

## Answer Options


A) Decreased urinary Na+
 , Decreased urinary K +
 , Decreased urinary Ca2+
 
B) Increased urinary Na+ , Increased urinary K+
 , Increased urinary Ca2+
 
C) Increased urinary Na+ , Decreased urinary K +
 , Decreased urinary Ca 2+
 
D) Increased urinary Na + , Increased urinary K +
 , Decreased urinary Ca 2+

## Correct Answer

B) Increased urinary Na+ , Increased urinary K + , Increased urinary Ca 2+

## Explanation

Loop diuretics (e.g., furosemide) inhibit the Na+ /K+ /2Cl−
  cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, which abolishes the lumen-positive potential needed for paracellular reabsorption of divalent cations. This leads to a significant increase in the urinary excretion of sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium ("Loops lose calcium").

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Puffy eyes + Tea-colored urine + RBC casts = Loop diuretic required → Inhibits Na+ /K + /2Cl −
 → Increased urinary Na+ , K+, and Ca 2+

## Wrong Options

### A) Decreased urinary Na +
 , Decreased urinary K +
 , Decreased urinary Ca 2+

This choice represents a state of generalized renal retention or the effect of a drug that increases reabsorption across all these ions, which contradicts the mechanism of a loop diuretic that blocks reabsorption to promote excretion.

**Buzzword Combo:** Puffy eyes + Tea-colored urine + RBC casts = Misinterpreting diuretic action as retaining electrolytes → Decreased urinary Na +, K +, and Ca 2+


### C) Increased urinary Na + , Decreased urinary K + , Decreased urinary Ca 2+

This pattern resembles the action of a potassium-sparing diuretic or aldosterone antagonist, which promotes sodium excretion while retaining potassium and calcium, unlike loop diuretics which waste both.

**Buzzword Combo:** Puffy eyes + Tea-colored urine + RBC casts = Confusing loop diuretics with potassium-sparing diuretics → Increased urinary Na +
 , Decreased urinary K +
 , Decreased urinary Ca 2+
 

### D) Increased urinary Na + , Increased urinary K + , Decreased urinary Ca 2+

This option describes the electrolyte excretion pattern of thiazide diuretics, which inhibit NaCl reabsorption in the early distal convoluted tubule and actively increase calcium reabsorption, thereby lowering urinary calcium levels.

**Buzzword Combo:** Puffy eyes + Tea-colored urine + RBC casts = Confusing Loop diuretic mechanism with Thiazide diuretic mechanism ("Thiazides save calcium") → Increased urinary Na + , Increased urinary K + , Decreased urinary Ca2+
