## Buzzword Question

A 4-week-old infant is brought to the pediatrician due to persistent cough and rapid breathing for 5 days. The mother received no prenatal care and had dysuria and mucopurulent cervical discharge during pregnancy. Physical examination shows tachypnea and bilateral diffuse crackles. The infant is afebrile. Chest x-ray demonstrates bilateral interstitial infiltrates. Laboratory studies show peripheral eosinophilia. Which of the following is the most likely cause of this infant’s condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Afebrile pneumonia; staccato cough; eosinophilia; interstitial infiltrates; neonatal infection; maternal cervicitis

## Answer Options

A) Neonatal Chlamydial Pneumonia

B) Respiratory Syncytial Virus Infection

C) Group B Streptococcal Infection

D) Neonatal Gonococcal Conjunctivitis

## Correct Answer

A) Neonatal Chlamydial Pneumonia

## Explanation

Neonatal Chlamydial Pneumonia classically presents at 2–12 weeks of age with afebrile pneumonia, staccato cough, eosinophilia, and diffuse interstitial infiltrates. Infection occurs during passage through an infected birth canal.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Afebrile infant + staccato cough + eosinophilia = Neonatal Chlamydial Pneumonia

## Wrong Options

### B) Respiratory Syncytial Virus Infection

RSV usually causes bronchiolitis with wheezing, fever, and hyperinflation rather than eosinophilia and afebrile staccato cough.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wheezing + bronchiolitis + winter infant infection = RSV

### C) Group B Streptococcal Infection

Group B Streptococcus typically presents in neonates with sepsis, pneumonia, or meningitis shortly after birth rather than subacute afebrile pneumonia weeks later.

**Buzzword Combo:** Early neonatal sepsis + meningitis + gram-positive cocci = Group B Streptococcus

### D) Neonatal Gonococcal Conjunctivitis

Neisseria gonorrhoeae classically causes severe purulent conjunctivitis in the first week of life, not afebrile pneumonia with eosinophilia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Purulent neonatal conjunctivitis + first week of life + gram-negative diplococci = Gonorrhea
