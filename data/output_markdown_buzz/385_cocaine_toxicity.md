## Buzzword Question

A 27-year-old man undergoes nasal surgery for recurrent epistaxis. During the procedure, a topical anesthetic is applied to the nasal mucosa. Shortly afterward, he develops severe hypertension, tachycardia, dilated pupils, and ventricular arrhythmias. Which local anesthetic was most likely administered?

## Buzzwords Used

Nasal surgery ; topical anesthetic ; severe hypertension ; tachycardia ; ventricular arrhythmia ; catecholamine reuptake inhibition

## Answer Options

A) Benzocaine
B) Cocaine
C) Bupivacaine
D) Prilocaine

## Correct Answer

B) Cocaine

## Explanation

Cocaine is the only local anesthetic that also inhibits reuptake of norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin. This causes sympathetic stimulation leading to hypertension, tachycardia, mydriasis, and arrhythmias.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Nasal surgery + hypertension/tachycardia + topical anesthetic = Cocaine

## Wrong Options

### A) Benzocaine

Benzocaine is associated with methemoglobinemia rather than severe sympathetic stimulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chocolate-brown blood + cyanosis + topical anesthetic = Benzocaine

### C) Bupivacaine

Bupivacaine is associated with severe cardiotoxicity and ventricular arrhythmias but not intense hypertension from catecholamine excess.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spinal anesthesia + refractory arrhythmia + cardiovascular collapse = Bupivacaine

### D) Prilocaine

Prilocaine classically causes methemoglobinemia due to oxidant metabolites.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low oxygen saturation + chocolate-brown blood + local anesthetic = Prilocaine
