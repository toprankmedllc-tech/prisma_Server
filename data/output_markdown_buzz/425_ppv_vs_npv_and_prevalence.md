## Buzzword Question

A test with 95% sensitivity and 95% specificity is deployed in two settings: a general population with 1% disease prevalence, and a disease-endemic region with 20% prevalence. Which of the following correctly describes how the predictive values differ between the two settings?

## Buzzwords Used

Sensitivity 95%; specificity 95%; prevalence 1% vs 20%; PPV and NPV comparison


## Answer Options

 A) PPV is higher and NPV is lower in the high-prevalence setting
 B) PPV and NPV are identical because sensitivity and specificity are fixed
 C) PPV is lower and NPV is higher in the high-prevalence setting 
D) Only NPV changes with prevalence; PPV remains constant

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

As prevalence increases: more true positives → PPV increases. Fewer true negatives relative to total negatives → NPV decreases. At 1% prevalence, PPV ≈ 16%, NPV ≈ 99.9%. At 20% prevalence, PPV ≈ 83%, NPV ≈ 98.7%. Both change with prevalence — PPV rises and NPV falls as disease becomes more common.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

↑ prevalence → ↑ PPV (more TP among positives) + ↓ NPV (more FN among negatives)

## Wrong Options

###  B) PPV and NPV are identical because sensitivity and specificity are fixed

A fundamental misconception. Sensitivity and specificity are fixed test properties, but PPV and NPV are population-dependent measures that change with every shift in disease prevalence. They are NOT the same as sensitivity and specificity.


**Buzzword Combo:** Sensitivity and specificity = fixed test properties; PPV and NPV = population-dependent, change with prevalence

### C) PPV is lower and NPV is higher in the high-prevalence setting

This is the reverse of the correct relationship. Higher prevalence means more true disease in the population → more true positives among test-positive patients → higher PPV, not lower. And fewer truly disease-free patients → proportionally more false negatives among test-negatives → lower NPV.

**Buzzword Combo:** ↓ prevalence → ↓ PPV + ↑ NPV (low disease burden makes negatives more reassuring)


### D) Only NPV changes with prevalence; PPV remains constant

Both PPV and NPV change with prevalence. PPV rises as prevalence rises (more true positives among positives). NPV falls as prevalence rises (more false negatives among negatives). Neither is immune to prevalence effects.

**Buzzword Combo:** Both PPV and NPV are prevalence-dependent; use Bayes theorem to calculate post-test probability
