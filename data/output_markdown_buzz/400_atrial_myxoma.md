## Buzzword Question

Echocardiography in a patient with positional dyspnea reveals a pedunculated left atrial mass prolapsing through the mitral valve in diastole. Which auscultatory finding is most likely?

## Buzzwords Used

pedunculated left atrial mass; prolapses through mitral valve in diastole

## Answer Options

A) High-pitched opening snap
B) Low-pitched "tumor plop"
C) Crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur
D) Holosystolic apical murmur

## Correct Answer

B) Low-pitched "tumor plop"

## Explanation


A pedunculated atrial myxoma prolapses through the mitral valve in diastole and strikes the valve apparatus, producing a low-pitched early diastolic "tumor plop."

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pedunculated atrial mass + diastolic mitral prolapse = tumor plop

## Wrong Options

### A) High-pitched opening snap

The hallmark of rheumatic mitral stenosis from leaflet thickening, not a mobile mass.

**Buzzword Combo:** High-pitched opening snap + history of rheumatic fever = mitral stenosis

### C) Crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur

The murmur of aortic stenosis — a fixed systolic obstruction, not a diastolic finding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur + RUSB = aortic stenosis

### D) Holosystolic apical murmur

The murmur of mitral regurgitation — systolic and radiating to the axilla, not diastolic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Holosystolic apical murmur + axillary radiation = mitral regurgitation
