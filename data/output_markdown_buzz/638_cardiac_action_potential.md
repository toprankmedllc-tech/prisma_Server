## Buzzword Question

The plateau phase (phase 2) of the ventricular myocyte action potential is most directly produced by which of the following?

## Buzzwords Used

Ventricular myocyte; phase 2 plateau; calcium influx; L-type calcium channels

## Answer Options


A) Calcium influx through L-type calcium channels balancing potassium efflux
B) Sodium influx through voltage-gated sodium channels
C) Potassium efflux through delayed rectifier channels
D) Chloride influx through transmembrane chloride channels

## Correct Answer

A) Calcium influx through L-type calcium channels balancing potassium efflux

## Explanation

The plateau phase (phase 2) of the ventricular myocyte action potential is produced by Ca²⁺ influx through L-type calcium channels, which is balanced by K⁺ efflux, maintaining depolarization for approximately 200 ms. This Ca²⁺ entry also triggers SR Ca²⁺ release (calcium-induced calcium release).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Phase 2 plateau + Ca²⁺ influx via L-type channels + balanced by K⁺ efflux = ventricular myocyte plateau

## Wrong Options

### B) Sodium influx through voltage-gated sodium channels

Sodium influx through voltage-gated Na⁺ channels produces phase 0 (rapid depolarization), not the phase 2 plateau.

**Buzzword Combo:** Phase 0 + rapid Na⁺ influx = ventricular myocyte upstroke

### 
C) Potassium efflux through delayed rectifier channels


K⁺ efflux through delayed rectifier channels produces phase 3 repolarization, returning the membrane potential to baseline.

**Buzzword Combo:** Phase 3 + K⁺ efflux + delayed rectifier = repolarization

### 
D) Chloride influx through transmembrane chloride channels

Chloride channels do not play a major role in the ventricular myocyte action potential.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chloride channels in epithelia + CFTR = cystic fibrosis (not cardiac AP)

## Related Concepts

Phase 0 sodium influx; Phase 3 potassium efflux; Calcium-induced calcium release
