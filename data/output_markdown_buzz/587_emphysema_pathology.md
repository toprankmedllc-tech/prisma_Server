## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old man with a long history of smoking presents with progressive dyspnea and reduced exercise tolerance. Examination shows a barrel chest and prolonged expiration. PFTs show ↓ FEV₁/FVC, ↑ TLC, and ↓ DLCO. Which of the following best explains the decreased DLCO?

## Buzzwords Used

Smoker; progressive dyspnea; barrel chest; hyperinflation; ↓ FEV₁/FVC; ↑ TLC; ↓ DLCO; emphysema

## Answer Options

A) Loss alveolar surface
B) Mucus plugging
C) Bronchospasm
D) Fibrotic thickening

## Correct Answer

A) Loss alveolar surface

## Explanation

Emphysema causes destruction of alveolar septa, leading to loss of alveolar-capillary surface area. This reduces diffusion capacity, resulting in decreased DLCO.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Smoker; alveolar destruction; ↓ DLCO = loss alveolar surface

## Wrong Options

### B) Mucus plugging

Mucus plugging is seen in chronic bronchitis and asthma, where airflow obstruction is due to airway secretions rather than alveolar destruction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cough; sputum; airway obstruction = chronic bronchitis

### C) Bronchospasm

Bronchospasm is characteristic of asthma and causes reversible airway narrowing without affecting DLCO.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wheezing; reversible obstruction; eosinophils = asthma

### D) Fibrotic thickening

Fibrotic thickening is seen in interstitial lung diseases like idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, which causes restrictive physiology rather than emphysema.

**Buzzword Combo:** velcro crackles; honeycombing; restriction = pulmonary fibrosis

## Related Concepts

Chronic bronchitis vs emphysema; DLCO interpretation in lung diseases; alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency vs smoking-related COPD
