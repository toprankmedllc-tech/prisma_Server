## Buzzword Question

A 41-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after being crushed in a construction accident. Pelvic radiography reveals multiple pelvic fractures. He complains of suprapubic pain, gross hematuria, and inability to void. CT cystography demonstrates contrast extravasation confined to the retropubic space surrounding the bladder. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Pelvic fracture; suprapubic pain; gross hematuria; inability to void; retropubic contrast extravasation

## Answer Options

A) Intraperitoneal bladder rupture

B) Extraperitoneal bladder rupture

C) Posterior urethral injury

D) Anterior urethral injury

## Correct Answer

B) Extraperitoneal bladder rupture

## Explanation

Pelvic fractures can cause bony fragments to perforate the anterior bladder wall or bladder neck, leading to urine leakage into the retropubic (extraperitoneal) space. Hematuria and difficulty voiding are common findings.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pelvic fracture + Retropubic urine leak + Hematuria = Extraperitoneal bladder rupture

## Wrong Options

### A) Intraperitoneal bladder rupture

Intraperitoneal rupture typically involves the bladder dome after blunt trauma to a distended bladder and causes urine accumulation within the peritoneal cavity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Full bladder + Seatbelt trauma + Intraperitoneal urine = Intraperitoneal bladder rupture

### C) Posterior urethral injury

Posterior urethral injury is suggested by blood at the urethral meatus and a high-riding prostate rather than bladder contrast extravasation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pelvic fracture + High-riding prostate + Blood at urethral meatus = Posterior urethral injury

### D) Anterior urethral injury

Anterior urethral injury usually follows a straddle injury and causes scrotal or perineal hematoma due to bulbar urethral disruption.

**Buzzword Combo:** Straddle injury + Bulbar urethra injury + Scrotal hematoma = Anterior urethral injury
