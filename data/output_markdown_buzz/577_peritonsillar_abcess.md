## Buzzword Question

A 19-year-old man presents with severe unilateral sore throat, fever, and difficulty swallowing. He reports that his voice sounds “muffled” and he is unable to open his mouth fully. Examination shows deviation of the uvula away from the affected side and pooling of saliva in the oral cavity. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Hot potato voice; Unilateral sore throat; Fever; Trismus; Uvular deviation; Dysphagia; Peritonsillar swelling; Muffled speech

## Answer Options

A) Acute epiglottitis
B) Peritonsillar abscess
C) Retropharyngeal abscess
D) Ludwig angina

## Correct Answer

B) Peritonsillar abscess

## Explanation

Peritonsillar abscess presents with severe unilateral throat pain, hot potato (muffled) voice, trismus, and uvular deviation away from the affected side due to peritonsillar space infection. It is often a complication of acute tonsillitis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hot potato voice + trismus + uvular deviation + unilateral sore throat = Peritonsillar abscess

## Wrong Options

### A) Acute epiglottitis

Epiglottitis presents with drooling, tripod position, and stridor rather than trismus or uvular deviation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Drooling + stridor + tripod position + no cough = Epiglottitis

### C) Retropharyngeal abscess

Retropharyngeal abscess presents with neck stiffness, fever, and posterior pharyngeal bulging rather than uvular deviation or hot potato voice.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neck stiffness + dysphagia + posterior pharyngeal bulge = Retropharyngeal abscess

### D) Ludwig angina

Ludwig angina is a bilateral submandibular space infection causing tongue elevation and airway compromise, not unilateral uvular deviation or hot potato voice.

**Buzzword Combo:** Floor of mouth swelling + tongue elevation + airway obstruction = Ludwig angina
