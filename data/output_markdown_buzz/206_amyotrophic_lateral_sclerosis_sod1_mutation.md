## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old man develops 1 year of progressive hand muscle wasting with fasciculations, brisk lower-extremity reflexes, and upgoing plantars. His father and paternal uncle both died of similar progressive weakness in their late 50s. Mutation in which gene is most commonly identified in this familial disease?

## Buzzwords Used

Mixed UMN + LMN signs; midlife onset; autosomal-dominant family history of similar motor neuron disease

## Answer Options

A) SOD1
B) SMN1
C) DMD 
D) HTT

## Correct Answer

A) SOD1

## Explanation

SOD1 is the most commonly identified gene in autosomal-dominant familial ALS (~20% of familial cases). Mutant SOD1 exerts a toxic gain of function that promotes motor neuron death.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

UMN + LMN signs + midlife onset + autosomal-dominant family history = SOD1 mutation → familial ALS

## Wrong Options

### SMN1

SMN1 deletion causes spinal muscular atrophy, a pure LMN disease of infants and children with hypotonia and tongue fasciculations, not adult-onset mixed UMN+LMN disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Floppy infant + tongue fasciculations + pure LMN signs + AR inheritance = SMN1 deletion → spinal muscular atrophy

### DMD

DMD mutations cause Duchenne muscular dystrophy, an X-linked disease of boys with proximal weakness, calf pseudohypertrophy, and elevated CK, not an adult motor neuron disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Boy + proximal weakness + calf pseudohypertrophy + Gowers sign + elevated CK = dystrophin mutation → Duchenne

### HTT

HTT CAG-repeat expansion causes Huntington disease: midlife chorea, psychiatric symptoms, and dementia with caudate atrophy, not motor neuron weakness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Midlife chorea + caudate atrophy + autosomal-dominant family history + CAG repeats = HTT → Huntington disease

## Related Concepts

Spinal muscular atrophy (pure LMN, infants); Primary lateral sclerosis (pure UMN); Werdnig-Hoffmann disease (infantile SMA type 1)
