## Buzzword Question

A patient with a large pericardial effusion is found to have beat-to-beat variation in QRS amplitude on ECG. This finding is most likely caused by:

## Buzzwords Used


Large pericardial effusion; beat-to-beat QRS variation

## Answer Options

A) Variable conduction through the AV node
B) Swinging motion of the heart within the pericardial fluid
C) Intermittent bundle branch block
D) Re-entry through an accessory pathway

## Correct Answer


B) Swinging motion of the heart within the pericardial fluid

## Explanation

In tamponade, fluid fills the pericardial sac and the heart literally swings back and forth inside it. Because the heart's position relative to the ECG leads changes with each beat, the QRS amplitude varies from beat to beat, this is electrical alternans, a classic sign of a large pericardial effusion.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Large pericardial effusion + beat-to-beat QRS variation + low-voltage QRS = electrical alternans (tamponade)

## Wrong Options

### Variable conduction through the AV node

Variable AV nodal conduction produces variable PR intervals or dropped beats, not beat-to-beat variation in QRS amplitude.

**Buzzword Combo:** Progressively lengthening PR + dropped beat = Mobitz I (Wenckebach)

### C) Intermittent bundle branch block

Intermittent bundle branch block produces alternating QRS morphology (wide vs. narrow), not alternating QRS amplitude.

**Buzzword Combo:** Rate-dependent wide QRS = intermittent BBB

### 
D) Re-entry through an accessory pathway

An accessory pathway produces a delta wave with a short PR interval (WPW), not amplitude alternans.

**Buzzword Combo:** Delta wave + short PR + wide QRS = Wolff-Parkinson-White
