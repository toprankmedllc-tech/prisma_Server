## Buzzword Question

A 4-year-old boy is brought to the pediatric clinic by his mother for a routine check-up. During the physical examination, the physician notices multiple hyperpigmented, flat macules with irregular borders ("café-au-lait spots") across his torso, along with multiple axillary freckles. Additionally, the child has several healing linear scabs on his shins and a small, isolated bruise on his right knee. The mother mentions he is very active and frequently falls while playing at the park. Which of the following best characterizes these physical findings?

## Buzzwords Used

Café-au-lait spots; Axillary freckling; Shin scabs; Bony prominence bruising; Normal active child

## Answer Options

A) Neurofibromatosis Type 1
B) Child Physical Abuse
C) Osteogenesis Imperfecta
D) Immune Thrombocytopenia

## Correct Answer

A) Neurofibromatosis Type 1

## Explanation

The presence of multiple café-au-lait spots and axillary freckling (Crowe sign) meets the diagnostic criteria for Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1), an autosomal dominant disorder caused by a mutation in the NF1 gene on chromosome 17. The small bruises and scabs on the shins and knees represent normal, expected childhood trauma from play on bony prominences, rather than physical abuse.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Café-au-lait spots + Axillary freckling + Lisch nodules = Neurofibromatosis Type 1

## Wrong Options

### B) Child Physical Abuse

While skin marks can raise suspicion for nonaccidental trauma, bruises located strictly over bony prominences (like knees and shins) are developmentally normal for an active 4-year-old. Misinterpreting normal childhood play injuries or genetic dermatological lesions as abuse creates a similar diagnosis confusion trap.

**Buzzword Combo:** Soft tissue bruising + Varying stages of healing + Defensive injuries = Child Physical Abuse

### C) Osteogenesis Imperfecta

Osteogenesis imperfecta is a defect in type I collagen synthesis that presents with recurrent fractures from minimal trauma, blue sclerae, and hearing loss, which are absent in this patient.

**Buzzword Combo:** Multiple fractures + Blue sclerae + Type I collagen defect = Osteogenesis Imperfecta

### D) Immune Thrombocytopenia

Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP) manifests with petechiae, purpura, and mucosal bleeding caused by anti-GpIIb/IIIa antibodies destroying platelets, which does not match the flat hyperpigmented macules seen here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Isolated thrombocytopenia + Petechiae/Purpura + Anti-platelet antibodies = Immune Thrombocytopenia
