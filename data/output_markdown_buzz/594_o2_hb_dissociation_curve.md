## Buzzword Question

A 67-year-old patient with sepsis is admitted to the ICU. He is febrile, acidotic, and has elevated lactate levels. Laboratory studies show increased 2,3-BPG production in RBCs, resulting in enhanced oxygen delivery to peripheral tissues despite reduced arterial oxygen content. Which of the following best describes the change in the oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation curve?

## Buzzwords Used

Sepsis; fever; acidosis; elevated lactate; increased 2,3-BPG; tissue hypoxia; oxygen delivery adaptation

## Answer Options

A) Left shift with decreased P50
B) Right shift with increased P50
C) Left shift with increased P50
D) No shift with unchanged P50

## Correct Answer

B) Right shift with increased P50

## Explanation

Sepsis increases 2,3-BPG and acidosis, both of which reduce hemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen, shifting the curve to the right. This facilitates oxygen unloading at tissues and increases P50 (oxygen tension at 50% saturation).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sepsis; ↑2,3-BPG; acidosis; right shift; ↑P50

## Wrong Options

### A) Left shift with decreased P50

Left shift occurs in conditions like HbF, alkalosis, or carbon monoxide poisoning, where oxygen affinity is increased. This would impair oxygen unloading, opposite of what is seen in sepsis.

**Buzzword Combo:** HbF; alkalosis; CO poisoning; ↑O₂ affinity

### C) Left shift with increased P50

Left shift is always associated with decreased P50, not increased P50. This option is physiologically inconsistent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increased affinity; impaired unloading; inconsistent physiology

### D) No shift with unchanged P50

Sepsis alters hemoglobin affinity via increased 2,3-BPG and acidosis, so a shift in the curve is expected. A normal curve would not explain improved tissue oxygen delivery adaptation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal physiology; no 2,3-BPG effect; unchanged affinity

## Related Concepts

Bohr effect vs Haldane effect; fetal hemoglobin and left shift; causes of right shift (exercise, fever, acidosis)
