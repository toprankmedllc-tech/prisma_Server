## Buzzword Question

A 30-year-old woman presents with anxiety, tingling in her fingers, and carpopedal spasm after a panic attack. RR is 28/min. ABG: pH 7.52, PaCO₂ 26 mmHg, HCO₃ 21 mEq/L. What is the primary acid-base disturbance?

## Buzzwords Used

Panic attack; Anxiety; Tingling; Carpopedal spasm; Tachypnea; pH 7.52; PaCO₂ 26; HCO₃ 21

## Answer Options

A) Metabolic alkalosis with respiratory compensation
B) Acute respiratory alkalosis with no significant renal compensation
C) Chronic respiratory alkalosis with complete renal compensation
D) Mixed respiratory alkalosis and metabolic alkalosis

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

pH is alkalemic and PaCO₂ is low — confirming primary respiratory alkalosis from hyperventilation during a panic attack. HCO₃ is only minimally decreased (21 vs. normal 24), consistent with acute onset — renal compensation (which lowers HCO₃ by 5 mEq per 10 mmHg drop in PaCO₂) requires days to occur. Carpopedal spasm results from hypocalcemia induced by alkalosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Panic attack + hyperventilation + low PaCO₂ + alkalemic pH + minimal HCO₃ drop = Acute respiratory alkalosis

## Wrong Options

### C) Chronic respiratory alkalosis with complete renal compensation

This is the premature closure trap — “mildly low” HCO₃ and assume compensation is complete and chronic. However, the pH of 7.52 is significantly alkalemic; truly compensated chronic respiratory alkalosis would bring pH close to normal. The acute panic attack context also makes chronic compensation impossible.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low HCO₃ + low PaCO₂ = Premature chronic label without recognizing pH is still significantly alkalemic and onset is acute

### A) Metabolic alkalosis with respiratory compensation

Primary metabolic alkalosis requires an elevated HCO₃ as the primary abnormality. Here HCO₃ is 21 — below normal — making metabolic alkalosis impossible. The primary driver is the low PaCO₂ from hyperventilation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alkalemic pH + “alkalosis” = Metabolic alkalosis label applied despite HCO₃ being low, not high

### D) Mixed respiratory alkalosis and metabolic alkalosis

A superimposed metabolic alkalosis would require an independently elevated HCO₃. Here HCO₃ is 21 — slightly below normal — providing no evidence of a separate metabolic alkalosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two abnormalities + alkalemia = Mixed label when HCO₃ is actually low, not independently elevated
