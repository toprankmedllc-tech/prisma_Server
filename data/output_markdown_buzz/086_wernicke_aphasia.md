## Buzzword Question

A 69-year-old right-handed man is brought to the emergency department after sudden onset of “confused speech” noted by his wife 2 hours ago. She reports that he is speaking continuously but his words make little sense. He appears frustrated when others do not understand him.

On examination, he is alert and has fluent speech with normal grammar and prosody. However, his speech is filled with incorrect words and nonsensical phrases. He is unable to follow simple commands such as “close your eyes” or “point to the ceiling.” When asked to repeat the sentence “no ifs, ands, or buts,” he is unable to do so. He is unaware of his speech errors.

Motor and sensory examination is normal. CT head shows no hemorrhage.

Which of the following is the most likely location of the lesion?

## Buzzwords Used

Fluent aphasia ; word salad ; impaired comprehension ; impaired repetition ; anosognosia ; paraphasic errors ; superior temporal gyrus ; dominant hemisphere

## Answer Options

A) Inferior frontal gyrus
B) Superior temporal gyrus
C) Angular gyrus
D) Primary motor cortex

## Correct Answer

B) Superior temporal gyrus

## Explanation

Wernicke Aphasia results from a lesion in the dominant superior temporal gyrus. It is characterized by fluent but meaningless speech, poor comprehension, impaired repetition, and lack of awareness of deficits (anosognosia).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Fluent speech + poor comprehension + impaired repetition = superior temporal gyrus lesion

## Wrong Options

### A) Inferior frontal gyrus

Broca Aphasia causes non-fluent, effortful speech with preserved comprehension but impaired repetition and naming.

**Buzzword Combo:** Non-fluent speech + intact comprehension + effortful output = Broca aphasia

### C) Angular gyrus

Gerstmann Syndrome causes calculation and spatial-language deficits, not fluent aphasia with word salad.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acalculia + agraphia + finger agnosia = angular gyrus lesion

### D) Primary motor cortex

Motor Cortex Stroke produces pure motor deficits (contralateral weakness) without language impairment or fluent speech disturbances.

**Buzzword Combo:** Contralateral weakness + UMN signs = motor cortex lesion
