## Buzzword Question

A boy develops jaundice after eating fava beans. His RBCs are unable to maintain glutathione in its protective reduced form during oxidative stress. Which of the following best explains the mechanism of this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Boy; jaundice after eating fava beans; unable to maintain glutathione in its protective reduced form; oxidative stress

## Answer Options

A) Defective beta-globin chain structure
B) Decreased NADPH production in the pentose phosphate pathway
C) Impaired ATP generation from glycolysis
D) Loss of RBC membrane cytoskeletal stability

## Correct Answer

B) Decreased NADPH production in the pentose phosphate pathway


## Explanation

G6PD deficiency decreases NADPH production in the pentose phosphate pathway. Without enough NADPH, RBCs cannot maintain reduced glutathione and become vulnerable to oxidative damage.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Fava bean-triggered hemolysis + decreased reduced glutathione + decreased NADPH = G6PD deficiency

## Wrong Options

### A) Defective beta-globin chain structure

This describes sickle cell disease, which is caused by an abnormal beta-globin chain. It causes hemoglobin polymerization rather than decreased NADPH production.

**Buzzword Combo:** Beta-globin mutation + hemoglobin polymerization + vaso-occlusive pain = sickle cell disease

### C) Impaired ATP generation from glycolysis

This describes pyruvate kinase deficiency. RBCs lose ATP and develop chronic hemolysis, but reduced glutathione protection is not the primary defect.

**Buzzword Combo:** Decreased RBC ATP + chronic hemolysis + echinocytes = pyruvate kinase deficiency

### D) Loss of RBC membrane cytoskeletal stability

This describes hereditary spherocytosis due to RBC membrane protein defects. The classic finding is spherocytes rather than oxidative hemolysis after fava beans.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spectrin/ankyrin defect + spherocytes + splenomegaly = hereditary spherocytosis

## Related Concepts

Oxidative hemolysis; Heinz bodies; sulfa drugs
