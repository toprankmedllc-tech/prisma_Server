## Buzzword Question

A patient with a large pericardial effusion presents with hypotension, jugular venous distention, and muffled heart sounds. This constellation of findings is known as:

## Buzzwords Used

Large pericardial effusion; hypotension; jugular venous distention; muffled heart sounds; Beck's triad

## Answer Options

A) Virchow's triad
B) Cushing's triad
C) Beck's triad
D) Charcot's triad

## Correct Answer

C) Beck's triad

## Explanation

Beck's triad — hypotension, jugular venous distention, and muffled heart sounds — is the classic clinical signature of cardiac tamponade. Rising intrapericardial pressure impairs cardiac filling, dampens transmitted heart sounds, and causes systemic venous backup.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hypotension + JVD + muffled heart sounds = Beck's triad (tamponade)

## Wrong Options

### A) Virchow's triad

Virchow's triad — venous stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability — describes the risk factors for venous thromboembolism, not tamponade.

**Buzzword Combo:** Stasis + endothelial injury + hypercoagulability = Virchow's triad (DVT/PE risk)

### B) Cushing's triad


Cushing's triad — hypertension, bradycardia, and irregular respirations — is the signature of elevated intracranial pressure, not tamponade.

**Buzzword Combo:** HTN + bradycardia + irregular respirations = Cushing's triad (↑ ICP)

### D) Charcot's triad

Charcot's triad — fever, jaundice, and right upper quadrant pain — is the classic presentation of acute ascending cholangitis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fever + jaundice + RUQ pain = Charcot's triad (cholangitis)
