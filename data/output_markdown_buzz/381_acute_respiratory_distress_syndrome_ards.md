## Buzzword Question

A patient develops severe hypoxemia and diffuse bilateral pulmonary infiltrates several days after septic shock. Echocardiogram is normal, and the PaO2/FiO2 ratio is 85 mmHg. Mechanical ventilation is initiated using low tidal volumes and PEEP. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Sepsis; diffuse bilateral infiltrates; severe hypoxemia; normal echocardiogram; low PaO2/FiO2 ratio; low tidal volume ventilation


## Answer Options

 A) Cardiogenic pulmonary edema
 B) Acute respiratory distress syndrome
 C) Pulmonary embolism
 D) COPD exacerbation

## Correct Answer

 B) Acute respiratory distress syndrome


## Explanation

ARDS is characterized by acute hypoxemic respiratory failure with bilateral infiltrates not explained by heart failure. Sepsis is the most common trigger, and management includes lung-protective ventilation with low tidal volume and PEEP.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sepsis + bilateral infiltrates + severe refractory hypoxemia = ARDS


## Wrong Options

###  A) Cardiogenic pulmonary edema

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema usually presents with evidence of heart failure such as cardiomegaly, elevated BNP, or reduced ejection fraction.


**Buzzword Combo:** Reduced EF + pulmonary edema + elevated BNP = Cardiogenic pulmonary edema


### C) Pulmonary embolism


Pulmonary embolism may cause hypoxemia but does not typically produce diffuse bilateral infiltrates on chest imaging.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Sudden dyspnea + pleuritic chest pain + clear lungs = Pulmonary embolism


###  D) COPD exacerbation


COPD exacerbations present with wheezing and hypercapnia rather than diffuse alveolar infiltrates and severe noncardiogenic pulmonary edema.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Smoking history + wheezing + hypercapnia = COPD exacerbation
