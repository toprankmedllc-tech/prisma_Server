## Buzzword Question

A young man develops jaundice and dark urine after eating fava beans. Peripheral smear shows bite cells and Heinz bodies. G6PD deficiency most directly leads to decreased production of which molecule?

## Buzzwords Used

jaundice; dark urine; fava beans; bite cells; Heinz bodies; oxidative stress; pentose phosphate pathway

## Answer Options

A) ATP
B) NADPH
C) Bilirubin
D) Pyruvate

## Correct Answer

B. NADPH

## Explanation

G6PD catalyzes the rate-limiting step of the pentose phosphate pathway, producing NADPH. NADPH is required to regenerate reduced glutathione, which protects RBCs from oxidative damage.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pentose phosphate pathway + glutathione protection = NADPH deficiency → G6PD deficiency

## Wrong Options

### A) ATP

ATP is produced via glycolysis, and its deficiency is seen in pyruvate kinase deficiency causing chronic hemolysis with burr cells. It is unrelated to G6PD function.

**Buzzword Combo:** ATP depletion + burr cells = Pyruvate kinase deficiency

### C) Bilirubin

Bilirubin is a breakdown product of heme metabolism and increases secondary to hemolysis. It is not the primary molecule affected in G6PD deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemolysis + indirect hyperbilirubinemia = RBC destruction

### D) Pyruvate

Pyruvate is a glycolysis end product and is unrelated to the pentose phosphate pathway. G6PD deficiency does not affect pyruvate production.

**Buzzword Combo:** Glycolysis + pyruvate production = metabolic pathway unrelated to G6PD
