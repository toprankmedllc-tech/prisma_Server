## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man has intention tremor, gait ataxia, and mild cognitive decline diagnosed as FXTAS. His daughter is an asymptomatic carrier. His 8-year-old grandson has severe intellectual disability, macroorchidism, and autism-like behaviors. The molecular mechanism causing progressively more severe disease in successive generations is:


## Buzzwords Used

Grandfather mild (FXTAS premutation); daughter carrier unaffected; grandson severe intellectual disability and macroorchidism; worsening across three generations; CGG repeat; FMR1; anticipation

## Answer Options

A) CGG trinucleotide repeat expansion in the FMR1 gene — anticipation causes expanding repeats and worsening severity across generations 
B) Variable expressivity of an autosomal dominant point mutation 
C) Genomic imprinting — parent-of-origin determines which allele is expressed 
D) Accumulation of somatic mutations in mitochondrial DNA across generations


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Fragile X syndrome demonstrates anticipation via CGG repeat expansion in FMR1: Normal ≤44 repeats; Premutation 55–200 repeats (grandfather with FXTAS,  tremor/ataxia in older males with premutation; female carriers risk premature ovarian insufficiency); Full mutation >200 repeats (grandson: intellectual disability, macroorchidism, autism). Repeats expand during maternal transmission, causing progressively more severe disease = anticipation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

CGG repeats expand with each generation through maternal transmission → premutation (FXTAS in males) → full mutation (intellectual disability + macroorchidism + autism) = Fragile X anticipation

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Variable expressivity of an autosomal dominant mutation

Variable expressivity describes unpredictable differences in severity among family members with the same mutation — without directional generational worsening. Fragile X shows predictable , directional worsening due to repeat expansion (anticipation). Also Fragile X is X-linked, not autosomal dominant.

**Buzzword Combo:** Same mutation + variable severity in family members without directional trend = Variable expressivity (not anticipation)

### 2: C) Genomic imprinting

Genomic imprinting = parent-of-origin-specific gene expression. Explains Prader-Willi (paternal 15q11-13 deletion or maternal UPD15) and Angelman syndrome (maternal deletion or paternal UPD15). FMR1 is not imprinted, the worsening across generations in Fragile X is due to CGG repeat expansion (anticipation), not imprinting

**Buzzword Combo:** Disease expression depends on which PARENT contributed the chromosome → Genomic imprinting (e.g., Prader-Willi vs Angelman depending on parent of origin)

### 3: D) Somatic mitochondrial mutation accumulation

Somatic mutations accumulate within one individual's lifetime but are not heritably transmitted to offspring through germline. The progressive expansion of CGG repeats in FMR1 is a heritable germline  phenomenon,  the expanded repeat is passed in the gamete. This is fundamentally different from somatic mutation accumulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mutations in non-germline cells accumulating during life = Somatic mutations (not heritable to offspring)
