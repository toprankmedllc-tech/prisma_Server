## Buzzword Question

A 40-year-old man with fatigue, episodic dark morning urine, hemoglobinuria, elevated LDH, low haptoglobin, and evidence of intravascular hemolysis most likely has which condition?

## Buzzwords Used

fatigue; dark morning urine; hemoglobinuria; elevated LDH; low haptoglobin; intravascular hemolysis

## Answer Options

A. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia
B. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria
C. G6PD deficiency
D. Hereditary spherocytosis

## Correct Answer

B. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria

## Explanation

PNH is an acquired stem cell disorder due to PIGA mutation causing loss of CD55 and CD59, leading to complement-mediated intravascular hemolysis. It classically presents with nocturnal hemolysis and morning hemoglobinuria.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Morning hemoglobinuria + CD55/CD59 deficiency + intravascular hemolysis = PNH

## Wrong Options

### A. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

AIHA causes extravascular hemolysis with a positive Coombs test and spherocytes, not intravascular hemolysis or hemoglobinuria.

**Buzzword Combo:** Positive Coombs + spherocytes + extravascular hemolysis = AIHA

### C. G6PD deficiency

G6PD deficiency causes episodic oxidative hemolysis triggered by stressors and shows bite cells and Heinz bodies, not chronic nocturnal hemoglobinuria.

**Buzzword Combo:** G6PD deficiency causes episodic oxidative hemolysis triggered by stressors and shows bite cells and Heinz bodies, not chronic nocturnal hemoglobinuria.

### D. Hereditary spherocytosis

Hereditary spherocytosis is a chronic extravascular hemolytic anemia with spherocytes and splenomegaly, not intravascular hemolysis or hemoglobinuria.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spherocytes + splenomegaly + osmotic fragility = Hereditary spherocytosis
