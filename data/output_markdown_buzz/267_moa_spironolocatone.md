## Buzzword Question

A 65-year-old man with a history of chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HEFREF, NYHA Class III) is admitted to the hospital due to an acute flare characterized by increasing dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, and severe bilateral lower extremity edema. He is placed on aggressive intravenous furosemide therapy. Over the next 4 days, his swelling improves markedly, but his serum potassium drops to 3.1 mEq/L and his serum bicarbonate rises to 33 mEq/L. To counteract these specific electrolyte and acid-base disturbances while optimizing his long-term heart failure survival, which of the following mechanisms of action should be targeted by the next medication added to his regimen?

## Buzzwords Used

Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction; Furosemide therapy; Serum potassium drops; Bicarbonate rises; Optimize long-term survival

## Answer Options

A) Blockade of Na +
  channels in the cortical collecting tubule
B) Competitive antagonism of aldosterone receptors
C) Direct inhibition of renin enzymatic activity
D) Inhibition of carbonic anhydrase in the proximal tubule

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (such as spironolactone or eplerenone) competitively block aldosterone receptors in the cortical collecting tubule, preventing Na+ reabsorption and K+/H+ secretion. This corrects both the hypokalemia and metabolic alkalosis caused by loop diuretics while providing a proven survival benefit in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Furosemide hypokalemia + Alkalosis + Heart failure survival benefit = Aldosterone antagonist action → Competitive antagonism of aldosterone receptors.

## Wrong Options

### A) Blockade of Na + channels in the cortical collecting tubule

This describes the mechanism of amiloride and triamterene; while they are potassium-sparing diuretics that could fix the hypokalemia, they do not carry the established long-term mortality benefits in severe heart failure that aldosterone antagonists possess.

**Buzzword Combo:** Furosemide hypokalemia + Alkalosis + Heart failure survival benefit = Confusing epithelial sodium channel blockers with mineralocorticoid receptor blockers → Blockade of Na+ channels in the cortical collecting tubule.

### C) Direct inhibition of renin enzymatic activity

This is the mechanism of aliskiren, a direct renin inhibitor that is used primarily for hypertension but is not indicated or proven to improve survival outcomes in severe heart failure regimens.

**Buzzword Combo:** Furosemide hypokalemia + Alkalosis + Heart failure survival benefit = Selecting an upstream RAAS inhibitor without mortality data in heart failure → Direct inhibition of renin enzymatic activity.

### D) Inhibition of carbonic anhydrase in the proximal tubule

While acetazolamide can treat contraction alkalosis by forcing bicarbonate excretion, it causes significant potassium wasting, which would worsen this patient’s pre-existing hypokalemia and has no survival benefit in heart failure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Furosemide hypokalemia + Alkalosis + Heart failure survival benefit = Prioritizing alkalosis correction over worsening hypokalemia and heart failure mortality → Inhibition of carbonic anhydrase in the proximal tubule.
