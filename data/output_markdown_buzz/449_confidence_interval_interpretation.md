## Buzzword Question

A statin RCT reports: mean LDL reduction = 38 mg/dL (95% CI: 32 to 44 mg/dL, p < 0.001). A second trial of a fibrate reports: mean LDL reduction = 5 mg/dL (95% CI: −2 to 12 mg/dL, p = 0.19). Which of the following correctly interprets these confidence intervals?


## Buzzwords Used

95% CI 32-44 does not include zero; statistically significant; 95% CI -2 to 12 includes zero; not statistically significant

## Answer Options

A) The statin result is statistically significant; the fibrate result is not, because its CI includes zero B) Both results are statistically significant because both show positive mean LDL reductions C) The fibrate is not significant because its CI is too wide, not because it crosses zero D) A 95% CI means 95% of patients will have LDL reductions within that range

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

A 95% CI that does NOT include the null value (0 for differences; 1 for ratios) corresponds to statistical significance (p < 0.05). The statin CI (32–44) does not include 0 → significant (p < 0.001 confirms). The fibrate CI (−2 to 12) includes 0 → not statistically significant (p = 0.19 confirms). The CI crossing zero means we cannot exclude zero difference (no effect) at the 5% significance level.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

CI excludes null value (0) = statistically significant; CI includes null value (0) = not statistically significant

## Wrong Options

### B) Both are statistically significant because both show positive mean LDL reductions

A positive point estimate alone does not confer statistical significance. The fibrate showed a positive mean (5 mg/dL) but the CI spans from −2 to +12, meaning zero reduction is within the plausible range of true effects. Statistical significance requires that the entire CI exclude the null value  not just that the point estimate favors one direction.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Positive point estimate + CI includes zero = Not statistically significant (cannot rule out no effect)

### C) The fibrate is not significant because its CI is too wide

The fibrate's non-significance is because its CI includes zero (the null value), not because it is wide. A wide CI reflects imprecision (small sample or high variability) and may or may not include zero. The fibrate CI happens to be wide AND includes zero, but zero-crossing is the determinant of significance, not width alone

**Buzzword Combo:** Wide CI = imprecision; CI crossing zero = non-significance; these are related but distinct concepts

### D) A 95% CI means 95% of patients will have LDL reductions in that range

This is a common misconception. A 95% CI describes uncertainty about the population parameter (the true mean LDL reduction), not the distribution of individual patient responses. If the study were repeated 100 times, approximately 95 of those CIs would contain the true population mean  it is a statement about the interval, not individual variation. Individual variation is described by prediction intervals.

**Buzzword Combo:** 95% CI = 95% of repeated study intervals contain true mean; NOT individual patient prediction
