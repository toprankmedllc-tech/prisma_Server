## Buzzword Question

An 18-year-old college student is brought to the emergency department after collapsing during a marathon. Her friends report that she drank massive amounts of water throughout the race to "stay hydrated." She is currently confused and disoriented. Laboratory studies show a serum sodium level of 118 mEq/L and a serum osmolality of 250 mOsm/kg. Urinalysis reveals a urine osmolality of 85 mOsm/kg and a urine sodium concentration of 12 mEq/L. Which of the following best explains the primary renal mechanism responsible for her condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Marathon runner; massive amounts of water; confused and disoriented; serum sodium 118 mEq/L; urine osmolality 85 mOsm/kg

## Answer Options

A) Exceeding the renal capacity for free water excretion
B) Syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH)
C) Hypovolemia-induced activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system
D) Decreased solute delivery to the diluting segments of the nephron

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The patient has psychogenic polydipsia/exercise-associated hyponatremia due to massive water intake that exceeds the kidney's maximum capacity to excrete free water (approximately 12 to 18 L/day). The appropriately low urine osmolality (less than 100 mOsm/kg) demonstrates that ADH is successfully suppressed, ruling out ADH-mediated water retention.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Marathon runner + Massive water intake + Urine osmolality less than 100 mOsm/kg = Exceeding renal free water excretion capacity


## Wrong Options

### B) Syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH)

SIADH would cause inappropriate water retention, but the urine osmolality would be inappropriately elevated (typically greater than 100 mOsm/kg, and often greater than serum osmolality) despite the hyponatremia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Marathon runner + Hyponatremia + Inappropriately high urine osmolality = SIADH

### C) Hypovolemia-induced activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system

Hypovolemic hyponatremia occurs with significant volume depletion (e.g., severe vomiting/diarrhea), which triggers ADH release and leads to a high urine osmolality, unlike this patient's maximally dilute urine.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dehydration + Low blood pressure + Concentrated urine = Hypovolemic hyponatremia

### D) Decreased solute delivery to the diluting segments of the nephron


Decreased solute delivery to the diluting segments occurs in states of severe volume depletion or advanced chronic kidney disease, limiting the kidney's ability to dilute urine, which is not the case here given the urine osmolality of 85 mOsm/kg.


**Buzzword Combo:** Advanced kidney disease + Low GFR + Inability to dilute urine = Decreased solute delivery to diluting segments
