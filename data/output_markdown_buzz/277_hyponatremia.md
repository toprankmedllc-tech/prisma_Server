## Buzzword Question

A patient with chronic hyponatremia undergoes rapid correction and subsequently develops dysarthria and quadriplegia. Which of the following neurologic complications is most likely responsible for this patient’s symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

A) Cerebral edema
B) Osmotic demyelination syndrome
C) Subarachnoid hemorrhage
D) Wernicke encephalopathy

## Answer Options

Rapid correction; Hyponatremia; Quadriplegia

## Correct Answer

B) Osmotic demyelination syndrome

## Explanation

Osmotic demyelination syndrome may occur when chronic hyponatremia is corrected too rapidly, causing osmotic injury to oligodendrocytes within the central pons. Patients may develop dysarthria, dysphagia, quadriplegia, and altered mental status

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hyponatremia + rapid Correction + Seizure = Osmotic demyelination syndrome

## Wrong Options

### Cerebral edema

Cerebral edema is more commonly associated with acute hyponatremia itself rather than rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia. Severe brain swelling may cause seizures, headache, and increased intracranial pressure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acute hyponatremia + Seizures + increased ICP = Cerebral edema

### Subarachnoid hemorrhage

Subarachnoid hemorrhage classically presents with sudden severe headache due to rupture of a berry aneurysm. Patients often describe a thunderclap headache with neck stiffness and photophobia

**Buzzword Combo:** Aneurysm rupture + Headache + Hypertension = Subarachnoid hemorrhage

### Wernicke encephalopathy

Wernicke encephalopathy results from thiamine deficiency and classically presents with confusion, ophthalmoplegia, and ataxia. It is commonly associated with chronic alcoholism and malnutrition

**Buzzword Combo:** Thiamine deficiency + ophthalmoplegia + ataxia = Wernicke encephalopathy
