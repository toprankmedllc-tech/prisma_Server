## Buzzword Question

A 61-year-old man presents 15 months after his wife's death. He continues to experience daily intense yearning for her, believes his life has no purpose without her, avoids all places associated with her, and has been unable to return to his volunteer work. He denies depressed mood outside the context of missing her and continues to find pleasure in watching his grandchildren. The feature most specific to prolonged grief disorder (rather than MDD) in this presentation is:

## Buzzwords Used

15 months post-loss; persistent yearning; identity disruption ('no purpose'); functional impairment; preserved non-grief pleasure; no anhedonia outside grief context

## Answer Options

A) Persistent yearning and longing for the deceased with grief-anchored identity disruption, in the presence of preserved capacity for non-grief-related pleasure 
 B) Weight loss and insomnia occurring in the context of a major loss 
 C) Passive wish to die in order to be reunited with the deceased  
D) Duration of symptoms exceeding 2 weeks with functional impairment

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Prolonged grief disorder is characterized by grief-specific symptoms: persistent yearning/longing for the deceased, identity disruption (loss of sense of self without the deceased), difficulty accepting the death, and bitterness,  all anchored specifically to the loss. Critically, the capacity for non-grief-related pleasure is preserved (he still enjoys grandchildren), which distinguishes PGD from MDD. In MDD, anhedonia is pervasive and not restricted to grief content. The combination of grief-specific distress + preserved pleasure capacity outside grief context = PGD, not MDD.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Yearning + identity disruption + functional impairment + preserved non-grief pleasure = Prolonged Grief Disorder (not MDD)


## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Weight loss and insomnia in context of loss

Weight loss and insomnia are neurovegetative symptoms that occur in both normal grief, PGD, and MDD. They do not distinguish PGD from MDD. The distinguishing feature is the grief-specificity of symptoms and the preservation of non-grief-related pleasure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neurovegetative symptoms (insomnia, weight loss) = present in grief AND MDD, non-specific for PGD diagnosis

### 2: C) Passive wish to die to be reunited with the deceased

A passive death wish to be reunited with the deceased ('I want to join him') is a grief-specific phenomenon common in both normal grief and PGD. It is not suicidal ideation in the MDD sense (which involves escape from worthlessness or hopelessness), and while it supports PGD, it is not the most specific distinguishing feature from MDD. The preserved pleasure capacity is a more specific differentiator.

**Buzzword Combo:** Passive death wish ('want to join deceased') = grief-specific but not the single most distinguishing feature from MDD

### 3: D) Duration exceeding 2 weeks with functional impairment

The 2-week minimum duration with functional impairment is the diagnostic threshold for MDD, not PGD. PGD requires a minimum of 12 months duration (DSM-5-TR). Two-week duration would not meet PGD criteria at all.

**Buzzword Combo:** ≥5/9 symptoms for ≥2 weeks with impairment = MDD duration threshold; PGD requires ≥12 months
