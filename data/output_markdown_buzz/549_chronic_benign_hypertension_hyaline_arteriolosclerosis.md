## Buzzword Question

A patient with longstanding mild-to-moderate hypertension dies of an unrelated cause. Microscopic examination of the renal arterioles reveals homogeneous, pink (eosinophilic) thickening of the arteriolar walls. This finding is most consistent with:

## Buzzwords Used

Longstanding hypertension; homogeneous pink arteriolar thickening; eosinophilic; hyaline arteriolosclerosis; benign nephrosclerosis

## Answer Options

A) Hyaline arteriolosclerosis
B) Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis
C) Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles
D) Atherosclerotic plaque deposition

## Correct Answer

A) Hyaline arteriolosclerosis

## Explanation

Hyaline arteriolosclerosis — homogeneous, pink thickening of arteriolar walls from plasma protein leakage and basement membrane material deposition — is the histologic hallmark of chronic benign hypertension and diabetes mellitus. It underlies benign nephrosclerosis with grossly granular, atrophic kidneys.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic benign HTN / diabetes + homogeneous pink arteriolar thickening = hyaline arteriolosclerosis

## Wrong Options

### B) Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis

Concentric laminated smooth muscle thickening seen in malignant hypertension, not benign HTN.

**Buzzword Combo:** Malignant HTN + onion-skin laminated arteriolar wall = hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis

### C) Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles

Bright pink, glassy necrotic arteriolar wall from plasma protein deposition — seen in malignant hypertension and polyarteritis nodosa.

**Buzzword Combo:** Malignant HTN / PAN + bright pink glassy necrotic arteriolar wall = fibrinoid necrosis

### D) Atherosclerotic plaque deposition


Affects large and medium-sized arteries with intimal lipid deposition, not arterioles.

**Buzzword Combo:** Large/medium arteries + intimal lipid plaque + fibrous cap = atherosclerosis
