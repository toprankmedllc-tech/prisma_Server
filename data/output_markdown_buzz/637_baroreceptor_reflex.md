## Buzzword Question

A trauma patient develops hemorrhagic shock with a marked drop in systemic arterial pressure. Which of the following best describes the expected baroreceptor reflex response?

## Buzzwords Used

Hemorrhagic shock; ↓ arterial pressure; ↓ baroreceptor firing; ↑ sympathetic output; ↓ parasympathetic output; tachycardia; vasoconstriction

## Answer Options

A) ↑ Baroreceptor firing producing bradycardia and vasodilation
B) ↓ Baroreceptor firing producing tachycardia and vasoconstriction
C) No change in baroreceptor firing until BP drops below 60 mm Hg
D) Direct activation of the parasympathetic nervous system to restore BP

## Correct Answer


B) ↓ Baroreceptor firing producing tachycardia and vasoconstriction

## Explanation

A drop in arterial pressure decreases baroreceptor stretch and firing at the carotid sinus and aortic arch. The reduced afferent signal to the NTS results in ↑ sympathetic outflow and ↓ parasympathetic outflow, producing tachycardia, increased contractility, and vasoconstriction to restore BP.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

↓ BP + ↓ baroreceptor firing → ↑ sympathetic + ↓ parasympathetic = tachycardia + vasoconstriction

## Wrong Options

### A) ↑ Baroreceptor firing producing bradycardia and vasodilation

This is the response to increased BP / baroreceptor stretch, not decreased BP.

**Buzzword Combo:** ↑ BP + ↑ baroreceptor firing → ↑ parasympathetic + ↓ sympathetic = bradycardia + vasodilation

### 
C) No change in baroreceptor firing until BP drops below 60 mm Hg

Baroreceptors fire continuously across a wide physiologic range and respond to changes well within normal BP — they do not have a low threshold of 60 mm Hg.

**Buzzword Combo:** Continuous baroreceptor firing across physiologic BP range = normal baroreflex

### D) Direct activation of the parasympathetic nervous system to restore BP

Parasympathetic activation would worsen hypotension by causing bradycardia and vasodilation — the opposite of the appropriate compensatory response.

**Buzzword Combo:** ↑ Parasympathetic + bradycardia + vasodilation = response to ↑ BP (counterproductive in shock)
