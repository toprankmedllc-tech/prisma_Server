## Buzzword Question

A 19-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department after intentionally ingesting a large quantity of aspirin tablets. She complains of nausea, ringing in her ears, and rapid breathing. Arterial blood gas analysis reveals a mixed respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis. She is started on intravenous sodium bicarbonate therapy.
The primary benefit of sodium bicarbonate administration in this patient is:

## Buzzwords Used

nausea; ringing ; rapid breathing; mixed respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis.

## Answer Options

A. Increased hepatic metabolism of salicylates
B. Enhanced renal excretion of salicylates by urine alkalinization
C. Prevention of gastric ulcer formation
D. Direct reversal of mitochondrial toxicity

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

Sodium bicarbonate is used in salicylate toxicity to alkalinize the urine, which traps salicylates in their ionized form and enhances renal excretion.
Urine Alkalinization→↑Salicylate Excretion

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Tinnitus + mixed acid-base disorder + NaHCO₃ = salicylate poisoning treatment

## Wrong Options

### A. Increased hepatic metabolism of salicylates

incorrect — Sodium bicarbonate does not significantly increase hepatic metabolism.

**Buzzword Combo:** Liver metabolism induction = not mechanism of bicarbonate therapy

### C. Prevention of gastric ulcer formation

Incorrect — Although aspirin can cause gastric injury, bicarbonate in overdose is primarily used to enhance elimination.

**Buzzword Combo:** Urine alkalinization = overdose management

### D. Direct reversal of mitochondrial toxicity

Incorrect — Sodium bicarbonate does not directly reverse mitochondrial dysfunction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Supportive elimination therapy ≠ antidote reversal
