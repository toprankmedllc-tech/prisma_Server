## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old woman with a history of SLE presents with progressive difficulty climbing stairs and rising from a chair over the past several months. She has been taking high-dose Prednisone for the past year due to recurrent disease flares. She denies muscle pain. Laboratory studies show a normal creatine kinase level.
Which of the following muscle biopsy findings is most likely in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

 taking high-dose Prednisone;  progressive difficulty climbing stairs; normal creatine kinase level

## Answer Options

A. Type II muscle fiber atrophy
B. Endomysial inflammatory infiltrates
C. Rimmed vacuoles within muscle fibers
D. Dystrophin deficiency with muscle necrosis

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Steroid-induced myopathy is caused by chronic glucocorticoid use and results in selective atrophy of type II muscle fibers due to increased protein catabolism.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic prednisone use + proximal weakness + normal CK = type II fiber atrophy

## Wrong Options

### B. Endomysial inflammatory infiltrates


Incorrect — This finding is characteristic of Polymyositis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Proximal weakness + elevated CK + inflammation = polymyositis

### C. Rimmed vacuoles within muscle fibers

Incorrect — Rimmed vacuoles are classically seen in Inclusion body myositis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Older patient + distal weakness + rimmed vacuoles = inclusion body myositis

### D. Dystrophin deficiency with muscle necrosis

Incorrect — This finding is seen in Duchenne muscular dystrophy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Gowers sign + calf pseudohypertrophy = Duchenne muscular dystrophy
