## Buzzword Question

A patient with constrictive pericarditis is examined. Inspection of the jugular venous pulse reveals an unusual finding. Which of the following is most characteristic of his condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Constrictive pericarditis; jugular venous pressure; paradoxical inspiratory rise; Kussmaul sign

## Answer Options


A) Paradoxical rise in jugular venous pressure with inspiration
B) Inspiratory drop in jugular venous pressure
C) Cannon A waves
D) Pulsus paradoxus

## Correct Answer


A) Paradoxical rise in jugular venous pressure with inspiration

## Explanation

Kussmaul sign — a paradoxical rise in jugular venous pressure during inspiration — is characteristic of constrictive pericarditis. Inspiration normally lowers intrathoracic pressure and increases venous return to the right heart, dropping JVP. With a rigid pericardium, the right heart cannot accommodate the added volume, so pressure backs up into the jugular veins.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Constrictive pericarditis + paradoxical inspiratory JVP rise = Kussmaul sign

## Wrong Options

### B) Inspiratory drop in jugular venous pressure

This is the normal physiologic response — inspiration lowers intrathoracic pressure, drawing venous blood into the right heart and reducing JVP.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Inspiration + ↓ intrathoracic pressure + ↓ JVP = normal physiology

### C) Cannon A waves


Cannon A waves occur in AV dissociation (third-degree heart block, ventricular tachycardia) when the right atrium contracts against a closed tricuspid valve — not related to constrictive pericarditis.

**Buzzword Combo:** AV dissociation + atrial contraction against closed tricuspid valve = cannon A waves

### D) Pulsus paradoxus

Pulsus paradoxus — an exaggerated inspiratory drop in systolic BP >10 mm Hg — is a feature of cardiac tamponade and severe asthma, not classically of constrictive pericarditis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Tamponade + inspiratory SBP drop >10 mm Hg = pulsus paradoxus
