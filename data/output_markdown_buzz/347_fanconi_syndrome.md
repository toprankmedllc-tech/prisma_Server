## Buzzword Question

A 19-year-old man is evaluated for glucosuria found incidentally on a routine sports physical. He is non-diabetic with a fasting glucose of 79 mg/dL. Further workup reveals: glucosuria, aminoaciduria, phosphaturia, and uricosuria. Serum bicarbonate is 18 mEq/L. He takes no medications. Which of the following best explains why this patient has glucosuria despite a normal plasma glucose?

## Buzzwords Used

Young male; glucosuria; normal glucose; aminoaciduria; phosphaturia; low bicarb; no medications

## Answer Options

A) Increased GFR overwhelming the transport maximum for glucose
B) SGLT2 inhibitor effect reducing the renal glucose threshold selectively
C) Global proximal tubule dysfunction impairing reabsorption of multiple solutes
D) Splay phenomenon causing early saturation of glucose transporters

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

The constellation of glucosuria, aminoaciduria, phosphaturia, uricosuria, and non-anion gap metabolic acidosis (low bicarb) in a young patient is the classic fingerprint of Fanconi syndrome — global PCT dysfunction. Unlike pregnancy or SGLT2i, this affects all PCT reabsorption processes, not glucose selectively.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Glucosuria + aminoaciduria + phosphaturia + low bicarb + normal glucose = Fanconi syndrome (global PCT dysfunction)

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased GFR

Increased GFR (as in pregnancy) causes isolated glucosuria by lowering the threshold, but does NOT cause aminoaciduria, phosphaturia, or metabolic acidosis. The multi-solute loss here clearly implicates a tubular defect, not a hemodynamic one.

**Buzzword Combo:** ↑GFR = isolated glucosuria only, NOT multi-solute wasting

### D) Splay phenomenon

Splay phenomenon explains the gradual rise in the glucose titration curve due to nephron heterogeneity, but it does not cause aminoaciduria or phosphaturia. Splay is a glucose-specific transport phenomenon.

**Buzzword Combo:** Splay = glucose titration curve only, NOT global solute wasting

### B) SGLT2 inhibitor effect

SGLT2 inhibitors cause selective glucosuria by blocking glucose reabsorption in the PCT, but they do not impair amino acid, phosphate, or bicarbonate reabsorption. This patient takes no medications, and the multi-solute loss pattern excludes a selective transporter block.

**Buzzword Combo:** SGLT2i = selective glucosuria only + patient takes no medications

## Related Concepts

Wilson’s disease causing Fanconi; multiple myeloma and light chain nephropathy; renal tubular acidosis type 2 (proximal RTA)
