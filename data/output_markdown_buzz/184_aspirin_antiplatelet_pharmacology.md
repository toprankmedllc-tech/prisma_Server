## Buzzword Question

A patient taking daily medication after an ischemic stroke develops easy bruising and prolonged bleeding from minor cuts. Platelet count, PT, and PTT are normal. Which of the following laboratory changes is most expected?

## Buzzwords Used

Daily medication after ischemic stroke; prolonged bleeding from minor cuts; normal platelet count, PT, and PTT

## Answer Options

A) Prolonged bleeding time
B) Prolonged prothrombin time
C) Prolonged partial thromboplastin time
D) Decreased platelet count

## Correct Answer

A) Prolonged bleeding time


## Explanation

Aspirin irreversibly inhibits platelet COX-1, decreasing thromboxane A2 and reducing platelet aggregation. This prolongs bleeding time while platelet count, PT, and PTT remain normal.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Ischemic stroke prevention + normal platelet count/PT/PTT + prolonged bleeding from cuts = aspirin effect

## Wrong Options

### B) Prolonged prothrombin time

Prolonged PT suggests extrinsic pathway dysfunction or warfarin effect. Aspirin does not primarily affect coagulation factors.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increased PT/INR first + vitamin K factor impairment + factor VII shortest half-life = warfarin effect

### C) Prolonged partial thromboplastin time

Prolonged PTT suggests intrinsic pathway dysfunction or heparin effect. Aspirin affects platelet aggregation, not the intrinsic coagulation pathway.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemarthrosis + normal bleeding time + prolonged PTT = hemophilia

### D) Decreased platelet count

Decreased platelet count indicates thrombocytopenia. Aspirin causes platelet dysfunction without reducing platelet number.

**Buzzword Combo:** Petechiae + mucosal bleeding + low platelet count = thrombocytopenia

## Related Concepts

Primary hemostasis; thrombocytopenia; coagulation studies
