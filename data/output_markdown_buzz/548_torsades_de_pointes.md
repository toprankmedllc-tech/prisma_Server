## Buzzword Question

A hemodynamically stable patient develops torsades de pointes on telemetry. Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line therapy?

## Buzzwords Used


Hemodynamically stable; torsades de pointes

## Answer Options

A) Intravenous magnesium sulfate
B) Intravenous amiodarone
C) Intravenous procainamide
D) Synchronized cardioversion

## Correct Answer

A) Intravenous magnesium sulfate

## Explanation


IV magnesium sulfate is the first-line treatment for torsades de pointes in hemodynamically stable patients, regardless of whether the serum magnesium level is normal. Magnesium suppresses the early afterdepolarizations that trigger the arrhythmia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Stable torsades de pointes = IV magnesium sulfate (first-line)

## Wrong Options

### B) Intravenous amiodarone

Amiodarone prolongs the QT interval and can worsen torsades; it is contraindicated in this setting.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Amiodarone + prolonged QT = worsens torsades (contraindicated)

### C) Intravenous procainamide

A Class IA antiarrhythmic that also prolongs the QT interval — contraindicated in torsades.

**Buzzword Combo:** Procainamide + QT prolongation = worsens torsades (contraindicated)

### D) Synchronized cardioversion


Cardioversion is reserved for hemodynamically unstable torsades. In a stable patient, IV magnesium is first-line.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Unstable torsades / pulseless polymorphic VT = defibrillation (not synchronized)
