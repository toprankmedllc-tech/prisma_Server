## Buzzword Question

A 35-year-old woman presents with progressive shortness of breath and dry cough. Lung biopsy shows noncaseating granulomas composed of epithelioid histiocytes and multinucleated giant cells involving the interstitium and lymphatics. Serum calcium is elevated. Which lymphatic region is most commonly involved in early disease?

## Buzzwords Used

Noncaseating granulomas; epithelioid histiocytes; multinucleated giant cells; interstitium; lymphatic involvement; hypercalcemia; dry cough; progressive dyspnea; young woman

## Answer Options

A) Pleural lymphatics
B) Subpleural and peribronchial lymphatics
C) Pulmonary arterial endothelium
D) Alveolar capillary basement membrane

## Correct Answer

B) Subpleural and peribronchial lymphatics

## Explanation

Sarcoidosis characteristically involves the lung in a lymphatic distribution, especially the subpleural and peribronchial regions. This explains the typical interstitial pattern and hilar lymphadenopathy seen early in disease.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Noncaseating granulomas + lymphatic distribution + interstitial lung involvement = Sarcoidosis

## Wrong Options

### A) Pleural lymphatics

Pleural lymphatics are not the primary early site of sarcoid granuloma deposition. Sarcoidosis preferentially follows the bronchovascular and subpleural interstitial lymphatic routes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pleural disease + effusion pattern + mesothelial inflammation = malignant effusion

### C) Pulmonary arterial endothelium

Sarcoidosis is not a primary vascular endothelial disease. Granulomas form in interstitium and lymphatics, not within pulmonary arterial endothelium.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vascular inflammation + endothelial injury + thrombosis ≠ granulomatous lymphatic disease

### D) Alveolar capillary basement membrane

Alveolar-capillary membrane injury is typical of ARDS or interstitial fibrosis, not granulomatous lymphatic diseases like sarcoidosis. It involves diffuse alveolar damage rather than lymphatic distribution.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyaline membranes + diffuse alveolar damage + gas exchange failure = ARDS
