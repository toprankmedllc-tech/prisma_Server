## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man with a long history of poorly controlled hypertension suddenly develops vomiting, severe headache, and right-sided weakness while watching television. On examination, he is confused and has leftward gaze deviation. CT scan of the head shows a hyperdense hemorrhage in the basal ganglia. Rupture of which of the following vessels is the most likely cause of this patient’s condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Chronic hypertension; basal ganglia hemorrhage; sudden focal deficits; intracerebral bleed; deep brain hemorrhage

## Answer Options

A) Lenticulostriate arteries

B) Middle meningeal artery

C) Bridging veins

D) Anterior communicating artery

## Correct Answer

A) Lenticulostriate arteries

## Explanation

Chronic hypertension causes Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms in small penetrating vessels such as the lenticulostriate arteries. Rupture leads to deep intracerebral hemorrhage, especially in the basal ganglia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic hypertension + basal ganglia bleed + focal neurologic deficits = Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage

## Wrong Options

### B) Middle meningeal artery

Middle meningeal artery rupture causes epidural hematoma after head trauma and presents with a lucid interval and lens-shaped hemorrhage.

**Buzzword Combo:** Temporal bone fracture + lucid interval + lens-shaped hematoma = Epidural hematoma

### C) Bridging veins

Bridging vein rupture causes subdural hematoma, usually in elderly patients after trauma, producing crescent-shaped bleeding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elderly patient + trauma + crescent-shaped hematoma = Subdural hematoma

### D) Anterior communicating artery

Anterior communicating artery aneurysm rupture causes subarachnoid hemorrhage with thunderclap headache and meningeal irritation rather than deep basal ganglia bleeding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Thunderclap headache + subarachnoid blood + berry aneurysm = Subarachnoid hemorrhage
