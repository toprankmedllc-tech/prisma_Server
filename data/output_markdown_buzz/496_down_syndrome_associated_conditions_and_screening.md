## Buzzword Question

A 3-year-old boy with trisomy 21 (Down syndrome) presents for a well-child visit. In addition to his known intellectual disability, which of the following associated conditions requires routine screening in ALL children with Down syndrome?

## Buzzwords Used

Trisomy 21; Down syndrome; well-child visit; intellectual disability; routine screening; associated conditions

## Answer Options

A) Hypothyroidism (TSH annually), atlantoaxial instability (cervical spine radiograph), and congenital heart disease (echocardiogram in infancy) 
B) Phenylketonuria (plasma phenylalanine level annually) and galactosemia (galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase) 
C) Neurofibromatosis (skin examination for café-au-lait spots) and tuberous sclerosis (renal ultrasound) 
D) Turner syndrome-associated bicuspid aortic valve screening and lymphedema assessment


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Down syndrome (trisomy 21) is associated with a constellation of systemic conditions requiring active surveillance: (1) Congenital heart defects (~50%), especially atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD/endocardial cushion defect) and VSD; echocardiogram in all newborns; (2) Hypothyroidism (~15–20%) — TSH at birth and annually; (3) Atlantoaxial instability,  cervical radiograph before contact sports participation; (4) Duodenal atresia ('double bubble' sign),  in newborns; (5) Hirschsprung disease; (6) Acute myeloid/lymphoid leukemia (~1%); (7) Obstructive sleep apnea; (8) Hearing and vision impairment. These are testable associations on USMLE Step 1.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Trisomy 21 associations: AVSD + hypothyroidism + atlantoaxial instability + duodenal atresia + AML/ALL + OSA + hearing/vision loss

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Phenylketonuria (plasma phenylalanine level annually) and galactosemia (galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase)

PKU and galactosemia are autosomal recessive inborn errors of metabolism screened on universal newborn screens, they are not specifically associated with Down syndrome. Down syndrome is a chromosomal aneuploidy, not an enzyme deficiency disorder.

**Buzzword Combo:** PKU + galactosemia = autosomal recessive enzyme deficiencies; newborn screen targets; not specifically associated with trisomy 21

### 2: C) Neurofibromatosis (skin examination for café-au-lait spots) and tuberous sclerosis (renal ultrasound)


Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1 gene, chromosome 17) and tuberous sclerosis (TSC1/TSC2) are neurocutaneous syndromes with autosomal dominant inheritance,  unrelated to trisomy 21.

**Buzzword Combo:** NF1 = café-au-lait + Lisch nodules + neurofibromas + chr 17; TSC = ash leaf + shagreen patch + cortical tubers; neither associated with trisomy 21

### 3: D) Turner syndrome-associated bicuspid aortic valve screening and lymphedema assessment

Turner syndrome (45,X) is associated with bicuspid aortic valve, coarctation of the aorta, lymphedema (webbed neck, cystic hygroma), and primary ovarian insufficiency. These are not features of Down syndrome (trisomy 21)

**Buzzword Combo:** Turner (45,X) = bicuspid AoV + coarctation + webbed neck + lymphedema + primary ovarian insufficiency; not features of trisomy 21
