## Buzzword Question

A 3-day-old male neonate has not yet passed meconium. On physical exam, the abdomen is distended. A digital rectal exam is followed by an explosive release of gas and stool. An abdominal radiograph shows dilated loops of bowel. A biopsy of the narrowed rectal segment would most likely show a complete absence of which of the following?

## Buzzwords Used

Failure to pass meconium; Abdominal distention; Explosive release of stool (Squirt sign); Absence of ganglia.

## Answer Options

A) Meissner and Auerbach plexuses
B) Neural tube derivatives
C) Celiac and mesenteric ganglia
D) Chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Hirschsprung disease is caused by the failure of neural crest cells to migrate into the wall of the colon. This results in an aganglionic segment (lacking Meissner/submucosal and Auerbach/myenteric plexuses) that cannot relax.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Neural crest migration failure + Aganglionic segment = Hirschsprung Disease

## Wrong Options

### B) Neural tube derivatives

Neural tube derivatives include the brain and spinal cord; Hirschsprung is a defect of neural crest cells (the "fourth germ layer"), not the neural tube.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neural tube + CNS = Brain/Spine

### C) Celiac and mesenteric ganglia

These are prevertebral sympathetic ganglia; while they are neural crest-derived, they are not absent in the bowel wall in this condition.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prevertebral ganglia + Sympathetics = Autonomic control

### D) Chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla

Chromaffin cells are neural crest-derived, but their absence would affect catecholamine production, not bowel motility.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chromaffin cells + Adrenal Medulla = Epinephrine/Norepinephrine
