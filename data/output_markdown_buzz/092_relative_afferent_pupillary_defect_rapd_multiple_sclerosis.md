## Buzzword Question

A 24-year-old woman presents with painful loss of vision in her right eye over 3 days. When a flashlight is shone in the right eye, both pupils constrict only minimally. When the light is swung to the left eye, both pupils constrict briskly. When the light is swung back to the right eye, both pupils dilate. This finding is best described as:

## Buzzwords Used

When the light is swung back to the right eye, both pupils dilate

## Answer Options

A) Marcus Gunn pupil (relative afferent pupillary defect)
B) Argyll Robertson pupil
C) Adie tonic pupil
D) Horner syndrome

## Correct Answer

A) Marcus Gunn pupil (relative afferent pupillary defect)

## Explanation

Optic neuritis (often the first presentation of MS) damages the afferent limb of the pupillary light reflex on one side. On the swinging-flashlight test, the affected eye fails to drive pupillary constriction, so swinging the light back to the diseased eye causes paradoxical dilation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Painful monocular vision loss + RAPD + young woman = optic neuritis / multiple sclerosis

## Wrong Options

### Argyll Robertson pupil

Small irregular pupils that accommodate but do not react to light: classic for neurosyphilis 

**Buzzword Combo:** Pupil accommodates but doesn't react = Argyll Robertson (neurosyphilis)

### Adie tonic pupil

Unilateral large pupil with poor light reaction and slow tonic response to accommodation. Benign, idiopathic, more common in young women. Often associated with diminished deep tendon reflexes (Holmes-Adie syndrome).

**Buzzword Combo:** Large dilated pupil + slow accommodation + diminished DTRs = Adie pupil

### Horner syndrome

Ipsilateral ptosis, MIOSIS (small pupil), and anhidrosis from a lesion in the oculosympathetic pathway. Pupil reacts normally to light.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ipsilateral ptosis + miosis + anhidrosis = Horner syndrome
