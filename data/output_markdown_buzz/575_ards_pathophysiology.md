## Buzzword Question

A 46-year-old woman develops severe hypoxemia 2 days after septic shock due to pyelonephritis. She is intubated but remains hypoxic despite 100% oxygen. Chest imaging shows diffuse bilateral infiltrates, and the pulmonary capillary wedge pressure is normal. Lung biopsy shows hyaline membranes lining alveoli. Which of the following cell types is most directly injured in this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Sepsis; Pyelonephritis; Acute respiratory distress; Refractory hypoxemia; Bilateral infiltrates; Normal wedge pressure; Hyaline membranes; Diffuse alveolar damage; Mechanical ventilation

## Answer Options

A) Type I pneumocytes
B) Type II pneumocytes
C) Alveolar macrophages
D) Pulmonary endothelial cells

## Correct Answer

A) Type I pneumocytes

## Explanation

In ARDS, the primary injury occurs to type I pneumocytes and the alveolar-capillary barrier due to cytokine-driven neutrophil activation. This leads to increased permeability, protein-rich edema, and hyaline membrane formation, causing severe hypoxemia and decreased lung compliance.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sepsis + neutrophil cytokines + alveolar-capillary damage + hyaline membranes = Type I pneumocyte injury

## Wrong Options

### B) Type II pneumocytes

Type II pneumocytes are secondarily affected and their surfactant production decreases, but they are not the primary site of injury in ARDS.

**Buzzword Combo:** Surfactant loss + decreased compliance + late injury = Type II pneumocyte dysfunction

### C) Alveolar macrophages

Alveolar macrophages initiate cytokine release but are not the primary structural cells damaged leading to hyaline membrane formation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cytokine release + TNF-α + IL-1 activation = Alveolar macrophages (initiators, not targets)

### D) Pulmonary endothelial cells

Endothelial injury contributes to increased permeability, but the hallmark structural damage in ARDS is to the alveolar epithelium (type I pneumocytes).

**Buzzword Combo:** Capillary leak + increased permeability + edema = Endothelial involvement (secondary role)
