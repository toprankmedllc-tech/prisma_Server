## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old man with chronic kidney disease develops bone pain, and laboratory studies show elevated parathyroid hormone and hyperphosphatemia. Which of the following additional findings is most likely present?

## Buzzwords Used

CKD; Hyperphosphatemia; Elevated PTH

## Answer Options

A) Decreased 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D levels
B) Increased erythropoietin production
C) Hypercalcemia due to increased intestinal absorption
D) Decreased fibroblast growth factor-23 activity

## Correct Answer

A) Decreased 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D levels

## Explanation

Chronic kidney disease impairs renal 1α-hydroxylase activity, decreasing conversion of vitamin D into its active form, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D. Reduced active vitamin D decreases intestinal calcium absorption, contributing to hypocalcemia and secondary hyperparathyroidism

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

CKD + hyperphosphatemia + secondary hyperparathyroidism = Low active vitamin D

## Wrong Options

### Increased erythropoietin production

Chronic kidney disease decreases erythropoietin synthesis, commonly causing normocytic anemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** CKD + decreased renal mass + normocytic anemia = Low erythropoietin

### Hypercalcemia due to increased intestinal absorption

Reduced vitamin D activation in CKD decreases intestinal calcium absorption, often leading to hypocalcemia

**Buzzword Combo:** CKD + low vitamin D activation + decreased calcium absorption = Hypocalcemia

### Decreased fibroblast growth factor-23 activity

FGF-23 activity is typically increased in chronic kidney disease in response to phosphate retention

**Buzzword Combo:** CKD + phosphate retention + osteocyte activation = Increased FGF-23
