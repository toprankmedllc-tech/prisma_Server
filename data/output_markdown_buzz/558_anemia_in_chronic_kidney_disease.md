## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old man with a history of long-standing type 2 diabetes mellitus and Stage 4 chronic kidney disease (eGFR = 22 mL/min/1.73 m 
2 ) comes to the clinic for a routine follow-up. He reports progressive, severe generalized fatigue over the past three months that limits his ability to walk short distances. Physical examination reveals conjunctival pallor but no peripheral edema. Laboratory studies demonstrate a hemoglobin level of 8.4 g/dL and an MCV of 88 fL. Iron panels, serum B 
12 , and folate levels are within normal limits. The patient is subsequently prescribed a subcutaneous recombinant hormone therapy to directly address his fatigue. Which of the following secondary adverse effects is most closely associated with the administration of this specific medication?

## Buzzwords Used

Stage 4 chronic kidney disease; Conjunctival pallor; Subcutaneous recombinant hormone; Normocytic normochromic anemia; Adverse effect


## Answer Options

A) New-onset or worsening systemic hypertension due to increased peripheral vascular resistance and blood viscosity.
B) Severe symptomatic hypocalcemia driven by acute primary calciferol degradation within proximal tubule cells.
C) Acute kidney injury precipitated by intense, localized afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction mimicking NSAID toxicity.
D) Profound natriuresis resulting from unexpected competitive inhibition of baseline dopamine synthesis in the proximal tubule.

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The patient has anemia of chronic kidney disease caused by a deficiency in erythropoietin, which is normally released by interstitial cells in the peritubular capillary bed. Recombinant human erythropoietin (EPO) therapy stimulates RBC proliferation but carries a well-known, high-yield adverse effect of causing or worsening hypertension due to rapid increases in blood viscosity and direct neurohormonal vascular smooth muscle reactivity.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recombinant erythropoietin+Peritubular capillary bed+ RBC proliferation+Erythropoietin-induced hypertension =Blood viscosity acceleration

## Wrong Options

### B

While chronic kidney disease does result in a loss of 1α-hydroxylase activity and subsequent hypocalcemia, administering an erythropoietin-stimulating agent does not actively drive calciferol degradation in the proximal tubule cells.

**Buzzword Combo:** Calciferol degradation + Proximal tubule cells + 1-alpha-hydroxylase inactivation = Vitamin D pathway confusion

### C

This choice describes the mechanism of an acute kidney injury caused by NSAIDs blocking renal-protective prostaglandins. Erythropoietin does not cause localized paracrine blockade of prostaglandins to constrict the afferent arteriole.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prostaglandin synthesis blockade + Afferent arteriolar constriction + NSAID-mimic toxicity = Paracrine vascular trap

### D

Dopamine is synthesized by proximal tubule cells to promote natriuresis and dilate renal vasculature at low doses, but its synthesis loop is independent of erythropoietin administration and is not competitively inhibited by it.

**Buzzword Combo:** Proximal tubule dopamine + Natriuresis promotion + Competitive synthesis inhibition = Paracrine dopamine mismatch
