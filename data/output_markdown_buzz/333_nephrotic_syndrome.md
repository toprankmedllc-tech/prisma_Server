## Buzzword Question

A patient develops severe edema, hypoalbuminemia, hyperlipidemia, and 4+ proteinuria on urinalysis. Which of the following complications is most likely to occur in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Proteinuria; Hypoalbuminemia; Hyperlipidemia

## Answer Options

A) Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage
B) Splenic sequestration crisis
C) Renal vein thrombosis
D) Subarachnoid hemorrhage

## Correct Answer

C) Renal vein thrombosis

## Explanation

Nephrotic syndrome causes urinary loss of anticoagulant proteins such as antithrombin III, creating a hypercoagulable state. Patients are therefore at increased risk for thrombotic complications, particularly renal vein thrombosis

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Proteinuria + antithrombin III loss + hypercoagulability = Renal vein thrombosis

## Wrong Options

### Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage

Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage is more commonly associated with pulmonary-renal syndromes such as Goodpasture syndrome and ANCA-associated vasculitis. Patients typically present with hemoptysis, anemia, and diffuse pulmonary infiltrates

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemoptysis + glomerulonephritis + pulmonary infiltrates = Pulmonary-renal syndrome

### Splenic sequestration crisis

Splenic sequestration crisis is associated with sickle cell disease and acute trapping of red blood cells within the spleen. Patients develop sudden splenomegaly, anemia, and hypovolemic shock

**Buzzword Combo:** Sickle cell disease + acute splenomegaly + hypovolemia = Splenic sequestration

### Subarachnoid hemorrhage

Subarachnoid hemorrhage is classically caused by rupture of a berry aneurysm. Patients typically present with sudden severe “thunderclap” headache, neck stiffness, and photophobia

**Buzzword Combo:** Berry aneurysm rupture + thunderclap headache + meningeal irritation = Subarachnoid hemorrhage
