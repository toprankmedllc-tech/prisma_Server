## Buzzword Question

ST-segment elevations in leads II, III, and aVF with reciprocal ST depression in leads I and aVL are most consistent with infarction of which of the following walls of the left ventricle?

## Buzzwords Used

ST elevation II, III, aVF; reciprocal ST depression I, aVL; inferior wall STEMI

## Answer Options

A) Inferior wall
B) Anterior wall
C) Lateral wall
D) Posterior wall

## Correct Answer

A) Inferior wall

## Explanation

ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF with reciprocal ST depression in leads I and aVL is the classic ECG pattern of an inferior wall STEMI, most often from RCA occlusion in right-dominant circulations.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

ST elevation II, III, aVF + reciprocal depression I, aVL = inferior wall STEMI (RCA)

## Wrong Options

### B) Anterior wall

Anterior STEMI shows ST elevation in V1–V4 from LAD occlusion.

**Buzzword Combo:** ST elevation V1–V4 = anterior wall STEMI (LAD)

### 
C) Lateral wall


Lateral STEMI shows ST elevation in leads I, aVL, V5, V6 from LCX or diagonal branch occlusion.

**Buzzword Combo:** ST elevation I, aVL, V5–V6 = lateral wall STEMI (LCX)

### D) Posterior wall

Posterior STEMI shows ST depression (not elevation) in V1–V3 with tall R waves — a "mirror image" pattern, often accompanying inferior STEMI from RCA or LCX occlusion.

**Buzzword Combo:** ST depression V1–V3 + tall R waves = posterior wall STEMI (mirror image)
