## Buzzword Question

A man with hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL is started on a medication that lowers VLDL/LDL by inhibiting adipose tissue lipolysis. He develops facial warmth and redness 30 minutes after dosing. What prevents these symptoms?

A) Diphenhydramine
B) Aspirin
C) Ranitidine
D) Montelukast

## Buzzwords Used

Medication decrease VLDL; inhibits lipolysis; facial redness after each dose 

## Answer Options

A) Diphenhydramine 
B) Aspirin 
C) Ranitidine 
D) Montelukast

## Correct Answer

B) Aspirin 

## Explanation

The medication described is niacin. Its characteristic flushing side effect is mediated by prostaglandin D2 release from Langerhans cells and skin mast cells, not by histamine. Because this is a prostaglandin-driven reaction, aspirin, a prostaglandin synthesis inhibitor, taken 30 minutes before the niacin dose significantly attenuates this reaction.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

niacin + flushing = prostaglandin release (give aspirin)

## Wrong Options

### A) Diphenhydramine 

Diphenhydramine is an H1 antihistamine. Because niacin flushing is mediated by prostaglandins rather than histamine, H1 blockade does not effectively prevent the reaction. 

**Buzzword Combo:**  H1 Antagonist + Antimuscarinic + Cross BBB = Diphenhydramine

### C) Ranitidine 

Ranitidine is an H2 antihistamine that reduces gastric acid secretion. It has no meaningful effect on prostaglandin-mediated skin flushing. 

**Buzzword Combo:** H2 Blocker + Gastric Acid Suppression = Ranitidine

### D) Montelukast 

Montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist used for asthma and allergic rhinitis. Niacin flushing is prostaglandin-mediated, not leukotriene-mediated, so montelukast would not effectively prevent this side effect. 

**Buzzword Combo:** leukotriene receptor antagonist + asthma = Montelukast
