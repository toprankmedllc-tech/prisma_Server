## Buzzword Question

A patient with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis has the following ABG findings:
PaO₂: decreased
PaCO₂: normal
A-a gradient: elevated
Which additional finding would most strongly support diffusion impairment as the cause of hypoxemia?

## Buzzwords Used

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis; hypoxemia; elevated A-a gradient; normal PaCO₂; diffusion impairment; oxygen therapy response

## Answer Options

A) No improvement with supplemental oxygen
B) Marked improvement with supplemental oxygen
C) Hypercapnia at rest
D) Normal DLCO

## Correct Answer

B) Marked improvement with supplemental oxygen

## Explanation

Diffusion impairment causes hypoxemia due to impaired oxygen transfer across the alveolar-capillary membrane. Increasing the alveolar oxygen concentration with supplemental oxygen enhances the diffusion gradient and significantly improves oxygenation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pulmonary fibrosis + elevated A-a gradient + ↓O₂ diffusion = hypoxemia improves with supplemental oxygen

## Wrong Options

### A) No improvement with supplemental oxygen

Failure to improve with supplemental oxygen is more characteristic of a right-to-left shunt, where blood bypasses ventilated alveoli entirely.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypoxemia + oxygen-resistant + blood bypasses lungs = right-to-left shunt

### C) Hypercapnia at rest

Carbon dioxide diffuses much more readily than oxygen, so patients with diffusion impairment usually maintain normal PaCO₂ until very advanced disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diffusion defect + normal ventilation + preserved CO₂ exchange = normal PaCO₂

### D) Normal DLCO

A reduced DLCO is a hallmark of diffusion impairment because thickening of the alveolar-capillary membrane decreases gas transfer.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pulmonary fibrosis + thickened membrane + impaired gas transfer = decreased DLCO
