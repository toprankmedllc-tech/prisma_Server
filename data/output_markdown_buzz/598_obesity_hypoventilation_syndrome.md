## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old  has dyspnea on exertion. Spirometry is normal, but breathing feels labored. Chest wall movement is restricted. His BMI is 38kg/m2? Which change best explains his lung compliance?

## Buzzwords Used

Obesity; BMI 38 kg/m²; dyspnea on exertion; normal spirometry; restricted chest wall movement; reduced chest expansion; increased work of breathing

## Answer Options

A) Increased lung compliance due to alveolar destruction
B) Decreased chest wall compliance due to obesity
C) Increased airway compliance due to low resistance
D) Normal lung compliance due to preserved alveoli

## Correct Answer

B) Decreased chest wall compliance due to obesity

## Explanation

Obesity reduces chest wall compliance due to increased abdominal pressure and mechanical restriction of diaphragmatic movement. Lung parenchyma may be normal, but overall respiratory system compliance decreases because the chest wall becomes stiff.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Obesity; ↑ abdominal pressure; ↓ chest wall expansion =↓ compliance

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased lung compliance due to alveolar destruction

This describes emphysema, which affects lung parenchyma rather than chest wall mechanics. Obesity does not cause alveolar destruction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Emphysema; alveolar wall destruction; loss of elastic recoil

### C) Increased airway compliance due to low resistance

Airway resistance may be normal or mildly increased in obesity, but “airway compliance” is not the primary mechanism affecting ventilation here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Airway resistance; ventilation mechanics; not chest wall stiffness

### D) Normal lung compliance due to preserved alveoli

Although lung parenchyma may be normal, overall respiratory system compliance is reduced due to chest wall restriction from obesity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal lungs; preserved alveoli; misleading normal physiology
