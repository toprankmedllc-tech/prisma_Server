## Buzzword Question

A 26-year-old woman in the postpartum period presents with headache, seizures, papilledema, and focal neurologic deficits. MRI venography confirms thrombosis of the superior sagittal sinus with associated hemorrhagic venous infarct. Despite the presence of intracranial hemorrhage, what is the most appropriate initial treatment?

## Buzzwords Used

Postpartum state ; seizures ; papilledema ; MR venography ; superior sagittal sinus thrombosis ; hemorrhagic infarct

## Answer Options

A) High-dose IV antibiotics
B) Thrombolysis with tPA
C) IV unfractionated heparin
D) Surgical evacuation of hematoma

## Correct Answer

C) IV unfractionated heparin

## Explanation

In Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis, anticoagulation with heparin is first-line treatment even if hemorrhagic venous infarction is present. It prevents thrombus propagation and improves venous drainage.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Postpartum + venous sinus thrombosis + hemorrhagic infarct = Heparin still indicated

## Wrong Options

### A) High-dose IV antibiotics

Used for septic conditions like Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis, not aseptic cerebral venous thrombosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fever + sinus infection + cranial nerve palsy = Cavernous sinus thrombosis

### B) Thrombolysis with tPA

Thrombolysis is not first-line due to high risk of worsening intracranial hemorrhage in venous infarction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Arterial ischemic stroke + early window = tPA (not CVST)

### D) Surgical evacuation of hematoma

Surgery is not initial therapy because bleeding is secondary to venous congestion; treating the thrombosis is primary.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mass effect + traumatic epidural hematoma = surgery (not venous thrombosis)
