## Buzzword Question

A patient with anemia, jaundice, and splenomegaly has increased osmotic fragility of red blood cells. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

anemia; jaundice; splenomegaly; increased osmotic fragility; RBC hemolysis

## Answer Options

A) Hereditary spherocytosis
B) G6PD deficiency
C) Iron deficiency anemia
D) Sickle cell disease

## Correct Answer

A. Hereditary spherocytosis

## Explanation

Hereditary spherocytosis is caused by defects in RBC membrane proteins (classically spectrin or ankyrin), leading to spherocyte formation. These cells are trapped and destroyed in the spleen, causing extravascular hemolysis and increased osmotic fragility.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Spherocytes + osmotic fragility + splenomegaly = Hereditary spherocytosis

## Wrong Options

### B. G6PD deficiency

G6PD deficiency causes episodic oxidative hemolysis with Heinz bodies and bite cells, usually triggered by drugs, infections, or fava beans. Osmotic fragility is not a key feature.

**Buzzword Combo:** Heinz bodies + bite cells + oxidative stress = G6PD deficiency

### C. Iron deficiency anemia

Iron deficiency anemia is a microcytic, hypochromic anemia due to reduced iron availability. It does not cause hemolysis, jaundice, or splenomegaly.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low ferritin + microcytosis + koilonychia = Iron deficiency anemia

### D. Sickle cell disease

Sickle cell disease causes vaso-occlusive crises and hemolysis with sickled cells and often autosplenectomy in adults, not splenomegaly or increased osmotic fragility.

**Buzzword Combo:** HbS + sickled cells + vaso-occlusion = Sickle cell disease
