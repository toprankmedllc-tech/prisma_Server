## Buzzword Question

A patient with sickle cell disease develops fever, chest pain, hypoxemia, and new pulmonary infiltrates on chest X-ray during hospitalization for a pain crisis. Which of the following is the most likely underlying mechanism of this complication?

## Buzzwords Used

sickle cell disease; chest pain; hypoxemia; pulmonary infiltrates; vaso-occlusive crisis

## Answer Options

A) Pulmonary infection with secondary neutrophilic alveolar exudates
B) Sickling-induced vaso-occlusion of pulmonary microvasculature
C) Immune-mediated destruction of pulmonary capillary basement membrane
D) Aspiration of gastric contents leading to chemical pneumonitis

## Correct Answer

B. Sickling-induced vaso-occlusion of pulmonary microvasculature

## Explanation

Acute chest syndrome occurs due to sickling within the pulmonary microvasculature, causing ischemia, inflammation, and pulmonary infiltrates. It is a major cause of mortality in sickle cell disease and may be triggered by infection or hypoxia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sickle cell disease + chest pain + hypoxemia + infiltrates = vaso-occlusive acute chest syndrome

## Wrong Options

### A. Pulmonary infection with secondary neutrophilic alveolar exudates

This describes bacterial pneumonia, which may mimic or precipitate acute chest syndrome but is not the primary vaso-occlusive mechanism.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fever + focal consolidation + productive cough = pneumonia

### C. Immune-mediated destruction of pulmonary capillary basement membrane

This describes Goodpasture syndrome, which presents with pulmonary hemorrhage and glomerulonephritis due to anti-GBM antibodies.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemoptysis + anti-GBM antibodies = Goodpasture syndrome

### D. Aspiration of gastric contents leading to chemical pneumonitis

Aspiration pneumonitis occurs after aspiration events, typically in patients with impaired consciousness or vomiting, and is unrelated to sickling.

**Buzzword Combo:** Aspiration risk + dependent infiltrates = aspiration pneumonitis/pneumonia
