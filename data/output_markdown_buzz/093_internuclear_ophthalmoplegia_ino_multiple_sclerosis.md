## Buzzword Question

A 27-year-old woman with multiple sclerosis presents with new-onset horizontal diplopia. On attempted left lateral gaze, her right eye fails to adduct past the midline, while her left eye displays horizontal nystagmus. Convergence is preserved. The lesion is most likely located in the:

## Buzzwords Used

Multiple sclerosis; On attempted left lateral gaze, her right eye fails to adduct past the midline

## Answer Options

A) Right medial longitudinal fasciculus
B) Left medial longitudinal fasciculus
C) Right abducens nucleus
D) Right oculomotor nerve

## Correct Answer

A) Right medial longitudinal fasciculus

## Explanation

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO) localizes to the medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF) on the side of the eye that FAILS TO ADDUCT. The MLF normally carries signals from the contralateral abducens nucleus to the ipsilateral medial rectus subnucleus of CN III. Preserved convergence shows that the medial rectus muscle and CN III itself are intact, and the deficit is purely in internuclear pathway. Bilateral INO in a young patient is virtually pathognomonic for MS.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Failure of adduction + contralateral abducting nystagmus + preserved convergence + young woman = MLF lesion (INO) / MS

## Wrong Options

### Left medial longitudinal fasciculus

The MLF lesion is ipsilateral to the eye that FAILS to adduct (the right eye here), not contralateral.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Side of failed adduction = side of MLF lesion

### Right abducens nucleus

A right abducens nucleus lesion would impair conjugate gaze TO THE RIGHT (both eyes fail to move right), because the nucleus contains both motor neurons (right lateral rectus) and interneurons that project via the LEFT MLF to the left medial rectus. This patient has failure of adduction, not failure of conjugate gaze.

**Buzzword Combo:** Inability to look to one side = ipsilateral abducens nucleus or contralateral frontal eye field lesion

### Right oculomotor nerve

CN III palsy causes a "down and out" eye with ptosis and a fixed/dilated pupil (with compressive lesions). Convergence would also be impaired. Preserved convergence rules out a CN III lesion.

**Buzzword Combo:** "Down and out" + ptosis + blown pupil = CN III palsy (e.g., PCA aneurysm, uncal herniation)

## Related Concepts

Abducens nucleus / one-and-a-half syndrome; CN III palsy and uncal herniation; Marcus Gunn pupil
