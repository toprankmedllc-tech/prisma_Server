## Buzzword Question

A 45-year-old woman presents with progressive palpitations, tremor, and weight loss. Thyroid scintigraphy demonstrates a solitary autonomously functioning thyroid nodule. Which of the following is most strongly associated with the development of this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

autonomously functioning thyroid nodule; palpitations; tremor; weight loss; thyroid scintigraphy

## Answer Options

A) Activating mutation of the TSH receptor gene

B) Anti-thyroid peroxidase autoantibodies

C) Recent viral upper respiratory infection

D) Long-term lithium therapy

## Correct Answer

A) Activating mutation of the TSH receptor gene

## Explanation

Toxic adenoma is strongly associated with activating mutations in the TSH receptor or Gs alpha pathway, causing autonomous thyroid hormone production independent of pituitary regulation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Autonomously functioning nodule + hyperthyroid symptoms + hot nodule = activating TSH receptor mutation

## Wrong Options

### B) Anti-thyroid peroxidase autoantibodies

Anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies are associated with Hashimoto thyroiditis and postpartum thyroiditis rather than toxic adenoma.

**Buzzword Combo:** Anti-TPO antibodies + hypothyroidism + Hurthle cells = Hashimoto thyroiditis

### C) Recent viral upper respiratory infection

A recent viral illness is associated with subacute granulomatous thyroiditis, which presents with painful thyroid enlargement and low uptake.

**Buzzword Combo:** Painful thyroid + viral illness + elevated ESR = de Quervain thyroiditis

### D) Long-term lithium therapy

Lithium therapy is associated with hypothyroidism and goiter, not autonomously functioning thyroid adenomas.

**Buzzword Combo:** Lithium use + hypothyroidism + diffuse goiter = lithium-induced thyroid dysfunction
