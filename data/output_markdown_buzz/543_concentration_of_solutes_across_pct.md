## Buzzword Question


A 45-year-old woman participates in a clinical trial evaluating a novel diuretic that acts exclusively on the loop of Henle. To establish baseline renal parameters before drug administration, her renal clearance is measured using a substance that undergoes free filtration at the glomerulus but exhibits zero reabsorption and zero secretion along the entire length of the nephron. Which of the following substances matches this profile and exhibits a steady, linear-to-exponential rise on the proximal tubule [TF/P] graph?


## Buzzwords Used

Novel diuretic ; acts on loop of henle; free filtration at glomerulus ; zero absorption; zero secretion 

## Answer Options

A. Creatinine

B.Inulin


C.Urea

D.Sodium

## Correct Answer

Inulin 

## Explanation

Inulin is freely filtered at the glomerulus and is neither reabsorbed nor secreted by the renal tubules, making it the gold standard for measuring GFR. Because water is progressively reabsorbed along the proximal tubule while the absolute amount of inulin remains constant, its tubular fluid-to-plasma [TF/P] ratio rises steadily.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Free filtration + Zero reabsorption + Zero secretion = Inulin

## Wrong Options

### A creatinine

While creatinine is used clinically to estimate GFR, it undergoes a small amount of proximal tubular secretion. This causes its clearance to slightly overestimate the true GFR compared to inulin.

**Buzzword Combo:** Free filtration + Zero reabsorption + Slight secretion = Creatinine

### C urea

Urea undergoes significant passive reabsorption in the proximal tubule and inner medullary collecting duct. Its clearance varies with urine flow rate, meaning its [TF/P] ratio rises much more slowly than inulin's.

**Buzzword Combo:** Free filtration + Passive reabsorption + Flow-dependent clearance = Urea

### D sodium 

Sodium is actively and heavily reabsorbed along the proximal tubule in parallel with water. Consequently, its [TF/P] ratio remains relatively flat (close to 1.0) throughout the entire length of the proximal tubule.

**Buzzword Combo:** Free filtration + Active reabsorption + Isosmotic transport = Sodium
