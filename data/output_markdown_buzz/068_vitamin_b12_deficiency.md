## Buzzword Question

A patient with vitamin B12 deficiency is found to have anemia. Bone marrow examination would most likely show erythroid precursors with delayed nuclear maturation. Which of the following best explains the increased MCV in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

vitamin B12 deficiency; anemia; delayed nuclear maturation; increased MCV

## Answer Options

A) Prolonged marrow maturation due to impaired DNA synthesis

B) Increased hemoglobin concentration inside mature erythrocytes

C) Fragmentation of red blood cells in small blood vessels

D) Failure of iron absorption in the duodenum


## Correct Answer

A) Prolonged marrow maturation due to impaired DNA synthesis


## Explanation

Vitamin B12 is required for normal DNA synthesis. When DNA synthesis is impaired, nuclear maturation is delayed, so erythroid precursors spend more time in the bone marrow. This produces large, immature red blood cells and causes megaloblastic anemia with increased MCV.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

B12 deficiency + delayed nuclear maturation + increased MCV = megaloblastic anemia

## Wrong Options

### B. Increased hemoglobin concentration inside mature erythrocytes


Macrocytosis in vitamin B12 deficiency is not due to excess hemoglobin; it is due to impaired nuclear maturation from defective DNA synthesis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Large RBCs + impaired DNA synthesis = macrocytic anemia

### C. Fragmentation of red blood cells in small blood vessels


This describes microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, which produces schistocytes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Schistocytes + thrombocytopenia + hemolysis = microangiopathic hemolytic anemia

### D. Failure of iron absorption in the duodenum


Iron absorption problems cause iron deficiency anemia, which is classically microcytic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low ferritin + high TIBC + microcytosis = iron deficiency anemia
