## Buzzword Question

A patient with Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome requires pharmacologic therapy to suppress conduction through the accessory pathway. Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line agent?

## Buzzwords Used

Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome; accessory pathway; procainamide; Class IA antiarrhythmic

## Answer Options

A) Adenosine
B) Intravenous diltiazem
C) Intravenous procainamide
D) Intravenous digoxin

## Correct Answer

C) Intravenous procainamide

## Explanation

Procainamide, a Class IA antiarrhythmic, slows conduction through the accessory pathway itself and is the first-line pharmacologic agent for WPW. AV nodal blockers (adenosine, β-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin) are avoided because they block the AV node and force conduction down the unopposed accessory pathway.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

WPW + accessory pathway suppression = IV procainamide (first-line)

## Wrong Options

### A) Adenosine


Blocks the AV node and is avoided in WPW, as it does not act on the accessory pathway.

**Buzzword Combo:** Adenosine = AV nodal blocker (avoided in WPW)

### B) Intravenous diltiazem


A non-dihydropyridine CCB that blocks the AV node; same limitation as adenosine in WPW.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Diltiazem = AV nodal blocker (avoided in WPW)

### 
D) Intravenous digoxin

Slows AV nodal conduction without affecting the accessory pathway, and is avoided in WPW.

**Buzzword Combo:** Digoxin = AV nodal blocker (avoided in WPW)
