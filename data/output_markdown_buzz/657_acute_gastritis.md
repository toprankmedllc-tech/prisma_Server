## Buzzword Question

A middle-aged patient with chronic joint pain presents with coffee-ground emesis after prolonged use of high-dose ibuprofen. Endoscopy reveals superficial gastric erosions.

What is the underlying mechanism?

## Buzzwords Used

joint pain; coffee-ground emesis; high-dose ibuprofen 

## Answer Options

A. Increased gastric acid secretion
B. Decreased prostaglandin synthesis
C. Autoimmune destruction of gastric glands
D. Increased bicarbonate secretion

## Correct Answer

B. Decreased prostaglandin synthesis

## Explanation

↓ Prostaglandins → ↓ mucus & bicarbonate → mucosal injury

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

chronic joint pain + coffee green emesis + ibuprofen (high dose) = Decease PG

## Wrong Options

### A. Increased gastric acid secretion

Gastric acid hypersecretion is the excessive production of hydrochloric acid by the stomach's parietal cells. It is primarily driven by the hormone gastrin and histamine. While normal acid aids digestion, hypersecretion damages the stomach lining, leading to conditions like peptic ulcer disease and severe GERD.

**Buzzword Combo:** Gastrin + Histamine = Increase Gastric acid secretion 

### C. Autoimmune destruction of gastric glands

Autoimmune destruction of gastric glands is the hallmark of Autoimmune Gastritis (AIG). It involves the immune system attacking the parietal and chief cells in the stomach's body and fundus, leading to gastric atrophy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Attack on parietal + attack on chief cells = Autoimmune destruction of gastric glands

### D. Increased bicarbonate secretion

Secretin: The primary hormone released from duodenal S-cells in response to acidic gastric chyme. It heavily stimulates the pancreas and Brunner's glands to secrete bicarbonate-rich fluid.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increase Secretin + Increase PG + Increase PNS =  Increased bicarbonate secretion
