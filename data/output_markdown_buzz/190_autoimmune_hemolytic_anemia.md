## Buzzword Question

A 34-year-old woman with SLE, fatigue, jaundice, dark urine, recent blood transfusion, normocytic anemia, elevated LDH, elevated indirect bilirubin, low haptoglobin, and mild splenomegaly needs confirmation of the diagnosis. Which test is most important?

## Buzzwords Used

SLE; fatigue; jaundice; dark urine; transfusion; normocytic anemia; elevated LDH; elevated indirect bilirubin; low haptoglobin; splenomegaly

## Answer Options

A. Osmotic fragility test
B. Direct Coombs test
C. Hemoglobin electrophoresis
D. G6PD enzyme level

## Correct Answer

B. Direct Coombs test

## Explanation

The direct Coombs (DAT) test detects antibodies or complement bound to RBCs and confirms immune-mediated hemolysis such as warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia, especially in SLE.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Positive direct Coombs + hemolysis + SLE = Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Wrong Options

### A. Osmotic fragility test

Osmotic fragility test is used to diagnose hereditary spherocytosis and does not detect immune-mediated hemolysis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increased osmotic fragility + spherocytes + negative Coombs = Hereditary spherocytosis

### C. Hemoglobin electrophoresis

Hemoglobin electrophoresis is used for hemoglobinopathies like sickle cell disease and thalassemia, not autoimmune hemolysis.

**Buzzword Combo:** HbS peak + sickling = Sickle cell disease

### D. G6PD enzyme level

G6PD testing is used for oxidative hemolysis disorders and should be avoided during acute hemolysis due to false negatives.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bite cells + Heinz bodies + oxidative trigger = G6PD deficiency
