## Buzzword Question

A woman with dysuria has sterile pyuria, and nucleic acid amplification testing identifies an obligate intracellular organism.

Which of the following organisms is the most likely cause of this infection?

## Buzzwords Used

Sterile pyuria; Intracellular; Dysuria

## Answer Options

A) Ureaplasma urealyticum
B) Neisseria gonorrhoeae
C) Chlamydia trachomatis
D) Mycoplasma hominis

## Correct Answer

C) Chlamydia trachomatis

## Explanation

Chlamydia urethritis commonly causes dysuria and sterile pyuria due to urethral inflammation without growth on routine bacterial culture. The organism is an obligate intracellular bacterium detected by nucleic acid amplification testing.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sterile pyuria + intracellular organism = Chlamydia

## Wrong Options

### Ureaplasma urealyticum

Ureaplasma infection may cause nongonococcal urethritis but is not an obligate intracellular organism. It lacks a cell wall and requires specialized media for growth.

**Buzzword Combo:** No cell wall + urease production = Ureaplasma

### Neisseria gonorrhoeae

Gonorrhea commonly causes urethritis with neutrophilic inflammation and can produce dysuria and purulent discharge. It is a gram-negative diplococcus rather than an obligate intracellular organism.

**Buzzword Combo:** Gram-negative diplococci + purulent urethritis = Gonorrhea

### Mycoplasma hominis

Mycoplasma infection lacks a cell wall and may colonize the genitourinary tract but is not classically associated with sterile pyuria due to obligate intracellular infection.

**Buzzword Combo:** No cell wall + atypical genital infection = Mycoplasma
