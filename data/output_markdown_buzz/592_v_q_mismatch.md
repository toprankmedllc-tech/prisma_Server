## Buzzword Question

A 63-year-old man develops sudden shortness of breath and pleuritic chest pain 2 days after hip surgery. He is tachycardic and hypoxemic on room air. CT pulmonary angiography confirms a large pulmonary embolus in the right pulmonary artery. After receiving supplemental oxygen, his oxygen saturation improves significantly. Which of the following best describes the V/Q status in the affected lung region?

## Buzzwords Used

Postoperative patient; sudden dyspnea; pleuritic chest pain; pulmonary embolism; CT pulmonary angiography; hypoxemia improves with oxygen; tachycardia

## Answer Options

A) V/Q = 0 due to alveolar flooding
B) V/Q = ∞ due to absent perfusion
C) V/Q = 1 due to matched ventilation and perfusion
D) V/Q < 1 due to shunt physiology

## Correct Answer

B) V/Q = ∞ due to absent perfusion

## Explanation

Pulmonary embolism blocks perfusion while ventilation is preserved, producing alveolar dead space (V/Q = ∞). Because alveoli are still ventilated, oxygen therapy improves hypoxemia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Post-op state; sudden PE; ventilated but no perfusion; oxygen responsive hypoxemia

## Wrong Options

### A) V/Q = 0 due to alveolar flooding

This describes shunt physiology (eg, pneumonia, ARDS) where alveoli are perfused but not ventilated. It typically does not improve with oxygen, unlike PE.

**Buzzword Combo:** Consolidation; fluid-filled alveoli; no ventilation = Physiologic shunt

### C) V/Q = 1 due to matched ventilation and perfusion

Normal lungs have V/Q ≈ 0.8–1, but PE causes complete mismatch due to absent perfusion, not normal matching.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal gas exchange; no vascular obstruction = normal lung

### D) V/Q < 1 due to shunt physiology

V/Q < 1 indicates low ventilation relative to perfusion (eg, pneumonia). PE is the opposite problem with zero perfusion, not reduced ventilation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low ventilation; mucus plugging; airway disease= COPD, asthma
