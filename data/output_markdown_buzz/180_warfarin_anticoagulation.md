## Buzzword Question

A patient is started on an oral anticoagulant for a prosthetic heart valve without initial overlap with an injectable anticoagulant. Several days later, painful dark skin lesions develop. Which early drug effect best explains this complication?

## Buzzwords Used

Oral anticoagulant for prosthetic heart valve; no initial injectable overlap; painful dark skin lesions

## Answer Options

A) Decreased functional activation of protein C
B) Irreversible inhibition of cyclooxygenase
C) Reduced synthesis of fibrinogen
D) Autoantibody formation against platelet factor 4

## Correct Answer

A) Decreased functional activation of protein C


## Explanation

Warfarin initially decreases functional activation of protein C, an anticoagulant protein with a short half-life. This can create a transient hypercoagulable state and cause skin necrosis, especially without heparin bridging.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

New oral anticoagulant + painful dark skin lesions + no injectable overlap = early protein C depletion from warfarin

## Wrong Options

### B) Irreversible inhibition of cyclooxygenase

This describes aspirin. Aspirin affects platelet thromboxane production and does not explain warfarin-related early hypercoagulability.

**Buzzword Combo:** Irreversible COX inhibition + decreased thromboxane A2 + platelet dysfunction = aspirin

### C) Reduced synthesis of fibrinogen

Warfarin does not primarily reduce fibrinogen synthesis. Its key effect is decreased activation of vitamin K–dependent factors and proteins C/S.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low fibrinogen + bleeding + consumptive coagulopathy pattern = disseminated intravascular coagulation

### D) Autoantibody formation against platelet factor 4

This describes heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. The clue pattern here points to early warfarin effect due to loss of protein C, not anti-PF4 antibodies.

**Buzzword Combo:** Heparin exposure + platelet factor 4 antibodies + thrombosis with thrombocytopenia = heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

## Related Concepts

Protein C deficiency; heparin bridging; heparin-induced thrombocytopenia
