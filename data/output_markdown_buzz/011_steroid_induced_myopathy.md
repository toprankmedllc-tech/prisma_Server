## Buzzword Question

old man with severe COPD has been treated with prolonged high-dose Prednisone. He now reports gradual onset of difficulty lifting objects overhead .Physical examination demonstrates painless proximal muscle weakness without sensory deficits. CK levels are within normal limits.
Which of the following is the most likely mechanism responsible for this patient’s condition?

## Buzzwords Used

 high-dose Prednisone ;difficulty lifting objects overhead ;CK levels are within normal limits

## Answer Options

A. Corticosteroid-induced protein catabolism
B. Autoantibody-mediated destruction of acetylcholine receptors
C. Immune-mediated muscle fiber inflammation
D. Dopamine depletion within the substantia nigra

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Chronic glucocorticoid exposure causes muscle protein breakdown and type II muscle fiber atrophy, leading to painless proximal muscle weakness.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Long-term steroids + proximal weakness + normal CK = steroid-induced myopathy

## Wrong Options

### B. Autoantibody-mediated destruction of acetylcholine receptors

Incorrect — This mechanism is seen in Myasthenia gravis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ptosis + diplopia + fatigable weakness = myasthenia gravis

### C. Immune-mediated muscle fiber inflammation

Incorrect — This mechanism is characteristic of inflammatory myopathies such as Polymyositis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elevated CK + proximal weakness = inflammatory myopathy

### D. Dopamine depletion within the substantia nigra

Incorrect — This mechanism is seen in Parkinson disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Resting tremor + bradykinesia = Parkinson disease
