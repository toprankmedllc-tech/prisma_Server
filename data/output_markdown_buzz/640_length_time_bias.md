## Buzzword Question

A screening program for lung cancer is introduced in a large population. After several years, investigators find that patients whose cancers were detected through screening survive substantially longer after diagnosis than patients whose cancers were diagnosed after symptoms developed. Further analysis shows that screening most commonly detects slowly growing tumors.

Which of the following best explains these findings?

## Buzzwords Used

Screening ; Survival after diagnosis ; Slowly growing tumors ; Cancer screening ; Epidemiology

## Answer Options

A) Recall bias

B) Lead-time bias

C) Length-time bias

D) Confounding

## Correct Answer

C) Length-time bias

## Explanation

Screening preferentially detects slowly progressive diseases that remain asymptomatic for longer periods. This can create the appearance of improved survival even when overall mortality is unchanged.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Screening + slow-growing disease preferentially detected + apparent survival advantage = length-time bias

## Wrong Options

### A) Recall bias

Recall bias results from inaccurate recollection of prior exposures and is unrelated to preferential detection of indolent disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Exposure recall issue + screening survival pattern = not recall bias

### B) Lead-time bias

Lead-time bias results from earlier diagnosis increasing measured survival time without affecting disease course. This vignette specifically emphasizes preferential detection of slow-growing tumors.

**Buzzword Combo:** Earlier diagnosis + no mention of tumor aggressiveness = lead-time bias

### D) Confounding

Confounding involves a third variable influencing both exposure and outcome and does not explain this screening phenomenon.

**Buzzword Combo:** Third-variable distortion + screening survival advantage = not confounding
