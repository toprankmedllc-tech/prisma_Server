## Buzzword Question

A 28-year-old man presents to his primary care physician reporting 3 weeks of depressed mood, insomnia, poor concentration, and loss of appetite following the sudden death of his mother in a car accident 4 weeks ago. He tearfully discusses his mother, visits her grave weekly, and says he still occasionally “feels her presence.” He denies hallucinations, suicidal ideation, and has returned to work. Which of the following is the most appropriate characterization of this patient’s condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Depressed mood; insomnia; poor concentration; bereavement; 4 weeks; feels her presence; returned to work; no suicidal ideation

## Answer Options

A) Major depressive disorder
B) Adjustment disorder
C) Normal grief response
D) Persistent complex bereavement disorder

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

Normal grief following bereavement includes depressive symptoms, sensing the deceased’s presence, and functional fluctuation — all within weeks of loss. This patient is only 4 weeks out, has returned to work, denies suicidality, and his symptoms are culturally expected responses to sudden loss, not a disorder.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recent major loss + depressive symptoms + functional return + sensing presence + 4 weeks = Normal grief

## Wrong Options

### A) Major depressive disorder

MDD requires 2+ weeks of symptoms causing significant functional impairment, but bereavement-related depressive symptoms in the acute period are not automatically classified as MDD. This patient has returned to work, and his symptoms are expected in context of sudden loss.

**Buzzword Combo:** 2+ weeks + depressed mood + insomnia + concentration → MDD (ignores bereavement context + functional recovery = premature closure)

### B) Adjustment disorder

Adjustment disorder applies when symptoms are in excess of what is expected for the stressor or cause marked impairment. Here, the patient’s response is proportionate to sudden traumatic loss and he has maintained function, making normal grief more appropriate.

**Buzzword Combo:** Stressor + mood symptoms → adjustment disorder (ignores that grief IS the expected response = over-pathologizing)

### D) Persistent complex bereavement disorder

Persistent complex bereavement disorder requires symptoms lasting more than 12 months (6 months in children) with functional impairment. This patient is only 4 weeks out and is functioning — this diagnosis cannot yet be applied.

**Buzzword Combo:** Intense grief + sensing presence → persistent bereavement (ignores 4-week timeframe = duration criterion not met)
