## Buzzword Question

A 23-year-old woman with recurrent severe abdominal pain, nausea, confusion, dark urine, and elevated urinary PBG and ALA during attacks most likely has deficiency of which enzyme?

## Buzzwords Used

abdominal pain; confusion; dark urine; elevated PBG; elevated ALA; neurovisceral attacks

## Answer Options

A) Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase
B) Ferrochelatase
C) Porphobilinogen deaminase
D) ALA synthase

## Correct Answer

C. Porphobilinogen deaminase

## Explanation

Acute intermittent porphyria is caused by deficiency of porphobilinogen deaminase, the third enzyme in heme synthesis. Deficiency leads to accumulation of ALA and PBG, causing neurovisceral symptoms without photosensitivity.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

PBG deaminase deficiency + elevated PBG + abdominal pain = AIP

## Wrong Options

### A. Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase

Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase deficiency causes porphyria cutanea tarda, characterized by photosensitive blistering skin lesions rather than abdominal and psychiatric symptoms.

**Buzzword Combo:** Photosensitivity + blistering + hypertrichosis = PCT

### B. Ferrochelatase

Ferrochelatase deficiency causes erythropoietic protoporphyria with painful photosensitivity due to protoporphyrin accumulation. It does not cause elevated PBG or neurovisceral attacks.

**Buzzword Combo:** Burning photosensitivity + ferrochelatase deficiency = Erythropoietic protoporphyria

### D. ALA synthase

ALA synthase is the rate-limiting enzyme of heme synthesis and is not deficient in porphyrias. Gain-of-function mutations are associated with X-linked sideroblastic anemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ring sideroblasts + ALA synthase mutation = Sideroblastic anemia
