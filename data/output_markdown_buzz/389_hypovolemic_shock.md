## Buzzword Question

A patient with severe diarrhea presents with tachycardia, hypotension, cool clammy extremities, decreased urine output, and elevated systemic vascular resistance due to decreased preload and cardiac output. What is the diagnosis?


## Buzzwords Used

Severe diarrhea ; tachycardia ; hypotension ; cool clammy skin ; decreased preload ; increased SVR


## Answer Options

 A) Septic shock
 B) Cardiogenic shock
 C) Hypovolemic shock
 D) Neurogenic shock


## Correct Answer

C) Hypovolemic shock


## Explanation

Severe fluid loss from diarrhea decreases intravascular volume, causing reduced preload, stroke volume, and cardiac output. Compensatory tachycardia and increased systemic vascular resistance are classic findings of hypovolemic shock.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Severe diarrhea + decreased preload + cool clammy skin = Hypovolemic shock


## Wrong Options

### A) Septic shock


Septic shock usually presents with warm extremities early due to systemic vasodilation rather than marked volume depletion.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Fever + warm flushed skin + infection = Septic shock


### B) Cardiogenic shock


Cardiogenic shock results from pump failure and is commonly associated with pulmonary edema and elevated jugular venous pressure.


**Buzzword Combo:** Chest pain + pulmonary edema + elevated JVP = Cardiogenic shock


###  D) Neurogenic shock


Neurogenic shock typically presents with hypotension and bradycardia due to loss of sympathetic tone after spinal cord injury.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Spinal cord injury + hypotension + bradycardia = Neurogenic shock
