## Buzzword Question

 A hospitalized patient develops acute respiratory failure 48 hours after aspiration during an episode of vomiting. Chest x-ray shows diffuse bilateral “white-out” infiltrates, and arterial blood gas demonstrates severe hypoxemia with an increased A-a gradient. Pulmonary capillary wedge pressure is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Aspiration; diffuse bilateral white-out infiltrates; severe hypoxemia; increased A-a gradient; normal pulmonary capillary wedge pressure


## Answer Options

 A) Cardiogenic pulmonary edema
 B) Acute respiratory distress syndrome
 C) Aspiration pneumonitis without respiratory failure
 D) Acute exacerbation of interstitial lung disease


## Correct Answer

B) Acute respiratory distress syndrome


## Explanation

ARDS commonly occurs after aspiration, sepsis, or trauma and presents with acute hypoxemic respiratory failure and diffuse bilateral infiltrates not explained by cardiac dysfunction. Normal pulmonary capillary wedge pressure supports noncardiogenic pulmonary edema.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Aspiration + bilateral white-out infiltrates + refractory hypoxemia = ARDS


## Wrong Options

### A) Cardiogenic pulmonary edema


Cardiogenic pulmonary edema is associated with elevated pulmonary capillary wedge pressure and evidence of left-sided heart failure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Reduced cardiac function + elevated wedge pressure + pulmonary edema = Cardiogenic pulmonary edema


### C) Aspiration pneumonitis without respiratory failure

Simple aspiration pneumonitis may cause focal inflammation, but severe diffuse bilateral infiltrates with refractory hypoxemia indicate progression to ARDS

**Buzzword Combo:** Recent aspiration + mild infiltrates + stable oxygenation = Aspiration pneumonitis


###  D) Acute exacerbation of interstitial lung disease


Interstitial lung disease exacerbations usually occur in patients with known chronic fibrotic lung disease rather than following acute aspiration events.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Chronic fibrosis + restrictive lung disease + worsening dyspnea = ILD exacerbation
