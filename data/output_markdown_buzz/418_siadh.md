## Buzzword Question

A patient with SIADH develops seizures and severe hyponatremia with serum sodium of 110 mEq/L. What is the most appropriate immediate treatment?


## Buzzwords Used

Seizures ; severe hyponatremia ; SIADH ; acute neurologic symptoms

## Answer Options

 A) Fluid restriction alone
 B) Oral salt tablets
 C) Hypertonic saline (3% saline)
 D) Desmopressin


## Correct Answer

C) Hypertonic saline (3% saline)


## Explanation

Severe symptomatic hyponatremia is a medical emergency requiring prompt treatment with hypertonic saline to prevent cerebral edema and brain herniation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

 Seizures + sodium 110 + SIADH = Hypertonic saline


## Wrong Options

### A) Fluid restriction alone


Fluid restriction is first-line for chronic mild SIADH but is insufficient in severe symptomatic hyponatremia.


**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic asymptomatic hyponatremia + euvolemia + stable patient = Fluid restriction

###  B) Oral salt tablets


Salt tablets may help chronic SIADH but are not appropriate emergency therapy for seizures due to severe hyponatremia.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Mild chronic SIADH + outpatient management + stable symptoms = Salt tablets


###  D) Desmopressin


Desmopressin is an ADH analog used in diabetes insipidus and would worsen SIADH-related water retention.


**Buzzword Combo:** Central diabetes insipidus + polyuria + hypernatremia = Desmopressin
