## Buzzword Question

A 14-year-old boy has tall stature, long fingers, and lens dislocation. His plasma homocysteine is markedly elevated and he has mild intellectual disability. His 11-year-old sister has the same lens dislocation. Both parents are phenotypically normal. The direction of lens dislocation and inheritance pattern are:


## Buzzwords Used

Tall; arachnodactyly; lens dislocation; elevated homocysteine; intellectual disability; unaffected carrier parents; sibling affected; autosomal recessive

## Answer Options

A) Inferonasal (downward) lens dislocation; autosomal recessive 
B) Superotemporal (upward) lens dislocation; autosomal dominant 
C) Inferonasal (downward) lens dislocation; autosomal dominant 
D) Superotemporal (upward) lens dislocation; X-linked recessive


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Homocystinuria (CBS deficiency): marfanoid habitus + INFERONASAL/DOWNWARD lens dislocation (opposite of Marfan's upward) + intellectual disability + thromboembolism + elevated homocysteine. Autosomal recessive— both parents unaffected carriers explains two affected children from phenotypically normal parents. CBS mutations: cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency → homocysteine accumulation → connective tissue and CNS effects.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Marfanoid + DOWNWARD ectopia lentis + intellectual disability + elevated homocysteine + unaffected carrier parents + AR = Homocystinuria (CBS deficiency)

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Superotemporal (upward) lens; autosomal dominant

Superotemporal (upward) ectopia lentis + AD + normal intelligence + FBN1 = Marfan syndrome. This family has two affected children from unaffected parents (AR pattern) + elevated homocysteine — consistent with homocystinuria, not Marfan.

**Buzzword Combo:** UPWARD (superotemporal) ectopia lentis + normal intelligence + AD + FBN1 mutation = Marfan syndrome

### 2: C) Inferonasal lens; autosomal dominant

Lens direction (downward) is correct for homocystinuria, but inheritance is AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE. Two affected siblings from two unaffected parents = classic AR pattern (each parent carries one mutated CBS allele, 25% risk per child). AD would require at least one affected parent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two affected siblings + two phenotypically normal parents = Autosomal recessive (carrier × carrier → 25% affected per child)


### 3: D) Superotemporal lens; X-linked recessive


Neither feature applies. X-linked recessive primarily affects males with carrier females,  but here a male and female sibling are both affected from unaffected parents (inconsistent with X-linked recessive). Lens dislocation in homocystinuria is downward, not upward.

**Buzzword Combo:** Males primarily affected + carrier mother + no father-to-son = X-linked recessive (incompatible with affected female sibling)
