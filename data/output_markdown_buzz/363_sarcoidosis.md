## Buzzword Question

A 34-year-old African American woman presents with progressive dry cough, dyspnea, and fatigue for 4 months. She also reports painful red nodules on her shins and intermittent blurry vision with eye redness. Chest X-ray shows bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy. Serum ACE levels are elevated, and serum calcium is increased. Which of the following is the most likely immune hypersensitivity type involved in this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

African American woman; dry cough; dyspnea; erythema nodosum; uveitis; bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy; elevated ACE; hypercalcemia; chronic multisystem disease

## Answer Options

A) Type IV hypersensitivity
B) Type I hypersensitivity
C) Type II hypersensitivity
D) Type III hypersensitivity

## Correct Answer

A) Type IV hypersensitivity

## Explanation

Sarcoidosis is a T-cell mediated (Th1) delayed-type hypersensitivity reaction, classified as Type IV hypersensitivity. It is characterized by macrophage activation and formation of non-caseating granulomas without antibody involvement.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

non-caseating granulomas + CD4 Th1 response + IFN-γ + chronic inflammatory disease

## Wrong Options

### B) Type I hypersensitivity

Type I hypersensitivity is IgE-mediated and involves mast cell degranulation, seen in asthma and allergies, not granulomatous disease like sarcoidosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** IgE mediated + mast cells + histamine release + anaphylaxis/asthma

### C) Type II hypersensitivity



Type II hypersensitivity involves antibody-mediated cytotoxicity against cell surface antigens (e.g., autoimmune hemolytic anemia), not granuloma formation.

**Buzzword Combo:** IgG/IgM antibodies + complement activation + cell destruction

### D) Type III hypersensitivity

Type III hypersensitivity involves immune complex deposition, causing vasculitis (e.g., SLE), not granulomatous inflammation seen in sarcoidosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** immune complex deposition + complement activation + vasculitis
