## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man with metastatic prostate cancer is started on a new medication. Over the next few weeks, he develops fatigue, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, and gynecomastia. Laboratory tests show low testosterone and low cortisol levels.

Despite normal pituitary function, both adrenal and gonadal steroid hormones are significantly reduced.

Which of the following best explains the mechanism of action of this drug?

## Buzzwords Used

metastatic prostate cancer ; decreased libido ; erectile dysfunction ; gynecomastia ; low testosterone ; low cortisol ; adrenal suppression ; gonadal suppression ; steroid synthesis inhibition

## Answer Options

A) Competitive antagonism of androgen receptors in target tissues
B) Inhibition of 5-alpha reductase conversion of testosterone to DHT
C) Inhibition of CYP17 enzyme in steroid synthesis pathway
D) Blockade of GnRH receptors in anterior pituitary

## Correct Answer

C) Inhibition of CYP17 enzyme in steroid synthesis pathway

## Explanation

Ketoconazole inhibits CYP17, a key enzyme in adrenal and gonadal steroid synthesis, leading to decreased production of testosterone and cortisol. This results in hypogonadism symptoms and adrenal suppression.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Prostate cancer + low testosterone + low cortisol = CYP17 inhibition (ketoconazole)

## Wrong Options

### A) Competitive antagonism of androgen receptors in target tissues

This describes Flutamide, which blocks androgen action but does not reduce cortisol levels.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prostate cancer + gynecomastia + preserved cortisol = androgen receptor blocker

### B) Inhibition of 5-alpha reductase conversion of testosterone to DHT

This describes Finasteride, which decreases DHT but does not affect cortisol or upstream steroid synthesis.

**Buzzword Combo:** BPH + decreased DHT + preserved testosterone = finasteride

### D) Blockade of GnRH receptors in anterior pituitary

This describes Degarelix, which suppresses LH/FSH but does not directly inhibit adrenal cortisol synthesis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prostate cancer + immediate LH suppression + no flare = GnRH antagonist
