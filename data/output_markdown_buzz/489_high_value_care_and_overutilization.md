## Buzzword Question

A 38-year-old man with 10 days of acute low back pain, no cancer history, no fever, no saddle anesthesia, no bowel or bladder dysfunction, and preserved bilateral lower extremity strength and sensation is ordered an MRI of the lumbar spine by his primary care physician at the first visit. In the context of high-value care principles, this represents:


## Buzzwords Used

Acute back pain <6 weeks; no red flags; MRI ordered at first visit; high-value care; Choosing Wisely

## Answer Options

A) Overutilization — MRI is not recommended for uncomplicated acute low back pain within the first 4–6 weeks without red flags 
B) Appropriate diagnostic workup given symptom duration and severity 
C) Evidence-based imaging per current musculoskeletal guidelines 
D) Underutilization — imaging in acute back pain prevents missed serious diagnoses


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Current guidelines (ACP, AAFP, ACR Appropriateness Criteria, Choosing Wisely) recommend conservative management (NSAIDs, heat, gradual activity) for uncomplicated acute low back pain for at least 4–6 weeks before imaging. MRI without red flags has no demonstrated benefit in clinical outcomes, increases costs, and leads to incidental findings and unnecessary interventions. Red flags requiring earlier imaging: saddle anesthesia, bowel/bladder dysfunction, progressive neurological deficits, fever/infection, cancer history, osteoporotic fracture risk, or failure to improve after 6 weeks.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Acute back pain <6 weeks + no red flags + MRI ordered = Overutilization (not guideline-concordant); conservative management ≥4-6 weeks first

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Appropriate given symptom duration and severity

Ten days of acute back pain without red flags does not meet any guideline threshold for MRI. Symptom duration of <6 weeks without neurological deficits, cancer, fever, or fracture risk is the standard conservative management period before imaging is indicated.

**Buzzword Combo:** Red flags present + back pain → Early imaging appropriate: saddle anesthesia, bowel/bladder, cancer, fever, progressive neuro deficit

### 2: C) Evidence-based imaging per current guidelines


Current evidence-based guidelines specifically recommend AGAINST routine imaging for uncomplicated acute low back pain in the first 4–6 weeks. Ordering MRI at day 10 without red flags is inconsistent with, not supported by, current guidelines.

**Buzzword Combo:** Conservative management (NSAIDs + activity modification) for 4–6 weeks without imaging = Evidence-based approach for uncomplicated acute low back pain

### 3: D) Underutilization — prevents missed diagnoses

For uncomplicated acute low back pain without red flags, imaging at <6 weeks is overutilization. The risk of missing serious pathology without red flags is very low. Withholding unnecessary imaging is guideline-recommended, not underutilization

**Buzzword Combo:** Withholding indicated test when red flags ARE present = Underutilization; ordering unnecessary test without indication = Overutilization
