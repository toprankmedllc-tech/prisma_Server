## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old man in the ICU develops acute respiratory failure after aspiration of gastric contents. He has severe hypoxemia that does not improve despite 100% oxygen. ABG shows markedly low PaO₂ and a significantly increased A–a gradient. Which of the following best explains the A–a gradient abnormality?

## Buzzwords Used

ICU patient; aspiration; ARDS; refractory hypoxemia; 100% oxygen failure; low PaO₂; increased A–a gradient

## Answer Options

A) Hypoventilation
B) Intrapulmonary shunt
C) V/Q mismatch
D) High altitude

## Correct Answer

B) Intrapulmonary shunt

## Explanation

In aspiration-induced ARDS, alveoli are filled with fluid and inflammatory debris, leading to perfusion without ventilation (true shunt). This causes severe hypoxemia that does not correct with 100% oxygen and produces a markedly increased A–a gradient.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

aspiration + alveolar flooding + refractory hypoxemia → intrapulmonary shunt → ↑A–a gradient

## Wrong Options

### A) Hypoventilation

Hypoventilation causes global reduction in oxygen but produces a normal A–a gradient because both PAO₂ and PaO₂ fall together.

**Buzzword Combo:** CO₂ retention + uniform alveolar under-ventilation + normal gas exchange gradient

### C) V/Q mismatch

V/Q mismatch improves at least partially with supplemental oxygen, unlike true shunt physiology seen in ARDS where oxygen response is minimal.

**Buzzword Combo:** uneven ventilation + partial oxygen response + COPD/pneumonia pattern

### D) High altitude

High altitude causes hypoxemia due to low inspired oxygen (low PAO₂) with a normal A–a gradient, not an increased gradient.

**Buzzword Combo:** low FiO₂ + environmental hypoxia + normal diffusion across alveoli
