## Buzzword Question

A 32-year-old unidentified man is brought by EMS after a high-speed MVC. He is unconscious (GCS 5), hemodynamically unstable (BP 72/40), and CT reveals a grade IV splenic laceration with active hemorrhage. No identification, no family, and no advance directives are available. The trauma surgeon must perform an emergency splenectomy. Which doctrine justifies proceeding without formal informed consent?

## Buzzwords Used

Unconscious; immediate life threat; no surrogate; delay harmful; emergency; implied consent; four conditions met

## Answer Options

A) Emergency exception — implied consent when patient is incapacitated with immediate life threat and no surrogate available 
B) Therapeutic privilege — withholding information to prevent patient distress 
C) Presumed competence — adults are presumed to have decision-making capacity
 D) Physician substituted judgment — physician decides what the patient would have wanted

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The emergency exception to informed consent applies when four conditions are simultaneously met: (1) immediate life-threatening emergency requiring urgent intervention; (2) patient lacks decision-making capacity; (3) no surrogate or advance directive available; (4) delaying treatment to obtain consent would cause serious harm or death. All four are met here. Implied consent = legal presumption that a reasonable person would consent to lifesaving emergency treatment

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Immediate life threat + incapacitated patient + no surrogate + delay harmful = Emergency exception (implied consent) — all 4 conditions required


## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Therapeutic privilege

Therapeutic privilege is the controversial doctrine that a physician may withhold information from a capable patient if disclosure would cause psychological harm interfering with rational decision-making. It applies to capable patients receiving information, not to unconscious patients in emergencies. It is not a justification for bypassing the consent process entirely.

**Buzzword Combo:** Withholding information from CAPABLE patient to prevent psychological harm from disclosure = Therapeutic privilege (controversial, narrow)

### 2: C) Presumed competence

Presumed competence is the baseline legal default that adults have decision-making capacity unless demonstrated otherwise. It is not an exception to informed consent, it is the assumption applied before consent is sought. This patient demonstrably lacks capacity (GCS 5). Presumed competence does not justify proceeding without consent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Adult patient + no evidence of incapacity → presumed to have DMC (default assumption, not exception to consent)


### 3: D) Physician substituted judgment

Substituted judgment is the surrogate decision-making standard where a designated surrogate decides what the patient would have wanted based on known values. It applies when a surrogate is making decisions for an incapacitated patient, not when there is no surrogate and emergency action is required immediately.

**Buzzword Combo:** Surrogate asks 'what would this patient have wanted?' based on their known values = Substituted judgment (surrogate standard)
