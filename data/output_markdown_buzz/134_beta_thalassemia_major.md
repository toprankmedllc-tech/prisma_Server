## Buzzword Question

A child with severe microcytic anemia, frontal bossing, and increased HbF most likely has:

## Buzzwords Used

child; severe microcytic anemia; frontal bossing; increased HbF; bone marrow expansion

## Answer Options

A) Iron deficiency anemia
B) Beta-thalassemia major
C) Sickle cell disease
D) Alpha-thalassemia trait

## Correct Answer

B. Beta-thalassemia major

## Explanation

Beta-thalassemia major is caused by absent or severely reduced beta-globin chain production, leading to severe microcytic anemia. Compensatory extramedullary hematopoiesis causes frontal bossing, and hemoglobin electrophoresis shows increased HbF (and HbA2) with markedly decreased HbA.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Frontal bossing + severe microcytic anemia + ↑HbF = Beta-thalassemia major

## Wrong Options

### A) Iron deficiency anemia

Iron deficiency anemia causes microcytic anemia due to low iron stores but does not cause bone deformities or increased HbF. It is associated with low ferritin and koilonychia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low ferritin + koilonychia + microcytosis = Iron deficiency anemia

### C) Sickle cell disease

Sickle cell disease is characterized by HbS, vaso-occlusive crises, and sickled red cells on smear. It does not typically present with severe microcytosis or markedly increased HbF in this pattern.

**Buzzword Combo:** HbS + sickled cells + pain crises = Sickle cell disease

### D) Alpha-thalassemia trait

Alpha-thalassemia trait causes mild microcytic anemia and is usually asymptomatic with normal or near-normal hemoglobin electrophoresis. It does not cause frontal bossing or severe anemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mild microcytosis + normal electrophoresis = Alpha-thalassemia trait
