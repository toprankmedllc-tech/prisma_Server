## Buzzword Question

A child is evaluated for anemia after developing pallor and scleral icterus. Peripheral smear shows spherocytes. Direct antiglobulin testing detects antibodies bound to the surface of RBCs. Which of the following best distinguishes this condition from hereditary spherocytosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Spherocytes + positive Coombs = autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Answer Options

A) Positive Coombs test

B) Increased osmotic fragility

C) Presence of spherocytes

D) Extravascular hemolysis


## Correct Answer

A. Positive Coombs test


## Explanation

Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia and hereditary spherocytosis can both cause spherocytes and extravascular hemolysis. The key difference is the Coombs test. Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia has antibodies against RBCs, so the Coombs test is positive. Hereditary spherocytosis is due to a membrane cytoskeleton defect and has a negative Coombs test.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Spherocytes + positive Coombs = autoimmune hemolytic anemia

## Wrong Options

### B. Increased osmotic fragility


Increased osmotic fragility is classically associated with hereditary spherocytosis because spherocytes rupture more easily in hypotonic solution.

**Buzzword Combo:** Increased osmotic fragility + negative Coombs = hereditary spherocytosis

### C. Presence of spherocytes


Spherocytes are not specific. They can be seen in both hereditary spherocytosis and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spherocytes alone ≠ hereditary spherocytosis

### D. Extravascular hemolysis


Both conditions can cause extravascular hemolysis because abnormal RBCs are removed by splenic macrophages.

**Buzzword Combo:** Splenic macrophage RBC removal = extravascular hemolysis
