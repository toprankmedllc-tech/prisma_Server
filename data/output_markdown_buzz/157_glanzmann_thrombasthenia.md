## Buzzword Question

A child has recurrent nosebleeds, easy bruising, and prolonged bleeding after minor cuts. Laboratory studies show normal platelet count, normal PT, normal PTT, and prolonged bleeding time; platelet testing shows failure of platelets to clump together after activation. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Recurrent nosebleeds and easy bruising; normal platelet count with normal PT/PTT; failure of platelets to clump together after activation

## Answer Options

A) Glanzmann thrombasthenia
B) Bernard-Soulier syndrome
C) Von Willebrand disease
D) Immune thrombocytopenia

## Correct Answer

A) Glanzmann thrombasthenia


## Explanation

Glanzmann thrombasthenia is caused by GpIIb/IIIa deficiency, leading to defective platelet aggregation. Platelet count is normal because platelet number is preserved, but platelet function is impaired.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Mucocutaneous bleeding + normal platelet count + defective platelet aggregation = Glanzmann thrombasthenia

## Wrong Options

### B) Bernard-Soulier syndrome

Bernard-Soulier syndrome is due to defective GpIb-mediated platelet adhesion. It is associated with large platelets and thrombocytopenia, not isolated defective platelet aggregation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mucocutaneous bleeding + giant platelets + defective platelet adhesion = Bernard-Soulier syndrome

### C) Von Willebrand disease

Von Willebrand disease causes impaired platelet adhesion and possible factor VIII-related PTT prolongation. The main defect is vWF-mediated adhesion, not GpIIb/IIIa-mediated aggregation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mucocutaneous bleeding + family history + prolonged PTT possible = von Willebrand disease

### D) Immune thrombocytopenia

Immune thrombocytopenia causes decreased platelet count due to immune destruction. This patient has normal platelet count with defective platelet function.

**Buzzword Combo:** Petechiae/purpura + isolated thrombocytopenia + antiplatelet antibodies = immune thrombocytopenia

## Related Concepts

Bernard-Soulier syndrome; von Willebrand disease; primary hemostasis
