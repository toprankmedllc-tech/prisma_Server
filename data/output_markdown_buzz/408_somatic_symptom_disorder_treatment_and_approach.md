## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old woman with somatic symptom disorder has been evaluated by multiple specialists over 3 years for fatigue and diffuse pain; all workups have been negative. She sees a new primary care physician who reviews her chart. Which of the following management strategies is most appropriate?

## Buzzwords Used

Multiple specialist visits; negative workup; 3 years; fatigue; diffuse pain; somatic symptom disorder; new physician


## Answer Options

A) Schedule regular, brief, time-limited appointments regardless of symptom status to reduce emergency visits and reinforce the therapeutic relationship
 B) Order comprehensive repeat workup to reassure the patient that no organic disease has been missed 
C) Tell the patient her symptoms are not real and refer her to psychiatry immediately without follow-up 
D) Avoid all further evaluation and discharge the patient from the practice to prevent unnecessary healthcare utilization


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The cornerstone of managing somatic symptom disorder is establishing a consistent, trusting therapeutic relationship with a single primary care provider. Regularly scheduled brief appointments (regardless of symptom exacerbation) serve several purposes: they reduce illness behavior-reinforcing crisis visits, provide a structured outlet for health concerns, allow the physician to monitor for genuine organic illness, and gradually shift the clinical encounter from symptom-focused to function-focused. Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) is the evidence-based psychotherapy of choice. SSRIs may help comorbid anxiety/depression. Ordering extensive repeat workups reinforces illness behavior and is counterproductive.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Somatic symptom disorder management = scheduled regular appointments + single consistent provider + CBT + avoid excessive workup

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Order comprehensive repeat workup to reassure the patient that no organic disease has been missed

Repeated extensive workups in somatic symptom disorder are counterproductive. Negative results rarely provide lasting reassurance and instead reinforce illness behavior, increase healthcare utilization, and expose the patient to procedural risks. The management goal is to shift focus from diagnosis-seeking to functional improvement.


**Buzzword Combo:** Repeat negative workup in SSD = reinforces illness behavior + escalates healthcare utilization; avoid

### 2: C) Tell the patient her symptoms are not real and refer her to psychiatry immediately without follow-up


Telling patients their symptoms are “not real” is both therapeutically harmful and factually incorrect  in SSD, symptoms are real and distressing, even if no organic etiology is identified. Abrupt psychiatric referral without maintaining the primary care relationship is likely to rupture trust and cause the patient to seek care elsewhere. The primary care physician should maintain ongoing involvement.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dismissing symptoms as “not real” + abrupt psychiatric referral = therapeutic rupture; damages treatment alliance in SSD

### 3: D) Avoid all further evaluation and discharge the patient from the practice to prevent unnecessary healthcare utilization

Abandoning patients with SSD increases the risk of missed organic pathology, worsens illness behavior by driving patients to emergency departments and urgent care, and is ethically inappropriate. The goal is structured engagement, not disengagement.

**Buzzword Combo:** Disengagement from care in SSD = missed organic disease risk + increased ER utilization; contraindicated
