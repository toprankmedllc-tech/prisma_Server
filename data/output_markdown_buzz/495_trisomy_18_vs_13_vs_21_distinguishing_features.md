## Buzzword Question

A neonate born at 35 weeks has clenched fists with overlapping fingers (index over middle), rocker-bottom feet, micrognathia, a prominent occiput, and a ventricular septal defect on echocardiogram. The most likely chromosomal diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Clenched overlapping fists; rocker-bottom feet; micrognathia; prominent occiput; VSD; premature neonate


## Answer Options

A) Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome)
 B) Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome) 
C) Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome) 
D) 45,X (Turner syndrome)

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome): clenched fists with overlapping fingers (index over middle, 5th over 4th), rocker-bottom feet, micrognathia, prominent occiput, ventricular septal defect. Second most common autosomal trisomy in live births. Prognosis very poor: 50% die within the first week, 90% by one year.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Clenched overlapping fingers + rocker-bottom feet + micrognathia + prominent occiput + VSD = Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome)

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome)


 Down syndrome: upslanting palpebral fissures, flat facial profile, single palmar crease, hypotonia, sandal gap, and complete AVSD, not clenched fists or rocker-bottom feet. Completely different dysmorphic phenotype.

**Buzzword Combo:** Upslanting palpebral fissures + flat face + single palmar crease + hypotonia + AVSD + sandal gap = Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome)

### 2: C) Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome)

Trisomy 13: severe midline defects (holoprosencephaly, cyclopia, midline cleft lip/palate), polydactyly, cutis aplasia of scalp, microphthalmia, coloboma. Entirely different phenotype from the clenched fist/rocker-bottom foot pattern of trisomy 18.


**Buzzword Combo:** Holoprosencephaly + polydactyly + midline cleft lip + cutis aplasia + microphthalmia = Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome)


### 3: D) 45,X (Turner syndrome)

Turner syndrome: phenotypic females with webbed neck, shield chest, widely spaced nipples, coarctation of aorta, primary amenorrhea, short stature,  recognized in childhood/adolescence, not by clenched fists or rocker-bottom feet in the neonatal period.

**Buzzword Combo:** Female + webbed neck + shield chest + coarctation + primary amenorrhea = Turner syndrome (45,X)
