## Buzzword Question

A 32-year-old man presents with difficulty initiating shoulder abduction after sustaining a shoulder injury while lifting heavy weights at the gym. On physical examination, he is unable to begin abduction of the arm from 0° to 15°, although abduction beyond this range is preserved. MRI reveals an injury involving the supraspinatus muscle.
Which of the following nerves was most likely damaged?

## Buzzwords Used

unable to begin abduction of the arm from 0° to 15°;supraspinatus muscle

## Answer Options

A. Axillary nerve
B. Musculocutaneous nerve
C. Suprascapular nerve
D. Thoracodorsal nerve

## Correct Answer

c

## Explanation

The supraspinatus muscle is innervated by the suprascapular nerve (C5–C6). This muscle initiates the first 15° of arm abduction before the deltoid muscle takes over.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Difficulty initiating abduction (0–15°) + supraspinatus injury = suprascapular nerve lesion

## Wrong Options

### A. Axillary nerve

Incorrect — The axillary nerve innervates the deltoid and teres minor muscles. Injury causes impaired abduction mainly from 15° to 90° and sensory loss over the lateral shoulder.

**Buzzword Combo:** Surgical neck fracture + loss of shoulder contour = axillary nerve injury

### B. Musculocutaneous nerve

Incorrect — This nerve innervates the anterior compartment of the arm (biceps brachii, brachialis, coracobrachialis) and is important for elbow flexion.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak elbow flexion + decreased lateral forearm sensation = musculocutaneous nerve injury

### D. Thoracodorsal nerve

Incorrect — The thoracodorsal nerve innervates the latissimus dorsi muscle, which is involved in extension, adduction, and medial rotation of the arm.

**Buzzword Combo:** Difficulty performing rowing or climbing movements + weakened arm extension/adduction =thoracodorsal nerve injury
