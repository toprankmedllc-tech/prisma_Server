## Vignette

The quality improvement committee at a 450-bed academic medical center reviews its CLABSI (central line-associated bloodstream infection) surveillance data and discovers its rate in the medical-surgical ICU is 3.2 infections per 1,000 catheter-days — more than three times the CDC national benchmark of 0.8 per 1,000 catheter-days. The committee determines this represents a preventable patient safety problem with significant morbidity, mortality, and cost implications.  The team hypothesizes that consistent implementation of a standardized central line insertion bundle, comprising maximal sterile barrier precautions (cap, mask, sterile gown, sterile gloves, large sterile drape), chlorhexidine gluconate skin antisepsis, optimal catheter site selection (subclavian or jugular preferred over femoral), daily reassessment of line necessity, and catheter-hub scrubbing protocols, will reduce CLABSI rates.  Rather than immediately rolling out the protocol across all 8 hospital ICUs, the committee decides to pilot the bundle exclusively in the 20-bed medical-surgical ICU for 90 days, measuring CLABSI rates and bundle compliance weekly. At day 90, CLABSI rates in the pilot unit have decreased to 0.9 per 1,000 catheter-days. The committee reviews the compliance data, identifies that two bundle components had consistently low staff adherence rates, revises the training protocol and checklist to address those specific gaps, and then expands the modified bundle to all 8 ICUs hospital-wide with the improvements.  Which quality improvement framework best describes the methodology used by this committee?

## Key Symptoms

Problem identified (CLABSI 3× benchmark); hypothesis formed (bundle will reduce CLABSI); pilot in one unit 90 days; outcomes measured; protocol modified based on findings; expanded hospital-wide = Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle
