## Chief Complaint

Six weeks of progressive headache, transient bilateral visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, and horizontal diplopia

## Vignette

A 27-year-old woman with a body mass index of 38 kg/m² presents with 6 weeks of progressively worsening daily headaches that are worst in the morning and worsen when she lies flat or bends over. Over the past 2 weeks she has had several brief episodes of gray-out of vision in both eyes lasting only seconds, triggered by standing up quickly or coughing. She also reports a constant whooshing sound in her right ear that pulses with her heartbeat. She has been taking minocycline daily for 8 months for acne and resumed combined oral contraceptives 3 months ago. She is 4 weeks postpartum after an uncomplicated vaginal delivery. On examination she is obese. Fundoscopy reveals bilateral swelling of the optic discs with blurred margins and small flame-shaped peripapillary hemorrhages. On lateral gaze to the right, the right eye fails to abduct fully and she reports horizontal double vision. Strength, sensation, gait, and reflexes are intact. Visual acuity is 20/40 right and 20/30 left, with enlargement of the blind spots on confrontation testing. Noncontrast CT of the head shows no mass, hemorrhage, or hydrocephalus.

## Key Symptoms

Daily morning-predominant headache worsened by lying flat or Valsalva (raised ICP pattern); transient bilateral visual grayouts triggered by postural change or cough (transient visual obscurations); pulsatile tinnitus; horizontal diplopia from incomplete right eye abduction (sixth nerve palsy as a false localizing sign of raised ICP); bilateral papilledema with enlarged blind spots; obesity; minocycline use; postpartum status; combined oral contraceptive use

## Labs

CBC: WBC 8.2 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 12.6 g/dL, Plt 245 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 138 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L, Cr 0.7 mg/dL, glucose 86 mg/dL; Beta-hCG negative; Urinalysis unremarkable; PT, aPTT, and INR within normal limits.

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT head: no mass, hemorrhage, or hydrocephalus. MRI brain and MR venography NOT yet performed

## Physical Exam

BMI 38 (obese). Bilateral papilledema with blurred disc margins and peripapillary flame hemorrhages. Right eye fails to abduct fully on rightward gaze with horizontal diplopia. Visual acuity 20/40 right, 20/30 left; enlarged blind spots on confrontation. Strength, sensation, reflexes, gait all intact

## Main Clue

Classic clinical picture of raised ICP (positional headache, transient visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, bilateral papilledema, false-localizing sixth nerve palsy) in an obese woman of reproductive age. Diagnosis suspicion: idiopathic intracranial hypertension

## Supporting Clue

This patient has SIMULTANEOUS strong risk factors for CEREBRAL VENOUS SINUS THROMBOSIS (postpartum state, combined oral contraceptive use) in addition to IIH risk factors. CVT can mimic IIH precisely, and CT is insensitive for CVT.
