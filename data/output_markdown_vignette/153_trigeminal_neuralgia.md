## Chief Complaint

Three months of episodic, brief, electric-shock-like right-sided face pain triggered by light touch and chewing

## Vignette

A 32-year-old woman presents with 3 months of recurring brief, lancinating right-sided face pain. The pain shoots from her right cheek up into her temple and down into her jaw, lasts only 1 to 2 seconds at a time, and recurs in clusters of multiple stabs over a few minutes. It is reliably triggered by brushing her teeth, chewing food, or even a cold breeze on her face. Between attacks she is comfortable. Eighteen months ago she had a 3-week episode of painful loss of vision in her left eye that resolved spontaneously. She has had no other medical illnesses. On examination she is alert, with a normal mental status. Visual acuity is 20/30 in the left eye and 20/20 in the right; the left eye has a subtle relative afferent pupillary defect on the swinging-flashlight test. Eye movements show that on left lateral gaze, the right eye fails to adduct fully while the left eye displays horizontal jerking. Facial sensation to light touch and pinprick is intact in all three trigeminal divisions on both sides between attacks. Corneal reflexes are present bilaterally. Strength, coordination, and gait are normal. Lower-extremity reflexes are brisk and symmetric, with downgoing plantars.

## Key Symptoms

Brief lancinating right V2 and V3 distribution facial pain triggered by trigger zones (toothbrushing, chewing, cold breeze); preserved facial sensation between attacks; prior episode of painful monocular vision loss with residual subtle RAPD (prior optic neuritis); subtle internuclear ophthalmoplegia on left gaze (right eye fails to adduct, left eye horizontal nystagmus); preserved strength and gait

## Labs

CBC: WBC 6.4 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 13.6 g/dL, Plt 235 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 139 mEq/L, K 4.1 mEq/L, Cr 0.7 mg/dL, glucose 90 mg/dL; TSH 1.6 mIU/L; ANA negative; HbA1c 5.3%.

## Physical Exam

Alert with normal mental status; subtle left relative afferent pupillary defect; left lateral gaze shows incomplete right-eye adduction with horizontal jerking of the abducting left eye (internuclear ophthalmoplegia); facial sensation intact between attacks; corneal reflexes intact bilaterally; preserved strength, coordination, gait; brisk symmetric DTRs with downgoing plantars

## Main Clue

Classic trigeminal neuralgia in a YOUNG patient (32 years old) carries a high probability of UNDERLYING MULTIPLE SCLEROSIS, especially when accompanied by other CNS demyelinating features (here, a prior episode of painful monocular vision loss consistent with optic neuritis and subtle internuclear ophthalmoplegia on examination)

## Supporting Clue

Idiopathic trigeminal neuralgia typically presents in patients OVER 50 years old and is usually due to vascular loop compression of the trigeminal root at its pontine entry zone. Trigeminal neuralgia in a young patient is a red flag for an underlying structural cause, especially MS. Up to 5% of MS patients develop trigeminal neuralgia, and TN can be the presenting symptom of MS
