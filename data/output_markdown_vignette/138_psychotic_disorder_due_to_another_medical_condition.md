## Chief Complaint

Visual hallucinations and confusion for 3 days

## Vignette

A 67-year-old man with a history of poorly controlled type 2 diabetes and recently diagnosed small cell lung cancer is brought to the emergency department by his wife because he has been “seeing things” and talking to people who aren’t there for the past 3 days. She reports he has also been increasingly confused and forgetful. He has no prior psychiatric history. He denies hearing voices but confirms seeing “small animals” in the room. He takes metformin and was recently started on prednisone for cancer-related inflammation.

## Key Symptoms

Visual hallucinations (seeing small animals); confusion; disorientation; no prior psychiatric history; acute onset

## Labs

Sodium 128 mEq/L (low); glucose 310 mg/dL; cortisol elevated; calcium 11.8 mg/dL (elevated — paraneoplastic)

## Imaging Findings

Chest CT shows central lung mass consistent with small cell lung cancer; brain MRI — no metastases

## Physical Exam

Alert but disoriented to time and place; no focal neurological deficits; no tremor

## Main Clue

Visual hallucinations with acute onset in a patient with NO prior psychiatric history and a known medical condition (small cell lung cancer + hypercalcemia + hyponatremia)

## Supporting Clue

Recent prednisone use (steroid psychosis possible), but hypercalcemia from paraneoplastic syndrome is the stronger organic driver; hallucinations are visual, not auditory
