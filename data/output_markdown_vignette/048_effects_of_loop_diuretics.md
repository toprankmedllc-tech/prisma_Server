## Chief Complaint

Worsening leg swelling and shortness of breath

## Vignette

A 62-year-old man with a known history of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (EF 30%), hypertension, and gout presents with worsening bilateral leg swelling and shortness of breath over the past week. He reports needing two pillows to sleep at night and becomes short of breath walking from his bedroom to the bathroom. He also notes a painful swollen right big toe that began 4 days ago. He has been on a diuretic for his heart failure for several months. His blood pressure is 158/94 mmHg, heart rate is 92 bpm, oxygen saturation is 93% on room air, and temperature is 37.1°C. Labs reveal sodium 138 mEq/L, potassium 3.1 mEq/L, magnesium 0.7 mg/dL, creatinine 1.4 mg/dL, uric acid 9.2 mg/dL, and BUN 24 mg/dL. Chest X-ray shows bilateral pulmonary vascular congestion with mild pleural effusions. On physical exam, he has jugular venous distension, an S3 gallop, and bilateral basilar crackles. Which of the following electrolyte and metabolic disturbance combinations is most consistent with his current diuretic therapy?
