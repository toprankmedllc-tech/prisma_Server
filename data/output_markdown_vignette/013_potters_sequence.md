## Chief Complaint

Respiratory distress immediately after birth

## Vignette

newborn male infant is delivered at 38 weeks gestation to a 24-year-old woman in her first pregnancy. The mother had poor prenatal care, and the pregnancy was complicated by severe oligohydramnios detected at 24 weeks gestation. Immediately after birth, the infant develops severe respiratory distress with cyanosis and a weak cry. He also has decreased urine output after delivery.

Vital signs reveal a blood pressure of 58/32 mmHg, heart rate of 168/min, pulse oximetry of 78% on room air, and temperature of 36.7°C. Physical examination demonstrates intercostal retractions, flattened facies with a flattened nose, low-set ears, recessed chin, and bilateral clubfoot deformities. Laboratory studies show elevated serum creatinine and metabolic acidosis. Prenatal ultrasound had demonstrated severe oligohydramnios with absent visualization of both kidneys. Chest radiograph reveals markedly small lung volumes. Which of the following best explains the underlying cause of this infant’s respiratory failure?
