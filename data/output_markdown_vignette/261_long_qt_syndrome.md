## Chief Complaint

Collapse during a swimming competition with brief loss of consciousness

## Vignette

A 16-year-old girl is brought to the emergency department after collapsing during a swimming competition. She regained consciousness spontaneously after approximately 30 seconds. Her mother reports two similar episodes over the past 2 years, both preceded by emotional stress. There is a family history of sudden cardiac death — her maternal uncle died unexpectedly at age 24 while exercising. The patient takes no medications and has no significant past medical history. On examination, vital signs are stable and the cardiac examination is unremarkable. ECG performed in the emergency department demonstrates a corrected QT interval of 510 ms. Electrolyte studies are within normal limits.
Which of the following is the most likely underlying cause of this patient's condition?

## Key Symptoms

Exertion- and stress-triggered syncope; recurrent episodes over 2 years; family history of sudden cardiac death

## Labs

Electrolytes within normal limits

## Physical Exam

 Unremarkable cardiac examination; ECG with corrected QT interval of 510 ms

## Main Clue

Young patient + exertion/stress-triggered syncope + family history of SCD + prolonged QTc (510 ms) + normal electrolytes + no QT-prolonging medications

## Supporting Clue

Stress-triggered syncope (especially with swimming, exercise, emotional stress) is characteristic of congenital long QT syndrome, particularly LQT1 (KCNQ1)
