## Chief Complaint

Progressive dyspnea on exertion, bilateral ankle swelling, persistent dry cough

## Vignette

A 68-year-old woman with a long-standing history of poorly controlled hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus presents to the cardiology clinic with progressive dyspnea on exertion over the past 3 months, bilateral ankle swelling, and a persistent dry cough. She denies orthopnea initially, but on further questioning admits she now sleeps on 3 pillows. She has gained 8 kg over the past 6 weeks. On physical examination, her blood pressure is 158/96 mmHg, heart rate is 92 bpm, and respiratory rate is 20 breaths/min. Jugular venous pressure is elevated to 10 cm above the sternal angle. Lung auscultation reveals bilateral crackles at both bases. Cardiac exam reveals a laterally displaced point of maximal impulse (PMI) and an audible S3 gallop. There is 2+ pitting edema to the mid-calf bilaterally. An echocardiogram shows an ejection fraction of 32%.
Which of the following hemodynamic profiles is most consistent with this patient's presentation?

A) ↓ CO, ↑ PCWP, ↑ SVR, ↑ RAP
B) ↑ CO, ↓ PCWP, ↓ SVR, ↓ RAP
C) ↓ CO, ↓ PCWP, ↑ SVR, ↓ RAP
D) ↑ CO, ↑ PCWP, ↓ SVR, ↑ RAP
E) ↓ CO, ↑ PCWP, ↓ SVR, ↓ RAP

(CO = Cardiac Output; PCWP = Pulmonary Capillary Wedge Pressure; SVR = Systemic Vascular Resistance; RAP = Right Atrial Pressure)

## Physical Exam

Elevated JVP (10 cm above sternal angle); Bilateral basilar crackles; Laterally displaced PMI; S3 gallop; 2+ bilateral pitting edema to mid-calf

## Main Clue

EF of 32% (severely reduced) + S3 gallop + elevated JVP + orthopnea = Classic HFrEF with biventricular congestion

## Supporting Clue

Laterally displaced PMI (dilated LV); Bilateral crackles (pulmonary edema from elevated PCWP); Bilateral edema + elevated JVP (right heart congestion); Hypertension as underlying etiology
