## Vignette

A pharmaceutical company develops a new oral medication for treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. Initial studies demonstrate acceptable pharmacokinetics, safety, efficacy, and superiority to placebo. The medication is approved and becomes widely prescribed. Three years later, several reports describe a rare but serious cardiac arrhythmia occurring in patients taking the medication. This adverse effect was not identified during preapproval clinical trials. The investigators now establish a national surveillance program to monitor additional cases and better characterize the risk.

This surveillance program is most consistent with which phase of clinical testing?
