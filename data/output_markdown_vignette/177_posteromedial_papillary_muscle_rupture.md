## Chief Complaint

Sudden onset severe dyspnea, diaphoresis, and pink frothy sputum

## Vignette

A 68-year-old man is admitted to the coronary care unit after presenting to the emergency department with 3 hours of crushing substernal chest pain. ECG shows ST-segment elevations in leads II, III, and aVF, and he undergoes successful percutaneous coronary intervention of an occluded right coronary artery. Four days later, while recovering on the cardiology floor, he develops the sudden onset of severe dyspnea, diaphoresis, and pink frothy sputum. His blood pressure is 82/54 mm Hg, pulse is 122/min, and respirations are 32/min. Pulse oximetry shows an oxygen saturation of 84% on room air. On examination, he is in obvious respiratory distress with diffuse pulmonary crackles. Cardiac auscultation reveals a new holosystolic murmur best heard at the apex that radiates to the axilla.
