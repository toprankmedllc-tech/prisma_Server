## Chief Complaint

Sudden dizziness and difficulty swallowing

## Vignette

A 67-year-old man is brought to the emergency department due to sudden onset dizziness and difficulty swallowing that began 2 hours ago while eating breakfast. He also reports nausea, hoarseness, and trouble walking. His wife states that his speech suddenly became “raspy.” The patient has a history of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and smoking.

On examination, the patient is alert but appears uncomfortable and is vomiting intermittently. Temperature is 36.8°C (98.2°F), blood pressure is 178/96 mm Hg, pulse is 88/min, and respirations are 18/min. Neurologic examination shows decreased pain and temperature sensation over the left side of the face and right side of the body. The patient has left-sided ptosis and miosis. The uvula deviates to the right when he says “ahh,” and gag reflex is decreased. He has left-sided limb ataxia and horizontal nystagmus.

MRI of the brain demonstrates infarction of the left lateral medulla.

Damage to which of the following brainstem nuclei is most responsible for this patient’s dysphagia and hoarseness?

## Main Clue

Dysphagia and hoarseness in lateral medullary syndrome

## Supporting Clue

Loss of gag reflex and uvular deviation indicating CN IX/X nucleus involvement
