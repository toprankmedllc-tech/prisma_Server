## Vignette

A 45-year-old female with a recently diagnosed small cell lung cancer presents with a 3-day history of progressive confusion, nausea, and headache. She denies fever, vomiting, or focal neurological symptoms. She has no history of renal disease, liver disease, or heart failure, and she is not taking any diuretics or medications that affect sodium balance. Her blood pressure is 118/74 mmHg, heart rate is 82 bpm, pulse oximetry is 97% on room air, and temperature is 37.1°C. On physical examination she is alert but confused, mucous membranes are moist, there is no peripheral edema, and she appears euvolemic. Laboratory studies reveal serum sodium of 118 mEq/L, serum osmolality of 245 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality of 520 mOsm/kg, potassium of 4.1 mEq/L, bicarbonate of 22 mEq/L, pH of 7.40, and urine sodium of 55 mEq/L. Chest CT confirms a known perihilar mass. Which of the following best explains the mechanism of hyponatremia in this patient?
