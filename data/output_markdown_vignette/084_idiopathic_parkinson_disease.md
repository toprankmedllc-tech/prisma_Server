## Chief Complaint

Progressive slowness of movement and right-hand resting tremor over 1 year

## Vignette

A 67-year-old man is brought to clinic by his wife, who is concerned about changes over the past year. His movements have become slower; he has difficulty initiating walking, his steps are short, and his right arm no longer swings. He has trouble buttoning his shirt and his handwriting has become progressively smaller. His wife says his face looks less expressive and he speaks more softly. On examination, vital signs are normal and his blood pressure does not change with standing. He has a 4-Hz tremor of the right hand that occurs at rest and disappears when he reaches for an object. When the examiner moves his right wrist and elbow passively, there is a ratchet-like resistance throughout the range of motion. He is slow to stand from a chair, takes several hesitant steps to begin walking, and does not swing his right arm. His sense of smell is markedly diminished. He scores 28/30 on the Mini-Mental State Examination. Vertical and horizontal eye movements are full. He has no history of antipsychotic medication use.

## Key Symptoms

Asymmetric resting tremor (right hand); ratchet-like passive resistance (cogwheel rigidity); slow movement (bradykinesia); shuffling, hesitant gait with reduced arm swing; reduced facial expressivity; smaller handwriting; soft speech; hyposmia

## Labs

NA

## Physical Exam

Asymmetric 4-Hz resting tremor (right > left), disappears with intentional movement; cogwheel rigidity at right wrist and elbow; bradykinesia; hypomimia; soft hypophonic speech; shuffling gait with reduced right arm swing; intact vertical and horizontal eye movements; no orthostatic hypotension; MMSE 28/30

## Main Clue

Asymmetric resting tremor + cogwheel rigidity + bradykinesia + hyposmia + intact cognition + intact eye movements + no orthostatic hypotension = idiopathic Parkinson disease (not an atypical parkinsonism)

## Supporting Clue

The ABSENCE of early dementia (rules out Lewy body dementia), vertical gaze palsy or early falls (rules out PSP), orthostatic hypotension or cerebellar signs (rules out MSA), and antipsychotic exposure (rules out drug-induced parkinsonism)
