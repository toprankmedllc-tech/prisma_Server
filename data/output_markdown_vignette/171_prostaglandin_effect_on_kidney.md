## Vignette

A 34-year-old male with a history of rheumatoid arthritis presents to his primary care physician for a routine follow-up. He reports no acute complaints but mentions he has been taking ibuprofen 800 mg three times daily for the past eight months to manage his joint pain. He denies any gross hematuria, flank pain, nausea, or vomiting. He does not smoke, drink alcohol, or use illicit drugs. He takes no other medications.

His vitals are notable for a blood pressure of 118/76 mmHg, heart rate of 72 bpm, temperature of 37.0°C, and oxygen saturation of 98% on room air.

On physical exam, he is a well-appearing male in no acute distress. There is no costovertebral angle tenderness. His abdomen is soft, non-tender, and non-distended. No edema is noted in the bilateral lower extremities.

Labs are drawn and reveal a serum creatinine of 1.8 mg/dL, BUN of 24 mg/dL, and a normal plasma protein concentration. Urinalysis shows no casts, no proteinuria, no hematuria, and no signs of infection. His creatinine was 0.9 mg/dL on labs drawn one year ago prior to starting ibuprofen.

Which of the following best describes the expected changes in glomerular filtration rate, renal plasma flow, and filtration fraction in this patient?

## Key Symptoms

Chronic daily NSAID use; no gross hematuria; no flank pain; asymptomatic

## Labs


Creatinine: 1.8 mg/dL (elevated); BUN: 24 mg/dL; Plasma protein: normal; Urinalysis: no casts, no proteinuria

## Main Clue

Chronic NSAID use — NSAIDs block prostaglandin synthesis, removing prostaglandins’ preferential afferent arteriole dilation, leading to afferent arteriole constriction

## Supporting Clue

Elevated creatinine suggests decreased GFR; filtration fraction (FF = GFR/RPF) is unchanged because both GFR and RPF fall proportionally with afferent constriction

## Question

Which of the following best describes the expected changes in glomerular filtration rate, renal plasma flow, and filtration fraction in this patient?

## Correct Answer

B) ↓ GFR, ↓ RPF, unchanged FF
