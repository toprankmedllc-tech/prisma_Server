## Chief Complaint

## Vignette

A 16-year-old boy is admitted to the hospital for treatment of a rapidly enlarging abdominal mass. Biopsy shows a high-grade B-cell lymphoma, and combination chemotherapy is started. Two days later, he develops nausea, flank discomfort, and decreased urine output. He also reports intermittent muscle cramping. Temperature is 37.2°C, blood pressure is 112/70 mm Hg, pulse is 108/min, and oxygen saturation is 99% on room air. Physical examination shows mild diffuse abdominal tenderness without rebound or guarding. Laboratory studies show potassium of 6.3 mEq/L, phosphate of 7.8 mg/dL, calcium of 7.1 mg/dL, uric acid of 13.5 mg/dL, creatinine of 2.2 mg/dL, and blood urea nitrogen of 34 mg/dL. ECG shows peaked T waves.
