## Chief Complaint

15-year-old adolescent, assigned female at birth; supportive parent; no psychiatric comorbidities

## Vignette

A 15-year-old who was assigned female at birth presents with a parent to a psychiatrist. The patient requests to be referred to using “he/him” pronouns. He reports that since age 6, he has felt that he is a boy, has always preferred masculine clothing and activities, and has been deeply distressed by the development of breasts and the onset of menstruation at age 13. He describes his distress as severe, affecting his school attendance and friendships. He has no history of psychosis, trauma, or substance use. He is not depressed, though he acknowledges significant anxiety about his body. The parent is supportive. He explicitly states, “I am not confused — I know who I am.”

## Key Symptoms

Incongruence between gender identity and assigned sex since age 6; distress at secondary sex characteristics (breasts, menstruation); strong desire to be treated as male; significant functional impairment (school, friendships); no psychosis, depression, or confusion

## Physical Exam

Tanner stage IV breast development; otherwise unremarkable

## Main Clue

Longstanding (since age 6) gender incongruence + significant distress caused by biological sex characteristics = gender dysphoria

## Supporting Clue

Duration since early childhood (not adolescent-onset identity confusion), no psychosis, preserved insight, clear and consistent narrative — distinguishes gender dysphoria from adjustment/psychosis
