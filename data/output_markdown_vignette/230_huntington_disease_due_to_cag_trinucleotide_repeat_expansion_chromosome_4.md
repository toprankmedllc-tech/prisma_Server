## Chief Complaint

Progressive behavioral changes and involuntary movements

## Vignette

A 46-year-old man is brought to the neurology clinic by his wife due to progressive behavioral changes over the past 2 years. She reports that he has become increasingly irritable, impulsive, and socially inappropriate. He has also developed difficulty with memory and organizing tasks at work, which has led to job loss.

Over the past 6 months, she notices intermittent, “dance-like” involuntary movements of his limbs and face. The patient is unaware of these movements. He also has mild difficulty swallowing and occasional choking episodes.

Family history reveals that his father died in his 50s after a prolonged period of psychiatric illness and motor decline.

On examination, the patient has:

* Jerky, non-rhythmic involuntary movements
* Impaired rapid alternating movements
* Mild dysarthria
* Difficulty with short-term recall

MRI brain shows atrophy of the caudate nucleus with enlargement of the lateral ventricles.

Genetic testing confirms an expanded CAG repeat in the HTT gene on chromosome 4.
