## Chief Complaint

Severe dizziness; difficulty swallowing

## Vignette

A 68-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after the sudden onset of severe dizziness, difficulty swallowing, and a hoarse voice that began 2 hours ago. He also reports numbness over the right side of his face and the left side of his body. His medical history is significant for poorly controlled hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and a 40-pack-year smoking history. On examination, blood pressure is 168/96 mm Hg and pulse is 88/min and regular. He is alert and oriented. There is right-sided ptosis and miosis. The right palate fails to elevate on phonation and the gag reflex is diminished on the right. Pain and temperature sensation are decreased over the right side of the face and over the left arm, trunk, and leg, while fine touch, vibration, and proprioception are preserved throughout. Finger-to-nose testing shows dysmetria on the right. Motor strength is 5/5 in all extremities. Noncontrast CT of the head is normal.

## Main Clue

Crossed sensory loss (ipsilateral face + contralateral body, pain/temp only) + ipsilateral Horner + dysphagia/hoarseness + ipsilateral ataxia, with preserved motor function

## Supporting Clue

Older patient with multiple vascular risk factors (HTN, hyperlipidemia, smoking) and sudden onset: supports ischemic stroke in the posterior circulation
