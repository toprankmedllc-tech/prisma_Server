## Chief Complaint

7-week history of persistent sadness, anhedonia, hypersomnia, fatigue, weight gain, concentration difficulty, guilt, passive suicidal ideation

## Vignette

A 44-year-old woman presents to her primary care physician for a routine follow-up and tearfully reports that she "hasn't felt like herself" for the past 7 weeks. She describes a persistent, pervasive sadness that began shortly after her youngest child left for college and has not lifted since. She states that activities she previously found deeply fulfilling — cooking elaborate meals for her family, gardening, and her weekly book club — now feel "completely pointless." She has been sleeping 11–12 hours per night but still wakes up feeling exhausted, and she has gained approximately 9 pounds over the past 2 months despite having little appetite, which she attributes to "stress eating" when she wakes up in the middle of the night.  She reports that her concentration at work has deteriorated significantly — she is a paralegal and recently made several filing errors that her supervisor had to correct, which is highly uncharacteristic for her. She describes a pervasive sense of guilt, repeatedly stating that her children "deserved a better mother" and that she "wasted the best years of her life." When directly asked about suicidal ideation, she pauses, then says "I've thought that everyone would be better off without me, but I would never actually do anything." She has no prior psychiatric history. She takes no medications. She denies alcohol use beyond one glass of wine per week.  Her past medical history is notable for well-controlled hypothyroidism on levothyroxine. Physical examination is unremarkable. Laboratory workup including complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, and thyroid-stimulating hormone is ordered and returns entirely within normal limits (TSH 2.1 mIU/L).  Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line pharmacological treatment for this patient's condition?
