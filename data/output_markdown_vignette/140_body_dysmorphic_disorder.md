## Chief Complaint

Preoccupation with perceived facial scarring

## Vignette

A 22-year-old man is referred to a psychiatrist after his dermatologist declined to perform a fifth laser treatment on his face. The patient insists he has severe acne scarring and that his face is “disfigured,” though the dermatologist noted only minimal, barely perceptible scarring. The patient spends 3–4 hours per day examining his face in the mirror, avoids going outdoors, has withdrawn from friends, and recently lost his job because he refused to attend in-person meetings. He has visited four dermatologists in the past year. He denies depressed mood but acknowledges he feels “disgraceful” about his appearance. He is not underweight, and his beliefs about his appearance are not shared by anyone else who knows him.

## Key Symptoms

Preoccupation with minimal/imagined appearance defect; repetitive mirror-checking (3–4 hours/day); social withdrawal; occupational impairment; doctor-shopping for cosmetic procedures; insight is poor

## Labs

NA

## Physical Exam

Minimal acne scarring noted by dermatologist; no objective disfigurement; patient disagrees strongly with this assessment

## Main Clue

Preoccupation with a perceived (not real) physical defect causing significant functional impairment — classic body dysmorphic disorder (BDD)

## Supporting Clue

Repetitive behaviors (mirror checking, doctor-shopping) and poor insight; the defect is either absent or minimal to outside observers
