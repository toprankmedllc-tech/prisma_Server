## Chief Complaint

## Vignette

A 67-year-old man presents to the emergency department with worsening pain in his right foot for 3 weeks. He has a history of poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus, peripheral neuropathy, chronic kidney disease stage 3, and hypertension. He reports progressive swelling of the right foot associated with intermittent fevers and difficulty walking. He denies recent trauma. Medications include metformin, insulin glargine, lisinopril, and atorvastatin.
Physical examination reveals temperature 38.3°C (100.9°F), blood pressure 142/86 mmHg, pulse 102/min, and respiratory rate 18/min. Examination of the right foot demonstrates a 3-cm plantar ulcer over the metatarsal head with surrounding erythema, warmth, and mild purulent drainage. The ulcer probes to bone. Sensation to monofilament testing is diminished bilaterally.
Laboratory findings:
Test	Result
WBC	13,900/μL
ESR	88 mm/hr
CRP	Elevated
HbA1c	10.4%
Plain radiograph of the foot demonstrates cortical irregularity and periosteal reaction involving the underlying metatarsal bone. MRI reveals marrow edema and cortical destruction adjacent to the ulcer.
