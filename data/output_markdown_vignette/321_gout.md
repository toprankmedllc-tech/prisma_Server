## Chief Complaint

Joint pain

## Vignette

A 71-year-old man presents to the emergency department with sudden severe pain in his right foot that began overnight and woke him from sleep. He reports that the pain became so intense that he cannot tolerate bedsheets touching the area. He attended a family gathering the previous evening where he consumed large amounts of red meat and seafood.
Past medical history includes hypertension, obesity, stage III chronic kidney disease, hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance, and ischemic cardiomyopathy. Medications include hydrochlorothiazide, aspirin, atorvastatin, metoprolol, and lisinopril.
Vital signs are normal.
Physical examination demonstrates:
* Marked erythema, warmth, and swelling of the right first metatarsophalangeal joint
* Severe tenderness to minimal palpation
* Small chalky nodules over the helix of the ear and olecranon region
Laboratory findings:
Test	Result
ESR	elevated
CRP	elevated
WBC	mildly elevated
Serum uric acid	5.6 mg/dL (normal)
Creatinine	elevated
Synovial fluid aspiration demonstrates numerous inflammatory cells and needle-shaped crystals that appear yellow when aligned parallel to polarized light.
