## Vignette

A 62-year-old male with a history of hypertension and chronic kidney disease stage 3 presents for a routine clinic visit. He reports mild muscle weakness and occasional headaches but denies chest pain, edema, or visual changes. He was recently found to have a small unilateral adrenal adenoma on CT imaging obtained during workup for his hypertension. His current antihypertensive regimen includes amlodipine and hydrochlorothiazide. His blood pressure is 158/96 mmHg despite dual therapy, heart rate is 76 bpm, pulse oximetry is 98%, and temperature is 36.8°C. Physical examination reveals mild proximal muscle weakness bilaterally, no peripheral edema, and no Cushingoid features. Laboratory studies show sodium of 148 mEq/L, potassium of 2.9 mEq/L, bicarbonate of 30 mEq/L, pH of 7.48, plasma renin activity is low, plasma aldosterone is elevated, and urine potassium is high. Which of the following best describes the mechanism by which this patient’s adrenal adenoma is producing hypokalemia?
