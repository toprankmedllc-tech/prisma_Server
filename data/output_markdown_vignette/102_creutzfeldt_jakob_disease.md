## Chief Complaint

Six-week rapidly progressive dementia with startle-induced myoclonus and ataxia

## Vignette

A 64-year-old retired engineer is brought to clinic by his wife because of 6 weeks of rapidly progressive cognitive decline, paranoia, and social withdrawal. Over the past 2 weeks he has developed brief shock-like jerks of the limbs that are reliably triggered by sudden noises or unexpected touch, and his gait has become unsteady. He has no prior psychiatric history, has not started any new medications, and has not traveled recently. He is not immunocompromised. On examination, he is alert but disoriented and answers questions slowly. His Mini-Mental State Examination score is 12/30. He has frequent, brief, shock-like involuntary muscle jerks involving the limbs and trunk, reliably triggered by hand-clap; he has a wide-based, unsteady gait and bilateral dysmetria on finger to nose testing. Cranial nerves are intact and there is no focal weakness. He is afebrile. A panel testing for autoimmune and paraneoplastic encephalitis (anti-NMDA receptor, anti-LGI1, anti-CASPR2, anti-Hu, anti-Yo, anti-Ma) is reported as negative.

## Key Symptoms

Rapidly progressive cognitive decline over weeks; behavioral and psychiatric changes; brief shock-like involuntary muscle jerks triggered by startle (myoclonus); cerebellar ataxia; afebrile; negative autoimmune and paraneoplastic encephalitis workup

## Labs

CBC: WBC 7.2 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 14.2 g/dL, Plt 230 × 10⁹/; BMP: Na 140 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L; Cr 0.9 mg/dL, glucose 95 mg/dL; TSH 2.1 mIU/L (0.4 to 4.0); Vitamin B12 480 pg/mL (200 to 900); HIV antibody negative; RPR non-reactive; Autoimmune and paraneoplastic encephalitis panel (anti-NMDA, anti-LGI1, anti-CASPR2, anti-Hu, anti-Yo, anti-Ma) all negative; CSF: opening pressure 14 cm H₂O; WBC 2/µL; RBC 0/µL; protein 78 mg/dL (mildly elevated, normal less than 45); glucose 64 mg/dL (serum 95 mg/dL); 14-3-3 protein POSITIVE.

## Imaging Findings

MRI brain (DWI / FLAIR): symmetric hyperintensity along the cerebral cortex ("cortical ribboning") and in the bilateral caudate and putamen. EEG: periodic, biphasic and triphasic sharp wave complexes at approximately 1 Hz

## Physical Exam

Afebrile; disoriented; MMSE 12/30; frequent startle-induced myoclonic jerks; wide-based ataxic gait; bilateral dysmetria; intact cranial nerves; preserved strength and sensation

## Main Clue

Rapidly progressive dementia (weeks rather than months or years) plus startle-induced myoclonus plus cerebellar ataxia plus characteristic MRI (cortical ribboning + basal ganglia hyperintensity) plus EEG (1 Hz periodic sharp waves) plus positive CSF 14-3-3 protein. Highly specific pattern for sporadic Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease

## Supporting Clue

Comprehensive workup for autoimmune and paraneoplastic encephalitis is negative, the patient is afebrile (arguing against viral encephalitis), and B12, TSH, HIV, and RPR are all within normal limits, excluding the major treatable mimics of rapid dementia
