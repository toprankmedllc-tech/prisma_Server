## Chief Complaint

Back pain

## Vignette

A 72-year-old woman presents with progressively worsening mid-back pain and a loss of height over the last 2 years. She reports increasing thoracic kyphosis and states that her clothes “fit differently” because she feels shorter. She slipped while stepping off a curb 3 months ago and developed acute lower back pain afterward without head trauma or loss of consciousness.
Medical history is significant for long-standing Rheumatoid Arthritis treated intermittently with oral glucocorticoids, poorly controlled vitamin D deficiency, and menopause at age 44. Medications include prednisone, ibuprofen, and calcium supplements.
Physical examination shows thoracic kyphosis with tenderness over the lower thoracic vertebrae. Neurologic examination is normal.
Laboratory studies:
* Serum calcium: Normal
* Serum phosphate: Normal
* Alkaline phosphatase: Normal
* Parathyroid hormone: Normal
* ESR: Mildly elevated
A lateral spine radiograph is described as showing multiple anterior wedge compression fractures of the thoracic vertebrae. A DXA scan demonstrates markedly decreased bone mineral density.
Which of the following best explains the mechanism underlying this patient’s disease?
