## Chief Complaint

Active generalized convulsive seizure activity for greater than 25 minutes, unresponsive to two doses of IV lorazepam

## Vignette

A 24-year-old man with a known history of generalized epilepsy is brought to the emergency department by EMS in active convulsion. According to the paramedics, the patient has had continuous rhythmic jerking of all four extremities with loss of consciousness for 25 minutes. He was witnessed to bite his tongue and was incontinent of urine. The patient was last known to take his usual antiepileptic medication 2 days ago; his girlfriend says he ran out of pills. He received 4 mg of intravenous lorazepam in the ambulance and another 4 mg of intravenous lorazepam on arrival in the emergency department without termination of the seizure. The airway has been secured with endotracheal intubation and he is being mechanically ventilated. Despite both doses of lorazepam, the patient continues to demonstrate generalized convulsive activity.

## Key Symptoms

Continuous generalized tonic-clonic activity for more than 25 minutes; tongue biting; urinary incontinence; intubated and mechanically ventilated; persistent convulsive activity after two appropriately dosed IV benzodiazepines

## Labs

Fingerstick glucose 102 mg/dL; CBC: WBC 11.4 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 14.6 g/dL, Plt 232 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 135 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L, Cr 0.9 mg/dL, glucose 102 mg/dL, bicarbonate 18 mEq/L; CK 1,250 U/L; Urine and serum toxicology negative; Serum alcohol negative; Serum levels of his usual antiepileptic are subtherapeutic.

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT head: no hemorrhage, mass, or hydrocephalus (obtained after partial airway control). MRI brain pending after seizure termination

## Physical Exam

Intubated and ventilated. Persistent rhythmic generalized tonic-clonic limb movements. Pupils 4 mm and reactive bilaterally. Tachycardia, hypertension, low-grade fever. No focal neurologic asymmetry between limbs.

## Main Clue

Generalized convulsive status epilepticus persisting beyond 25 minutes despite two appropriately dosed IV benzodiazepines. The patient has progressed from initial first-line benzodiazepine therapy into the SECOND-LINE (urgent control) phase of the status epilepticus pathway

## Supporting Clue

The treatment phases for convulsive status epilepticus are well-defined and time-driven: (1) stabilization 0 to 5 min, (2) first-line benzodiazepine (IV lorazepam, IM midazolam, or IV diazepam) 5 to 20 min, (3) second-line urgent control IV antiepileptic 20 to 40 min, and (4) refractory anesthesia 40+ min. This patient has failed phase 2 and must be advanced to phase 3 with a second-line antiepileptic, not given a third dose of benzodiazepine
