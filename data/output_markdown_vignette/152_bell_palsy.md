## Chief Complaint

Acute unilateral facial weakness on awakening, with taste alteration and sound hypersensitivity

## Vignette

A 38-year-old previously healthy woman presents because she woke up this morning unable to fully move the right side of her face. She also reports that sounds in her right ear, including the running water in her shower, seem unusually loud and uncomfortable. Food has tasted bland over the front portion of the right side of her tongue since yesterday. She had a mild upper respiratory illness 10 days ago that has since resolved. She has no prior history of facial weakness or other neurologic symptoms. On examination she is alert. Vital signs are noted below. When asked to wrinkle her forehead, the right eyebrow does not rise. When asked to close her eyes tightly, her right eye does not close completely and the eyeball rolls upward and outward as she attempts to close it. She cannot smile symmetrically; the right corner of her mouth droops and the right nasolabial fold is flattened. Sensation over her face is intact to light touch and pinprick in all three trigeminal divisions. Hearing thresholds are equal in both ears. Extraocular movements, pupillary responses, and the rest of the cranial nerve examination are normal. Motor strength is 5/5 in all four extremities.

## Key Symptoms

Acute right-sided facial weakness involving the FOREHEAD (cannot wrinkle, cannot close the right eye fully); upward and outward rolling of the eyeball with attempted closure (Bell phenomenon); flattened right nasolabial fold; intolerance for loud sounds in the right ear (suggesting paralysis of the nerve to stapedius); decreased taste over the anterior part of the right side of the tongue (suggesting involvement of the chorda tympani branch); preserved facial sensation in all trigeminal divisions; preserved hearing thresholds; recent viral prodrome

## Labs

CBC: WBC 6.8 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 13.4 g/dL, Plt 240 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 139 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L, Cr 0.8 mg/dL, glucose 92 mg/dL; HbA1c 5.4%; HIV antibody negative

## Physical Exam

Acute right-sided lower motor neuron facial weakness with FOREHEAD involvement (cannot wrinkle right brow, cannot fully close right eye, eyeball rolls superolaterally on attempted closure); flattened right nasolabial fold; decreased taste over anterior right hemitongue; subjective hyperacusis in the right ear with normal pure tone hearing; intact trigeminal sensation; otherwise normal cranial nerves, strength, and sensation

## Main Clue

Acute unilateral facial weakness WITH FOREHEAD INVOLVEMENT (the hallmark distinguishing peripheral CN VII palsy from central facial palsy) accompanied by loss of taste on the anterior two-thirds of the tongue and hypersensitivity to loud sounds on the SAME side. This combination implies a peripheral CN VII lesion PROXIMAL to where the chorda tympani and nerve-to-stapedius branches arise, which localizes the lesion to the geniculate ganglion within the facial canal of the temporal bone

## Supporting Clue

Forehead muscles receive BILATERAL cortical input via corticobulbar fibers, so central (UMN) lesions SPARE the forehead. A lesion that affects forehead muscles must therefore be peripheral (LMN). The branching pattern of CN VII allows finer localization: hyperacusis indicates a lesion proximal to the nerve to stapedius, and taste loss indicates a lesion proximal to the chorda tympani. Both branches arise within the facial canal, so the lesion is at or just before the geniculate ganglion, the classic site of Bell palsy
