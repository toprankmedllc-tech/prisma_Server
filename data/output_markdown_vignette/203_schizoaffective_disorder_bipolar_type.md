## Chief Complaint

Manic episode with concurrent psychotic features; psychosis persisting after mood resolves

## Vignette

A 31-year-old woman is admitted to the inpatient psychiatry unit after her husband called emergency services when he found her standing on their roof at 2 AM declaring that she had been "chosen by celestial forces" to deliver a special message to humanity. Her husband reports that over the past 5 weeks she has barely slept — sometimes only 1–2 hours per night — while remaining extraordinarily energetic, has been spending money recklessly on strangers, speaks so rapidly that he cannot understand her, and has been making grandiose plans to launch a global spiritual movement. He also reports that for approximately 3 weeks of this episode she began insisting that strangers on the street were receiving her thoughts through a wireless transmission, and that she could hear a divine voice narrating her actions throughout the day.  On mental status examination, the patient is hyperverbal with pressured speech, tangential thought process, expansive and labile affect, and markedly elevated mood. She endorses auditory hallucinations (one voice narrating her actions) and thought broadcasting. She denies depressive symptoms and denies substance use; urine toxicology is negative.  The treatment team initiates appropriate pharmacological management and the patient's mood improves significantly over the following 4 weeks. However, despite full resolution of her manic symptoms, she continues to endorse auditory hallucinations and paranoid delusions for an additional 7 weeks, during which she has no perceptible mood disturbance.  Thyroid function tests, complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, MRI brain, and urine toxicology are all within normal limits or negative.  Which of the following diagnoses is most consistent with the longitudinal course of this patient's illness?


## Key Symptoms

Manic episode ×5 weeks: decreased sleep, grandiosity, pressured speech, reckless spending, elevated mood; concurrent psychosis: auditory hallucinations (narrating voice) + thought broadcasting; psychosis persisting 7 weeks after full mood resolution; no depressive episode; negative toxicology; normal medical workup

## Labs

## Imaging Findings

## Physical Exam

Hyperverbal; pressured speech; expansive then later euthymic affect; thought broadcasting on initial exam; auditory hallucinations persist after mood normalizes; no focal neurological deficits

## Main Clue

Psychotic symptoms (hallucinations + delusions) persisting ≥2 weeks in complete absence of mood symptoms — this is the defining criterion separating schizoaffective disorder from bipolar I with psychotic features

## Supporting Clue

Major mood episode (mania) was present for majority of the illness; psychosis did not resolve with mood treatment; 7-week persistence of psychosis post-mood resolution exceeds 2-week threshold
