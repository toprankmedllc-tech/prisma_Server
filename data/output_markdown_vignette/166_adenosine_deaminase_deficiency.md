## Chief Complaint

Persistent cough

## Vignette

A 6-month-old boy is brought to the emergency department because of persistent cough, poor feeding, and watery diarrhea for 1 week. He has had two prior hospitalizations for pneumonia and recently completed treatment for oral thrush. He was born at term after an uncomplicated pregnancy. His immunizations were delayed because of repeated infections. There is no family history of immunodeficiency. Temperature is 38.4°C, blood pressure is 82/50 mm Hg, pulse is 156/min, and oxygen saturation is 93% on room air. Physical examination shows white plaques on the tongue and buccal mucosa, diffuse crackles over both lung fields, and poor weight gain. Laboratory studies show leukocytes of 4,200/mm³, absolute lymphocyte count of 420/mm³, CD3+ T cells of 95/mm³, IgG of 145 mg/dL, IgA of 8 mg/dL, and IgM of 12 mg/dL. Chest x-ray shows bilateral interstitial infiltrates and a small thymic shadow. Biochemical testing of lymphocytes shows accumulation of deoxyadenosine and dATP.
