## Chief Complaint

Recurrent syncopal episodes provoked by neck stimulation

## Vignette

A 78-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after a syncopal episode that occurred while he was buttoning a tight shirt collar. He recovered consciousness within 30 seconds without confusion. He has had two similar episodes over the past 4 months, both occurring while shaving or turning his head sharply. His past medical history is significant for hypertension and hyperlipidemia. On examination, his blood pressure is 132/78 mm Hg and pulse is 72/min. Cardiac and neurologic examinations are unremarkable. 
Which of the following best explains this patient's syncope?

## Key Symptoms

Sudden syncope without postictal confusion; episodes triggered by buttoning a tight shirt collar, shaving, or sharp head turning; three episodes over 4 months

## Main Clue

Elderly patient with recurrent syncope provoked by mechanical neck stimulation + reproducible bradycardia and hypotension on carotid sinus massage (>3-second pause and/or >50 mm Hg SBP drop)

## Supporting Clue


The provoking triggers (tight collar, shaving, head turning) all mechanically stretch the carotid sinus, producing an exaggerated baroreceptor reflex with both cardioinhibitory (vagal) and vasodepressor (sympathetic withdrawal) components
