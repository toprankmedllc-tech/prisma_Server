## Chief Complaint

Fatigue and increased urinary frequency for the past three weeks

## Vignette



A 19-year-old male with no significant past medical history presents to the emergency department with a three-week history of fatigue, increased urinary frequency, and excessive thirst. He also reports an unintentional weight loss of approximately 5 kilograms over the past two months. He denies dysuria, flank pain, fever, or chills. He takes no medications, does not smoke, and denies alcohol or illicit drug use.

His vitals are notable for a blood pressure of 104/68 mmHg, heart rate of 98 bpm, temperature of 37.1°C, and oxygen saturation of 98% on room air.

On physical exam he appears fatigued and mildly ill. His mucous membranes are dry and skin turgor is decreased. Mild diffuse abdominal tenderness is noted on palpation without rebound or guarding. There is no costovertebral angle tenderness.

Labs are drawn and reveal a fasting plasma glucose of 524 mg/dL, serum bicarbonate of 14 mEq/L, and arterial pH of 7.28, consistent with a metabolic acidosis with an elevated anion gap. His serum creatinine is 1.1 mg/dL. Urinalysis reveals 3+ glucosuria and 2+ ketonuria with no proteinuria and no signs of infection.

Which of the following most accurately describes the status of this patient’s renal glucose handling at his current plasma glucose level?

## Main Clue

Plasma glucose of 524 mg/dL far exceeds the renal threshold of approximately 200 mg/dL AND the transport maximum of 375 mg/min, meaning both the threshold and Tm have been surpassed

## Supporting Clue

Ketonuria and metabolic acidosis indicate type 1 diabetes with diabetic ketoacidosis; glucose transport maximum is saturated and all excess filtered glucose is excreted
