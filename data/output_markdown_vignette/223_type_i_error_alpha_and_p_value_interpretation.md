## Vignette

A team of cardiologists at a university hospital designs a double-blind placebo-controlled randomized controlled trial to evaluate whether a new beta-blocker reduces 30-day all-cause mortality following acute myocardial infarction (AMI) compared to standard care alone. The investigators pre-specify a two-sided significance level (alpha) of 0.05 and a minimum clinically important difference of 3 percentage points in 30-day mortality. They enroll 800 patients and randomize them 1:1 to receive the new beta-blocker plus standard care or placebo plus standard care.  After the trial is complete, the statistical analysis reveals that the 30-day mortality was 9.2% in the treatment group and 11.8% in the placebo group (p = 0.038). The investigators conclude that the new beta-blocker significantly reduces 30-day mortality following AMI and submit their results for publication.  An independent biostatistician reviewing the manuscript for the journal notes an important concern: based on the pre-trial evidence base and physiological data, she believes the new beta-blocker has no true effect on 30-day AMI mortality  it is identical to placebo in the actual population. If she is correct, a statistical error has been made in this study.  Which of the following best identifies the type of error that has occurred and quantifies its probability?

## Key Symptoms

Alpha set at 0.05; p = 0.038 (< alpha → H0 rejected); null hypothesis is actually true (no real effect); rejecting a true H0 = Type I error; probability of Type I error = alpha = 5%

## Main Clue

H0 is actually true + investigators rejected H0 (p < alpha) = Type I error (false positive); probability = alpha = 5%, NOT the p-value


## Supporting Clue

p-value is the probability of observing results as extreme as these given H0 is true  it is NOT the probability of error; alpha is the pre-specified Type I error rate
