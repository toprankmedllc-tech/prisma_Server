## Vignette

A 72-year-old man presents to the emergency department with decreased urine output over the past several days. His past medical history is significant for hypertension, hyperlipidemia, a 50 pack-year smoking history, and known severe atherosclerotic disease of the aorta. His surgical history is notable for percutaneous coronary intervention with coronary angiography 3 weeks ago for stable angina. On examination, his blood pressure is 158/92 mm Hg, pulse is 88/min, and temperature is 37.4°C (99.3°F). His toes are dusky blue with intact distal pulses, and a lacy, reticular purplish rash is noted on both calves. Laboratory studies are significant for a creatinine of 3.1 mg/dL (baseline 1.1 mg/dL).
Which of the following best explains the underlying mechanism of this patient's condition?

## Key Symptoms

Progressive bluish discoloration of the toes; netlike purplish rash on lower extremities (livedo reticularis); decreased urine output; recent PCI with coronary angiography

## Labs


Creatinine 3.1 mg/dL

## Physical Exam

Dusky blue toes with intact distal pulses (blue toe syndrome); lacy reticular purplish rash on both calves (livedo reticularis)

## Main Clue

Elderly atherosclerotic patient + recent PCI with coronary angiography (3 weeks prior) + blue toes with intact distal pulses + livedo reticularis + acute kidney injury

## Supporting Clue


The 3-week interval between the invasive vascular procedure and onset of symptoms distinguishes this syndrome from contrast-induced nephropathy, which occurs within 24–72 hours
