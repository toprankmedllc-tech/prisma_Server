## Chief Complaint

Follow-up after starting anticoagulation for DVT

## Vignette

A 67-year-old man comes to the clinic for follow-up after starting treatment for a deep venous thrombosis diagnosed 5 days ago. He was initially treated with an injectable anticoagulant and was also started on an oral medication intended for long-term anticoagulation. He has a history of hypertension and nonvalvular atrial fibrillation. He has no active bleeding. Laboratory studies show a prothrombin time of 26 seconds and an INR of 2.6. The physician explains that the injectable medication can be discontinued once the oral medication has reached a therapeutic effect.

## Key Symptoms

Recent DVT; need for long-term anticoagulation; no active bleeding

## Labs

Prothrombin time 26 seconds; INR 2.6; PT increased; therapeutic anticoagulation effect

## Main Clue

Oral long-term anticoagulant with increased PT/INR after several days of therapy.

## Supporting Clue

Initial injectable anticoagulant use suggests heparin bridging during early warfarin therapy.
