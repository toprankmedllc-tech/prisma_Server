## Chief Complaint

Fever and migratory joint pain

## Vignette

A 9-year-old boy is brought to the pediatrician with fever and joint pain. Three weeks ago he had untreated strep throat. His joint pain started in his right knee, then moved to his left ankle, and is now in his right wrist. Cardiac auscultation reveals a new holosystolic murmur at the apex. He has painless nodules over both elbows and a pink, ring-shaped rash with central clearing on his trunk. Laboratory studies show a positive ASO titer and elevated ESR.
Which of the following best explains the underlying mechanism of this patient's condition?

## Key Symptoms


Fever; migratory polyarthritis (right knee → left ankle → right wrist); recent untreated strep throat 3 weeks prior; painless nodules over elbows; pink ring-shaped rash with central clearing on the trunk

## Labs

Positive anti-streptolysin O (ASO) titer; elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)

## Physical Exam

New holosystolic murmur at the apex (mitral regurgitation); painless subcutaneous nodules over both elbows; erythema marginatum on the trunk

## Main Clue

Child 2–4 weeks post–untreated strep + migratory polyarthritis + new apical holosystolic murmur + subcutaneous nodules + erythema marginatum + positive ASO titer

## Supporting Clue

Multiple major Jones criteria fulfilled simultaneously with documented evidence of preceding group A streptococcal infection
