## Chief Complaint

Progressive symmetric ascending weakness over 5 days

## Vignette

A 28-year-old previously healthy man presents to the emergency department because of 5 days of progressively worsening weakness in both legs that started in his feet and is now affecting his thighs and hands. He cannot climb stairs and is now having trouble holding a cup. Two weeks ago he had three days of crampy abdominal pain and bloody diarrhea after a backyard cookout, which resolved on its own. He denies fever, headache, neck pain, or back pain. On examination, he is alert and anxious. Vital signs are as listed below. He is taking shallow breaths and using his neck muscles to breathe. Strength is 3/5 symmetrically in the lower extremities and 4/5 symmetrically in the upper extremities. Light touch and pinprick sensation are subjectively normal. Deep tendon reflexes cannot be elicited at the ankles, knees, biceps, or triceps. Cranial nerve examination is normal. Bedside spirometry shows a forced vital capacity of 50% of predicted.

## Key Symptoms

Symmetric weakness beginning in the feet and ascending to the thighs and hands; preserved sensation; absent deep tendon reflexes throughout; respiratory muscle weakness; antecedent gastrointestinal infection 2 weeks earlier

## Labs

CBC and basic metabolic panel within normal limits; CK normal

## Physical Exam

Alert, in respiratory distress; using accessory muscles; symmetric flaccid weakness 3/5 lower and 4/5 upper extremities; sensation grossly intact; deep tendon reflexes absent throughout; cranial nerves intact; FVC 50% of predicted

## Main Clue

Subacute, symmetric, ascending flaccid weakness with areflexia and preserved sensation after a recent diarrheal illness, pattern of acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy

## Supporting Clue

Bloody diarrhea 2 weeks prior is the classic trigger (Campylobacter jejuni), whose lipopolysaccharide shares epitopes with peripheral nerve gangliosides (molecular mimicry)
