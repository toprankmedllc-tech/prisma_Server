## Vignette

A 67-year-old man presents with progressive proximal muscle pain and weakness for 6 weeks. He reports difficulty rising from a chair, climbing stairs, and lifting grocery bags overhead. Three months ago, he suffered a non–ST elevation myocardial infarction and was started on intensive lipid-lowering therapy. Medical history is significant for type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, stage 3 chronic kidney disease, and hypothyroidism treated inconsistently. He denies rash, sensory deficits, fever, or recent trauma.
Physical examination demonstrates symmetric proximal muscle weakness involving the hip and shoulder girdles. Deep tendon reflexes are preserved. Sensation is intact. No heliotrope rash, Gottron papules, or muscle tenderness is present.
Laboratory findings:
* Creatine kinase (CK): markedly elevated
* ESR: normal
* TSH: mildly elevated
* AST/ALT: mildly elevated
Urinalysis: positive for blood on dipstick with few erythrocytes on microscopy.
Muscle biopsy is described as showing myofiber necrosis without substantial inflammatory infiltrates.
Which of the following best explains the mechanism responsible for this patient’s symptoms?
