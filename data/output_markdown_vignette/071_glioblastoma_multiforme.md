## Chief Complaint

Generalized tonic-clonic seizure

## Vignette

A 62-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after a witnessed generalized tonic-clonic seizure at work. His wife reports that over the past 8 weeks he has had progressively worsening headaches that are worst in the morning, occasional non-bilious vomiting, and increasing difficulty finding words. He has no known prior medical conditions. On examination, he is alert but slow to respond. There is a mild right central facial droop and a right pronator drift. Visual fields are full to confrontation and there is no papilledema. MRI of the brain with gadolinium shows a heterogeneously enhancing mass centered in the left frontal lobe that extends across the corpus callosum into the right frontal lobe, with central areas of necrosis, peripheral ring enhancement, and surrounding vasogenic edema. Stereotactic biopsy reveals marked nuclear atypia, frequent mitoses, microvascular proliferation, and zones of necrosis surrounded by densely packed, palisading tumor nuclei. Tumor cells stain strongly positive for glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP).
