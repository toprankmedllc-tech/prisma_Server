## Chief Complaint

Three weeks of clustered sudden flexion spasms and loss of previously achieved developmental milestones

## Vignette

An 18-month-old girl is brought to clinic by her mother for evaluation of new episodes that have been occurring multiple times daily for the past 3 weeks. The mother describes clusters of 5 to 10 sudden, brief jerks in which the infant flings her arms outward and flexes her trunk, often when waking from a nap. The infant had been crawling and beginning to walk and babble but has lost the ability to roll over and is no longer babbling. On examination, head circumference is at the 30th percentile (was 50th at 12 months). There are at least 6 oval, sharply demarcated hypopigmented areas of skin scattered over the trunk and limbs, more obvious when the skin is examined under an ultraviolet (Wood) lamp. There is also a 4 cm thickened, leathery, dimpled patch of skin overlying the lower back. The mother has small reddish papules clustered on her cheeks and around the nose. EEG shows chaotic, high-amplitude, disorganized slow waves with multifocal independent spikes between brief electrodecremental responses corresponding to each cluster of spasms. Brain MRI shows multiple cortical and subcortical T2 hyperintense lesions, calcified nodules along the ventricular surfaces, and no obstructive hydrocephalus. Echocardiogram shows a 1.2 cm well-circumscribed mass within the left ventricular wall.

## Key Symptoms

Clusters of sudden, brief, symmetric flexor spasms (infantile spasms); developmental regression; multiple hypopigmented oval skin macules visible under Wood lamp (ash-leaf spots); thickened leathery skin patch over the lower back (shagreen patch); maternal facial papules (angiofibromas); EEG with disorganized chaotic background and multifocal independent spikes (hypsarrhythmia); cortical and subcortical lesions plus calcified subependymal nodules on MRI; intracardiac mass on echocardiogram (rhabdomyoma)

## Labs

CBC: WBC 9.2 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 11.6 g/dL, Plt 280 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 138 mEq/L, K 4.4 mEq/L, Cr 0.3 mg/dL, glucose 96 mg/dL; LFTs within normal limits; Urinalysis unremarkable.

## Imaging Findings

Brain MRI: multiple cortical and subcortical T2 hyperintense lesions, calcified subependymal nodules along the ventricular surfaces; no SEGA or hydrocephalus. Echocardiogram: 1.2 cm well-circumscribed intramural left ventricular mass

## Physical Exam

Alert but with reduced age-appropriate engagement; head circumference at 30th percentile (dropped from 50th); at least 6 oval hypopigmented skin macules on trunk and limbs visible under Wood lamp; 4 cm raised patch over the lower back; witnessed cluster of brief flexor spasms during the visit

## Main Clue

Infant with infantile spasms and developmental regression PLUS multiple ash-leaf hypopigmented macules PLUS shagreen patch PLUS cortical tubers and subependymal calcified nodules PLUS cardiac rhabdomyoma PLUS autosomal-dominant family history (mother with angiofibromas). This is tuberous sclerosis complex

## Supporting Clue

For infantile spasms associated with TSC, VIGABATRIN (an irreversible inhibitor of GABA transaminase that raises CNS GABA) is the FIRST-LINE antiepileptic and is uniquely effective. For non-TSC (idiopathic or cryptogenic) infantile spasms, ACTH or oral corticosteroids are first-line.
