## Chief Complaint

Recurrent skin infections and persistent cough

## Vignette

A 5-year-old boy is brought to the clinic because of recurrent skin infections and persistent cough. Since infancy, he has required several courses of antibiotics for draining skin abscesses and cervical lymphadenitis. Six months ago, he was hospitalized for pneumonia complicated by a liver abscess. His parents report that a maternal uncle died during childhood after repeated severe infections. Physical examination shows several healed scars on the lower extremities and tender right cervical lymphadenopathy. Temperature is 38.4°C, heart rate is 118/min, blood pressure is 96/58 mm Hg, and pulse oximetry is 96% on room air. Laboratory studies show leukocytes 17,600/mm³, absolute neutrophil count 12,400/mm³, IgG 910 mg/dL, IgA 120 mg/dL, and IgM 95 mg/dL. Culture from a draining lymph node grows Staphylococcus aureus. Dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry shows absent fluorescence after neutrophil stimulation.
