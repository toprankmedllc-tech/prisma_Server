## Chief Complaint

Confusion, vomiting, and yellowish-green visual disturbance following ingestion of grandfather's cardiac pills

## Vignette

A 28-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department after her roommate found her confused and vomiting repeatedly at home. The roommate noticed several empty medication bottles on the nightstand belonging to the woman's grandfather, who has chronic heart failure and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation. The patient is lethargic but responds to voice. She reports persistent nausea and says that everything around her appears yellowish-green. Temperature is 36.9°C, heart rate is 44/min, and blood pressure is 94/60 mmHg. Abdominal examination reveals diffuse tenderness without peritoneal signs. ECG shows first-degree AV block, scooped ST-segment changes in the lateral leads, and multiple premature ventricular complexes.
Which of the following electrolyte abnormalities is most likely present?

A) Decreased serum sodium 
B) Decreased serum potassium 
C) Increased serum calcium 
D) Increased serum potassium 
E) Decreased serum magnesium


## Key Symptoms

Lethargy; Nausea and vomiting; Xanthopsia (yellow-green visual tint); Bradycardia; Diffuse abdominal tenderness

## Physical Exam

Lethargic but arousable; Diffuse abdominal tenderness; No peritoneal signs; ECG: First-degree AV block; Scooped lateral ST-segment changes; Multiple PVCs

## Main Clue

Grandfather's medications for HF and AF + bradycardia + PR prolongation + xanthopsia + GI symptoms 

## Supporting Clue

Yellow-green visual disturbance (xanthopsia) is a hallmark of digoxin toxicity; scooped ST changes are a recognized ECG effect of digoxin; PVCs reflect enhanced automaticity from intracellular calcium excess; empty pill bottles support acute ingestion
