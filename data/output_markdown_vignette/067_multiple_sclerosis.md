## Chief Complaint

New-onset double vision

## Vignette

A 28-year-old woman presents to the neurology clinic for evaluation of new-onset double vision. She reports that 18 months ago she developed painful blurred vision in her right eye over a few days, which resolved spontaneously over 6 weeks. Approximately 6 months ago she had a 3-week episode of numbness and tingling in her right leg, which also resolved on its own. Over the past week, she has noticed horizontal double vision, particularly when looking to the left. On examination, visual acuity is 20/40 on the right and 20/20 on the left, with a right relative afferent pupillary defect. On left lateral gaze, the right eye fails to adduct past the midline while the left eye displays horizontal nystagmus; convergence is preserved. Deep tendon reflexes are 3+ in both lower extremities. MRI of the brain with gadolinium shows multiple ovoid T2 hyperintense lesions in the periventricular white matter and corpus callosum, some of which enhance and some of which do not. Lumbar puncture shows oligoclonal bands in the CSF.

## Main Clue

Young woman with relapsing-remitting neurologic episodes separated in space and time + INO + optic neuritis + periventricular MRI lesions + CSF oligoclonal bands

## Supporting Clue

Simultaneously enhancing and non-enhancing MRI lesions = dissemination in time on a single scan (McDonald criteria)
