## Chief Complaint

Progressive depression of consciousness after head trauma with fixed dilated pupil and hemiparesis, both ipsilateral to a large supratentorial subdural hematoma

## Vignette

A 58-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after being struck on the left side of the head by falling debris at a construction site 4 hours ago. He was awake at the scene and answered questions appropriately for the first hour, then progressively became drowsy and is now unresponsive. On arrival, his GCS is 6 (E1V1M4). Vital signs include rising blood pressure with falling pulse rate. Noncontrast CT of the head shows a 2.5 cm thick acute left subdural hematoma along the convexity of the left cerebral hemisphere, with 14 mm of midline shift to the right and effacement of the left perimesencephalic cistern. On examination, the left pupil measures 7 mm and does not constrict to bright light shone in either eye, while the right pupil is 3 mm and reactive. When the examiner applies painful sternal pressure, his right arm and leg withdraw briskly, but the left arm and leg do not move at all (0/5 strength on the left). Plantar response is upgoing on the left and flexor on the right. 

## Key Symptoms

Lucid interval followed by progressive depression of consciousness; left pupil fixed and dilated (CN III compression ipsilateral to mass); left-sided hemiparesis with upgoing plantar; rising blood pressure with falling heart rate (Cushing response); CT showing left subdural hematoma with marked midline shift and obliteration of the left perimesencephalic cistern 

## Labs

CBC: WBC 12.2 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 14.0 g/dL, Plt 230 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 140 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L, Cr 0.9 mg/dL, glucose 142 mg/dL; PT, aPTT, INR within normal limits; Blood ethanol 0.08 g/dL; Urine drug screen negative.

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT head: 2.5 cm thick acute hyperdense crescent-shaped collection along the left cerebral convexity (acute subdural hematoma); 14 mm of midline shift to the right; effacement of the left perimesencephalic cistern with herniation of the left uncus medially through the tentorial notch; the midbrain is displaced to the right and the contralateral cerebral peduncle is being pressed against the firm edge of the right tentorium

## Physical Exam

GCS 6 (E1V1M4); left pupil 7 mm fixed and unreactive; right pupil 3 mm reactive; complete left hemiparesis (0/5) with upgoing plantar on the left; intact withdrawal on the right

## Main Clue

The patient has a left-sided supratentorial mass causing transtentorial UNCAL HERNIATION (medial temporal lobe pushed through the tentorial notch), with ipsilateral CN III compression (left fixed dilated pupil). The hemiparesis is on the left (ipsilateral to the mass) rather than on the right (contralateral, as would be predicted from compression of the ipsilateral cerebral peduncle)

## Supporting Clue

When a large supratentorial mass pushes the brainstem far enough sideways, the contralteral (right) cerebral peduncle can be compressed against the firm tentorial edge on the opposite side. Because the corticospinal tract decussates at the medulla, compression of the right cerebral peduncle produces left-sided weakness (which is ipsilateral to the mass). This is the Kernohan notch phenomenon, a classic false localizing sign that misleads clinicians who anchor on the rule that hemiparesis is contralateral to the mass

## Correct Answer

C) Compression of the contralateral cerebral peduncle against the opposite tentorial edge as the brainstem is pushed sideways
