## Chief Complaint

Sudden onset of the worst headache of her life with brief loss of consciousness

## Vignette

A 47-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department 2 hours after the sudden onset of an extraordinarily severe headache that began while she was lifting heavy grocery bags. She tells the physician it is the most severe headache she has ever experienced and that it reached peak intensity within a minute. She had a brief episode of unresponsiveness lasting about 30 seconds at the onset, followed by nausea and one episode of vomiting. She finds the overhead fluorescent lights of the room intensely uncomfortable. Her medical history is significant for long-standing hypertension, and an abdominal imaging study 5 years ago revealed multiple bilateral kidney cysts. Her mother died unexpectedly at age 51 of what the family described as "a burst blood vessel in the head." On examination, she is alert but in obvious distress, holding her head in her hands. Vital signs are noted below. When the examiner attempts to flex her neck forward toward her chest, she resists movement and reports sharp pain. Pupils are 3 mm and equally reactive to light; the optic discs appear sharp and pink with no swelling. Cranial nerve examination is otherwise unremarkable, and there is full strength and intact sensation in all extremities. Noncontrast CT of the head shows no acute hemorrhage, mass effect, or midline shift

## Key Symptoms

Sudden "thunderclap" headache reaching peak within 1 minute; brief loss of consciousness at onset; nausea and vomiting; photophobia; meningismus (resists neck flexion); no focal neurologic deficit

## Labs

NA (CBC, basic metabolic panel, coagulation studies pending and unremarkable)

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT of the head: no acute intracranial hemorrhage, no mass effect, no midline shift, ventricles normal in size

## Physical Exam

Alert, oriented, in obvious distress; resists passive neck flexion with reproducible pain (meningismus); pupils equal and reactive; no papilledema; cranial nerves II-XII intact; motor 5/5 throughout; sensation intact; reflexes 2+ symmetric; no Babinski sign

## Main Clue

Sudden "worst headache of life" peaking in seconds-to-minutes + brief LOC + meningismus + risk profile (ADPKD, family history of cerebral aneurysm rupture) — strongly suggests subarachnoid hemorrhage even though noncontrast CT is negative

## Supporting Clue

Noncontrast CT sensitivity for SAH falls from ~98% within the first 6 hours to ~85% by 24 hours; a negative CT does NOT rule out SAH when clinical suspicion is high — the workup must continue
