## Chief Complaint

Recurrent episodes of palpitations with abrupt onset and spontaneous resolution

## Vignette

A 22-year-old man presents to clinic for evaluation of recurrent episodes of palpitations that begin abruptly and resolve spontaneously after several minutes. He has had three such episodes over the past 6 months, each accompanied by mild lightheadedness. He has no significant past medical history and takes no medications. On examination, his vital signs are within normal limits, and cardiac auscultation is unremarkable. A baseline 12-lead ECG performed in clinic shows a short PR interval, a slurred upstroke at the beginning of the QRS complex, and a widened QRS.
Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Key Symptoms

Recurrent abrupt-onset palpitations; spontaneous resolution after several minutes; associated mild lightheadedness; three episodes over 6 months

NA

## Physical Exam

Unremarkable cardiac auscultation; 12-lead ECG with short PR interval, slurred upstroke of the QRS complex (delta wave), and widened QRS

## Main Clue

Young patient with recurrent paroxysmal palpitations + ECG showing short PR + delta wave + wide QRS

## Supporting Clue


Abrupt onset/offset of palpitations is characteristic of a re-entrant tachycardia involving an accessory pathway
