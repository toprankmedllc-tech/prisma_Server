## Chief Complaint

Joints Pain and stiffness 

## Vignette

A 48-year-old woman presents with progressively worsening pain and stiffness of the hands over the past 11 months. She reports morning stiffness lasting approximately 50 minutes that improves with activity. Over the last several months, she has also developed swelling of several fingers and intermittent heel pain that worsens when walking.
Her past medical history is significant for obesity, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome. Family history is notable for autoimmune disease in her father.
Physical examination reveals:
* Scaly erythematous plaques over the extensor elbows and scalp
* Nail pitting and distal nail separation
* Fusiform swelling involving the entire third digit of the left hand
* Tenderness at the Achilles tendon insertion site
* Swelling and tenderness of the distal interphalangeal joints bilaterally
Laboratory findings:
Test	Result
ESR	elevated
CRP	elevated
Rheumatoid factor	negative
Anti-CCP antibody	negative
Uric acid	normal
Radiograph of the hand demonstrates:
Irregular erosions with narrowing of joint spaces and deformity in which the distal phalanx appears tapered and seated within an eroded proximal segment.
Which of the following best explains the pathophysiologic basis of this patient’s disease?
