## Chief Complaint

18 months of involuntary jerky movements, personality change, and cognitive decline

## Vignette

A 42-year-old man is brought to clinic by his wife because of 18 months of progressively worsening involuntary movements and personality changes. She reports irregular, brief, rapid jerky movements of his hands, face, and trunk that he cannot suppress and that he sometimes incorporates into purposeful actions to make them look intentional. He has become increasingly irritable and apathetic, has lost his job because of careless errors, and has been treated for new-onset major depression. His father developed similar movements and behavioral changes at age 56 and died from complications of pneumonia at age 64. On examination, he is alert and oriented but has reduced verbal fluency. He has continuous, irregular, non-rhythmic involuntary movements of his hands and face. Muscle tone is reduced. Strength and sensation are normal; deep tendon reflexes are brisk and plantar responses are flexor. He scores 24/30 on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment with deficits in executive function. Routine labs including TSH, B12, ceruloplasmin, and HIV antibody are normal. Genetic testing is pending.

## Key Symptoms

Irregular, brief, rapid involuntary movements of limbs and face (chorea); irritability and apathy; major depression; executive dysfunction; reduced muscle tone; brisk reflexes; positive autosomal-dominant family history; symptom onset 14 years earlier than the father

## Labs

TSH, B12, ceruloplasmin, HIV antibody, RPR all normal; CBC and metabolic panel normal

## Physical Exam

Continuous irregular involuntary movements of hands and face; reduced verbal fluency; hypotonia; brisk DTRs with flexor plantars; preserved strength and sensation; MoCA 24/30 with executive dysfunction

## Main Clue

Midlife onset of chorea + behavioral/cognitive changes + autosomal-dominant family history with earlier onset in the affected son than the father: anticipation in a trinucleotide repeat disorder, consistent with Huntington disease

## Supporting Clue

Normal ceruloplasmin and HIV testing exclude Wilson disease and HIV-related movement disorder; the absence of recent streptococcal infection or skin findings argues against Sydenham chorea
