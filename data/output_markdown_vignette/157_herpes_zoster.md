## Chief Complaint

Five days of unilateral painful dermatomal vesicular rash on the right trunk that does not cross the midline

## Vignette

A 68-year-old man presents because of 5 days of progressively worsening painful rash on his right chest. He first noticed burning, tingling, and exquisite sensitivity to clothing across a strip of skin on his right back and chest, followed 2 days later by clustered fluid-filled blisters in the same area. The rash forms a band that wraps from the middle of his back around the right side of his trunk to the midline of the front of his chest, stopping abruptly at the midline. He recalls having had chickenpox at age 6. He has type 2 diabetes mellitus and was recently treated with high-dose corticosteroids for a flare of polymyalgia rheumatica. On examination he is afebrile and in moderate distress from pain. The skin of his right upper back, lateral chest wall, and right anterior chest, in a band that ends precisely at the spinal and sternal midlines, shows clustered umbilicated vesicles on an erythematous base, some of which have crusted over. There is marked allodynia (pain to light touch) over the involved area. There is no rash in any other dermatome and no rash on the face, especially no vesicles on the tip of the nose. The remainder of the examination is normal.

## Key Symptoms

Burning, tingling, and allodynic prodrome preceding the rash by 2 days; unilateral dermatomal vesicular eruption (right T4 or T5 dermatome) that DOES NOT cross the midline; umbilicated vesicles on an erythematous base with some crusting; recent immunosuppression (corticosteroids) as the trigger for reactivation; remote history of primary varicella (chickenpox) at age 6

## Labs

CBC: WBC 6.6 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 13.2 g/dL, Plt 220 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 138 mEq/L, K 4.2 mEq/L, Cr 1.0 mg/dL, glucose 168 mg/dL; HbA1c 7.4%; HIV antibody negative

## Physical Exam

Afebrile, in pain. Right hemithorax: clustered umbilicated vesicles on an erythematous base in a unilateral T4 or T5 dermatomal distribution, NOT crossing the midline; marked allodynia within the band; no vesicles elsewhere; no tip-of-nose vesicles (Hutchinson sign absent); CN exam intact; no facial palsy or ear vesicles

## Main Clue

Unilateral painful dermatomal vesicular rash that does not cross the midline in an older immunocompromised adult with a remote history of chickenpox is the classic presentation of herpes zoster (varicella-zoster virus reactivation). Reactivated VZV travels antidromically from a dorsal root ganglion down the sensory nerve to produce the vesicular eruption in the corresponding dermatome

## Supporting Clue

Bedside cytology of fluid scraped from the base of an unroofed vesicle (Tzanck smear) is a rapid, inexpensive supportive test that cannot distinguish VZV from herpes simplex virus but is highly suggestive of a herpesvirus etiology. PCR of vesicle fluid is the most sensitive and specific confirmatory test and can distinguish VZV from HSV
