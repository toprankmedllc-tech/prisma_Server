## Chief Complaint

Hemarthrosis after trivial trauma; recurrent joint bleeds; disproportionate bruising; prolonged bleeding after dental extraction

## Vignette

A 4-year-old boy is brought to the emergency department by his parents after falling off a step stool onto his right knee. Despite the minor mechanism, the knee is now markedly swollen, warm, and exquisitely tender, with flexion limited to 30 degrees due to pain and tense effusion. X-ray shows a large joint effusion without fracture. His mother reports that since he began walking at 14 months, he has had multiple episodes of disproportionately large bruising from minor bumps, two previous episodes of swollen painful joints following trivial trauma, and a prolonged 4-hour gum bleeding episode following a dental extraction at age 3. His birth history includes a significant cephalohematoma requiring close monitoring.  His maternal grandfather has "a blood problem" and was advised to avoid aspirin and NSAIDs. There is no paternal family history of bleeding disorders.  Laboratory evaluation: platelet count 240,000/μL (normal); peripheral smear with normal platelet morphology; prothrombin time (PT) 12.0 seconds (normal); activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) 82 seconds (reference 25–35 seconds; markedly prolonged). A 1:1 mixing study is performed — patient plasma mixed with normal pooled plasma, and the aPTT corrects fully to 34 seconds (within normal range).  The pediatric hematologist discusses the findings with the family and orders specific clotting factor assays.  Which of the following clotting factor deficiencies most likely underlies this patient's bleeding phenotype?

## Key Symptoms

Hemarthrosis (hallmark of hemophilia); disproportionate bruising; prolonged post-extraction bleeding; cephalohematoma at birth; maternal grandfather bleeding disorder (X-linked pedigree — not paternal); platelets normal; PT normal; aPTT 82 sec (prolonged, intrinsic pathway); aPTT CORRECTS on mixing (= factor deficiency, NOT inhibitor)

## Main Clue

Prolonged aPTT + corrects on mixing (= factor deficiency not inhibitor) + normal PT + normal platelets + hemarthrosis + X-linked family history (maternal grandfather) = Hemophilia A (Factor VIII deficiency); ~80% of hemophilias; intrinsic pathway defect

## Supporting Clue

Hemarthrosis = hallmark of hemophilia A/B (NOT VWD which causes mucosal bleeding); aPTT correction = factor deficiency (acquired inhibitor would NOT correct on mixing); X-linked recessive: carrier mothers, affected males, maternal grandfather affected confirms X-linked pedigree; Hemophilia A (80%) >> B (20%) by prevalence
