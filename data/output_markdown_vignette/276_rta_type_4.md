## Vignette

A 58-year-old male with a 15-year history of type 2 diabetes mellitus and stage 3 chronic kidney disease presents to his primary care physician for a routine follow-up visit. He reports progressive fatigue and mild generalized weakness over the past several weeks but denies chest pain, palpitations, shortness of breath, nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea. He has had no recent changes in his diet and denies any new medications. His current medications include metformin and lisinopril, which he has been taking for several years. He does not smoke or drink alcohol.

His vitals are notable for a blood pressure of 148/90 mmHg, heart rate of 78 bpm, temperature of 37.1°C, and oxygen saturation of 97% on room air.

On physical exam, he is a well-appearing male in no acute distress. There is mild decreased muscle strength bilaterally in the lower extremities. No peripheral edema is noted. His abdomen is soft, non-tender, and non-distended. There is no costovertebral angle tenderness. No Kussmaul breathing is observed.

Routine labs are drawn and reveal the following: serum sodium 139 mEq/L, serum potassium 6.1 mEq/L, serum bicarbonate 18 mEq/L, serum chloride 112 mEq/L, BUN 32 mg/dL, creatinine 1.9 mg/dL, and serum glucose 118 mg/dL. Arterial blood gas shows a pH of 7.31 and a pCO2 of 38 mmHg. Urine pH is measured at 5.2. Plasma renin activity is low and plasma aldosterone level is also low. His creatinine one year ago was 1.1 mg/dL.

Which of the following best explains the acid-base and electrolyte abnormalities seen in this patient?

## Key Symptoms

Fatigue; mild generalized weakness; no chest pain; no palpitations; no diarrhea; no vomiting; well-controlled diabetes on metformin

## Labs

## Physical Exam

No edema; no Kussmaul breathing; mild decrease in muscle strength bilaterally in lower extremities; no flank tenderness

## Main Clue

Low renin, low aldosterone, hyperkalemia, and normal anion gap metabolic acidosis in a diabetic patient with CKD — classic Type 4 RTA (hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism)

## Supporting Clue

Urine pH of 5.2 confirms the kidney CAN acidify urine appropriately, distinguishing this from Type 1 RTA where the distal tubule cannot lower urine pH below 5.5

## Question

Which of the following best explains the acid-base and electrolyte abnormalities seen in this patient?

## Correct Answer

B) Decreased aldosterone activity leading to impaired H+ and K+ secretion in the collecting duct despite intact ability to acidify urine
