## Chief Complaint

Prenatal diagnosis of trisomy 21; classic Down syndrome phenotype confirmed on examination

## Vignette

: A 36-year-old woman, gravida 2 para 1, presents at 16 weeks gestation for prenatal counseling. Maternal serum quad screen results show: decreased alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), elevated human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), elevated inhibin A, and decreased unconjugated estriol (uE3)  yielding a calculated trisomy 21 risk of 1 in 85. She elects to proceed with diagnostic amniocentesis, which reveals a 47,XX,+21 karyotype. She is counseled and decides to continue the pregnancy.  The infant is delivered at 38 weeks gestation. On newborn examination, the neonatologist notes: birth weight 2.7 kg; upslanting palpebral fissures with epicanthal folds; flat facial profile with depressed nasal bridge; low-set small ears with simplified helices; protruding tongue with small oral cavity; abundant nuchal skinfolds; a single transverse palmar crease on the right hand; bilateral widened gap between the first and second toes (sandal gap deformity); and generalized hypotonia with a high-pitched weak cry. On cardiac auscultation, a harsh holosystolic murmur is heard at the left lower sternal border with a palpable thrill. Echocardiography on day 1 of life reveals a complete atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD) with a large primum atrial septal defect and a large inlet ventricular septal defect, consistent with endocardial cushion maldevelopment.  The neonatologist counsels the parents and arranges subspecialty referrals to pediatric cardiology, ophthalmology, audiology, and developmental pediatrics.  Which of the following cytogenetic mechanisms most commonly underlies the karyotype found in this infant?

## Physical Exam

## Main Clue

47,XX,+21 (free trisomy) = meiotic nondisjunction accounts for ~95% of Down syndrome; risk increases exponentially with maternal age due to oocyte arrest in prophase I for decades; advanced maternal age (36 years) = major risk factor

## Supporting Clue

Complete AVSD = classic cardiac defect in Down syndrome (~40%); quad screen classic for trisomy 21; Robertsonian translocation (3-4%) not age-related; mosaic trisomy (1-2%) = milder phenotype with two cell lines
