## Chief Complaint

Return of exertional chest pain after initial improvement

## Vignette

A 63-year-old man with stable coronary artery disease and hypercholesterolemia returns for a 3-month follow-up. When first diagnosed, he experienced substernal pressure after climbing a single flight of stairs. Following initiation of a new cardiac regimen, his symptoms resolved completely. Over the past 5 weeks, his exertional chest pain has gradually returned. He denies missing doses, dietary changes, or new stressors. Current medications are daily aspirin, rosuvastatin, bisoprolol, and isosorbide mononitrate four times daily. Heart rate is 54/min and blood pressure is 122/78 mmHg. Repeat exercise stress testing shows onset of chest pain at 13 minutes, compared to 27 minutes at his last visit.
Which of the following is most appropriate to address this patient's recurrent symptoms?

A) Refer for coronary angiography 
B) Increase bisoprolol dose
C) Avoid isosorbide mononitrate at bedtime 
D) Add daily amlodipine 
E) Switch from isosorbide mononitrate to isosorbide dinitrate
