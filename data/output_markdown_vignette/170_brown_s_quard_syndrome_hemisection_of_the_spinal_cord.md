## Chief Complaint

Weakness and numbness after stab injury

## Vignette

A 29-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after being stabbed in the back during an assault. The knife entered the right side of his thoracic spine. Following the injury, he is unable to move his right leg properly and complains of numbness.

On examination, strength is 1/5 in the right lower extremity and 5/5 in the left lower extremity. Vibration sense and proprioception are absent in the right leg below the umbilicus. Pain and temperature sensation are absent in the left leg beginning several segments below the lesion. Reflexes are initially decreased in the right leg but become brisk several days later with an upgoing plantar reflex.

MRI demonstrates hemisection of the right spinal cord at T10.

Which of the following best explains the pattern of sensory loss seen in this patient?

## Key Symptoms

Ipsilateral leg weakness ; ipsilateral loss of vibration/proprioception ; contralateral loss of pain/temperature ; hyperreflexia ; Babinski sign

## Physical Exam

Right lower extremity weakness ; ipsilateral loss of vibration/proprioception below lesion ; contralateral pain and temperature loss beginning 1–2 levels below lesion ; hyperreflexia ; positive Babinski sign

## Main Clue

Ipsilateral motor/vibration loss with contralateral pain and temperature loss

## Supporting Clue

Penetrating spinal cord hemisection injury
