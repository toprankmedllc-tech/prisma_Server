## Chief Complaint

 Severe headache, blurred vision, and nausea

## Vignette

A 52-year-old man presents to the emergency department with a severe headache, blurred vision, and nausea that began earlier in the day. He has a long history of poorly controlled hypertension and has been intermittently noncompliant with his antihypertensive medications. On examination, his blood pressure is 232/138 mm Hg in both arms, pulse is 96/min, and respirations are 20/min. He is awake but appears uncomfortable. Funduscopic examination reveals flame-shaped hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, and papilledema. Cardiac and pulmonary examinations are unremarkable. Laboratory studies show a creatinine of 2.4 mg/dL (baseline 1.0 mg/dL) and urinalysis with proteinuria and red blood cell casts.
Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line therapy?

## Main Clue


Severely elevated BP (232/138) + hypertensive retinopathy with papilledema + acute kidney injury with proteinuria and RBC casts

## Supporting Clue


End-organ damage involving brain (encephalopathic symptoms), retina (papilledema), and kidneys (AKI with active urinary sediment) defines this as a hypertensive emergency requiring IV antihypertensive therapy
