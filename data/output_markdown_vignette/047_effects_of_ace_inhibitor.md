## Vignette

A 48-year-old woman who is 16 weeks pregnant presents for a follow-up appointment regarding blood pressure management. She has a known history of chronic hypertension and was recently found to have bilateral renal artery stenosis on Doppler ultrasound, which demonstrated high-resistance waveforms in both renal arteries. She was started on an antihypertensive medication approximately 6 weeks ago. Today she reports intermittent headaches and mild swelling in her legs but denies visual changes, chest pain, or decreased fetal movement. Her blood pressure is 162/100 mmHg, heart rate is 84 bpm, oxygen saturation is 99%, and temperature is 36.9°C. Laboratory results show creatinine of 1.8 mg/dL, up from her baseline of 1.1 mg/dL three months ago, potassium of 5.6 mEq/L, BUN of 28 mg/dL, and 2+ proteinuria on urine dipstick. An abdominal bruit is audible on auscultation. Fundal height is appropriate for gestational age. Which of the following best explains the acute rise in this patient’s creatinine and represents the most dangerous aspect of her current antihypertensive regimen?

## Main Clue

Pregnant patient with bilateral renal artery stenosis started on an ACE inhibitor — now presenting with rising creatinine and hyperkalemia

## Supporting Clue

ACE inhibitors are teratogens AND are contraindicated in bilateral renal artery stenosis because they further reduce GFR → renal failure; ARBs and Aliskiren share the same contraindications in pregnancy
