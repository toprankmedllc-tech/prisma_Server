## Chief Complaint

Pre-participation screening → aortic root dilation and MVP; marfanoid habitus with ectopia lentis; family history of sudden death during sport

## Vignette

A 17-year-old male competitive basketball player is referred to cardiology after his school athletic trainer noted an abnormal finding on pre-participation physical examination. The trainer has also observed that the patient requires custom orthotics for bilateral pes planus and has always been the tallest player on every team he has joined. The patient's father, described as "very tall and thin with a bony chest," died suddenly at age 36 while playing recreational tennis; no autopsy was performed.  On physical examination: height 193 cm (6'4"), weight 71 kg (BMI 19.1); arm span 204 cm (arm span exceeds height by 11 cm); upper-to-lower segment ratio 0.82 (reduced, reflecting long lower extremities); positive thumb sign (thumb protrudes past ulnar border of closed fist) and positive wrist sign (thumb and fifth finger overlap when wrapped around opposite wrist), both indicating arachnodactyly; pectus carinatum bilaterally; bilateral pes planus; palms flat on floor with full knee extension (marked ligamentous hyperlaxity). Slit-lamp biomicroscopy reveals bilateral upward and temporal dislocation of the crystalline lens (ectopia lentis, superotemporal). Cardiac auscultation reveals a midsystolic click followed by a late systolic murmur loudest at the apex, and a soft early diastolic murmur at the left sternal border. Echocardiography confirms mitral valve prolapse and aortic root dilation with a Z-score of +4.2 at the sinuses of Valsalva.  The cardiologist explains to the patient and his mother that genetic testing will be performed and that all first-degree relatives should be evaluated.  What is the most likely inheritance pattern and causative gene mutation for this patient's condition?

## Main Clue

: FBN1 gene (fibrillin-1, chromosome 15q21.1) → Marfan syndrome → autosomal dominant; father's sudden death at 36 during sport = likely aortic dissection = affected parent → vertical (father-to-son) transmission = confirms AD; rules out X-linked (no father-to-son X transmission) and mitochondrial (maternal only)

## Supporting Clue

Superotemporal/upward ectopia lentis = Marfan (vs inferonasal/downward in homocystinuria); MVP + aortic root dilation = classic cardiovascular; AD = 50% per offspring; management: beta-blocker/losartan, serial aortic imaging, avoid contact sports
