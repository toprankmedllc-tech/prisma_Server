## Vignette

A 26-year-old woman who is 28 weeks pregnant presents to her obstetrician for a routine prenatal visit. She is G2P1 with an uncomplicated obstetric history and reports no complaints today. She denies polyuria, polydipsia, blurry vision, fatigue, or excessive thirst. She has no personal or family history of diabetes mellitus. She takes only prenatal vitamins and has no known drug allergies.
Her vitals are notable for a blood pressure of 112/70 mmHg, heart rate of 78 bpm, temperature of 36.8°C, and oxygen saturation of 99% on room air. On physical exam she is a well-appearing woman in no acute distress. Her uterus is gravid and consistent with 28 weeks of gestation. There is no costovertebral angle tenderness bilaterally. No peripheral edema is noted. Routine prenatal labs are drawn. Her fasting plasma glucose returns at 88 mg/dL and her HbA1c is 5.3%, both within normal limits. A one-hour glucose challenge test is also within normal range. Her urinalysis, however, reveals 2+ glucosuria with no ketonuria, no proteinuria, and no signs of urinary tract infection. Her serum creatinine is 0.7 mg/dL. Which of the following best explains the mechanism responsible for this patient’s glucosuria?

## Key Symptoms

No polyuria; no polydipsia; no blurry vision; no fatigue; asymptomatic

## Labs

Fasting plasma glucose: 88 mg/dL (normal); Urinalysis: 2+ glucosuria; HbA1c: 5.3% (normal); No ketonuria; No proteinuria

## Physical Exam

Gravid uterus consistent with 28 weeks; no costovertebral angle tenderness; no edema

## Main Clue

Glucosuria in the setting of a completely normal fasting plasma glucose and normal HbA1c in a pregnant patient

## Supporting Clue

Normal pregnancy is associated with increased GFR, which increases filtration of all substances including glucose, causing the renal threshold for glucosuria to occur at lower plasma glucose concentrations

## Question

Which of the following best explains the mechanism responsible for this patient’s glucosuria?

## Correct Answer

B) Increased GFR during pregnancy resulting in filtered glucose load exceeding transporter reabsorptive capacity at normal plasma glucose levels
