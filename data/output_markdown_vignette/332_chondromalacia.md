## Chief Complaint

## Vignette

A 26-year-old woman presents with progressive anterior knee pain for 8 months. She recently began training for a half marathon after several years of sedentary lifestyle. Medical history is significant for obesity (BMI 33 kg/m²) and poorly controlled hypothyroidism treated intermittently with levothyroxine. She reports worsening pain when climbing stairs, squatting, or standing after prolonged sitting at work. She denies fever, trauma, or morning stiffness.

Physical examination shows mild genu valgum and tenderness along the posterior surface of the patella. Passive flexion and extension of the knee while compressing the patella against the femoral condyles reproduces pain and a grinding sensation. There is no erythema, joint warmth, or significant effusion. Range of motion is preserved.

A sagittal MRI of the knee is described as showing focal softening and fissuring of the articular cartilage on the posterior patellar surface without meniscal injury or ligament disruption.

Which of the following best explains the pathophysiology of this patient’s condition?
