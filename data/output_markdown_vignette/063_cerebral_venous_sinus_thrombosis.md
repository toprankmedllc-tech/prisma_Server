## Chief Complaint

Severe headache and seizure

## Vignette

A 29-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department because of worsening headache and confusion for 3 days. The headache began gradually shortly after delivery of her first child 2 weeks ago and is now severe and diffuse. Earlier today, she had a generalized tonic-clonic seizure lasting approximately 1 minute followed by confusion and lethargy. She has no history of migraines or seizures. She does not smoke or drink alcohol.

On examination, she appears drowsy but arousable. Funduscopic examination reveals bilateral papilledema. Neurologic examination shows mild weakness of the right upper extremity and expressive aphasia. Neck stiffness is absent.

Laboratory studies are unremarkable. Noncontrast CT scan of the head demonstrates a small hemorrhagic infarct in the left frontal lobe. MRI with venography shows absence of flow in a large venous channel located between the periosteal and meningeal layers of the dura mater near the superior aspect of the brain.

Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?
