## Vignette

A 39-year-old woman comes to the clinic requesting contraception. She recently became sexually active after a divorce and wants a method that also helps regulate her menstrual cycles. The patient reports a history of migraine headaches associated with flashing lights and blurry vision prior to headache onset. She smokes 1 pack of cigarettes daily and has done so for the past 20 years. Medical history is otherwise significant for hypertension controlled with medication.

She asks about starting a daily pill containing ethinyl estradiol and a progestin. The physician explains that estrogen-containing contraceptives suppress ovulation by inhibiting pituitary gonadotropin release, while the progestin component thickens cervical mucus and limits endometrial proliferation.

Which of the following is the strongest contraindication to prescribing this patient combined hormonal contraception?
