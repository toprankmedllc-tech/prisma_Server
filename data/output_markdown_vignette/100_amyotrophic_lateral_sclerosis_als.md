## Chief Complaint

10 months of progressive weakness with new bulbar symptoms (slurred speech, choking)

## Vignette

A 58-year-old man presents with 10 months of progressive weakness. He first noticed difficulty turning a key and buttoning his shirt; over the past 4 months he has developed slurred speech and choking on thin liquids, and his wife has noted twitching in his arm and shoulder muscles. He denies pain, numbness, or any change in bladder or bowel function. His past medical history is unremarkable. On examination, he is alert and oriented. His speech is slurred with reduced volume. The tongue shows visible twitching of small muscle bundles even at rest, and there is loss of tongue bulk on both sides. There is wasting of the small muscles of both hands with continuous twitching of muscle fascicles. Upper extremity strength is 3/5 distally and 4/5 proximally; lower extremity strength is 4+/5 throughout. Deep tendon reflexes are 4+ at the patella and Achilles bilaterally, and plantar responses are upgoing. Pinprick, light touch, vibration, and joint position sense are intact throughout. Anal sphincter tone is normal. MRI of the cervical spine is pending.

## Key Symptoms

Progressive distal hand weakness; visible fasciculations of tongue and limb muscles; tongue atrophy; muscle wasting of intrinsic hand muscles; brisk reflexes with upgoing plantars in lower extremities; preserved sensation; preserved sphincter function; bulbar symptoms (dysarthria, dysphagia)

## Labs

CBC: WBC 6.8 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 14.1 g/dL, Plt 245 × 10⁹/L. BMP: Na 139 mEq/L, K 4.2 mEq/L, Cr 0.9 mg/dL, glucose 94 mg/dL. TSH 1.7 mIU/L (0.4-4.0). Vitamin B12 510 pg/mL (200-900). CK 380 U/L. RPR non-reactive. HIV antibody negative.

## Physical Exam

Alert; dysarthric speech; tongue atrophy with fasciculations at rest; bilateral intrinsic hand muscle wasting with continuous fasciculations; upper extremity strength 3-4/5; lower extremity strength 4+/5 with hyperreflexia (4+ patellar, 4+ Achilles) and bilateral upgoing plantars; preserved sensation; intact sphincter tone

## Main Clue

COEXISTING upper motor neuron signs (hyperreflexia, upgoing plantars) and lower motor neuron signs (atrophy, fasciculations, weakness) in MULTIPLE body regions including the bulbar region (tongue atrophy with fasciculations), with completely preserved sensation and sphincter function, pattern of motor neuron disease (ALS)

## Supporting Clue

Tongue atrophy with fasciculations localizes pathology ABOVE the cervical spinal cord, a finding that CANNOT be explained by cervical spinal cord compression and is one of the most specific distinguishers of ALS from cervical spondylotic myelopathy

## Question

Which examination finding most strongly argues against a diagnosis of cervical spondylotic myelopathy and supports amyotrophic lateral sclerosis in this patient?

## Correct Answer

A) Tongue atrophy with visible fasciculations at rest
