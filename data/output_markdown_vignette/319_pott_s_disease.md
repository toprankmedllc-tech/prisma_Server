## Chief Complaint

Back pain

## Vignette

A 73-year-old man presents with progressively worsening mid-back pain for 5 months. The pain is dull, persistent, and worsens at night. He also reports fatigue, intermittent low-grade fever, drenching night sweats, and an unintentional 8-kg weight loss over the past several months. He immigrated recently and has a history of poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus and long-term corticosteroid use for chronic obstructive lung disease.
Over the last 3 weeks, he has developed bilateral lower extremity weakness and difficulty walking. He denies recent trauma.
Physical examination reveals:
* Temperature: 37.9°C (100.2°F)
* Mild thoracic kyphosis
* Point tenderness over the lower thoracic vertebrae
* Weakness in bilateral hip flexion and knee extension (4/5)
* Hyperreflexia of the lower extremities
Laboratory findings:
Test	Result
ESR	markedly elevated
CRP	elevated
WBC	mildly elevated

MRI of the thoracic spine is described as:
Destruction of adjacent vertebral bodies with narrowing of the intervertebral disk space, paravertebral soft-tissue swelling, and a rim-enhancing collection extending into the psoas musculature.
A biopsy specimen demonstrates granulomatous inflammation with multinucleated giant cells and central acellular eosinophilic debris.
Which of the following best explains the mechanism responsible for this patient’s spinal disease?
