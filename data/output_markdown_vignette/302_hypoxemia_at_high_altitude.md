## Chief Complaint

Shortness of breath at high altitude

## Vignette

A 26-year-old healthy male joins a high-altitude trekking expedition in the Andes at 4,800 meters above sea level. Within 48 hours, he develops progressive dyspnea on exertion, headache, dizziness, and difficulty sleeping. On examination, he is tachypneic and mildly cyanotic with an oxygen saturation of 78% on room air. Chest auscultation reveals faint crackles at the lung bases. Arterial blood gas shows: pH 7.48, PaCO₂ 28 mmHg, PaO₂ 50 mmHg. Over the next day, his symptoms worsen with development of cough productive of frothy sputum. Chest X-ray shows patchy bilateral infiltrates. Which of the following mechanisms is the primary initial physiologic response to the low ambient oxygen pressure at high altitude?

## Key Symptoms

Dyspnea on exertion; headache; dizziness; insomnia; cyanosis; cough with frothy sputum; worsening hypoxemia

## Labs

## Imaging Findings

## Physical Exam

Mild cyanosis; faint basal crackles

## Main Clue

High altitude exposure + hypoxemia with respiratory alkalosis + bilateral infiltrates (HAPE pattern)

## Supporting Clue

Frothy sputum + rapid progression of symptoms after ascent

## Question

Which of the following is the primary initial physiologic response to low ambient oxygen at high altitude?
