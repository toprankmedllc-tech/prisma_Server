## Buzzword Question

A 60-year-old chronic alcoholic is evaluated 6 months after an episode of acute confusion, ophthalmoplegia, and ataxia. He is now unable to form new memories and fills in the gaps with elaborate, fabricated stories that he believes to be true. MRI shows symmetric atrophy of the mammillary bodies. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Chronic alcoholic; prior Wernicke episode; anterograde amnesia; confabulation; bilateral mammillary body atrophy

## Answer Options

A) Alzheimer disease
B) Korsakoff syndrome
C) Transient global amnesia
D) Dissociative amnesia

## Correct Answer

B) Korsakoff syndrome

## Explanation

Korsakoff syndrome is the chronic amnestic sequela of untreated or inadequately treated Wernicke encephalopathy. The hallmark features are anterograde amnesia and confabulation, with bilateral atrophy of the mammillary bodies.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic alcoholic + prior Wernicke + anterograde amnesia + confabulation + mammillary body atrophy = Korsakoff syndrome

## Wrong Options

### Alzheimer disease

AD presents with insidious progressive amnesia and global cognitive decline in the elderly, with hippocampal atrophy on imaging, not isolated mammillary body atrophy or alcohol-related confabulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elderly + insidious amnestic decline + hippocampal atrophy + β-amyloid plaques + tau tangles = Alzheimer disease

### Transient global amnesia

TGA is a sudden, isolated, self-limited episode of anterograde amnesia lasting <24 hours, often after exertion or emotional stress, not a chronic amnestic syndrome with mammillary body atrophy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden isolated amnesia + resolves in <24 hours + after exertion or emotional stress = transient global amnesia

### Dissociative amnesia

Dissociative amnesia is a psychiatric disorder with selective inability to recall autobiographical information after a traumatic event, there is no structural lesion and no confabulation pattern as described.

**Buzzword Combo:** Selective memory loss for traumatic event + normal imaging + no anterograde deficit = dissociative amnesia
