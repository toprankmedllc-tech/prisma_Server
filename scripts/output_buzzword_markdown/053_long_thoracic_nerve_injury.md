## Buzzword Question

A 27-year-old man presents with difficulty pushing open heavy doors and raising his arm above shoulder level after sustaining a stab wound to the lateral chest wall. On physical examination, protrusion of the medial border of the scapula is observed when he pushes against a wall. Muscle strength testing reveals impaired upward rotation of the scapula.
Which of the following nerves was most likely damaged?

## Buzzwords Used

difficulty pushing open heavy doors;  stab wound to the lateral chest wall

## Answer Options

A. Axillary nerve
B. Long thoracic nerve
C. Dorsal scapular nerve
D. Thoracodorsal nerve

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

The long thoracic nerve innervates the serratus anterior muscle. Injury causes weakness of scapular protraction and upward rotation, producing a classic “winged scapula.”

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Winged scapula + difficulty pushing against wall = long thoracic nerve injury

## Wrong Options

### A. Axillary nerve

Incorrect — The axillary nerve innervates the deltoid and teres minor muscles. Injury leads to impaired arm abduction from 15° to 90° and sensory loss over the lateral shoulder

**Buzzword Combo:** Surgical neck fracture + flattened shoulder contour = axillary nerve injury

### C. Dorsal scapular nerve

Incorrect — This nerve innervates the rhomboids and levator scapulae, which retract and elevate the scapula.

**Buzzword Combo:** Difficulty retracting scapula + rhomboid weakness = dorsal scapular nerve injury

### D. Thoracodorsal nerve

Incorrect — The thoracodorsal nerve innervates the latissimus dorsi muscle, important for extension, adduction, and medial rotation of the arm.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak climbing or rowing movements + impaired arm adduction = latissimus dorsi dysfunction
