## Buzzword Question

A 35-year-old man presents with 4 days of double vision, an unsteady wide-based gait, and absent deep tendon reflexes throughout. Limb strength is preserved. He had a viral upper respiratory infection 2 weeks ago. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Ophthalmoplegia; ataxia; areflexia; antecedent infection; preserved strength

## Answer Options

A) Wernicke encephalopathy
B) Miller Fisher syndrome
C) Cerebellar stroke
D) Multiple sclerosis

## Correct Answer

B) Miller Fisher syndrome

## Explanation

Miller Fisher syndrome is a GBS variant defined by the triad of OPHTHALMOPLEGIA, ATAXIA, and AREFLEXIA after a recent infection. It is associated with anti-GQ1b antibodies, which target gangliosides concentrated in cranial nerves III, IV, and VI.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Ophthalmoplegia + ataxia + areflexia + recent infection = anti-GQ1b antibodies → Miller Fisher syndrome

## Wrong Options

### Wernicke encephalopathy

Wernicke is thiamine deficiency, classically in alcohol use, presenting with the triad of confusion, ophthalmoplegia, and ataxia, but reflexes are typically preserved, and confusion is prominent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alcoholic + confusion + ophthalmoplegia + ataxia = thiamine (B1) deficiency → Wernicke

###  Cerebellar stroke

Cerebellar stroke produces sudden ipsilateral limb ataxia and vertigo with possible dysarthria, but reflexes are normal or hyperactive, not absent throughout.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden ipsilateral limb ataxia + vertigo + crossed sensory loss = PICA (Wallenberg) stroke

###  Multiple sclerosis

MS produces CNS demyelinating episodes (optic neuritis, INO, sensory or motor deficits) separated in space and time, not a peripheral pattern with global areflexia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Young woman + INO + optic neuritis + oligoclonal bands = MS
