## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old man with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (LDL 295 mg/dL despite maximum-dose rosuvastatin) is started on evolocumab. His LDL decreases by 62% within 6 weeks.
Which of the following best explains the mechanism of LDL reduction by evolocumab?

A) Direct HMG-CoA reductase inhibition
B) Monoclonal antibody binding to PCSK9 
C) Binding of bile acids in the intestinal lumen  
D) Inhibition of intestinal NPC1L1


## Buzzwords Used

 Evolocumab; LDL decreases by 62%

## Answer Options

A) Direct HMG-CoA reductase inhibition
B) Monoclonal antibody binding to PCSK9 
C) Binding of bile acids in the intestinal lumen  
D) Inhibition of intestinal NPC1L1

## Correct Answer

B) Monoclonal antibody binding to PCSK9 

## Explanation

PCSK9 is a hepatocyte-secreted enzyme that binds to LDL receptors, diverting them to lysosomes for degradation. This reduces receptor recycling and increases plasma LDL. Evolocumab and alirocumab are monoclonal antibodies that bind to circulating PCSK9, preventing this interaction and increasing LDL clearance. 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

PCSK9 inhibition + ↑ LDL receptor recycling = Evolocumab mechanism

## Wrong Options

### A) Direct HMG-CoA reductase inhibition

HMG-CoA reductase inhibition is the mechanism of statins. PCSK9 inhibitors work by increasing the number of functional LDL receptors available rather than reducing cholesterol synthesis. 

**Buzzword Combo:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibition + ↓ cholesterol synthesis = Statin mechanism 

### C) Binding of bile acids in the intestinal lumen  

Bile acid sequestrants (like cholestyramine and colesevelam) bind bile acids in the gut, forcing their excretion. To replace these lost acids, the liver uses its cholesterol reserves. This drop in liver cholesterol triggers a compensatory increase in LDL receptors, which pulls more LDL from the blood and lowers circulating levels.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bile acid sequestrant + upregulation of LDL receptors = cholestyramine mechanism

### D) Inhibition of intestinal NPC1L1

NPC1L1 inhibition is the mechanism of ezetimibe, which reduces intestinal cholesterol absorption. PCSK9 inhibitors are injectable monoclonal antibodies with systemic hepatic mechanism, not intestinal absorption inhibitors. 

**Buzzword Combo:** NPC1L1 + brush border + intestinal cholesterol = Ezetimibe mechanism
