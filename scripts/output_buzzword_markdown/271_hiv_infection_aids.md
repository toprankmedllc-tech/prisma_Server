## Buzzword Question

A patient with progressive helper T-cell loss has a CD4+ count of 160/mm3 and develops fever, dry cough, shortness of breath, and diffuse bilateral interstitial infiltrates. Which of the following infections is most strongly associated with this immune status?

## Buzzwords Used

Progressive helper T-cell loss; CD4+ count of 160/mm3; dry cough with bilateral interstitial infiltrates

## Answer Options

A) Cytomegalovirus
B) Pneumocystis jirovecii
C) Mycobacterium avium complex
D) Toxoplasma gondii

## Correct Answer

B) Pneumocystis jirovecii


## Explanation

In HIV/AIDS, CD4+ T-cell loss below 200/mm3 increases risk for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia. This reflects impaired helper T-cell–mediated immunity, not isolated antibody deficiency.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

CD4 <200 + dry cough/shortness of breath + bilateral interstitial infiltrates = Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia in HIV/AIDS

## Wrong Options

### A) Cytomegalovirus

Cytomegalovirus infection is classically associated with more advanced CD4+ depletion, especially CD4 <50/mm3, and can cause retinitis or colitis.

**Buzzword Combo:** CD4 <50 + painless vision changes + retinal hemorrhage/necrosis = cytomegalovirus

### C) Mycobacterium avium complex

Mycobacterium avium complex is classically associated with CD4 <50/mm3 and causes disseminated infection with fever, weight loss, night sweats, and anemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** CD4 <50 + fever/weight loss + disseminated mycobacterial infection = Mycobacterium avium complex

### D) Toxoplasma gondii

Toxoplasma gondii is classically associated with CD4 <100/mm3 and causes brain abscesses with ring-enhancing lesions.

**Buzzword Combo:** CD4 <100 + focal neurologic deficits + ring-enhancing brain lesions = Toxoplasma gondii
