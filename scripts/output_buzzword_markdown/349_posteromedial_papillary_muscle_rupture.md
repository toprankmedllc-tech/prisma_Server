## Buzzword Question

The posteromedial papillary muscle is the most common site of post-MI rupture because of which feature of its blood supply?

## Buzzwords Used

posteromedial papillary muscle;  post-MI rupture; blood supply

## Answer Options

A. Sole supply from the posterior descending artery
B. Dual supply from the LAD and LCX
C. Supply from the left main coronary artery
D. Supply from septal perforators of the LAD

## Correct Answer

Sole supply from the posterior descending artery

## Explanation

The posteromedial papillary muscle receives blood from only the posterior descending artery (a branch of the RCA in right-dominant circulations). This single blood supply makes it uniquely vulnerable to ischemic rupture after inferior MI. The anterolateral papillary muscle, in contrast, has dual blood supply from the LAD and LCX and rarely ruptures.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

 Sole PDA blood supply + inferior MI + acute MR = posteromedial papillary muscle rupture

## Wrong Options

###  Anterolateral papillary muscle 

Dual blood supply protects this papillary muscle from ischemic injury, so rupture is rare even in large anterior infarcts.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Dual LAD + LCX supply = anterolateral papillary muscle (protected)

### Chordae tendineae 

The chordae are avascular connective tissue structures and are not directly perfused by a named coronary artery. Chordal rupture is a feature of myxomatous degeneration (mitral valve prolapse), not post-MI ischemic necrosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Myxomatous degeneration + chordal rupture = mitral valve prolapse

###  Anterior mitral leaflet

The mitral valve leaflets are avascular and do not have a coronary blood supply. Leaflet pathology in post-MI acute MR is mechanical (flail from loss of papillary support), not ischemic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Flail mitral leaflet + post-MI = secondary to papillary muscle rupture
