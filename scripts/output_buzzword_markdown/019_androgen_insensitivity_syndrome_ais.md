## Buzzword Question

A 15-year-old phenotypic female is evaluated for a lack of secondary sexual characteristics. Physical exam reveals a short vaginal blind pouch and an absence of a uterus or ovaries. Lab results show high levels of testosterone and luteinizing hormone. Karyotype is 46,XY. Which of the following embryological structures regressed appropriately in this patient during the first trimester?

## Buzzwords Used

Phenotypic female; Vaginal blind pouch; Absent uterus; 46,XY; High testosterone.

## Answer Options

A)Gubernaculum
B) Mesonephric ducts
C) Urogenital sinus
D) Paramesonephric ducts

## Correct Answer

D

## Explanation

The patient has Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (AIS). Because she has testes (functioning Y chromosome and SRY gene), Sertoli cells produce Müllerian Inhibitory Factor (MIF), which correctly causes the regression of the Paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts. However, the lack of androgen receptors prevents the development of the mesonephric ducts and male external genitalia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

MIF + Sertoli cells = Paramesonephric duct regression

## Wrong Options

### B) Mesonephric ducts

Mesonephric (Wolffian) ducts should have developed into male internal structures (SEED: Seminal vesicles, Epididymis, Ejaculatory duct, Ductous deferens), but they regressed due to the inability to respond to testosterone.

**Buzzword Combo:** Testosterone resistance + Wolffian ducts = Mesonephric regression

### C) Urogenital sinus

The urogenital sinus develops into the bladder, urethra, and (in females) the lower vagina. In this patient, it developed into female external genitalia because it did not receive a dihydrotestosterone (DHT) signal.

**Buzzword Combo:** No DHT + Urogenital Sinus = Female external genitalia

### A) Gubernaculum

The gubernaculum is responsible for the descent of the gonads. In AIS, the testes are usually present (often in the labia majora or inguinal canal) but the gubernaculum itself does not "regress" in a way that explains the internal anatomy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Gubernaculum + Descent = Scrotal/Inguinal testes
