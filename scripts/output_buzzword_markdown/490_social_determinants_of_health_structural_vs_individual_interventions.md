## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old uninsured man with poorly controlled hypertension and type 2 diabetes lives in a food desert, works two jobs, and cannot afford his medications ($99/month). His physician counsels him extensively on diet and exercise at each visit with minimal improvement. Which of the following represents the MOST structurally impactful intervention to address the root cause of his poor control?

## Buzzwords Used

Uninsured; food desert; two jobs; cannot afford medications; poor control; counseling not working; structural intervention

## Answer Options

A) Connect the patient with a patient assistance program, a federally qualified health center, and a social worker to address insurance, medication cost, and food access 
B) Intensify medication counseling and provide written dietary instructions at each visit 
C) Refer the patient to an endocrinologist for more specialized management 
D) Add a third medication to improve glycemic and blood pressure control

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Social determinants of health (SDOH),  economic stability, food access, healthcare access, neighborhood environment, education, are upstream drivers of chronic disease outcomes that clinical treatment alone cannot address. This patient's poor control is driven by unaffordability (economic barrier) and food insecurity (food desert), not by insufficient clinical intensity. Structural interventions that address SDOH include: patient assistance programs (medication cost), federally qualified health centers (income-based sliding-scale care), food banks/SNAP enrollment, and social worker referral. These address root causes, unlike repeated counseling that places burden on the patient without addressing barriers.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

SDOH barriers (cost, food access, insurance) = root cause; structural interventions (patient assistance, FQHC, social work) address root cause; counseling alone = insufficient

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Intensify medication counseling and provide written dietary instructions at each visit


Repeated counseling without addressing the structural barriers (cost, food access) places the responsibility on a patient who lacks the resources to act on advice. This approach ignores SDOH and reflects an individual-level rather than structural intervention. Evidence shows counseling alone is ineffective when structural barriers persist.


**Buzzword Combo:** Counseling = individual-level; insufficient when structural SDOH barriers (cost, access) are the root cause


### 2: C) Refer the patient to an endocrinologist for more specialized management

Specialist referral addresses clinical complexity but not the fundamental barrier,  the patient cannot afford his current medications. Adding specialist involvement increases cost and does not resolve medication unaffordability or food insecurity

**Buzzword Combo:** Specialist referral = clinical intensification; does not address SDOH root causes (cost, food access, insurance)


### 3: D) Add a third medication to improve glycemic and blood pressure control

Adding medications when the patient cannot afford his current two medications would worsen adherence barriers and increase out-of-pocket costs. Clinical intensification without addressing affordability is counterproductive in this context.

**Buzzword Combo:** Adding medications ≠ addressing root cause; cost barrier makes additional medications less adherent, not more
