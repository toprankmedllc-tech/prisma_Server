## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old chronic smoker presents with progressive dyspnea and chronic cough for several years. Pulmonary function tests show decreased FEV₁/FVC ratio and increased total lung capacity. He has a barrel-shaped chest and decreased breath sounds. Which of the following best describes the type of emphysema most likely in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Chronic smoker; progressive dyspnea; chronic cough; decreased FEV₁/FVC; increased TLC; barrel chest; emphysema

## Answer Options

A) Centriacinar
B) Panacinar
C) Paraseptal
D) Irregular

## Correct Answer

A) Centriacinar

## Explanation

Centriacinar emphysema is strongly associated with smoking and primarily affects the respiratory bronchioles in the upper lobes. It is the most common type seen in COPD patients with a significant smoking history.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Smoking; upper lobes; respiratory bronchioles = centriacinar emphysema

## Wrong Options

### B) Panacinar

Panacinar emphysema is associated with alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency and predominantly affects the lower lobes rather than smokers.

**Buzzword Combo:** Young non-smoker; lower lobes; liver disease = panacinar emphysema

### C) Paraseptal

Paraseptal emphysema affects distal alveoli near pleura and is associated with spontaneous pneumothorax, not chronic smoker-related COPD.

**Buzzword Combo:** Apical blebs + spontaneous pneumothorax  + subpleural = paraseptal emphysema

### D) Irregular

Irregular emphysema is associated with scarring from previous lung injury and is usually clinically insignificant, not a cause of COPD symptoms.

**Buzzword Combo:** Post-inflammatory scarring; asymptomatic; incidental = irregular emphysema
