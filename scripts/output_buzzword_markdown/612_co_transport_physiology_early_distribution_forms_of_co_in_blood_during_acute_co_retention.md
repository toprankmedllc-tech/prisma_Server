## Buzzword Question

A patient with metabolic alkalosis has decreased ventilation and CO₂ retention. Which form of CO₂ increases first in blood?

## Buzzwords Used

Metabolic alkalosis; hypoventilation; CO₂ retention; acute respiratory compensation; blood CO₂ transport; gas exchange dynamics

## Answer Options

A) Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)
B) Carbaminohemoglobin
C) Dissolved CO₂
D) Carbonic acid

## Correct Answer

C) Dissolved CO₂

## Explanation

The first form of CO₂ to increase in blood is dissolved CO₂ because CO₂ enters blood physically and equilibrates immediately with plasma. Conversion into bicarbonate and carbaminohemoglobin occurs afterward as slower, enzymatic and protein-binding processes.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hypoventilation + CO₂ retention + immediate equilibrium → dissolved CO₂ rises first

## Wrong Options

### A) Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)

Bicarbonate formation requires carbonic anhydrase inside RBCs and is a slower downstream buffering step, not the first change.

**Buzzword Combo:** CO₂ transport + RBC enzyme dependence + delayed buffering response

### B) Carbaminohemoglobin

Carbaminohemoglobin formation requires binding of CO₂ to deoxygenated hemoglobin and is a secondary, slower process compared to simple dissolution.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemoglobin binding + protein interaction + slower equilibration step

### D) Carbonic acid

Carbonic acid forms transiently inside RBCs after CO₂ hydration but is not the primary measurable increase in blood CO₂ content.

**Buzzword Combo:** CO₂ hydration + unstable intermediate + intracellular conversion step
