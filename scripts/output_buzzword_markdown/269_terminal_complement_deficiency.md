## Buzzword Question

A patient with repeated infections by gram-negative diplococci has complement testing showing failure of pore formation in bacterial membranes. Which of the following proteins is most likely deficient?

## Buzzwords Used

Repeated infections by gram-negative diplococci; failure of pore formation; bacterial membranes

## Answer Options

A) C1 inhibitor
B) C3b
C) C7
D) Factor D

## Correct Answer

C) C7


## Explanation

C7 is a terminal complement protein. Deficiency of a C5-C9 protein prevents membrane attack complex formation, impairing direct lysis of Neisseria species.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Gram-negative diplococci + failed pore formation + C5-C9 protein deficiency = terminal complement deficiency

## Wrong Options

### A) C1 inhibitor

C1 inhibitor deficiency causes hereditary angioedema due to excess bradykinin production, not recurrent Neisseria infections.

**Buzzword Combo:** Episodic swelling + low C4 + excess bradykinin = C1 inhibitor deficiency

### B) C3b

C3b is the major opsonin in the complement system. Deficiency causes impaired phagocytosis of encapsulated bacteria, not defective membrane pore formation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Encapsulated bacterial infections + impaired opsonization + low C3b = C3 deficiency

### D) Factor D

Factor D is part of the alternative complement pathway. Its deficiency can impair alternative pathway activation, but the classic recurrent Neisseria association is terminal C5-C9 deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alternative pathway defect + impaired C3 convertase formation + reduced complement activation = factor D deficiency
