## Buzzword Question

A patient with malignant hypertension dies of multi-organ failure. Microscopic examination of the kidney reveals arterioles with a bright pink, glassy thickening of their walls and luminal narrowing. Which of the following best describes this finding?

## Buzzwords Used


Malignant hypertension; bright pink glassy arteriolar wall thickening

## Answer Options

A) Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles
B) Hyaline arteriolosclerosis
C) Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis
D) Atherosclerotic plaque deposition

## Correct Answer

A) Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles

## Explanation

Fibrinoid necrosis is the histologic hallmark of malignant hypertension, where extreme intraluminal pressure injures the arteriolar wall and allows plasma proteins (including fibrin) to leak into the media. This produces a bright pink, glassy, necrotic appearance with luminal narrowing.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Malignant HTN + bright pink glassy arteriolar wall + plasma protein deposition = fibrinoid necrosis

## Wrong Options

### 
B) Hyaline arteriolosclerosis

Pink, homogeneous thickening of arteriolar walls seen in benign (long-standing) essential hypertension and diabetes mellitus — without the necrosis seen in malignant HTN.

**Buzzword Combo:** Benign HTN / diabetes + pink homogeneous arteriolar thickening = hyaline arteriolosclerosis

### C) Hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis

Concentric, laminated thickening of smooth muscle layers seen in malignant hypertension, often coexisting with fibrinoid necrosis but distinct from it histologically.

**Buzzword Combo:** Malignant HTN + concentric laminated smooth muscle thickening = hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis

### D) Atherosclerotic plaque deposition


Atherosclerosis affects large and medium-sized elastic and muscular arteries with intimal lipid deposition and fibrous cap formation — not arterioles.

**Buzzword Combo:** Large/medium arteries + intimal lipid plaque + fibrous cap = atherosclerosis
