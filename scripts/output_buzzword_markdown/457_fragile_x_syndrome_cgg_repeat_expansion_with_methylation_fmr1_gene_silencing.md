## Buzzword Question

A 6-year-old boy is brought to the pediatric clinic because of developmental delay and behavioral problems. His teachers report poor attention, hand-flapping movements, and difficulty with social interaction. He avoids eye contact and has episodes of aggressive outbursts.

On physical examination, he has a long, narrow face, large protruding ears, and hyperextensible joints. Testicular examination reveals macroorchidism. Family history reveals that his maternal uncle had intellectual disability.

Genetic testing shows CGG repeat expansion in a gene located on the X chromosome with abnormal hypermethylation.

Which of the following best explains the underlying genetic mechanism?

## Buzzwords Used

Developmental delay ; autism-like behavior ; long face ; large ears ; macroorchidism ; X-linked inheritance ; CGG repeat expansion ; hypermethylation ; intellectual disability ; fragile X

## Answer Options

A) Anticipation due to trinucleotide repeat expansion
B) Genomic imprinting with paternal gene silencing
C) X-linked CGG repeat expansion with promoter hypermethylation causing gene silencing
D) Mitochondrial DNA mutation with maternal inheritance

## Correct Answer

C) X-linked CGG repeat expansion with promoter hypermethylation causing gene silencing

## Explanation

Fragile X Syndrome is caused by CGG repeat expansion in the FMR1 gene on the X chromosome. This leads to promoter hypermethylation and silencing of the gene, resulting in intellectual disability, autism-like behavior, and macroorchidism

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

CGG repeat expansion + X-linked inheritance + hypermethylation = Fragile X syndrome (FMR1 silencing)

## Wrong Options

### A) Anticipation due to trinucleotide repeat expansion

While Fragile X does show anticipation, this option is incomplete because it does not specify the key mechanism of methylation-induced gene silencing on the X chromosome.

**Buzzword Combo:** CAG expansion + neurodegeneration + caudate atrophy = Huntington disease

### B) Genomic imprinting with paternal gene silencing

Imprinting disorders involve parent-of-origin expression differences (e.g., Prader-Willi, Angelman), not CGG repeat expansion or methylation-induced FMR1 silencing.

**Buzzword Combo:** Maternal/paternal deletion + obesity or seizures = imprinting disorders

### D) Mitochondrial DNA mutation with maternal inheritance

Mitochondrial disorders cause multisystem disease with maternal inheritance and high-energy tissue involvement, not X-linked CGG repeat expansion with macroorchidism.

**Buzzword Combo:** Maternal inheritance + muscle weakness + lactic acidosis = mitochondrial disease
