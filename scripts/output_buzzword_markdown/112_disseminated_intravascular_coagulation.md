## Buzzword Question

A patient with sepsis develops diffuse bleeding, thrombocytopenia, prolonged PT/PTT, and elevated D-dimer. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

sepsis; diffuse bleeding; thrombocytopenia; prolonged PT; prolonged PTT; elevated D-dimer

## Answer Options

A) Hemophilia A
B) Disseminated intravascular coagulation
C) Immune thrombocytopenic purpura
D) von Willebrand disease

## Correct Answer

B. Disseminated intravascular coagulation

## Explanation

DIC is caused by systemic activation of the coagulation cascade (commonly in sepsis via tissue factor release), leading to widespread microthrombi formation. This results in consumption of platelets and clotting factors with secondary fibrinolysis, causing bleeding and elevated D-dimer.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sepsis + bleeding + thrombocytopenia + ↑D-dimer + prolonged PT/PTT = DIC

## Wrong Options

### A. Hemophilia A

Hemophilia A causes isolated prolonged PTT due to factor VIII deficiency. PT, platelets, and D-dimer remain normal, and it presents with hemarthroses rather than diffuse bleeding in sepsis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Factor VIII deficiency + isolated prolonged PTT + hemarthrosis = Hemophilia A

### C. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura

ITP causes isolated thrombocytopenia due to antibody-mediated platelet destruction. PT, PTT, and fibrinogen are normal, and it is not associated with sepsis-induced coagulation activation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Isolated thrombocytopenia + normal PT/PTT = ITP

### D. von Willebrand disease

von Willebrand disease causes mucocutaneous bleeding due to impaired platelet adhesion. It typically shows normal platelets and PT, with only mild PTT prolongation and no D-dimer elevation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mucosal bleeding + platelet dysfunction + normal coagulation factors = von Willebrand disease
