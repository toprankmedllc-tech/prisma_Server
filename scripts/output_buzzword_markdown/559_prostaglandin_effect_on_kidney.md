## Buzzword Question

A 64-year-old woman with severe osteoarthritis and underlying mild volume depletion from chronic loop diuretic therapy is hospitalized after taking high doses of over-the-counter ibuprofen for worsening knee pain. On presentation, her blood pressure is 105/65 mm Hg, and laboratory studies reveal a significant acute rise in serum creatinine from a baseline of 0.9 mg/dL to 2.1 mg/dL, alongside a severe reduction in calculated GFR. If an intrarenal biochemical analysis could be performed during this acute event, which of the following secondary molecular or physiological alterations would be observed within her nephrons as a direct consequence of the drug's mechanism of action?

## Buzzwords Used

Osteoarthritis; Over-the-counter ibuprofen; Volume depletion; Acute rise in serum creatinine; Paracrine secretion failure

## Answer Options

A) Loss of paracrine-mediated afferent arteriolar vasodilation, resulting in unopposed vasoconstriction that slashes renal blood flow (RBF) and plummets GFR under low-flow states.
B) Over-activation of proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) 1α-hydroxylase, causing an immediate, unregulated systemic surge of 1,25-(OH)2 vitamin D3
C) Paradoxical up-regulation of high-dose dopamine pathways in the proximal tubule, causing intense interlobular artery constriction that completely overrides loop diuretic action.
D) Accelerated conversion of 25-OH vitamin D3to inactive metabolites inside the peritubular capillary bed due to acute hypoxia-induced factor degradation.

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

The patient is experiencing NSAID-induced acute kidney injury. Under volume-depleted or low-renal-blood-flow states, renal-protective prostaglandins (PGE2 /PGI 2) are crucial paracrine vasodilators of the afferent arteriole to maintain baseline RBF and GFR; by taking ibuprofen (an NSAID that blocks cyclooxygenase), this protective mechanism is lost, causing profound, unopposed afferent arteriolar constriction and a rapid drop in filtration pressure.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Ibuprofen toxicity + Prostaglandin synthesis inhibition + Afferent arteriole constriction = NSAID-induced acute kidney injury

## Wrong Options

### B

Proximal convoluted tubule 1α-hydroxylase is regulated by Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) and phosphate levels to convert calcidiol to active calcitriol. It is not directly up-regulated or driven into an unregulated systemic surge by an acute prostaglandin synthesis blockade.

**Buzzword Combo:** PCT 1-alpha-hydroxylase + Calcitriol synthesis surge + PTH-independent activation = Vitamin D metabolic trap

### C

While low doses of dopamine dilate renal vessels and high doses constrict them, NSAIDs do not trigger a paradoxical up-regulation of high-dose dopamine pathways to cause interlobular artery constriction; the vasoconstriction is strictly driven by the loss of vasodilatory prostaglandins.

**Buzzword Combo:** Proximal tubule dopamine + Interlobular artery constriction + Vasoconstrictor shift = Dopamine dose-dependent fallacy


### D

The conversion of vitamin D3 happens in the PCT cells, while erythropoietin is produced in the peritubular capillary bed. This option mixes up the anatomical locations and endocrine products of the kidney while falsely attributing vitamin D inactivation to acute hypoxia-induced factor degradation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Peritubular capillary bed + Calcidiol conversion + Hypoxia-induced degradation = Endocrine anatomy displacement trap
