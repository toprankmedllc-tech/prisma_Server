## Buzzword Question

A young patient has experienced multiple recurrent episodes of AVNRT requiring emergency department visits. Which of the following is the most appropriate definitive long-term treatment?

## Buzzwords Used

AVNRT; definitive treatment

## Answer Options

A) Catheter ablation of the slow pathway
B) Lifelong oral amiodarone
C) Permanent pacemaker placement
D) Implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD)

## Correct Answer

A) Catheter ablation of the slow pathway

## Explanation

Catheter ablation of the slow pathway within the AV node is the definitive curative treatment for recurrent AVNRT, with success rates >95% and a low complication rate.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Recurrent AVNRT + definitive cure = catheter ablation of the slow pathway

## Wrong Options

### B) Lifelong oral amiodarone


Amiodarone is reserved for refractory or complex arrhythmias and carries significant long-term toxicities (pulmonary fibrosis, thyroid dysfunction, hepatotoxicity). It is not the treatment of choice for a curable arrhythmia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Refractory arrhythmia + structural heart disease = amiodarone (last-line)

### C) Permanent pacemaker placement


Pacemakers treat bradyarrhythmias (complete AV block, symptomatic sinus node dysfunction), not tachyarrhythmias like AVNRT.

**Buzzword Combo:** Third-degree AV block / symptomatic bradycardia = permanent pacemaker

### 
D) Implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD)

ICDs treat ventricular arrhythmias at risk for sudden cardiac death (sustained VT, prior cardiac arrest, severe LV dysfunction), not benign supraventricular re-entrant tachycardias.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sustained VT / prior SCD / EF <35% = ICD placement
