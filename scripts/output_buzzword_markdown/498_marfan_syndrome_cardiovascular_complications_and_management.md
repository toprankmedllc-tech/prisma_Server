## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old man with Marfan syndrome is found to have an aortic root diameter of 4.7 cm (upper limit of normal 4.0 cm) on echocardiogram. He is asymptomatic. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?


## Buzzwords Used

Marfan syndrome; aortic root 4.7 cm; dilated; asymptomatic; management; FBN1


## Answer Options

A) Start beta-blocker therapy to reduce aortic wall stress and refer for cardiothoracic surgery evaluation for prophylactic aortic root replacement 
B) Reassure the patient that aortic dilation is expected in Marfan syndrome and recheck echocardiogram in 5 years 
C) Start high-intensity exercise training to improve cardiovascular conditioning 
D) Perform emergent aortic dissection repair because the aortic root exceeds 4.0 cm

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Marfan syndrome (FBN1 mutation → deficient fibrillin-1 → impaired TGF-β sequestration → aortic medial degeneration) causes progressive aortic root dilation leading to aortic regurgitation and life-threatening aortic dissection (most common cause of death). Management of dilated aortic root: (1) Beta-blockers (reduce dP/dt, aortic wall stress), first-line; losartan (ARB) is an alternative/adjunct via TGF-β pathway; (2) Activity restriction, avoid contact sports, isometric exercise, Valsalva; (3) Prophylactic aortic root replacement is indicated when diameter reaches ~4.5–5.0 cm (guidelines vary) or when growth rate exceeds 0.5 cm/year. At 4.7 cm this patient meets surgical referral threshold.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Marfan aortic root dilation: beta-blocker to slow progression + surgical referral at ≥4.5–5.0 cm; avoid contact sports/isometric exercise

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Reassure the patient that aortic dilation is expected in Marfan syndrome and recheck echocardiogram in 5 years


While aortic dilation is expected in Marfan syndrome, it is not benign and requires active management. Aortic root dilation above 4.0 cm requires beta-blocker therapy, annual echocardiography (not 5-year intervals), activity restriction, and surgical referral as the diameter approaches operative threshold. Reassurance without intervention at 4.7 cm would be negligent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Aortic root >4.0 cm = active management required; annual echo + beta-blocker + surgical referral near threshold; 5-year interval insufficient


###  C) Start high-intensity exercise training to improve cardiovascular conditioning

Patients with Marfan syndrome should AVOID strenuous exercise, particularly isometric exercise, contact sports, and activities causing Valsalva maneuver. These raise aortic wall stress and accelerate dilation risk. Competitive athletics are contraindicated once significant aortic root dilation is present.

**Buzzword Combo:** Marfan + aortic dilation = AVOID isometric exercise, contact sports, Valsalva; high-intensity exercise contraindicated

### 3: D) Perform emergent aortic dissection repair because the aortic root exceeds 4.0 cm

This patient is asymptomatic with dilation (4.7 cm) but no dissection. Emergency surgery is indicated for acute aortic dissection (severe tearing chest/back pain, pulse differentials, widened mediastinum). Prophylactic elective repair is planned when the root approaches 4.5–5.0 cm; emergent repair is not indicated without dissection.

**Buzzword Combo:** Aortic dissection = emergent repair; asymptomatic dilation = prophylactic elective repair at 4.5–5.0 cm threshold; not emergent
