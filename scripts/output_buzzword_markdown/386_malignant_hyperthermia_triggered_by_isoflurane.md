## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old man undergoes emergency surgery under general anesthesia. Shortly after induction with an inhaled anesthetic and succinylcholine, he develops rapidly increasing temperature, generalized muscle rigidity, tachycardia, and elevated end-tidal CO2. Laboratory testing later shows markedly elevated creatine kinase levels and hyperkalemia. Which inhaled anesthetic agent most likely triggered this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

General anesthesia ; succinylcholine ; hyperthermia ; muscle rigidity ; elevated CK ; hyperkalemia ; malignant hyperthermia ; inhaled anesthetic

## Answer Options

A) Nitrous oxide
B) Propofol
C) Isoflurane
D) Etomidate

## Correct Answer

C) Isoflurane

## Explanation

Isoflurane is a volatile inhaled anesthetic that can trigger malignant hyperthermia, especially when combined with succinylcholine. Malignant hyperthermia is caused by uncontrolled calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum via defective ryanodine receptors.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Succinylcholine + hyperthermia + muscle rigidity = Isoflurane-triggered malignant hyperthermia

## Wrong Options

### A) Nitrous oxide

Nitrous oxide is not a classic trigger for malignant hyperthermia and is mainly associated with expansion of gas-filled spaces.

**Buzzword Combo:** Rapid induction + gas expansion + low potency = Nitrous oxide

### B) Propofol

Propofol is an IV anesthetic commonly used for induction and is associated with hypotension and respiratory depression rather than malignant hyperthermia.

**Buzzword Combo:** IV induction + respiratory depression + hypotension = Propofol

### D) Etomidate

Etomidate is hemodynamically stable but can cause adrenal suppression and postoperative nausea/vomiting.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemodynamic stability + adrenal suppression + induction agent = Etomidate
