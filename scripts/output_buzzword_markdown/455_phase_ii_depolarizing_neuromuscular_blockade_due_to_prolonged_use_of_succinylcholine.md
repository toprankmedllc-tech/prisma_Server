## Buzzword Question

A 42-year-old man is admitted to the ICU for mechanical ventilation after severe head trauma. He has been receiving continuous infusion of a neuromuscular blocking agent for ventilator synchrony. After 48 hours, the patient shows incomplete recovery of muscle function. Train-of-four stimulation now shows “fade” in response rather than sustained contraction.

The ICU team attempts reversal with an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, and partial improvement in muscle strength is observed.

Which of the following best explains the mechanism of the patient’s current neuromuscular blockade?

## Buzzwords Used

ICU mechanical ventilation ; continuous neuromuscular blocker infusion ; prolonged paralysis ; train-of-four fade ; partial response to neostigmine ; succinylcholine exposure ; muscle weakness in ICU

## Answer Options

A) Persistent depolarization of motor end plate preventing repolarization

B) Competitive inhibition of Nm nicotinic receptors at neuromuscular junction

C) Desensitization of Nm receptors after prolonged depolarization with repolarized membrane

D) Blockade of presynaptic acetylcholine release

## Correct Answer

C) Desensitization of Nm receptors after prolonged depolarization with repolarized membrane

## Explanation

Phase II block occurs after prolonged exposure to Succinylcholine when the membrane repolarizes but nicotinic receptors become desensitized. This produces a clinical picture resembling nondepolarizing blockade with train-of-four fade and partial response to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Prolonged succinylcholine infusion + train-of-four fade + partial neostigmine response = Phase II block (desensitized receptors)

## Wrong Options

### A) Persistent depolarization of motor end plate preventing repolarization

This describes Phase I block of Succinylcholine, which shows no fade and is not improved by acetylcholinesterase inhibitors.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fasciculations + immediate paralysis + no fade = Phase I depolarizing block

### B) Competitive inhibition of Nm nicotinic receptors at neuromuscular junction

This describes nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers such as Rocuronium, which always show fade and are fully reversible with neostigmine or sugammadex.

**Buzzword Combo:** No fasciculations + surgical paralysis + full reversal with sugammadex = nondepolarizing block

### D) Blockade of presynaptic acetylcholine release

This mechanism is seen in botulinum toxin poisoning, which causes flaccid paralysis but does not produce train-of-four fade patterns related to neuromuscular junction receptor desensitization.

**Buzzword Combo:** Descending paralysis + autonomic symptoms + toxin exposure = botulism
