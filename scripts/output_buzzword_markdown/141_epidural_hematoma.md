## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old man is struck in the temple by a baseball. He briefly loses consciousness, then awakens, walks home, and feels normal for 4 hours before becoming progressively confused and unresponsive. CT shows a hyperdense collection shaped like a biconvex lens beneath the squamous temporal bone that does NOT extend across the cranial suture lines. Which vessel is most likely torn?

## Buzzwords Used

Lateral head trauma (pterion); lucid interval; biconvex / lens-shaped hyperdense collection; does not cross suture lines

## Answer Options

A) Bridging cortical vein
B) Anterior communicating artery
C) Middle meningeal artery
D) Lenticulostriate artery

## Correct Answer

C) Middle meningeal artery

## Explanation

Trauma to the temple (pterion, where the skull is thinnest) classically fractures the temporal bone and lacerates the middle meningeal artery, producing an arterial epidural hematoma. Blood collects between the dura and the inner table of the skull, forming a biconvex (lens-shaped) hyperdensity that cannot cross suture lines because the dura is firmly attached there. The brief lucid interval reflects the time taken for the arterial bleed to expand and cause mass effect.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Temple trauma + lucid interval + biconvex / lens-shaped hyperdensity + does NOT cross suture lines = middle meningeal artery → epidural hematoma

## Wrong Options

### Bridging cortical vein

Bridging vein rupture produces a SUBDURAL hematoma: a crescent-shaped hyperdensity that DOES cross suture lines, typically in elderly patients or those on anticoagulation after low-impact trauma. Onset is usually subacute to chronic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elderly + low-impact fall + anticoagulation + crescent-shaped hyperdensity + crosses suture lines = bridging cortical vein → subdural hematoma

### Anterior communicating artery

Anterior communicating artery aneurysm rupture causes spontaneous subarachnoid hemorrhage with a "worst headache of life", not a post-traumatic lens-shaped epidural bleed.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden worst-headache-of-life + meningismus + ADPKD = anterior communicating artery → subarachnoid hemorrhage

### Lenticulostriate artery

Lenticulostriate arteries rupture in chronic hypertension at sites of Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms, producing hypertensive intraparenchymal hemorrhage in the basal ganglia, not traumatic epidural bleeding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic uncontrolled hypertension + basal ganglia (putamen) bleed + Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysm = lenticulostriate artery → intraparenchymal hemorrhage

## Related Concepts

Subdural hematoma (bridging veins, crescent shape); Subarachnoid hemorrhage (anterior communicating artery); Hypertensive intraparenchymal hemorrhage (lenticulostriate / Charcot-Bouchard)
