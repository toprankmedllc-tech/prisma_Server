## Buzzword Question

A consultant observes that a colleague has been making repeated prescribing errors over several days, appears dishevelled, and has missed multiple clinical meetings. Today, the colleague appears confused during a medication review for a high-risk patient. The consultant is uncertain whether this is due to burnout, substance use, or an underlying medical condition.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

## Buzzwords Used

Impaired performance ; Prescribing errors ; Unclear cause ; Patient safety concern

## Answer Options

A) Ignore the situation unless a confirmed diagnosis of impairment is made
B) Reduce the colleague’s workload informally without escalating the concern
C) Report the concern to the appropriate occupational/physician health service for formal assessment
D) Confront the colleague and request an immediate explanation in front of patients

## Correct Answer

C) Report the concern to the appropriate occupational/physician health service for formal assessment

## Explanation

When impairment is suspected but not confirmed, the appropriate step is formal referral for assessment through occupational or physician health services to ensure patient safety and proper support.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Suspected impairment + unclear cause + patient risk = formal assessment referral

## Wrong Options

### A) Ignore the situation unless a confirmed diagnosis of impairment is made

Waiting for confirmation places patients at risk and delays necessary intervention.

**Buzzword Combo:** Suspected impairment + inaction + confirmation bias = unsafe delay

### B) Reduce the colleague’s workload informally without escalating the concern

Informal adjustment does not address underlying impairment or ensure patient safety adequately.

**Buzzword Combo:** Impaired performance + informal management + no escalation = insufficient response

### D) Confront the colleague and request an immediate explanation in front of patients

Public confrontation is inappropriate and does not follow proper institutional pathways for handling impaired practitioners.

**Buzzword Combo:** Impairment + public confrontation + improper process = unprofessional handling
