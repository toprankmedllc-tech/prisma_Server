## Buzzword Question

Patient presents with progressive dysphagia to both solids and liquids, nocturnal regurgitation of undigested food, and weight loss. A barium swallow shows a “bird’s beak” appearance at the distal esophagus. Which neurotransmitter abnormality is most responsible for this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Progresive dypshagia; both solids and liquids;nocturnal regurgitation; weight loss; bird's beak 

## Answer Options

A) Increased nitric oxide and VIP release
B) Loss of inhibitory neurons producing nitric oxide and VIP
C) Increased acetylcholine release from the myenteric plexus
D) Degeneration of skeletal muscle in the upper esophagus

## Correct Answer

B) Loss of inhibitory neurons producing nitric oxide and VIP


## Explanation

Achalasia: ↓NO+↓VIP⇒failure of LES relaxation
Normally:

Nitric oxide (NO) → relaxes smooth muscle
VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide) → relaxes smooth muscle
Acetylcholine (ACh) and Substance P → promote contraction

dysphagia + weight loss + bird beak apperance = achalasia

Degeneration of the skeletal muscle in the upper esophagus . This condition is often associated with age-related changes, systemic neuromuscular diseases, or specific myogenic disorders such as Sarcopenic Dysphagia, inclusion body myositis (IBM), Myotonic Dystrophy, SLE.
