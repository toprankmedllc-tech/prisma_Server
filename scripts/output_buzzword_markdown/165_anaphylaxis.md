## Buzzword Question

A 17-year-old boy develops diffuse itching, wheezing, facial swelling, and hypotension within minutes of eating a peanut-containing dessert. Which of the following mechanisms most directly causes the immediate reaction?

## Buzzwords Used

17-year-old boy; symptoms within minutes of peanut exposure; itching, wheezing, facial swelling, and hypotension

## Answer Options

A) Cross-linking of IgE bound to mast cells
B) IgG binding to antigen on host cell surfaces
C) Immune complex deposition in small vessels
D) T-cell cytokine release after antigen exposure

## Correct Answer

A) Cross-linking of IgE bound to mast cells


## Explanation

Anaphylaxis is a type I hypersensitivity reaction caused by antigen cross-linking of IgE on mast cells and basophils. This triggers immediate degranulation with release of histamine and tryptase.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Minutes after allergen exposure + wheezing/hypotension + IgE cross-linking on mast cells = type I hypersensitivity

## Wrong Options

### B) IgG binding to antigen on host cell surfaces

This describes type II hypersensitivity, which involves IgG or IgM directed against cell surface or tissue antigens. It does not explain immediate allergic degranulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Antibody against cell surface antigen + complement activation + cell injury = type II hypersensitivity

### C) Immune complex deposition in small vessels

This describes type III hypersensitivity due to immune complex deposition. It causes complement-mediated inflammation rather than immediate IgE-mediated mast-cell degranulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Immune complex deposition + complement activation + tissue inflammation = type III hypersensitivity

### D) T-cell cytokine release after antigen exposure

This describes type IV hypersensitivity, a delayed T-cell-mediated reaction. It does not occur within minutes through IgE cross-linking.

**Buzzword Combo:** Delayed reaction + T-cell cytokines + macrophage activation = type IV hypersensitivity

## Related Concepts

Type II hypersensitivity; Type III hypersensitivity; Type IV hypersensitivity
