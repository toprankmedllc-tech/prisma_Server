## Buzzword Question

A child with recurrent dactylitis, autosplenectomy, and painful vaso-occlusive episodes is diagnosed with sickle cell disease. Sickling of erythrocytes in this disorder is most likely triggered by which of the following conditions?

## Buzzwords Used

dactylitis; autosplenectomy; vaso-occlusive crises; sickling; HbS

## Answer Options

A) Increased oxygen tension
B) Deoxygenation of hemoglobin
C) Increased spectrin production
D) Decreased erythropoietin levels

## Correct Answer

B. Deoxygenation of hemoglobin

## Explanation

Deoxygenated HbS polymerizes due to exposure of the abnormal hydrophobic valine residue in beta-globin, causing RBC sickling and vaso-occlusion. Sickling is promoted by hypoxia, acidosis, dehydration, and infection.



## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Deoxygenation + HbS polymerization + sickling + vaso-occlusive pain = Sickle cell disease

## Wrong Options

### A. Increased oxygen tension

Oxygenated HbS remains soluble and does not polymerize. High oxygen tension helps reverse sickling and is used therapeutically during vaso-occlusive crises.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low oxygen tension + acidosis + dehydration = Sickling triggers

### C. Increased spectrin production

Spectrin abnormalities are associated with hereditary spherocytosis, not sickle cell disease. Spectrin defects lead to membrane instability and spherocyte formation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spectrin deficiency + spherocytes + osmotic fragility = Hereditary spherocytosis

### D. Decreased erythropoietin levels

Low erythropoietin causes hypoproliferative anemia, commonly seen in chronic kidney disease. It does not cause HbS polymerization or vaso-occlusive crises.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low EPO + normocytic anemia + low reticulocytes = Anemia of chronic kidney disease
