## Buzzword Question

A new oral medication for chronic low back pain is introduced following several large randomized controlled trials. The studies demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in pain scores compared with standard therapy (mean reduction 1.3 points on a 10-point scale, p < 0.001). The medication is associated with fewer mild gastrointestinal side effects than existing treatments. A hospital formulary committee is deciding whether to add this medication as a first-line option for chronic low back pain.

Which of the following is the most important factor to consider when making this decision?

## Buzzwords Used

Chronic low back pain ; Randomized controlled trials ; Statistically significant reduction in pain score ; Formulary decision ; Clinical implementation

## Answer Options

A) Whether the medication shows a statistically significant reduction in pain scores
B) Whether the reduction in pain scores is large enough to be clinically meaningful in routine practice
C) Whether the medication has fewer mild gastrointestinal side effects compared with existing treatments
D) Whether patients are likely to adhere to a once-daily dosing regimen in real-world use

## Correct Answer

B) Whether the reduction in pain scores is large enough to be clinically meaningful in routine practice

## Explanation

Clinical adoption depends on whether the magnitude of benefit is meaningful to patients in real-world practice, not just whether results are statistically significant. A small reduction in pain score can reach statistical significance in large trials without providing meaningful clinical benefit.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Large RCT + statistically significant outcome + small absolute effect size + real-world decision = clinical significance determines adoption

## Wrong Options

### A) Whether the medication shows a statistically significant reduction in pain scores

Statistical significance alone does not determine clinical usefulness, especially when effect size is small.

**Buzzword Combo:** p-value significance + ignores effect size = misleading interpretation of efficacy

### C) Whether the medication has fewer mild gastrointestinal side effects compared with existing treatments

Safety profile is important but cannot override minimal clinical efficacy when determining first-line use.

**Buzzword Combo:** Safety advantage + minimal efficacy consideration = imbalanced evaluation

### D) Whether patients are likely to adhere to a once-daily dosing regimen in real-world use

Adherence affects effectiveness but does not replace assessment of whether the treatment benefit itself is clinically meaningful.

**Buzzword Combo:** Adherence factor + ignores effect size = indirect decision factor misuse
