## Buzzword Question

A 79-year-old man experiences a sudden episode of lightheadedness immediately after standing up from a seated position. His blood pressure drops from 135/82 mm Hg while supine to 108/64 mm Hg upon standing. Despite this significant drop in blood pressure, his heart rate only increases from 68/min to 70/min. A normal, age-related decline in which of the following is most responsible for this patient's blunted chronotropic response?

## Buzzwords Used

79-year-old man; lightheadedness standing up; blood pressure drops; heart rate only increases slightly

## Answer Options

A) Atrioventricular nodal conduction velocity
B) Parasympathetic vagal resting tone
C) Renin secretion from the afferent arteriole
D) Carotid sinus stretch receptor sensitivity

## Correct Answer

D) Carotid sinus stretch receptor sensitivity

## Explanation

Normal aging is associated with structural stiffening of the carotid sinus and aortic arch, leading to reduced baroreceptor (stretch receptor) sensitivity. When an elderly patient stands, gravity causes blood to pool in the lower extremities, leading to a transient drop in blood pressure (orthostasis). Because the baroreceptors are less sensitive, they fail to adequately detect this pressure drop and trigger the compensatory sympathetic reflex, resulting in a blunted heart rate response and symptomatic orthostatic hypotension.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Elderly + orthostatic hypotension + blunted reflex tachycardia = Reduced carotid sinus sensitivity

## Wrong Options

### A) Atrioventricular nodal conduction velocity

Although conduction cell degeneration occurs with age (causing a slightly lower resting HR and prolonged PR interval), the specific failure to rapidly initiate a reflex tachycardia in response to hypotension is a primary defect of the sensory baroreceptors

**Buzzword Combo:** Prolonged PR interval + elderly = AV node degeneration

### B) Parasympathetic vagal resting tone

A decrease in parasympathetic vagal tone would result in a higher baseline resting heart rate. The failure to mount a compensatory tachycardia is due to a failure to activate the sympathetic system, not an issue with baseline parasympathetic tone.

**Buzzword Combo:** High resting heart rate + loss of vagal tone = Decreased parasympathetic tone

### C) Renin secretion from the afferent arteriole

The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) helps regulate blood volume and pressure over hours to days. It does not mediate the immediate, second-to-second reflex heart rate changes required upon standing.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic blood pressure regulation + hours to days = Renin secretion
