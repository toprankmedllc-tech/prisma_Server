## Buzzword Question

A man with Huntington disease developed symptoms at age 38. His father became symptomatic at age 56 and his paternal grandfather at age 64. The mechanism best explaining the earlier age of onset in successive generations is:

## Buzzwords Used

Earlier onset in successive generations; paternal transmission; autosomal-dominant family history of Huntington disease

## Answer Options

A) Expansion of CAG trinucleotide repeats during spermatogenesis 
B) Genomic imprinting with silencing of the maternal allele
C) Mitochondrial inheritance through the maternal lineage
D) Variable expressivity due to environmental modifiers

## Correct Answer

A) Expansion of CAG trinucleotide repeats during spermatogenesis

## Explanation

Huntington disease shows anticipation: CAG repeats in the HTT gene expand during gametogenesis (particularly spermatogenesis), so successive generations inherit longer expansions, resulting in earlier symptom onset and often more severe disease, especially with paternal transmission.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Autosomal dominant + earlier onset in successive generations + paternal transmission + CAG repeats = anticipation → Huntington disease

## Wrong Options

### Genomic imprinting with silencing of the maternal allele

Imprinting underlies Prader-Willi (paternal 15q deletion) and Angelman (maternal 15q deletion) syndromes, not Huntington.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyperphagia + obesity + hypogonadism + chromosome 15 (paternal) = Prader-Willi (imprinting)

### Mitochondrial inheritance through the maternal lineage

Mitochondrial inheritance affects all children of an affected mother but no children of an affected father, opposite of this paternal transmission pattern. Mitochondrial disorders affect high-energy tissues (muscle, CNS).

**Buzzword Combo:** Maternal transmission + ragged red fibers + lactic acidosis = mitochondrial disease (MELAS/MERRF)

### Variable expressivity due to environmental modifiers

Variable expressivity describes differences in severity for a given mutation, but does not explain a consistent generational SHIFT in age of onset, that pattern is anticipation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Same mutation + different severity across family members (no generational shift) = variable expressivity

## Related Concepts

Fragile X syndrome (CGG repeat anticipation); Myotonic dystrophy (CTG repeat anticipation); Friedreich ataxia (GAA repeat in frataxin gene)
