## Buzzword Question

A 64-year-old man comes to the emergency department with severe substernal chest pain and diaphoresis for the past hour. An ECG shows prominent ST-segment elevations in leads V1 through V4. He is taken for an emergent percutaneous coronary intervention. To reach the most likely site of the culprit lesion, the catheter must be advanced from the ascending aorta directly into which of the following sequences of structures?

A) Right aortic sinus → right coronary artery → posterior descending artery 
B) Left aortic sinus → left main coronary artery → left anterior descending artery 
C) Left aortic sinus → left main coronary artery → left circumflex artery 
D) Right aortic sinus → right coronary artery → acute marginal artery


## Buzzwords Used

ST-elevations V1-V4

## Answer Options

A) Right aortic sinus → right coronary artery → posterior descending artery 
B) Left aortic sinus → left main coronary artery → left anterior descending artery 
C) Left aortic sinus → left main coronary artery → left circumflex artery 
D) Right aortic sinus → right coronary artery → acute marginal artery

## Correct Answer

B) Left aortic sinus → left main coronary artery → left anterior descending artery 

## Explanation

This patient's ECG shows ST-segment elevations in leads V1–V4, which is the classic presentation of an anteroseptal myocardial infarction. This territory is supplied by the left anterior descending (LAD) artery. To engage the left coronary circulation during catheterization, the catheter must pass from the ascending aorta into the left aortic sinus, through the left main coronary artery, and finally into the LAD to reach the occlusion.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

V1–V4 ST elevations + LAD occlusion = Anteroseptal MI

## Wrong Options

### A) Right aortic sinus → right coronary artery → posterior descending artery 

The posterior descending artery (PDA) supplies the inferior wall of the left ventricle. An occlusion of the RCA or PDA presents with ST-segment elevations in the inferior leads (II, III, aVF), not V1–V4.

**Buzzword Combo:** II, III, aVF ST elevations + RCA/PDA occlusion = Inferior wall MI

### C) Left aortic sinus → left main coronary artery → left circumflex artery 

The left circumflex (LCX) artery supplies the lateral wall of the left ventricle. An occlusion here would produce ST-segment elevations in the lateral leads (I, aVL, V5, V6), not the anterior precordial leads seen in this patient.

**Buzzword Combo:**  I, aVL, V5-V6 ST elevations + LCX occlusion = Lateral wall MI

### D) Right aortic sinus → right coronary artery → acute marginal artery

The acute marginal artery supplies the right ventricle. Occlusion of this branch causes a right ventricular infarction, presenting with hypotension, elevated jugular venous pressure, clear lungs, and ST elevation in right-sided precordial leads (e.g., V4R), rather than standard leads V1–V4.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypotension + JVD + clear lungs = Right Ventricular MI
