## Buzzword Question

A 19-year-old woman is brought to the ED after being found unresponsive. Empty pill bottles of opioids are found nearby. On exam she is bradypneic with a respiratory rate of 6/min. ABG shows pH 7.22, PaCO₂ 70 mmHg, HCO₃ 27 mEq/L. What is the primary acid-base disturbance?

## Buzzwords Used

Opioid overdose; Bradypnea; pH 7.22; PaCO₂ 70; HCO₃ 27; Acute respiratory acidosis

## Answer Options

A) Metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation
B) Acute respiratory acidosis with no metabolic compensation
C) Chronic respiratory acidosis with appropriate metabolic compensation
D) Mixed respiratory and metabolic acidosis

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

The pH is acidemic, PaCO₂ is elevated — confirming respiratory acidosis. For acute respiratory acidosis, HCO₃ rises only 1 mEq per 10 mmHg rise in PaCO₂. PaCO₂ rose ~30 mmHg above normal, so expected HCO₃ = 24 + 3 = 27 — but this rise is acute buffering, not true renal compensation, which takes 3–5 days. The clinical context (acute opioid OD) confirms this is acute.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Opioid OD + bradypnea + acute onset + HCO₃ only minimally elevated = Acute respiratory acidosis

## Wrong Options

### C) Chronic respiratory acidosis with appropriate metabolic compensation

This is the premature closure trap — the HCO₃ of 27 looks “appropriately elevated” which looks like compensation is complete. However, chronic compensation requires 3–5 days of renal bicarbonate retention; an acute opioid overdose has no time for this, making chronic compensation impossible here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elevated HCO₃ + high PaCO₂ = Premature assumption of chronic compensation without considering acuity of onset

### A) Metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation

In metabolic acidosis, the primary abnormality is a low HCO₃ with a compensatory low PaCO₂. Here the HCO₃ is normal/slightly elevated and PaCO₂ is high — the opposite pattern. The primary driver is respiratory, not metabolic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acidemic pH + abnormal labs = Incorrect assignment of primary disorder to metabolic axis when PaCO₂ elevation is the driver

### D) Mixed respiratory and metabolic acidosis

A mixed disorder would require both an independently elevated PaCO₂ and an independently low HCO₃. Here the HCO₃ is 27 — not low — so there is no independent metabolic acidosis present.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two abnormal values + acidosis = Incorrect mixed disorder label when HCO₃ is not independently depressed
