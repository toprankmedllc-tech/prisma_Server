## Buzzword Question

A 60-year-old man has 8 months of progressive limb weakness with visible muscle twitching, intrinsic hand muscle wasting, brisk lower extremity reflexes, upgoing plantars, and entirely preserved sensation and sphincter function. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Mixed UMN signs (hyperreflexia, upgoing plantars) and LMN signs (atrophy, fasciculations); preserved sensation; preserved sphincter function

## Answer Options

A) Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
B) Multiple sclerosis
C) Guillain-Barré syndrome
D) Myasthenia gravis

## Correct Answer

A) Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

## Explanation

Coexisting UMN and LMN signs without sensory loss or sphincter involvement is the signature of motor neuron disease (ALS).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

UMN signs + LMN signs + no sensory loss + no sphincter dysfunction = amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

## Wrong Options

### Multiple sclerosis

MS causes CNS demyelination with episodes separated in space and time, classically including optic neuritis and INO, and typically has sensory involvement, not pure motor signs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Young woman + INO + optic neuritis + oligoclonal bands = multiple sclerosis

### Guillain-Barré syndrome

GBS produces acute symmetric ascending flaccid paralysis with AREFLEXIA, opposite of the hyperreflexia and upgoing plantars seen here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ascending flaccid paralysis + areflexia + recent Campylobacter diarrhea = Guillain-Barré syndrome

### Myasthenia gravis

MG presents with fatigable ocular/bulbar weakness, normal reflexes, and decremental EMG response, not mixed UMN/LMN signs with atrophy and fasciculations.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fatigable ptosis + diplopia + decremental EMG + thymoma = myasthenia gravis (anti-AChR)

## Related Concepts

Spinal muscular atrophy (pure LMN, infants); Primary lateral sclerosis (pure UMN); Werdnig-Hoffmann disease (infantile SMA type 1)
