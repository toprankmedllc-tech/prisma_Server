## Buzzword Question

Obese man has a long history of gastroesophageal reflux disease with nightly regurgitation and chronic heartburn. Endoscopy shows salmon-colored mucosa extending upward from the gastroesophageal junction. Biopsy demonstrates intestinal-type goblet cells replacing the normal distal esophageal lining. Which of the following best describes the pathological process occurring in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Obese;GERD;goblet cells; heartburn

## Answer Options

A) Dysplasia
B) Hyperplasia
C) Metaplasia
D) Neoplasia

## Correct Answer

C) Metaplasia

## Explanation

Goblet cell metaplasia is a reversible, adaptive change where glandular or epithelial tissues develop an increased number of mucus-producing goblet cells in response to chronic injury, infection, or irritation. It is most common in the respiratory tract (causing excessive mucus in asthma/COPD) and the gastrointestinal tract (known as intestinal metaplasia).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Goblet cells + heartburn + regurgitaiton = GERD 

## Wrong Options

### A) Dysplasia

Dysplasia refers to the abnormal development or growth of cells, tissues, or bones, characterized by changes in cell size, shape, and organization. While often precancerous in adults (e.g., cervical or gastrointestinal), it can also refer to congenital developmental issues in children, such as skeletal or hip dysplasia.

**Buzzword Combo:** HPV + cervix = Cervical dysplasia 

### B) Hyperplasia


Hyperplasia is an increase in the number of cells in an organ or tissue, causing it to enlarge. While the cells appear normal under a microscope, they divide faster than usual, often in response to hormonal stimulation, compensation for tissue loss, or chronic irritation. It is generally a benign (non-cancerous) condition, though it can indicate a precancerous state.

**Buzzword Combo:** Exercise + Muscle = Musclar hypertrophy 

### D) Neoplasia

Neoplasia is the uncontrolled, abnormal growth of cells (a neoplasm or tumor) that persists even after the initiating stimulus stops. Neoplasms are characterized by clonal expansion, which can be benign (localized, slow-growing) or malignant (invasive, fast-growing/cancerous). Treatment involves surgery, radiation, or chemotherapy. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Oncogene mutation + Weight loss = Neoplasia
