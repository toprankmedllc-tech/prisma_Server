## Buzzword Question

A 74-year-old man with atrial fibrillation, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes is discharged after a 5-day hospitalization for pneumonia. His outpatient medications include warfarin, lisinopril, and metformin. The discharge medication list omits warfarin and lists metformin twice. Which of the following systems-level interventions would MOST directly prevent this type of error?


## Buzzwords Used

Discharge; medication list; warfarin omitted; metformin duplicated; transition of care; reconciliation error

## Answer Options

A) Structured pharmacist-led medication reconciliation at discharge comparing inpatient medications to the pre-admission medication list 
B) Having the attending physician sign all discharge summaries within 24 hours
 C) Requiring patients to memorize their home medication list before discharge 
D) Limiting hospital stays to reduce the number of medication changes made during admission

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Medication reconciliation is the formal process of comparing a patient's medication orders across care transitions to identify and resolve discrepancies (omissions, duplications, dose changes, contraindications). The Joint Commission mandates reconciliation at every care transition. Pharmacist-led reconciliation at discharge, comparing the inpatient medication orders to a verified pre-admission medication list (PAML), is the most evidence-based intervention to prevent omission errors (like warfarin) and duplication errors (like double metformin). This catches errors before the patient leaves the hospital.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Medication reconciliation = compare inpatient orders to pre-admission medication list at every transition; pharmacist-led = most effective; prevents omissions + duplications

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Having the attending physician sign all discharge summaries within 24 hours

Timely discharge summary completion improves communication but does not specifically address medication list accuracy at the point of discharge. Without a structured comparison process (reconciliation), errors in the medication list may persist even in a signed summary.

**Buzzword Combo:** Timely documentation ≠ medication reconciliation; reconciliation requires active comparison of medication lists, not just signing

### C) Requiring patients to memorize their home medication list before discharge



Patient recall is unreliable and subject to health literacy barriers, cognitive impairment, and stress. Placing reconciliation burden on patients is not a systems-level intervention and does not meet Joint Commission standards. The system should verify accuracy independently of patient memory.

**Buzzword Combo:** Patient-dependent recall = unreliable, not systems-level; reconciliation must be provider-led with verified source documents

### 3 D) Limiting hospital stays to reduce the number of medication changes made during admission

D) Limiting hospital stays to reduce the number of medication changes made during admission

**Buzzword Combo:** Limiting LOS ≠ medication reconciliation; omission of pre-existing medications occurs regardless of how many changes were made during admission
