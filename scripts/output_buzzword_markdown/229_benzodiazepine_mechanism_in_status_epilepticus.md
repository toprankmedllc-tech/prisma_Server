## Buzzword Question

A patient in generalized convulsive status epilepticus is given IV lorazepam, which terminates the seizure within 2 minutes. The drug acts by:

## Buzzwords Used

Lorazepam; status epilepticus termination; GABA-A receptor; chloride channel

## Answer Options

A) Blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in axonal membranes
B) Inhibiting NMDA glutamate receptors
C) Blocking T-type calcium channels in thalamic neurons
D) Binding the GABA-A receptor and increasing the frequency of chloride channel opening

## Correct Answer

D) Binding the GABA-A receptor and increasing the frequency of chloride channel opening

## Explanation

Benzodiazepines bind a specific allosteric site on the GABA-A receptor and increase the FREQUENCY of chloride channel opening, enhancing inhibitory tone and rapidly suppressing seizure activity. Barbiturates differ by increasing chloride channel DURATION.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Status epilepticus + IV lorazepam + rapid seizure termination + GABA-A potentiation = benzodiazepine (increases Cl channel opening FREQUENCY)

## Wrong Options

### Blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in axonal membranes

Describes phenytoin, fosphenytoin, carbamazepine, and lamotrigine. These are second-line agents for status epilepticus, not the mechanism of lorazepam.

**Buzzword Combo:** Second-line status epilepticus + Na channel blocker = fosphenytoin / phenytoin

### Inhibiting NMDA glutamate receptors

Ketamine is an NMDA antagonist sometimes used in super-refractory status, but it is not the mechanism of lorazepam.

**Buzzword Combo:** Super-refractory status + NMDA antagonist + dissociative anesthesia = ketamine

### Blocking T-type calcium channels in thalamic neurons

Ethosuximide blocks T-type calcium channels in thalamic relay neurons and is first-line for absence seizures, not for generalized convulsive status epilepticus

**Buzzword Combo:** 3 Hz spike-and-wave + child + absence seizures = T-type Ca channel blocker → ethosuximide

## Related Concepts

Barbiturates (increase Cl channel DURATION); Phenytoin (Na channel blocker, second-line); Flumazenil (benzodiazepine receptor antagonist for overdose)
