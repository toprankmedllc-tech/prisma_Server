## Buzzword Question

A medical student is reviewing the physiology of the neuromuscular junction using an isolated skeletal muscle preparation. During the experiment, an action potential travels normally down a motor neuron and reaches the presynaptic terminal. This depolarization opens voltage-gated calcium (Ca²⁺) channels, allowing calcium to enter the nerve terminal. Immediately afterward, synaptic vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release their contents into the synaptic cleft, leading to depolarization of the motor end plate and subsequent skeletal muscle contraction.
Which of the following substances is most directly released as a result of opening presynaptic voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels?

## Buzzwords Used

 Immediately afterward, synaptic vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release their contents into the synaptic cleft

## Answer Options

A. Acetylcholine
B. Calcium
C. Dopamine
D. Troponin

## Correct Answer

A. Acetylcholine

## Explanation

At the neuromuscular junction, arrival of an action potential at the presynaptic motor neuron terminal causes membrane depolarization. This opens voltage-gated calcium channels, allowing calcium to enter the nerve terminal. The substance directly released due to opening of presynaptic Ca²⁺ channels is acetylcholine.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Presynaptic Ca²⁺ influx + vesicle fusion + neuromuscular junction = acetylcholine release

## Wrong Options

### B. Calcium

Calcium enters the presynaptic terminal through voltage-gated channels but is not the neurotransmitter released into the synaptic cleft.

**Buzzword Combo:** Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels + intracellular signaling + vesicle exocytosis = trigger for neurotransmitter release

### C. Dopamine

Dopamine is a neurotransmitter in the central nervous system and is not the primary neurotransmitter at the skeletal neuromuscular junction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Substantia nigra + basal ganglia + reward pathways = dopamine

### D. Troponin

Troponin is a regulatory protein within skeletal muscle fibers that binds calcium during contraction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Thin filament + Ca²⁺ binding + tropomyosin movement = troponin activation
