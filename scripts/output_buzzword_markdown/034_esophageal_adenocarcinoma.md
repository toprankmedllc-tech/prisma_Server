## Buzzword Question

Man with longstanding Barrett esophagus develops progressive dysphagia and weight loss. Endoscopic biopsy reveals invasive adenocarcinoma of the distal esophagus. Which of the following molecular abnormalities most likely contributed to progression from Barrett esophagus to malignancy?

C) Increased acetylcholine release
D) Loss of dystrophin

## Wrong Options

### A) Decreased nitric oxide production

Decreased or impaired nitric oxide (NO) production in the esophagus is primarily linked to severe motility disorders, most notably achalasia, where the lack of NO synthase in the gastro-esophageal junction leads to failure of the lower esophageal sphincter (LES) to relax. Nitric oxide acts as a crucial inhibitory neurotransmitter that relaxes esophageal smooth muscle.

**Buzzword Combo:** Decrease NO + Regurgitation = Achlasia 

### C) Increased acetylcholine release

Increased acetylcholine (ACh) release in the esophagus is a key factor in the pathophysiology of various motility disorders and esophageal conditions, generally resulting in excessive contraction and spasm of the esophageal smooth muscle. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Increase ACH + dysphagia = Diffuse Esophageal Spasm

### D) Loss of dystrophin

Loss of dystrophin, a protein critical for stabilizing muscle fibers, has significant impacts on the esophagus in diseases like Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD). The absence of dystrophin leads to progressive weakening of the smooth and striated muscles in the esophagus, causing severe dysphagia (swallowing difficulties) and motility issues.

**Buzzword Combo:** Loss of dystrophin + dysphagia = Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy
