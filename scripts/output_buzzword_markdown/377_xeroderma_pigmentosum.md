## Buzzword Question

A child develops severe blistering sunburns after minimal sunlight exposure and later develops multiple skin cancers at a young age due to defective DNA nucleotide excision repair. What is the diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

 Blistering sunburns ; defective nucleotide excision repair ; early skin cancers


## Answer Options

 A) Ataxia-telangiectasia
 B) Xeroderma pigmentosum
 C) Bloom syndrome
 D) Fanconi anemia


## Correct Answer

 B) Xeroderma pigmentosum


## Explanation

Xeroderma pigmentosum is caused by defective nucleotide excision repair leading to inability to repair UV-induced DNA damage. Patients develop severe photosensitivity and early skin malignancies.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Blistering UV sensitivity + defective nucleotide excision repair + childhood skin cancers = Xeroderma pigmentosum


## Wrong Options

### A) Ataxia-telangiectasia


Ataxia-telangiectasia presents with cerebellar ataxia, telangiectasias, and IgA deficiency due to ATM mutation rather than UV sensitivity.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Ataxia + telangiectasias + recurrent infections = Ataxia-telangiectasia


### C) Bloom syndrome


Bloom syndrome involves defective DNA helicase activity with photosensitivity and increased malignancy risk but does not classically involve nucleotide excision repair defects.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Short stature + photosensitivity + DNA helicase defect = Bloom syndrome

###  D) Fanconi anemia


Fanconi anemia causes bone marrow failure and congenital abnormalities due to impaired DNA repair but not severe UV-induced skin damage.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Pancytopenia + radial defects + aplastic anemia = Fanconi anemia
