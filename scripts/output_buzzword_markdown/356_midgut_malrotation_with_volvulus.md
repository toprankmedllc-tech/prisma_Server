## Buzzword Question

A neonate with bilious vomiting undergoes upper GI series showing a corkscrew appearance of the duodenum and proximal jejunum, with the duodenojejunal junction located to the right of the midline. Which imaging finding identifies the underlying predisposing condition rather than its acute complication?

## Buzzwords Used

bilious vomiting neonate; corkscrew sign; duodenojejunal junction right of midline; upper GI series; intestinal obstruction

## Answer Options

A) Corkscrew appearance of duodenum and proximal jejunum
B) Abnormal position of duodenojejunal junction to the right of midline
C) Distended stomach with decreased distal bowel gas
D) Dilated proximal duodenum with air-fluid levels

## Correct Answer

B) Abnormal position of duodenojejunal junction to the right of midline

## Explanation

The abnormal position of the duodenojejunal junction indicates intestinal malrotation, the underlying anatomical defect. The corkscrew appearance reflects midgut volvulus, the acute complication arising from this abnormal fixation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Abnormal DJ junction position = malrotation

## Wrong Options

### A) Corkscrew appearance of duodenum and proximal jejunum

The corkscrew appearance represents twisting of bowel loops around the mesenteric axis, indicating midgut volvulus rather than the underlying congenital malrotation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Corkscrew sign = midgut volvulus 

### C) Distended stomach with decreased distal bowel gas

This is a nonspecific pattern of proximal intestinal obstruction and does not localize the anatomical abnormality or distinguish malrotation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Distension + distal gas paucity = bowel obstruction

### D) Dilated proximal duodenum with air-fluid levels

This pattern suggests proximal duodenal obstruction such as duodenal atresia or annular pancreas, not malrotation with volvulus.

**Buzzword Combo:** Double bubble / proximal dilation = duodenal obstruction

## Related Concepts

Duodenal atresia; Annular pancreas; Hirschsprung disease
