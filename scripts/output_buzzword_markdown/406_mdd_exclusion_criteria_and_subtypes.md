## Buzzword Question

A 38-year-old woman presents with 5 weeks of depressed mood, anhedonia, hypersomnia, hyperphagia with weight gain of 8 lbs, leaden paralysis, and extreme sensitivity to rejection that has caused her to quit two jobs this year. She brightens considerably when her children visit. TSH is 3.1 mIU/L (normal); toxicology is negative. She has never had a manic or hypomanic episode. Which subtype of her diagnosis best fits this presentation?

## Buzzwords Used

Depressed mood; anhedonia; hypersomnia; hyperphagia; weight gain; leaden paralysis; rejection sensitivity; mood brightens with positive events; no mania

## Answer Options

A) Atypical features (mood reactivity + ≥2 of: hypersomnia, hyperphagia, leaden paralysis, rejection sensitivity) 
B) Melancholic features (pervasive anhedonia, early morning awakening, psychomotor changes, excessive guilt, mood worst in morning) 
C) Psychotic features (hallucinations or delusions occurring during mood episode)
 D) Catatonic features (stupor, waxy flexibility, echolalia, or purposeless motor activity)

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

MDD with atypical features is characterized by mood reactivity (brightening in response to actual or potential positive events) PLUS ≥2 of the following: hypersomnia, hyperphagia/weight gain, leaden paralysis (heavy, leaden feeling in arms or legs), and rejection sensitivity causing significant social/occupational impairment. This patient demonstrates all four additional features plus clear mood reactivity (brightens when children visit). Atypical features are the most common MDD specifier on USMLE and are associated with younger age of onset, bipolar spectrum disorders, and a history of rejection sensitivity preceding the depressive episode

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Mood reactivity + hypersomnia + hyperphagia + leaden paralysis + rejection sensitivity = MDD with Atypical Features

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Melancholic features (pervasive anhedonia, early morning awakening, psychomotor changes, excessive guilt, mood worst in morning)

Melancholic features are characterized by pervasive anhedonia (inability to experience pleasure even transiently), mood that is qualitatively distinct from grief, early morning awakening, psychomotor agitation or retardation, and excessive guilt. The key distinguishing point is that melancholic features involve LOSS of mood reactivity  mood does not improve even temporarily with positive stimuli. This patient’s mood brightens with her children’s visits, which is the hallmark of atypical features and excludes melancholia

**Buzzword Combo:** Pervasive anhedonia + absent mood reactivity + early AM awakening + psychomotor change + excessive guilt = MDD with Melancholic Features

### 2: C) Psychotic features (hallucinations or delusions occurring during mood episode)

MDD with psychotic features requires the presence of hallucinations or delusions during the major depressive episode. No perceptual disturbances or fixed false beliefs are described in this vignette. The patient’s symptoms are fully explained by the atypical feature specifier without any psychotic components.

**Buzzword Combo:** Depressed mood + hallucinations or delusions = MDD with Psychotic Features (mood-congruent or mood-incongruent)


### 3: D) Catatonic features (stupor, waxy flexibility, echolalia, or purposeless motor activity)

Catatonic features include stupor, catalepsy, waxy flexibility, mutism, negativism, posturing, stereotypies, echolalia, and echopraxia. None of these motor/behavioral phenomena are described. The leaden paralysis in atypical features is a subjective heaviness in the limbs, distinct from the objective motor abnormalities of catatonia.


**Buzzword Combo:** Mutism + waxy flexibility + posturing + echolalia = Catatonic features (not atypical features)
