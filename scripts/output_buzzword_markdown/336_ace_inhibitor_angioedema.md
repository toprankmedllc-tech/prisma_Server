## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old woman on lisinopril due to hypertension for 3 years develops acute tongue and lip swelling without urticaria or pruritus. She has no prior allergic history. Which of the following best explains this reaction?

A) IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity to lisinopril
B) Bradykinin accumulation causing increased vascular permeability
C) Complement activation via the classical pathway
D) Direct mast cell degranulation without IgE sensitization

## Buzzwords Used

lisinopril; tongue and lip swelling

## Answer Options

A) IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity to lisinopril
B) Bradykinin accumulation causing increased vascular permeability
C) Complement activation via the classical pathway
D) Direct mast cell degranulation without IgE sensitization

## Correct Answer

B) Bradykinin accumulation causing increased vascular permeability

## Explanation

ACEi angioedema is bradykinin-mediated (non-allergic), not IgE-mediated. Bradykinin excess increases vascular permeability in deep dermal and submucosal tissues → non-pitting edema without urticaria. It can occur years after starting the drug, not just at initiation. Treatment: stop ACEi; B₂ receptor antagonist or C1-esterase inhibitor for severe cases. Epinephrine may help airway compromise but antihistamines and steroids are less effective (not histamine-mediated).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

ACEi + tongue/lip swelling + no urticaria or pruritus = Bradykinin angioedema (not IgE-mediated)

## Wrong Options

### A) IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity

 IgE-mediated reactions require prior sensitization and cause urticaria, pruritus, and bronchospasm features absent here. ACEi angioedema is bradykinin-mediated (non-IgE), explaining why antihistamines and steroids are ineffective.

**Buzzword Combo:** IgE-mediated angioedema + urticaria + pruritus + bronchospasm = Type I hypersensitivity

### C) Complement classical pathway activation

Complement-mediated angioedema occurs in hereditary angioedema (C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency and C1q, C4, C2 consumption). ACE inhibitor-induced angioedema does not involve classical complement pathway activation.

**Buzzword Combo:** ↓ C4 + C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency + hereditary angioedema = Complement-mediated

### D) Direct mast cell degranulation without IgE

Non-IgE mast cell degranulation (e.g., from opioids, vancomycin) releases histamine → urticaria + flushing. ACEi angioedema is not histamine driven it is bradykinin driven, which explains the lack of urticaria and poor response to antihistamines.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vancomycin + urticaria + flushing = Direct mast cell degranulation (Non-IgE mast cell degranulation)
