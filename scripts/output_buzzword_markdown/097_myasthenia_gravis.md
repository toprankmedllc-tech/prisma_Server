## Buzzword Question

A 28-year-old woman with several months of fatigable ptosis and diplopia receives intravenous edrophonium, with dramatic but brief improvement in eyelid position. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Dramatic improvement in eyelid position with IV edrophonium

## Answer Options

A) Multiple sclerosis
B) Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome
C) Myasthenia gravis
D) Botulism

## Correct Answer

C) Myasthenia gravis

## Explanation

Edrophonium is an ultrashort-acting acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. Transient improvement of ptosis and diplopia after IV administration (the historical Tensilon test) is the classic buzzword for MG because raising synaptic acetylcholine briefly overcomes anti-AChR antibody-mediated receptor loss.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Fatigable ptosis + improvement with edrophonium (Tensilon) = myasthenia gravis

## Wrong Options

### Multiple sclerosis

MS causes INO and optic neuritis in young women, but ptosis is not the classic finding and edrophonium does not produce symptomatic improvement.

**Buzzword Combo:** Young woman + INO + optic neuritis + oligoclonal bands = MS

### Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome

LEMS causes proximal weakness that briefly improves with EXERCISE (not edrophonium), with diminished reflexes and autonomic features.

**Buzzword Combo:** Proximal weakness improves with exercise + dry mouth + SCLC = LEMS

### Botulism

Botulinum toxin cleaves SNARE proteins and blocks presynaptic ACh release. Edrophonium does NOT reverse it because the problem is failure of vesicle docking, not AChE excess.

**Buzzword Combo:** Floppy baby + honey + descending paralysis = botulism

## Related Concepts

Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (anti-VGCC, small cell lung cancer); Pyridostigmine (long-acting AChE inhibitor for MG); Botulism (presynaptic SNARE protein cleavage)
