## Buzzword Question

A 20-year-old man presents to the emergency department after falling onto an outstretched hand while playing basketball. He complains of persistent wrist pain and difficulty gripping objects. Physical examination reveals mild swelling over the lateral wrist. Initial radiographs do not show a definite fracture, but the physician remains highly suspicious for a scaphoid injury.
Which of the following physical examination findings is most characteristic of this patient’s condition?

## Buzzwords Used

 falling onto an outstretched hand ;swelling over the lateral wrist

## Answer Options

A. Tenderness over the medial epicondyle of the humerus
B. Numbness over the palmar surface of the first three digits
C. Inability to extend the wrist against resistance
D.  Pain and tenderness in the anatomical snuffbox

## Correct Answer

D. Pain and tenderness in the anatomical snuffbox

## Explanation

This patient most likely has a scaphoid fracture, a common injury that occurs after a fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH injury). The classic physical examination finding is tenderness in the anatomical snuffbox.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

FOOSH injury + negative initial X-ray + anatomical snuffbox tenderness = scaphoid fracture

## Wrong Options

### A. Tenderness over the medial epicondyle of the humerus

Incorrect — Medial epicondylitis causes pain at the elbow rather than the wrist.


**Buzzword Combo:** Repetitive wrist flexion + medial elbow pain = golfer’s elbow

### B. Numbness over the palmar surface of the first three digits

Incorrect — This finding is more consistent with median nerve compression such as carpal tunnel syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Nighttime hand paresthesias + thenar weakness = carpal tunnel syndrome

### C. Inability to extend the wrist against resistance

Incorrect — This suggests radial nerve injury affecting wrist extensors.


**Buzzword Combo:** Wrist drop + impaired wrist extension = radial nerve palsy
