## Buzzword Question

A 46-year-old woman presents with chronic shoulder pain that worsens when reaching overhead. Physical examination reveals weakness during abduction and a positive drop-arm test. An MRI of the shoulder is obtained and confirms a rotator cuff tear.
Which of the following MRI findings is most consistent with this diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

positive drop-arm test; ; rotator cuff tear

## Answer Options

A. Fluid-filled gap within the supraspinatus tendon
B. Crescent-shaped meniscal tear within the glenohumeral joint
C. Anterior displacement of the humeral head from the glenoid cavity
D. Diffuse ossification of the coracoacromial ligament

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Rotator cuff tears, especially of the supraspinatus tendon, classically appear on MRI as a fluid-filled defect or discontinuity within the tendon.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Positive drop-arm test + fluid-filled supraspinatus defect on MRI = rotator cuff tear

## Wrong Options

### B. Crescent-shaped meniscal tear within the glenohumeral joint

Incorrect — Menisci are found in the knee, not the shoulder joint.

**Buzzword Combo:** Twisting knee injury + locking sensation = meniscal tear

### C. Anterior displacement of the humeral head from the glenoid cavity

Incorrect — This finding suggests anterior shoulder dislocation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Abducted externally rotated arm + anterior humeral displacement = anterior shoulder dislocation

### D. Diffuse ossification of the coracoacromial ligament

Incorrect — This is not a classic imaging feature of rotator cuff tears.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic impingement symptoms + acromial spurring = shoulder impingement syndrome
