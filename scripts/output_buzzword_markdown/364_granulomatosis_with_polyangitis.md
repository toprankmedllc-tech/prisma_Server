## Buzzword Question

A 48-year-old man presents with recurrent episodes of sinusitis, epistaxis, and chronic nasal crusting. He also reports hemoptysis and hematuria. Chest imaging shows multiple cavitary lung nodules.  What is the most likely underlying mechanism of pathognesis?

A) c-ANCA (PR3) neutrophil activation leading to  necrotizing granulomatous vasculitis

## Correct Answer

A) c-ANCA (PR3) neutrophil activation leading to necrotizing granulomatous vasculitis

## Explanation

Granulomatosis with polyangiitis is caused by c-ANCA (anti-PR3) antibodies that activate neutrophils, leading to necrotizing granulomatous inflammation of small vessels. This explains the triad of upper airway disease, lung cavitary lesions, and renal involvement.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

c-ANCA + PR3 + sinusitis + cavitary lung lesions + hematuria

## Wrong Options

### B) p-ANCA vasculitis

p-ANCA is associated with microscopic polyangiitis and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, which lack upper airway granulomatous disease and cavitary lung lesions.

**Buzzword Combo:** p-ANCA + MPO + non-granulomatous vasculitis

### C) IgE eosinophilic inflammation

This describes asthma or EGPA, which features asthma and eosinophilia, not PR3-positive vasculitis with renal and cavitary lung involvement.

**Buzzword Combo:** asthma + eosinophilia + allergic disease

### D) Anti-GBM antibody disease

Goodpasture syndrome causes pulmonary hemorrhage and glomerulonephritis but lacks upper airway involvement and granulomatous inflammation.

**Buzzword Combo:** hemoptysis + hematuria + linear IF + anti-GBM
