## Buzzword Question

A 28-year-old man presents with 2 years of progressive bilateral hearing loss, bilateral tinnitus, and unsteady gait. MRI of the brain shows enhancing masses at BOTH cerebellopontine angles arising from cranial nerve VIII. Family history is notable for similar tumors in his father. The most likely underlying genetic disorder is:

## Buzzwords Used

Enhancing masses at BOTH cerebellopontine angles arising from cranial nerve VIII

## Answer Options

A) Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)
B) Neurofibromatosis type 2 (NF2)
C) Von Hippel-Lindau disease
D) Tuberous sclerosis complex

## Correct Answer

B) Neurofibromatosis type 2 (NF2)

## Explanation

Bilateral vestibular schwannomas (acoustic neuromas) are essentially pathognomonic for NF2, an autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutation of the NF2 gene on chromosome 22, which encodes the tumor suppressor MERLIN. NF2 patients are also predisposed to meningiomas and ependymomas.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Bilateral vestibular schwannomas (acoustic neuromas) + chromosome 22 + merlin = NF2

## Wrong Options

### Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)

NF1 (von Recklinghausen disease) is caused by mutation in the NF1 gene on chromosome 17. Features include café-au-lait macules, axillary/inguinal freckling, cutaneous neurofibromas, Lisch nodules of the iris, OPTIC NERVE GLIOMAS, and pheochromocytomas. NF1 is NOT associated with bilateral vestibular schwannomas.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Café-au-lait + Lisch nodules + optic glioma + chromosome 17 = NF1

### Von Hippel-Lindau disease

VHL (chromosome 3) features CNS and retinal HEMANGIOBLASTOMAS, bilateral renal cell carcinomas (clear cell), pheochromocytomas, and pancreatic cysts. Cerebellar hemangioblastomas are sometimes associated with secondary erythrocytosis from EPO production.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cerebellar hemangioblastoma + clear cell RCC + pheochromocytoma + chromosome 3 = VHL

### Tuberous sclerosis complex

TSC (TSC1/TSC2 mutations) features ash-leaf hypopigmented macules, facial angiofibromas, cardiac RHABDOMYOMAS, renal angiomyolipomas, and SUBEPENDYMAL GIANT CELL ASTROCYTOMAS. It is not associated with vestibular schwannomas.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ash-leaf spots + cardiac rhabdomyoma + SEGA = tuberous sclerosis
