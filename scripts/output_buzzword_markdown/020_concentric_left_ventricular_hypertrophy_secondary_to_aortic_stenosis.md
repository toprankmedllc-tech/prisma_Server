## Buzzword Question

A patient with a harsh systolic ejection murmur radiating to the carotids is found to have significant thickening of the left ventricular free wall. Which of the following cellular adaptations most likely occurred in this patient's myocardium?

## Buzzwords Used

Harsh systolic ejection murmur; radiating to carotids; thickening of left ventricular free wall

## Answer Options

A) Addition of sarcomeres in series
B) Addition of sarcomeres in parallel
C) Myocardial fatty infiltration
D) Ventricular myocyte hyperplasia

## Correct Answer

B) Addition of sarcomeres in parallel

## Explanation

Pressure overload (e.g., aortic stenosis, chronic hypertension) drastically increases ventricular wall stress. To compensate according to Laplace's Law, the myocardium undergoes concentric hypertrophy, characterized by the addition of sarcomeres in parallel to increase wall thickness and reduce stress.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Systolic murmur (AS) + pressure overload + thickened LV wall  = Addition of sarcomeres in parallel (Concentric hypertrophy)

## Wrong Options

### A) Addition of sarcomeres in series

Addition of sarcomeres in series causes eccentric hypertrophy (chamber dilation), which occurs in response to volume overload states like aortic regurgitation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diastolic murmur (AR) + volume overload + dilated chamber = Addition of sarcomeres in series

### C) Myocardial fatty infiltration

Myocardial fatty infiltration is a feature of arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, which presents with arrhythmias and right heart failure, not secondary to pressure overload. 

**Buzzword Combo:**  Right heart failure + arrhythmias + sudden death = Fatty infiltration 

### D) Ventricular myocyte hyperplasia

Adult ventricular myocytes are permanent cells and generally cannot undergo hyperplasia (cell division); they adapt to stress via hypertrophy (increase in size).

**Buzzword Combo:** Permanent cells + increased stress = Hypertrophy, not hyperplasia
