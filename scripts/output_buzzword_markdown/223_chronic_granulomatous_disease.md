## Buzzword Question

A 5-year-old boy has recurrent skin abscesses and pneumonia caused by Staphylococcus aureus and Aspergillus species. Biopsy of an infected lymph node shows organized collections of macrophages surrounding persistent microbes. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

5-year-old boy; recurrent skin abscesses and pneumonia; Staphylococcus aureus and Aspergillus infections; organized collections of macrophages surrounding persistent microbes

## Answer Options

A) Chronic granulomatous disease
B) Severe combined immunodeficiency
C) Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia
D) Leukocyte adhesion deficiency

## Correct Answer

A) Chronic granulomatous disease


## Explanation

Chronic granulomatous disease is caused by NADPH oxidase deficiency, leading to defective respiratory burst and impaired phagocyte killing. Patients classically develop recurrent bacterial and fungal infections, especially with catalase-positive organisms.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

5-year-old boy + recurrent Staphylococcus aureus/Aspergillus infections + granuloma formation = Chronic granulomatous disease

## Wrong Options

### B) Severe combined immunodeficiency

SCID causes severe T- and B-cell dysfunction with recurrent infections of all types beginning from birth or early infancy. The clue pattern here is a phagocyte killing defect with bacterial and fungal infections.

**Buzzword Combo:** 2-month-old infant + infections since neonatal period + viral/fungal/bacterial/protozoal infections = Severe combined immunodeficiency

### C) Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia

Bruton agammaglobulinemia causes recurrent encapsulated bacterial infections starting around 6 months of age due to failed B-cell maturation. It does not primarily involve defective oxidative burst.

**Buzzword Combo:** 6-month-old boy + recurrent encapsulated bacterial infections + absent tonsils/lymph nodes = Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia

### D) Leukocyte adhesion deficiency

Leukocyte adhesion deficiency causes defective neutrophil migration, delayed umbilical cord separation, and recurrent infections without pus. This question instead points to impaired intracellular killing after phagocytosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Delayed umbilical cord separation + recurrent infections without pus + neutrophilia = Leukocyte adhesion deficiency
