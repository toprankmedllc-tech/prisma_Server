## Buzzword Question

A 31-year-old woman undergoes laparoscopic cholecystectomy under general anesthesia. To facilitate intubation and muscle relaxation, a neuromuscular blocking agent is administered. The patient has no muscle fasciculations after injection and develops flaccid paralysis.

At the end of surgery, the anesthesiologist administers a reversal agent that rapidly restores muscle strength within minutes without causing bradycardia or increased secretions. The patient regains spontaneous breathing almost immediately.

Which of the following drugs was most likely used for neuromuscular blockade?

## Buzzwords Used

general anesthesia ; no fasciculations ; flaccid paralysis ; rapid reversal ; no muscarinic side effects ; laparoscopic surgery ; immediate recovery of muscle strength

## Answer Options

A) Succinylcholine

B) Rocuronium

C) Atropine

D) Neostigmine

## Correct Answer

B) Rocuronium

## Explanation

Nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers such as Rocuronium cause flaccid paralysis without fasciculations by competitively blocking Nm nicotinic receptors. They can be rapidly and specifically reversed by Sugammadex, which encapsulates the drug and restores neuromuscular transmission.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

No fasciculations + surgical paralysis + rapid reversal without muscarinic effects = rocuronium + sugammadex

## Wrong Options

### A) Succinylcholine

Succinylcholine causes fasciculations followed by depolarizing blockade and is not reversed by sugammadex. It is also associated with hyperkalemia and malignant hyperthermia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fasciculations + hyperkalemia risk + malignant hyperthermia = succinylcholine

### C) Atropine

Atropine is a muscarinic antagonist used to treat bradycardia and secretions, not to induce or reverse neuromuscular paralysis. It does not affect Nm receptors.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bradycardia + salivation + cholinergic toxicity = atropine

### D) Neostigmine

Neostigmine reverses nondepolarizing blockade but requires co-administration of antimuscarinics and is slower than sugammadex. It does not directly bind or inactivate the neuromuscular blocker.

**Buzzword Combo:** Nondepolarizing block + bradycardia prevention + muscarinic effects = neostigmine + atropine/glycopyrrolate
