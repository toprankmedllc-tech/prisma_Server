## Buzzword Question

A new national CT screening program for lung cancer is implemented. Five years later, published data show that patients diagnosed through screening have a median survival of 72 months, compared to 18 months in patients diagnosed after presenting with symptoms. However, analysis of national mortality registries shows no change in the age-standardized lung cancer death rate. This phenomenon is most consistent with:

## Buzzwords Used

Earlier diagnosis; longer apparent survival; unchanged mortality; screening program; survival time from diagnosis artificially extended

## Answer Options

A) Lead-time bias 
B) Length-time bias 
C) Recall bias 
D) Confounding by health-conscious behavior

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Lead-time bias occurs when screening detects disease earlier in its natural history, extending the measured survival time from diagnosis to death without actually delaying death. Patients are diagnosed earlier (lead time), so the clock starts earlier, making survival appear longer, but the actual time of death is unchanged. The absence of any change in population mortality confirms no true benefit; the survival difference is entirely an artifact of earlier diagnosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Earlier detection + longer apparent survival from diagnosis + unchanged population mortality = Lead-time bias

## Wrong Options

### B) Length-time bias

Length-time bias occurs when screening preferentially detects slower-growing, less aggressive tumors (because they have a longer preclinical detectable phase). Screen-detected tumors then appear to have better prognoses not because screening helped but because less aggressive cancers were selectively found. The key difference from lead-time bias: length bias involves detecting different (less aggressive) tumors; lead-time bias involves detecting the same tumors earlier. Both can produce better apparent survival without improving mortality.

**Buzzword Combo:** Screening detects slow-growing less aggressive tumors + apparently better prognosis = Length-time bias

### C) Recall bias

Recall bias is a retrospective case-control study phenomenon involving differential self-recall of past exposures. It has no application to this prospective screening cohort comparison of survival outcomes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Retrospective recall + cases report more exposure than controls = Recall bias (case-control specific)

### D) Confounding by health-conscious behavior

While it is true that people who attend screening programs may be healthier overall (healthy volunteer effect), the question specifically describes the scenario where unchanged population mortality is documented  confirming that the longer survival is a measurement artifact, not a real survival benefit from either screening or healthier behaviors. Lead-time bias most precisely explains the mechanism.


**Buzzword Combo:** Healthier screening participants + better outcomes unrelated to intervention = Healthy volunteer effect / healthy user bias
