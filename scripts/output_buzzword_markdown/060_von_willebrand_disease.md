## Buzzword Question

A young woman has heavy menstrual bleeding and prolonged bleeding from a small finger cut. Her father has a history of frequent nosebleeds. Laboratory studies show normal platelet count, normal PT, and normal PTT. Ristocetin cofactor assay is abnormal. Which of the following proteins is deficient or dysfunctional?

## Buzzwords Used

Autosomal dominant bleeding + abnormal ristocetin + normal platelets = von Willebrand disease

## Answer Options

A. von Willebrand factor
B. Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa
C. Factor IX
D. ADAMTS13

## Correct Answer

A. von Willebrand factor


## Explanation

von Willebrand disease is autosomal dominant and causes defective platelet adhesion. vWF normally binds platelet glycoprotein Ib and exposed collagen. PTT may be increased because vWF stabilizes factor VIII, but PTT can also be normal because factor VIII stabilization is only a secondary role of vWF.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Autosomal dominant bleeding + abnormal ristocetin + normal platelets = von Willebrand disease

## Wrong Options

### B. Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa


GpIIb/IIIa deficiency causes Glanzmann thrombasthenia. The problem is platelet aggregation, not platelet adhesion.

**Buzzword Combo:** GpIIb/IIIa defect + impaired aggregation = Glanzmann thrombasthenia

### C. Factor IX


Factor IX deficiency causes hemophilia B, which presents with deep tissue bleeding and hemarthroses rather than prominent mucosal bleeding.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemarthroses + increased PTT + factor IX deficiency = hemophilia B

### D. ADAMTS13


ADAMTS13 deficiency causes thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura due to accumulation of large vWF multimers, leading to platelet-rich microthrombi.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fever + neurologic symptoms + thrombocytopenia + renal injury = TTP
