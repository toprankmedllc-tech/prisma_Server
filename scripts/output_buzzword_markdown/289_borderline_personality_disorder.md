## Buzzword Question

A 45-year-old woman with a long history of unstable relationships, recurrent self-harm by cutting, and multiple suicide attempts presents after an overdose. She states she felt “completely abandoned” after her therapist went on vacation. She describes chronic feelings of emptiness and reports that she has idealized and then suddenly despised multiple people in her life. Which of the following personality disorders is most consistent with this presentation?

## Buzzwords Used

Unstable relationships; recurrent self-harm; suicide attempts; abandonment fears; chronic emptiness; idealization and devaluation 

## Answer Options

A) Narcissistic personality disorder
B) Histrionic personality disorder
C) Borderline personality disorder
D) Antisocial personality disorder

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is defined by fear of abandonment, identity disturbance, impulsivity, recurrent self-harm/suicidality, chronic emptiness, and splitting (idealization→devaluation). The trigger of therapist absence precipitating overdose is a classic BPD scenario.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Fear of abandonment + self-harm + splitting (idealize/devalue) + chronic emptiness = Borderline PD

## Wrong Options

### A) Narcissistic personality disorder

Narcissistic PD involves grandiosity, entitlement, and lack of empathy. While these patients may devalue others, they do not characteristically engage in self-harm or have profound abandonment fears driving suicidality.

**Buzzword Combo:** Devaluation of others → narcissistic PD (ignores self-harm + abandonment fear = key BPD features missed)

### B) Histrionic personality disorder

Histrionic PD features attention-seeking, emotional dramatization, and seductiveness, but lacks the self-harm, suicide attempts, and intense abandonment fears central to this case. Students may be drawn to “dramatic” presentation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Emotional + dramatic behavior → histrionic (ignores self-harm + splitting + abandonment = BPD-defining features)

### D) Antisocial personality disorder

Antisocial PD requires a history of conduct disorder before age 15, deceitfulness, and disregard for others’ rights — not self-directed harm driven by abandonment. This patient’s harm is self-directed, not toward others.

**Buzzword Combo:** Repeated harmful behavior → antisocial (ignores self-directed harm + abandonment trigger = wrong target of behavior)
