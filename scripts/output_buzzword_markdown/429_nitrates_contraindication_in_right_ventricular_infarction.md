## Buzzword Question

A 67-year-old man presents with an acute inferior STEMI. Blood pressure on arrival is 84/52 mmHg, jugular venous pressure is elevated, and lung fields are clear bilaterally. After receiving sublingual nitroglycerin for chest pain, his blood pressure drops further to 60/40 mmHg. A right-sided ECG shows ST elevation in lead V4R.
Which of the following best explains the hemodynamic deterioration following nitroglycerin?

A) Nitroglycerin-induced coronary vasospasm 
B) Preload reduction worsening LV underfilling  
C) Reflex bradycardia reducing cardiac output 
D) Direct negative inotropic effect on the right ventricle


## Buzzwords Used

Inferior STEM; Elevated JVP; Clear Lungs; Nitroglycerin; BP drops to 60/40; ST elevation in V4R

## Answer Options

A) Nitroglycerin-induced coronary vasospasm 
B) Preload reduction worsening LV underfilling  
C) Reflex bradycardia reducing cardiac output 
D) Direct negative inotropic effect on the right ventricle


## Correct Answer

B) Preload reduction worsening LV underfilling  

## Explanation

In right ventricular infarction, the ischemic RV cannot generate adequate forward flow through the pulmonary circulation to fill the left ventricle. LV filling is therefore entirely dependent on maintaining RV preload. Nitroglycerin's primary action is venodilation, which reduces venous return and critically lowers RV preload. 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

RV infarction + nitroglycerin = absolute contraindication

## Wrong Options

### A) Nitroglycerin-induced coronary vasospasm 

Nitroglycerin is a vasodilator used to treat coronary vasospasm. The hemodynamic collapse here is from preload reduction, not vasospasm. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Coronary vasospasm + ST elevation at rest + normal coronaries = Prinzmetal angina (nitroglycerin is the treatment)

### C) Reflex bradycardia reducing cardiac output 

Nitroglycerin typically causes reflex tachycardia via baroreceptor activation. Bradycardia in inferior STEMI results from AV nodal ischemia, not from nitroglycerin's hemodynamic effect. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Bradycardia + inferior MI = AV nodal ischemia (RCA supplies AV node in 90% of individuals)

### D) Direct negative inotropic effect on the RV 

Nitroglycerin has no direct negative inotropic effect on the myocardium. Its mechanism is smooth muscle relaxation via cGMP, not myocardial depression. 

**Buzzword Combo:**  Beta-blockers and non-dihydropyridine CCBs = Negative inotropy
