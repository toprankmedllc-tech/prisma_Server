## Buzzword Question

A 42-year-old man presents to the clinic with difficulty walking several weeks after sustaining a deep laceration to the posterior aspect of his knee during a construction accident. He reports weakness when trying to stand on his toes and difficulty pushing off while walking upstairs. He also complains of numbness involving the sole of the foot. Physical examination reveals weakness of plantarflexion and inversion of the foot. Ankle dorsiflexion is preserved. Sensory testing demonstrates decreased sensation over the plantar surface of the foot.
Injury to which of the following nerves is the most likely cause of this patient’s symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

weakness when trying to stand on his toes; numbness involving the sole of the foot; weakness of plantarflexion and inversion of the foot

## Answer Options

A. Common peroneal nerve
B. Femoral nerve
C. Obturator nerve
D. Tibial nerve

## Correct Answer

D. Tibial nerve

## Explanation

This patient most likely has injury to the tibial nerve, which supplies the posterior compartment of the leg and most intrinsic muscles of the foot. Patients may develop: Weak plantarflexion ,Difficulty standing on tiptoes ,Impaired foot inversion ,Sensory loss over the sole of the foot 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Posterior knee injury + inability to plantarflex + sole numbness = tibial nerve injury

## Wrong Options

### A. Common peroneal nerve

Incorrect — Common peroneal nerve injury causes foot drop with weakness of dorsiflexion and sensory loss over the dorsum of the foot.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fibular neck injury + foot drop = common peroneal neuropathy

### B. Femoral nerve

Incorrect — Femoral nerve injury causes weak knee extension and decreased patellar reflex.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak quadriceps + reduced patellar reflex = femoral neuropathy

### C. Obturator nerve

Incorrect — Obturator nerve injury causes weakness with thigh adduction and medial thigh sensory loss.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak thigh adduction + medial thigh numbness = obturator neuropathy
