## Buzzword Question

A 28-year-old woman develops throat tightness, wheezing, generalized hives, and lightheadedness shortly after a bee sting. The immediate phase of this reaction is most associated with release of which of the following substances from mast cells and basophils?

## Buzzwords Used

28-year-old woman; shortly after a bee sting; throat tightness, wheezing, generalized hives, and lightheadedness

## Answer Options

A) Histamine and tryptase
B) IL-2 and interferon-gamma
C) C3b and IgG
D) IL-8 and leukotriene B4

## Correct Answer

A) Histamine and tryptase


## Explanation

The immediate phase of anaphylaxis is caused by IgE-mediated mast-cell and basophil degranulation. This releases histamine and tryptase, producing rapid allergic symptoms.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Bee sting + immediate hives/wheezing + histamine and tryptase release = type I hypersensitivity

## Wrong Options

### B) IL-2 and interferon-gamma

These cytokines are linked to T-cell activation and delayed cell-mediated inflammation. They do not explain immediate IgE-mediated allergic symptoms.

**Buzzword Combo:** Activated T cells + IL-2/interferon-gamma + delayed inflammation = type IV hypersensitivity

### C) C3b and IgG

C3b and IgG are major opsonins that promote phagocytosis. They are not the key mediators released during mast-cell degranulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bacterial coating + enhanced phagocytosis + C3b and IgG = opsonization

### D) IL-8 and leukotriene B4

IL-8 and leukotriene B4 recruit neutrophils during acute inflammation. They are not the classic immediate mast-cell granule mediators in anaphylaxis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neutrophil recruitment + chemotaxis + IL-8/leukotriene B4 = acute inflammation
