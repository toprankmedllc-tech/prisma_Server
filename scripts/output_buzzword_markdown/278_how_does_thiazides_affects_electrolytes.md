## Buzzword Question

A 48-year-old woman with a history of recurrent calcium oxalate nephrolithiasis and idiopathic hypercalciuria presents to her primary care physician. She is highly motivated to prevent future painful stone episodes. The physician decides to start her on a maintenance diuretic regimen that will help lower her urinary calcium excretion while managing her mild borderline hypertension. Weeks later, she returns for lab work which reveals mild hypokalemia and elevated serum calcium levels. Which of the following medications was most likely prescribed to this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Recurrent calcium oxalate nephrolithiasis; Idiopathic hypercalciuria; Lower urinary calcium; Hypokalemia; Elevated serum calcium

## Answer Options

A) Furosemide
B) Acetazolamide
C) Hydrochlorothiazide
D) Spironolactone

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

Thiazide diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide) inhibit the NaCl cotransporter in the early distal convoluted tubule. This decreases intracellular sodium, which enhances the activity of the basolateral Na+ /Ca 2+ exchanger, driving calcium reabsorption out of the lumen and into the blood, making them ideal for treating idiopathic hypercalciuria and calcium stones ("Thiazides save calcium").

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Calcium oxalate stones + Lower urinary calcium + Elevated serum calcium = Thiazide effect → Hydrochlorothiazide.

## Wrong Options

### A) Furosemide

Furosemide is a loop diuretic that inhibits the Na+/K+ /2Cl− cotransporter, increasing urinary calcium excretion ("Loops lose calcium"), which would make her recurrent calcium stones worse.

**Buzzword Combo:** Calcium oxalate stones + Lower urinary calcium + Elevated serum calcium = Confusing loop diuretics with thiazides → Furosemide.

### B) Acetazolamide

Acetazolamide alkalinizes the urine by causing bicarbonate wasting, which actually promotes the precipitation of calcium phosphate stones in the renal tubules and causes hypokalemia without increasing serum calcium.

**Buzzword Combo:** Calcium oxalate stones + Lower urinary calcium + Elevated serum calcium = Confusing urine alkalization with calcium retention → Acetazolamide.

### D) Spironolactone

Spironolactone is a potassium-sparing diuretic that blocks aldosterone receptors in the cortical collecting tubule, which would cause hyperkalemia rather than the hypokalemia noted in the patient's lab results.

**Buzzword Combo:** Calcium oxalate stones + Lower urinary calcium + Elevated serum calcium = Selecting a drug that causes hyperkalemia instead of hypokalemia → Spironolactone.
