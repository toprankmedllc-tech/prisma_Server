## Buzzword Question

A medical student is reviewing a glucose titration curve and notices that glucosuria begins at a plasma glucose of ~180 mg/dL in one nephron, but not until ~220 mg/dL in another. The overall curve shows a gradual rather than sharp rise in excreted glucose before the transport maximum (Tₘ) is reached. Which of the following best describes the mechanism responsible for this gradual rise?

## Buzzwords Used

Titration curve; gradual rise; before Tₘ; heterogeneity of nephrons

## Answer Options

A) Downregulation of SGLT2 transporters at high glucose concentrations
B) Saturation of all nephron transporters simultaneously at the renal threshold
C) Variable Tₘ across individual nephrons due to differing transporter capacities
D) Increased GFR overwhelming a single uniform transport maximum

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

The splay phenomenon occurs because individual nephrons have slightly different transport maximums. Some nephrons saturate and spill glucose at lower plasma glucose levels while others continue reabsorbing, creating a gradual rather than sharp inflection on the titration curve between the renal threshold and the overall Tₘ.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Nephron heterogeneity + different Tₘ points + gradual curve = Splay phenomenon

## Wrong Options

### B) Simultaneous saturation at renal threshold

This is the premature closure trap — students assume all nephrons behave identically and saturate at once, which would produce a sharp rise. The splay exists precisely because nephrons are NOT uniform in their transport capacity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Renal threshold + all nephrons saturate together = sharp rise (not splay)

###  A) SGLT2 downregulation

SGLT2 transporters are not downregulated by high glucose concentrations under physiologic conditions. The splay is a structural/anatomical phenomenon related to nephron heterogeneity, not transporter regulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** High glucose + SGLT2 change = splay (incorrect mechanism)

### D) Increased GFR overwhelming uniform Tₘ

Increased GFR shifts the threshold leftward (as in pregnancy) but does not create the gradual splay pattern. Splay is specifically due to inter-nephron variability in Tₘ, not a GFR-driven phenomenon.

**Buzzword Combo:** ↑GFR + uniform Tₘ = splay (incorrect — GFR affects threshold, not splay)
