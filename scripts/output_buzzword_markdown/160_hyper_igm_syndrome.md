## Buzzword Question

A 5-year-old boy has recurrent bacterial infections and produces antibodies, but most antibodies remain in the first isotype made by naive B cells. Which of the following interactions is most directly required for switching to IgG, IgA, or IgE?

## Buzzwords Used

5-year-old boy; recurrent bacterial infections; antibodies remain in the first isotype made by naive B cells

## Answer Options

A) CD40 on B cells binding CD40L on CD4+ T cells
B) MHC I on nucleated cells binding CD8+ T cells
C) IL-2 receptor signaling during T-cell proliferation
D) AIRE-mediated deletion of self-reactive T cells

## Correct Answer

A) CD40 on B cells binding CD40L on CD4+ T cells


## Explanation

CD40 on B cells must bind CD40L on activated CD4+ T cells for class switching. Defective CD40L prevents switching from IgM to IgG, IgA, or IgE.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

B cell stuck at IgM + failed class switching + CD40-CD40L defect = Hyper-IgM syndrome

## Wrong Options

### B) MHC I on nucleated cells binding CD8+ T cells

This describes cell-mediated antigen presentation to cytotoxic T cells. It is not the T-cell/B-cell interaction required for immunoglobulin class switching.

**Buzzword Combo:** Viral infection + MHC I presentation + CD8+ T-cell killing = cell-mediated immunity

### C) IL-2 receptor signaling during T-cell proliferation

IL-2 signaling promotes T-cell proliferation after activation. It does not directly explain why B cells remain stuck producing IgM.

**Buzzword Combo:** Activated T cells + IL-2 secretion + clonal expansion = T-cell proliferation

### D) AIRE-mediated deletion of self-reactive T cells

AIRE promotes central tolerance by exposing developing T cells to self-antigens in the thymus. It is unrelated to B-cell class switching.

**Buzzword Combo:** Thymic medulla + self-antigen expression + deletion of autoreactive T cells = AIRE-mediated central tolerance
