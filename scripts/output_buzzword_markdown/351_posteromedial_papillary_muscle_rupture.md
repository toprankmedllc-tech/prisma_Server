## Buzzword Question

A patient dies 5 days after a myocardial infarction from acute mitral regurgitation due to a transected papillary muscle. Histologic examination of the adjacent infarcted myocardium is most likely to show.

## Buzzwords Used

Papillary muscle rupture; 3–7 days post-MI

## Answer Options

A. Coagulative necrosis with neutrophils
B. Macrophage infiltration with yellow softening
C. Granulation tissue with type III collagen
D. Dense fibrous scar with type I collagen

## Correct Answer

Macrophage infiltration with yellow softening

## Explanation

Days 3–7 post-MI are dominated by macrophage-mediated phagocytosis of necrotic myocytes, producing gross yellow softening of the infarcted tissue. This is when the myocardium is mechanically weakest, which is why mechanical complications — papillary muscle rupture, ventricular free wall rupture, and interventricular septal rupture — cluster in this window. The patient's papillary muscle transection on day 5 places him squarely in this peak rupture period.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Day 3–7 + macrophages + yellow softening = peak rupture risk

## Wrong Options

###  Coagulative necrosis with neutrophils

 Days 1–3 post-MI; the myocardium is still structurally intact at this stage and rupture is uncommon this early.

**Buzzword Combo:** Day 1–3 + coagulative necrosis + neutrophils = early post-MI

### Granulation tissue with type III collagen

Days 7–10 post-MI; mechanical strength is beginning to be restored and rupture risk declines.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Day 7–10 + granulation tissue + type III collagen = healing phase

### Dense fibrous scar with type I collagen

Weeks to months post-MI; represents complete healing and is the substrate for late complications such as ventricular aneurysm, not acute rupture.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weeks to months + type I collagen scar = ventricular aneurysm risk
