## Buzzword Question

A 25-year-old man participates in a physiology research study evaluating skeletal muscle adaptations in endurance athletes. A biopsy sample is obtained from the gastrocnemius muscle of a marathon runner. Microscopic examination demonstrates a predominance of slow oxidative muscle fibers with abundant mitochondria and dense capillary networks. The researcher notes that these fibers appear dark red compared with other skeletal muscle fibers.
Which of the following best explains the color of type I muscle fibers?

## Buzzwords Used

color of type I muscle fibers; predominance of slow oxidative muscle fibers with abundant mitochondria and dense capillary networks

## Answer Options

A. High myoglobin content and rich capillary supply
B. Increased glycogen storage within the sarcoplasm
C. Large diameter and abundant thick filaments
D. High concentration of anaerobic glycolytic enzymes

## Correct Answer

A. High myoglobin content and rich capillary supply

## Explanation

Type I (slow oxidative) muscle fibers appear red because they contain:High concentrations of myoglobin, an oxygen-binding pigment ,Extensive capillary networks ,Numerous mitochondria supporting aerobic metabolism .

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Red muscle + endurance exercise + high myoglobin + rich blood supply = Type I fibers

## Wrong Options

### B. Increased glycogen storage within the sarcoplasm

Glycogen serves as an energy reserve but does not primarily determine the red color of type I fibers.

**Buzzword Combo:** Energy storage + carbohydrate reserve + muscle metabolism = glycogen

### C. Large diameter and abundant thick filaments

Fiber diameter and thick filament content do not explain the red appearance of slow oxidative fibers.

**Buzzword Combo:** Muscle size + force generation + myosin filaments = structural proteins

### D. High concentration of anaerobic glycolytic enzymes

Anaerobic glycolytic fibers are typically pale or white because they contain lower myoglobin levels.

**Buzzword Combo:** Anaerobic metabolism + rapid fatigue + pale muscle fibers = glycolytic fibers
