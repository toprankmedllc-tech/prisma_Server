## Buzzword Question

A 31-year-old man presents to the clinic with difficulty walking several days after sustaining a fracture of the fibular neck during a skiing accident. He reports that his right foot drags while walking, forcing him to lift his knee higher than usual to avoid tripping. He also complains of numbness over the lateral aspect of the leg and the dorsum of the foot. Physical examination reveals weakness of ankle dorsiflexion and foot eversion. Plantarflexion is preserved.
Injury to which of the following nerves is the most likely cause of this patient’s symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

 fracture of the fibular neck; right foot drags while walking; numbness over the lateral aspect of the leg and the dorsum of the foot

## Answer Options

A. Tibial nerve
B. Femoral nerve
C. Common peroneal nerve
D. Obturator nerve

## Correct Answer

C. Common peroneal nerve

## Explanation

This patient most likely has injury to the common peroneal nerve, which is highly vulnerable to trauma at the fibular neck. Patients may develop:Foot drop due to loss of dorsiflexion ,Weak foot eversion ,Sensory loss over the lateral leg and dorsum of the foot ,Steppage gait .

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Fibular neck injury + foot drop + dorsum foot sensory loss = common peroneal nerve injury

## Wrong Options

### A. Tibial nerve

Incorrect — Tibial nerve injury causes weakness of plantarflexion and sensory loss over the sole of the foot.

**Buzzword Combo:** Loss of plantarflexion + sole numbness = tibial neuropathy

### B. Femoral nerve

Incorrect — Femoral nerve injury causes weak knee extension and decreased patellar refle

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak quadriceps + reduced patellar reflex = femoral neuropathy

### D. Obturator nerve

Incorrect — Obturator nerve injury causes weakness of thigh adduction and sensory loss over the medial thigh.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak thigh adduction + medial thigh numbness = obturator neuropathy
