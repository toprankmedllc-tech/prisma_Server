## Buzzword Question

Ipsilateral intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia, limb ataxia, “drunk-like” coordination, lesion involving the major cerebellar output pathway that decussates in the midbrain and carries fibers from the dentate nucleus most strongly indicates damage to which structure?

## Buzzwords Used

Intention tremor; dysdiadochokinesia; limb ataxia; dentate nucleus; cerebellar output pathway; decussates in midbrain; superior cerebellar peduncle

## Answer Options

A) Inferior cerebellar peduncle
B) Middle cerebellar peduncle
C) Superior cerebellar peduncle
D) Medial longitudinal fasciculus

## Correct Answer

C) Superior cerebellar peduncle

## Explanation

The superior cerebellar peduncle is the primary efferent pathway from the cerebellum carrying fibers from the dentate nucleus to the contralateral red nucleus and thalamus. Lesions produce ipsilateral cerebellar signs such as intention tremor and limb ataxia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Dentate nucleus + cerebellar efferent fibers + midbrain decussation = Superior cerebellar peduncle

## Wrong Options

### A) Inferior cerebellar peduncle

The inferior cerebellar peduncle mainly carries afferent vestibular and spinocerebellar inputs to the cerebellum. Lesions are more associated with balance and vestibular dysfunction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vestibular input + spinocerebellar fibers + balance dysfunction = Inferior cerebellar peduncle

### B) Middle cerebellar peduncle

The middle cerebellar peduncle carries pontocerebellar afferent fibers from the contralateral cortex. It is the major input pathway to the cerebellum rather than the main output tract.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pontine nuclei + cortical input + afferent cerebellar pathway = Middle cerebellar peduncle

### D) Medial longitudinal fasciculus

The medial longitudinal fasciculus coordinates conjugate eye movements between cranial nerve nuclei. Lesions classically cause internuclear ophthalmoplegia rather than cerebellar ataxia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diplopia + impaired adduction + internuclear ophthalmoplegia = Medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion
