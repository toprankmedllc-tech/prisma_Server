## Buzzword Question

A 3-year-old boy is diagnosed with Kawasaki disease. Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line treatment?

## Buzzwords Used

Kawasaki disease; IVIG; high-dose aspirin; coronary artery aneurysm prevention; only pediatric aspirin indication

## Answer Options

A) Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) plus high-dose aspirin
B) High-dose corticosteroids alone
C) Acetaminophen and supportive care
D) Penicillin V plus rheumatic fever prophylaxis

## Correct Answer


A) Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) plus high-dose aspirin

## Explanation

IVIG plus high-dose aspirin is the first-line treatment for Kawasaki disease, ideally given within the first 10 days of fever onset. This combination reduces the risk of coronary artery aneurysm from ~25% to <5%. Kawasaki is the only pediatric indication for aspirin, with the risk of coronary aneurysm outweighing the Reye syndrome concern.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Kawasaki disease + within 10 days of fever onset = IVIG + high-dose aspirin (reduces aneurysm risk to <5%)

## Wrong Options

### B) High-dose corticosteroids alone


Steroids are reserved for IVIG-resistant Kawasaki, not first-line therapy.

**Buzzword Combo:** IVIG-resistant Kawasaki + persistent fever = corticosteroids (second-line)

### C) Acetaminophen and supportive care

Supportive care alone allows the vasculitis to progress, leaving the patient at ~25% risk of developing coronary artery aneurysm.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Untreated Kawasaki + supportive care only = ~25% coronary aneurysm risk

### 
D) Penicillin V plus rheumatic fever prophylaxis

Penicillin prophylaxis is the treatment for acute rheumatic fever to prevent recurrence — not for Kawasaki disease, which is not a streptococcal sequela.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
ARF + recurrence prophylaxis = long-term penicillin
