## Buzzword Question

A 2-year-old child is brought to the emergency department with a 2-day history of runny nose, low-grade fever, and a progressively worsening “barking” cough. At night, the child develops inspiratory stridor that improves with exposure to cool air. Examination reveals hoarseness and mild respiratory distress without drooling. Which of the following organisms is the most likely cause of this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Barking cough; Inspiratory stridor; Hoarseness; Low-grade fever; URTI prodrome; Subglottic inflammation; Worse at night; Improves with cool air; Toddler age group

## Answer Options

A) Haemophilus influenzae type b
B) Parainfluenza virus
C) Respiratory syncytial virus
D) Corynebacterium diphtheriae

## Correct Answer

B) Parainfluenza virus

## Explanation

Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis) is most commonly caused by parainfluenza virus, leading to subglottic edema. It presents with a barking cough, inspiratory stridor, and hoarseness, often improving with cool air exposure.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Barking cough + inspiratory stridor + hoarseness + subglottic narrowing = Parainfluenza virus (croup)

## Wrong Options

### A) Haemophilus influenzae type b

Hib causes epiglottitis, which presents with drooling, toxic appearance, and tripod position—not a barking cough or improvement with cool air.

**Buzzword Combo:** Drooling + high fever + tripod position + no cough = Epiglottitis (Hib)

### C) Respiratory syncytial virus

RSV causes bronchiolitis in infants with wheezing and crackles, not barking cough or stridor.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wheezing + crackles + infant + respiratory distress = RSV bronchiolitis

### D) Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Diphtheria presents with gray pseudomembrane in the throat and systemic toxicity, not a barking cough or stridor relieved by cool air.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pseudomembrane + sore throat + myocarditis + “bull neck” = Diphtheria
