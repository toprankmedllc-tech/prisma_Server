## Buzzword Question

A 3-week-old infant is brought to the emergency department by his father, who reports that the baby suddenly stopped breathing and became unresponsive at home. Physical examination reveals a lethargic infant with a bulging anterior fontanelle, retinal hemorrhages bilaterally, and no external signs of trauma. A CT scan of the head shows a subdural hematoma and diffuse axonal injury. The father states the child must have fallen off the couch, but the injuries are highly inconsistent with this mechanism. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Retinal hemorrhages; Subdural hematoma; Bulging anterior fontanelle; Inconsistent history; Nonaccidental trauma

## Answer Options

A) Abusive Head Trauma
B) Accidental Fall from Couch
C) Glioblastoma Multiforme
D) Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding

## Correct Answer

A) Abusive Head Trauma

## Explanation

Abusive Head Trauma (inflicted head trauma/shaken baby syndrome) classically presents with the triad of subdural hematoma, retinal hemorrhages, and encephalopathy (bulging fontanelle, lethargy) due to repetitive acceleration-deceleration forces. The mechanism provided by the caregiver is clinically incompatible with the severity of intracranial and intraocular findings.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Retinal hemorrhages + Subdural hematoma + Inconsistent history = Abusive Head Trauma

## Wrong Options

### B) Accidental Fall from Couch

A simple, low-height fall from a couch does not generate the massive rotational forces required to cause extensive bilateral retinal hemorrhages and diffuse axonal injury. Presenting this option exploits the "Similar Diagnosis Confusion" trap by confusing a falsely reported mechanism with the actual medical reality.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low-height fall + Intracranial hemorrhage + Caregiver story = Accidental Fall from Couch

### C) Glioblastoma Multiforme

Glioblastoma multiforme is a high-grade malignant astrocytoma that presents with signs of increased intracranial pressure, but it is extremely rare in neonates and typically presents as a distinct, enhancing mass on neuroimaging rather than a subdural hematoma.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bulging fontanelle + Increased intracranial pressure + Mass lesion = Glioblastoma Multiforme

### D) Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding

While Vitamin K deficiency can cause intracranial hemorrhage in neonates who did not receive prophylactic Vitamin K at birth, it typically presents with generalized systemic bleeding (e.g., umbilicus, GI tract) and would not explain diffuse axonal injury.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neonatal hemorrhage + Coagulopathy + Prolonged PT/PTT = Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding
