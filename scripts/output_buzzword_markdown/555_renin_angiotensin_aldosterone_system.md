## Buzzword Question

A 34-year-old woman comes to the physician for a follow-up evaluation after being diagnosed with severe fibromuscular dysplasia affecting her right renal artery. Her blood pressure is 172/105 mm Hg. Physical examination reveals an audible abdominal bruit loudest on the right side. Laboratory studies demonstrate elevated plasma renin activity (PRA) and elevated serum aldosterone levels. The patient is subsequently prescribed an oral medication that directly blocks the active site of angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE). Three days after initiating this therapy, which of the following sets of physiological changes is most likely to be observed in this patient's systemic circulation and renal arterioles?

## Correct Answer

A) Angiotensin I,Angiotensin II,Bradykinin,Efferent Arteriolar Resistance
Increased,Decreased,Increased,Decreced

## Explanation

Inhibiting ACE prevents the conversion of Angiotensin I to Angiotensin II, causing Angiotensin I to accumulate while Angiotensin II drops. Because ACE also degrades bradykinin, its inhibition leads to increased bradykinin levels; the loss of Angiotensin II removes its preferential vasoconscriptive effect on the efferent arteriole, causing efferent arteriolar resistance to decrease.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

ACE inhibitor mechanics= Angiotensin I accumulation+Bradykinin degradation block+ Efferent arteriolar dilation+ Renin-angiotensin decoupling


## Wrong Options

### B

This option describes the baseline physiological state of the patient's stenosed right kidney before starting the ACE inhibitor, where uninhibited RAAS activation generates high Angiotensin II and maintains high efferent tone.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Unopposed ACE activity=Angiotensin II dominance+Efferent vasoconstriction

### C

This choice incorrectly states that Angiotensin II increases and bradykinin decreases under an ACE inhibitor, which completely reverses the known biochemical targets of the drug.

**Buzzword Combo:** Paradoxical ACE activation=Bradykinin clearance acceleration+Erroneous substrate tracking+Angiotensin II surge trap

### D

While this choice correctly identifies the bradykinin increase, it incorrectly predicts a decrease in Angiotensin I (which must rise due to loss of negative feedback on renin) and an increase in efferent resistance (which actually falls as Angiotensin II drops). Upstream substrates accumulate and downstream vascular tone dilates when a blocking enzyme is introduced.
*

**Buzzword Combo:** Upstream depletion trap=Misaligned arteriolar resistance+Bradykinin tracking success+Feedback loop misinterpretation
