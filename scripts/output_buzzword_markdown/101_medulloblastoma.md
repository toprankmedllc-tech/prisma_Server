## Buzzword Question

A 7-year-old boy is brought to the pediatrician because of 2 months of morning headaches, recurrent vomiting, and a progressively unsteady gait. MRI of the brain shows a solid, contrast-enhancing midline mass arising from the cerebellar vermis, with obstructive hydrocephalus from compression of the fourth ventricle. Biopsy reveals sheets of small round blue cells with hyperchromatic nuclei, scant cytoplasm, and tumor cells surrounding a central neuropil. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Sheets of small round blue cells with hyperchromatic nuclei, scant cytoplasm, and tumor cells surrounding a central neuropil (Homer wright rosettes)

## Answer Options

A) Pilocytic astrocytoma
B) Medulloblastoma
C) Ependymoma
D) Craniopharyngioma

## Correct Answer

B) Medulloblastoma

## Explanation

Medulloblastoma is a highly malignant embryonal tumor (WHO grade IV) of the cerebellar VERMIS, classically presenting in school-aged children with truncal ataxia, obstructive hydrocephalus, and signs of increased ICP. Histology shows sheets of SMALL ROUND BLUE CELLS with HOMER-WRIGHT ROSETTES. Medulloblastoma characteristically spreads through the CSF, producing "DROP METASTASES" to the spinal cord.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

 Child + cerebellar vermis mass + Homer-Wright rosettes + drop metastases = medulloblastoma

## Wrong Options

### Pilocytic astrocytoma

Pilocytic astrocytoma is the MOST COMMON pediatric brain tumor and a benign WHO grade I lesion. It classically appears as a CYSTIC mass with an enhancing MURAL NODULE in the cerebellar hemisphere (not midline vermis). Histology shows ROSENTHAL FIBERS (eosinophilic corkscrew structures) and eosinophilic granular bodies; tumor cells are GFAP+. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Child + cystic cerebellar mass with mural nodule + Rosenthal fibers + GFAP+ = pilocytic astrocytoma

### Ependymoma

Ependymoma typically arises within or adjacent to the FOURTH VENTRICLE in children, causing hydrocephalus from CSF outflow obstruction. Histology shows PERIVASCULAR PSEUDOROSETTES (tumor cells radially arranged around blood vessels) and "true" ependymal rosettes. It does not have Homer-Wright rosettes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Child + 4th ventricle mass + perivascular pseudorosettes + hydrocephalus = ependymoma

### Craniopharyngioma

Craniopharyngioma is a benign SUPRASELLAR tumor derived from Rathke pouch remnants. It classically calcifies, has cystic spaces containing "MOTOR OIL" cholesterol-rich fluid, and presents in children or older adults with bitemporal hemianopia (optic chiasm compression), growth failure, or diabetes insipidus. It is not located in the cerebellum.

**Buzzword Combo:** Child + suprasellar mass + calcifications + bitemporal hemianopia + "motor oil" cyst = craniopharyngioma
