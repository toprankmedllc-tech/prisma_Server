## Buzzword Question

A hospitalized patient develops fever, purulent sputum, and a new infiltrate on day 6 of admission. Sputum culture shows ESBL-producing Gram-negative rods.
Which antibiotic is most appropriate?

## Buzzwords Used

Pulmonary infiltrates ; ESBL Gram-negative rods

## Answer Options

 A) Piperacillin–tazobactam
 B) Ceftriaxone
 C) Meropenem
 D) Azithromycin

## Correct Answer

 C) Meropenem

## Explanation

ESBL-producing organisms hydrolyze most penicillins and cephalosporins. Carbapenems remain stable against ESBL enzymes and are first-line therapy for severe hospital-acquired pneumonia caused by these pathogens.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

ESBL enzyme + carbapenem stability = Meropenem efficacy

## Wrong Options

### Piperacillin–tazobactam

β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor combination; may have activity in some infections but is unreliable against ESBL-producing organisms in severe HAP.

**Buzzword Combo:**  Piperacillin activity + β-lactamase inhibition = partial ESBL coverage (not definitive therapy)

### Ceftriaxone

Third-generation cephalosporin hydrolyzed by ESBL enzymes; ineffective against resistant hospital-acquired Gram-negative organisms

**Buzzword Combo:** β-lactam antibiotic + transpeptidase inhibition + impaired peptidoglycan synthesis = Ceftriaxone

### Azithromycin

Macrolide targeting atypical organisms via 50S ribosomal inhibition; no activity against Gram-negative rods causing HAP.

**Buzzword Combo:**  50S ribosomal inhibition + atypical pathogens = Azithromycin (no Gram-negative HAP coverage)
