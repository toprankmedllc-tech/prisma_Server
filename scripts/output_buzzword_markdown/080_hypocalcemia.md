## Buzzword Question

A 34-year-old woman develops perioral numbness and carpopedal spasm several days after thyroid surgery.
Which of the following electrolyte abnormalities is most likely present?

## Buzzwords Used

Thyroid surgery; Perioral numbness; Carpopedal spasm


## Answer Options

A) Hypermagnesemia
B) Hyperphosphatemia
C) Hypocalcemia
D) Hyperkalemia

## Correct Answer

C) Hypocalcemia

## Explanation

Hypocalcemia may occur after accidental removal or damage to the parathyroid glands during thyroid surgery. Decreased calcium levels increase neuromuscular excitability, leading to tetany, perioral numbness, and carpopedal spasm.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Thyroid surgery + tetany + carpopedal spasm = Hypocalcemia

## Wrong Options

### Hyperphosphatemia

Hyperphosphatemia may occur in renal failure but does not directly explain tetany after thyroid surgery.

**Buzzword Combo:** Renal failure + elevated phosphate = Hyperphosphatemia

### Hypermagnesemia

Hypermagnesemia commonly causes decreased reflexes and muscle weakness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Loss of reflexes + magnesium excess = Hypermagnesemia

### Hyperkalemia

Hyperkalemia primarily affects cardiac conduction and causes peaked T waves on ECG.

**Buzzword Combo:** Peaked T waves + potassium excess = Hyperkalemia
