## Buzzword Question

A 60-year-old man with a Stanford Type B aortic dissection has a blood pressure of 192/110 mm Hg and a heart rate of 108/min. Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line agent to control his blood pressure?

## Buzzwords Used

Stanford Type B aortic dissection; hypertension; tachycardia; shear stress; IV β-blocker

## Answer Options

A) Intravenous β-blocker (labetalol)
B) Intravenous sodium nitroprusside
C) Intravenous hydralazine
D) Oral amlodipine

## Correct Answer


A) Intravenous β-blocker (labetalol)

## Explanation

The first-line agent for blood pressure control in aortic dissection is an IV β-blocker (e.g., labetalol, esmolol). β-blockers reduce both heart rate and left ventricular contractility, lowering aortic wall shear stress (dP/dt) — the key driver of dissection propagation. Vasodilators may be added afterward if BP control is inadequate, but they must never be used before β-blockade because reflex tachycardia would increase shear stress and worsen the dissection.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Aortic dissection + ↓ shear stress (dP/dt) = IV β-blocker first (labetalol/esmolol)

## Wrong Options

### B) Intravenous sodium nitroprusside


Nitroprusside is an effective vasodilator but causes reflex tachycardia, which increases aortic shear stress and can worsen dissection. It is only used after adequate β-blockade.

**Buzzword Combo:** Nitroprusside without β-blocker = reflex tachycardia + worsened dissection

### C) Intravenous hydralazine


Hydralazine causes reflex tachycardia and increased shear stress, and is therefore avoided as first-line therapy in dissection.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hydralazine + reflex tachycardia = contraindicated in dissection

### 
D) Oral amlodipine

Oral agents do not provide the rapid, titratable BP control needed in an acute dissection. Dihydropyridine CCBs also cause reflex tachycardia.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Oral amlodipine = inadequate for acute dissection BP control
