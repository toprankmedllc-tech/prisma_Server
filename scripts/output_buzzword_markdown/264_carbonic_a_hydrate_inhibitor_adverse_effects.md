## Buzzword Question

A 54-year-old woman with a history of severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) presents to the emergency department with acute shortness of breath and a productive cough. Arterial blood gas analysis on room air shows a pH of 7.25, PaCO2  of 65 mmHg, and HCO3− of 28 mEq/L. She is admitted for an acute exacerbation of COPD. During her hospital stay, she complains of severe, throbbing right eye pain accompanied by blurred vision and seeing halos around lights. Acute angle-closure glaucoma is suspected, and she is promptly given an intravenous dose of a medication that inhibits carbonic anhydrase. Which of the following adverse effects or contraindications is most critical to consider in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

COPD; Throbbing eye pain; Halos around lights; Acute angle-closure glaucoma; Inhibits carbonic anhydrase

## Answer Options

A) Severe hyperkalemia
B) Hypercalcemia and kidney stones
C) Worsening metabolic alkalosis
D) Accumulation of acid and worsening acidemia

## Correct Answer

D) Accumulation of acid and worsening acidemia

## Explanation

Acetazolamide is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor that causes a self-limiting NaHCO3 diuresis, leading to a drop in total body bicarbonate stores and a proximal renal tubular acidosis (Type 2 RTA). Because this patient already has an underlying respiratory acidosis from her severe COPD, the development of a metabolic acidosis can cause catastrophic worsening of her acidemia.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

COPD + Glaucoma + Acetazolamide = Type 2 RTA induced by bicarbonate  loss → Accumulation of acid and worsening acidemia.

## Wrong Options

### A) Severe hyperkalemia

Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors cause a significant increase in urinary potassium excretion, which leads to hypokalemia, not hyperkalemia, due to increased delivery of sodium and water to the collecting duct.

**Buzzword Combo:** COPD + Glaucoma + Acetazolamide = Confusing proximal RTA potassium shifts with hyperkalemia → Severe hyperkalemia.



### B) Hypercalcemia and kidney stones

While acetazolamide does increase the risk of calcium phosphate kidney stones because it alkalizes the urine (making calcium phosphate insoluble), it does not cause systemic hypercalcemia; systemic calcium levels remain largely normal or low.

**Buzzword Combo:** COPD + Glaucoma + Acetazolamide = Confusing urinary stone precipitation risk with systemic plasma calcium elevation → Hypercalcemia and kidney stones.

### C) Worsening metabolic alkalosis

Acetazolamide causes a metabolic acidosis due to the heavy wasting of bicarbonate in the urine, making it a treatment option for metabolic alkalosis rather than a cause of it.

**Buzzword Combo:** COPD + Glaucoma + Acetazolamide = Reversing the acid-base impact of bicarbonate wasting → Worsening metabolic alkalosis.
