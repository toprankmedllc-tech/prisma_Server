## Buzzword Question

A 65-year-old man with dilated cardiomyopathy is noted to have an extra heart sound occurring shortly after S2, best heard at the apex with the bell of the stethoscope in the left lateral decubitus position. Which of the following best explains the mechanism of this additional heart sound?

A) Turbulent flow across a stenotic mitral valve during diastole
B) Abrupt deceleration of blood flow into a non-compliant, volume-overloaded left ventricle 
C) Atrial contraction against a non-compliant, hypertrophied left ventricle in late diastole
D) Sudden tensing of redundant chordae tendineae during mid-to-late systole

## Buzzwords Used

Dilated cardiomyopathy; Extra heart sound; Shortly after S2; 

## Answer Options

A) Turbulent flow across a stenotic mitral valve during diastole
B) Abrupt deceleration of blood flow into a non-compliant, volume-overloaded left ventricle 
C) Atrial contraction against a non-compliant, hypertrophied left ventricle in late diastole
D) Sudden tensing of redundant chordae tendineae during mid-to-late systole

## Correct Answer

B) Abrupt deceleration of blood flow into a non-compliant, volume-overloaded left ventricle 

## Explanation

The S3 gallop (third heart sound) is produced by the abrupt deceleration of a large volume of blood entering the LV during the rapid filling phase of early diastole. In HFrEF, the dilated, poorly contractile LV has increased end-systolic volume, causing a larger volume of blood to rush in during early diastole, producing the S3. 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Early diastole + apex + dilated cardiomyopathy + after S2 = S3 gallop (volume overload marker)

## Wrong Options

### A) Turbulent flow across stenotic mitral valve

Turbulent flow across a stenotic mitral valve during diastole produces the low-pitched, mid-diastolic rumble of mitral stenosis not an S3 gallop. Mitral stenosis also produces an opening snap preceding the rumble.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mid-diastolic rumble + opening snap + apex = Mitral stenosis

### C) Atrial contraction against stiff LV in late diastole

This describes the S4 gallop, which occurs in late diastole just before S1 when the atrium contracts against a stiff, non-compliant (hypertrophied) LV. S4 is associated with hypertension, LVH, and ischemia not volume overload

**Buzzword Combo:** Late diastole + just before S1 + non-compliant hypertrophied LV = S4 gallop (pressure overload)

### D) Sudden tensing of redundant chordae tendineae during mid-to-late systole

This describes a mid-systolic click, which is the hallmark sound of Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP). It occurs because myxomatous degeneration weakens the valve tissue.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mid-systolic click + Connective tissue disorders such as Marfan Syndrome or Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome = Mitral Valve Prolapse
