## Buzzword Question


In a child with tetralogy of Fallot, which of the following factors most directly determines the severity of cyanosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Tetralogy of Fallot

## Answer Options


A) The degree of pulmonary (right ventricular outflow) stenosis
B) The size of the ventricular septal defect
C) The extent of right ventricular hypertrophy
D) The degree of aortic override

## Correct Answer


A) The degree of pulmonary (right ventricular outflow) stenosis

## Explanation

The degree of pulmonary stenosis is the key determinant of cyanosis severity in tetralogy of Fallot. More severe RV outflow obstruction raises RV pressure above LV pressure, increasing the right-to-left shunt across the VSD and driving more deoxygenated blood into the systemic circulation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Severity of cyanosis in TOF = degree of pulmonary stenosis (RV outflow obstruction)

## Wrong Options

### 
B) The size of the ventricular septal defect

The VSD is typically large and non-restrictive in TOF, so it does not limit shunting; the direction and volume of shunt flow are governed by the pressure gradient created by the pulmonary stenosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Large non-restrictive VSD = allows free shunting (driven by pressure gradient, not VSD size)

### C) The extent of right ventricular hypertrophy


RVH is a secondary consequence of chronic pressure overload from pulmonary stenosis, not the primary driver of cyanosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** RVH in TOF = secondary to pulmonary stenosis (consequence, not cause)

### D) The degree of aortic override

The overriding aorta straddles the VSD and contributes to mixing, but variations in override are not the primary determinant of cyanosis severity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Overriding aorta = anatomic feature of TOF (not the main severity driver)
