## Buzzword Question

A 59-year-old man with COPD undergoes pulmonary function testing. He has significant airflow obstruction and hyperinflated lungs. What change is expected in vital capacity?

## Buzzwords Used

COPD; airflow obstruction; hyperinflated lungs; smoking history; obstructive lung disease; spirometry; air trapping

## Answer Options

A) Increased vital capacity due to hyperinflation
B) Decreased vital capacity due to air trapping
C) Normal vital capacity due to preserved inspiration
D) Absent vital capacity due to airway collapse

## Correct Answer

B) Decreased vital capacity due to air trapping

## Explanation

In COPD, airflow obstruction leads to air trapping and increased residual volume, which reduces the amount of air that can be forcefully exhaled after maximal inspiration. This results in a decreased vital capacity (VC = TLC − RV) despite hyperinflated lungs.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

COPD; air trapping; ↑RV = ↓VC

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased vital capacity due to hyperinflation

Although TLC may increase in emphysema, VC does not increase because residual volume rises disproportionately due to air trapping.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyperinflation; emphysema = ↑TLC,  not  ↑ VC

### C) Normal vital capacity due to preserved inspiration

In COPD, both inspiratory and expiratory mechanics are impaired due to dynamic airway collapse, so VC is not preserved.

**Buzzword Combo:** Obstruction; airflow limitation; reduced expiratory flow

### D) Absent vital capacity due to airway collapse

Vital capacity is reduced but never absent in COPD; complete absence is incompatible with life and not seen in obstructive lung disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Severe obstruction; airway collapse; exaggerated trap

## Related Concepts

TLC vs RV relationship; obstructive vs restrictive spirometry patterns; dynamic airway collapse in COPD
