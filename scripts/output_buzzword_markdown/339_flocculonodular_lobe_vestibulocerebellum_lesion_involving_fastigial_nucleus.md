## Buzzword Question

Drunk-like gait, truncal ataxia, vertigo, horizontal nystagmus, inability to maintain balance while standing, preserved limb coordination, and lesion of the vestibulocerebellum projecting through the fastigial nucleus most strongly indicate damage to which cerebellar region?

## Buzzwords Used

Drunk-like gait; truncal ataxia; vertigo; horizontal nystagmus; preserved limb coordination; vestibulocerebellum; fastigial nucleus

## Answer Options

A) Dentate nucleus
B) Interposed nuclei
C) Flocculonodular lobe
D) Lateral cerebellar hemisphere

## Correct Answer

C) Flocculonodular lobe

## Explanation

The flocculonodular lobe is the vestibulocerebellum and is responsible for balance and eye movements. Lesions cause truncal ataxia, vertigo, and nystagmus with relatively preserved limb coordination.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Vertigo + truncal ataxia + nystagmus = Flocculonodular lobe lesion

## Wrong Options

### A) Dentate nucleus

Dentate nucleus lesions produce intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia, and limb ataxia due to impaired motor planning. Balance and vestibular symptoms are less prominent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Intention tremor + dysdiadochokinesia + scanning speech = Dentate nucleus lesion

### B) Interposed nuclei

Interposed nuclei (emboliform and globose) are associated with limb movement correction and cause ipsilateral limb ataxia rather than predominant truncal instability.

**Buzzword Combo:** Limb ataxia + movement correction deficit + intermediate hemisphere lesion = Interposed nuclei

### D) Lateral cerebellar hemisphere

The lateral cerebellar hemisphere coordinates skilled voluntary movement and motor planning. Lesions typically cause limb coordination deficits rather than isolated balance dysfunction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Intention tremor + impaired motor planning + dysdiadochokinesia = Lateral cerebellar hemisphere lesion
