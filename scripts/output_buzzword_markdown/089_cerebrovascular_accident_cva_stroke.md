## Buzzword Question

A 70-year-old woman presents with vertigo, dysphagia, right-sided Horner syndrome, crossed face/body pain and temperature loss, ipsilateral limb ataxia, AND additionally right-sided facial paralysis and ipsilateral sensorineural hearing loss. The most likely occluded artery is:

## Buzzwords Used

crossed face/body pain and temperature loss; ipsilateral limb ataxia; right-sided facial paralysis and ipsilateral sensorineural hearing loss

## Answer Options

A) Right middle cerebral artery
B) Right posterior inferior cerebellar artery
C) Right anterior inferior cerebellar artery
D) Right anterior spinal artery

## Correct Answer

C) Right anterior inferior cerebellar artery

## Explanation

Adding ipsilateral facial paralysis (CN VII) and ipsilateral sensorineural hearing loss (CN VIII) to a Wallenberg-like picture localizes the stroke up to the LATERAL PONS, which is supplied by AICA. The phrase "facial droop means AICA's pooped" captures the key distinguisher.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Wallenberg features + ipsilateral facial paralysis + sensorineural hearing loss = AICA (lateral pontine syndrome)

## Wrong Options

### Right middle cerebral artery

MCA strokes produce contralateral face/arm > leg weakness and aphasia or hemineglect — no crossed brainstem signs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Contralateral face/arm > leg weakness + aphasia = MCA stroke

### Right posterior inferior cerebellar artery

PICA produces Wallenberg (lateral medullary) syndrome but does NOT cause facial paralysis or sensorineural hearing loss, because CN VII and VIII originate in the pons, not the medulla.

**Buzzword Combo:** Crossed face/body sensory + dysphagia + Horner WITHOUT facial droop = PICA (Wallenberg)

### Right anterior spinal artery

ASA causes MEDIAL medullary syndrome with contralateral arm/leg weakness, contralateral loss of vibration/proprioception, and ipsilateral tongue deviation, not lateral signs and not facial paralysis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Contralateral arm/leg weakness + ipsilateral tongue deviation = medial medullary (ASA)

## Related Concepts

Wallenberg syndrome (PICA); Medial medullary syndrome (ASA); MCA stroke and middle cerebral artery distribution
