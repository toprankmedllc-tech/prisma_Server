## Buzzword Question

A sexually active woman with dysuria has a gram-positive, catalase-positive, coagulase-negative urinary pathogen. Which organism is the most likely cause?


## Buzzwords Used

Sexually active; Coagulase-negative; Dysuria


## Answer Options

A) Enterococcus faecalis
B) Proteus mirabilis
C) Staphylococcus saprophyticus
D) Klebsiella pneumoniae


## Correct Answer

C) Staphylococcus saprophyticus

## Explanation

Staphylococcus saprophyticus urinary tract infection is a common cause of urinary tract infections in sexually active young women. It is a gram-positive, catalase-positive, coagulase-negative organism.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Young + sexually active+ coagulase-negative = S. saprophyticus

## Wrong Options

### Proteus mirabilis

Proteus is a urease-positive gram-negative rod associated with struvite stones and alkaline urine.

**Buzzword Combo:** Urease-positive + staghorn calculi = Proteus

### Enterococcus faecalis

Enterococcus is a gram-positive cocci that can grow in bile and high salt concentrations.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bile growth + nosocomial UTI = Enterococcus

### Klebsiella pneumoniae

Klebsiella is a lactose-fermenting gram-negative rod with a thick polysaccharide capsule.

**Buzzword Combo:** Currant jelly sputum + encapsulated gram-negative rod = Klebsiella
