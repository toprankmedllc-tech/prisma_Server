## Buzzword Question

What virulence mechanism allows Mycobacterium tuberculosis to survive inside alveolar macrophages despite being phagocytosed?

## Buzzwords Used

mycobacterium tuberculosis; phagocytosed by macrophages; survive within macrophages

## Answer Options

 A. Inhibition of phagolysosome fusion
B. Production of exotoxin that destroys macrophages
C. Activation of complement-mediated lysis
D. Increased opsonization via capsule formation

## Correct Answer

A. Inhibition of phagolysosome fusion

## Explanation

Mycobacterium is taken up by macrophages, resulting in the formation of a phagosome. Normally, a phagosome binds a lysosome to form a phagolysosome to induce bacterial killing, but the mycobacterial cell wall is made up of mycolic acid and contains sulfatides and lipoarabinomannan (LAM), which inhibit acidification and fusion of the phagosome with the lysosome, hence favor intracellular survival of mycobacteria

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

mycobacterium + survival within macrophages = inhibition of phagolysosome fusion 

## Wrong Options

### B. Production of exotoxin that destroys macrophages

Mycobacterium tuberculosis does not produce exotoxins. TB pathogenesis is due to intracellular survival and immune response, not toxin-mediated cell destruction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Shigella + Corynebacterium + Staph aureus + Strep pyogenes = Production of exotoxin that destroys macrophages

### C. Activation of complement-mediated lysis

Complement primarily targets extracellular bacteria, mainly encapsulated bacteria

**Buzzword Combo:** encapsulated bacteria (Neisseria spp., H. influenza, S. pneumoniae, E.coli)= complement-mediated bacterial lysis

###  D. Increased opsonization via capsule formation

 TB virulence is due to a lipid-rich cell wall (mycolic acid), not capsule formation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Klebsiella + Cryptococcus + H. influenza+ Neisseria spp. + S. pneumoniae = Increase opsonization via capsule formation/ C3b mediated phagocytosis
