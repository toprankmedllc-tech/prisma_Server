## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man with stable angina uses sublingual nitroglycerin as needed. He fills a prescription from his urologist for erectile dysfunction and takes both medications on the same evening. He presents shortly after with syncope and a blood pressure of 62/38 mmHg.
Which of the following medications most likely precipitated this event?

A) Finasteride 
B) Tamsulosin 
C) Sildenafil 
D) Oxybutynin

## Buzzwords Used

 Sublingual nitroglycerin; Erectile dysfunction; BP 62/38 mmHg

## Answer Options

A) Finasteride 
B) Tamsulosin 
C) Sildenafil 
D) Oxybutynin

## Correct Answer

C) Sildenafil 

## Explanation

Nitrates increase cyclic GMP by activating soluble guanylate cyclase through nitric oxide, producing vascular smooth muscle relaxation. Sildenafil is a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor that prevents cGMP degradation. When both are taken together, synergistic cGMP accumulation produces profound, uncontrolled systemic vasodilation and life-threatening hypotension. This combination is absolutely contraindicated. 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Nitrate + sildenafil = Severe hypotension

## Wrong Options

### A) Finasteride 

Finasteride is a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor used for benign prostatic hyperplasia and androgenetic alopecia. It has no vasoactive properties and does not interact with the nitrate-cGMP pathway. 

**Buzzword Combo:** 5-alpha reductase inhibitor + BPH or androgenetic alopecia = Finasteride 

### B) Tamsulosin 

Tamsulosin is an alpha-1A antagonist that used for benign prostatic hyperplasia, but it does not act on the cGMP pathway and would not produce the severe hemodynamic collapse described here. 

**Buzzword Combo:** alpha-1A antagonist + BPH = Tamsulosin 

### D) Oxybutynin

Oxybutynin is an anticholinergic agent for overactive bladder with no interaction with the nitrate-cGMP pathway and no significant hemodynamic effects in this context. 

**Buzzword Combo:** anticholinergic + overactive bladder = Oxybutynin
