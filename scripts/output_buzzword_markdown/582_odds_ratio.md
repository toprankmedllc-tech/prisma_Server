## Buzzword Question

A case-control study investigates the association between smoking and a rare lung disease. The study includes 100 patients with the disease and 100 healthy controls. Among patients with the disease, 60 report a history of smoking. Among healthy controls, 30 report a history of smoking.

Which of the following expressions correctly calculates the odds ratio for the association between smoking and the disease?

## Buzzwords Used

Case-control study ; Smoking ; Lung disease ; Odds ratio ; 2×2 table

## Answer Options

A) (60 × 70) / (40 × 30)

B) (60/100) ÷ (30/100)

C) (60/100) − (30/100)

D) [(60/100) − (30/100)] ÷ (30/100)

## Correct Answer

A) (60 × 70) / (40 × 30)

## Explanation

The odds ratio in a 2×2 table is calculated using the cross-product formula: (a×d)/(b×c). This compares the odds of exposure among cases with the odds of exposure among controls.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Case-control study + 2×2 table + odds ratio calculation = (a×d)/(b×c)

## Wrong Options

### B) (60/100) ÷ (30/100)

This calculates a relative risk (risk ratio) by comparing the proportion of disease among exposed and unexposed groups. Relative risk is typically calculated in cohort studies, not case-control studies.

**Buzzword Combo:** Incidence in exposed ÷ incidence in unexposed = Relative risk

### C) (60/100) − (30/100)

This calculates the absolute risk reduction/risk difference, which measures the absolute change in risk between two groups.

**Buzzword Combo:** Risk in exposed − risk in unexposed = Absolute risk difference

### D) [(60/100) − (30/100)] ÷ (30/100)

This calculates the relative risk reduction/increase, which expresses the proportional change in risk relative to the baseline risk.

**Buzzword Combo:** Risk difference ÷ baseline risk = Relative risk reduction/increase
