## Buzzword Question

A 59-year-old man with early Parkinson disease is started on a medication to delay initiation of levodopa therapy. Several months later, his wife reports that he has developed compulsive gambling behavior and excessive daytime sleepiness. Which medication is most likely responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

Early Parkinson disease ; delay levodopa ; compulsive gambling ; impulse control disorder ; daytime sleepiness ; dopamine agonist

## Answer Options

A) Pramipexole
B) Carbidopa
C) Trihexyphenidyl
D) Selegiline

## Correct Answer

A) Pramipexole

## Explanation

Pramipexole is a non-ergot dopamine agonist commonly used in younger patients with early Parkinson disease to delay levodopa therapy. Dopamine agonists are strongly associated with impulse control disorders such as gambling, hypersexuality, and compulsive shopping.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Young Parkinson patient + compulsive gambling + daytime sleepiness = Pramipexole

## Wrong Options

### B) Carbidopa

Carbidopa inhibits peripheral DOPA decarboxylase and is combined with levodopa to reduce peripheral adverse effects. It is not associated with impulse control disorders.

**Buzzword Combo:** Peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibition + reduced nausea + combined with levodopa = Carbidopa

### C) Trihexyphenidyl

Trihexyphenidyl is an antimuscarinic drug used mainly for tremor and rigidity. Common adverse effects are dry mouth, urinary retention, and confusion rather than gambling behavior.

**Buzzword Combo:** Tremor predominant Parkinson + antimuscarinic + dry mouth = Trihexyphenidyl

### D) Selegiline

Selegiline is an MAO-B inhibitor that decreases dopamine breakdown in the brain. It may cause insomnia but is not classically linked to impulse control disorders.

**Buzzword Combo:** MAO-B inhibition + dopamine preservation + insomnia = Selegiline
