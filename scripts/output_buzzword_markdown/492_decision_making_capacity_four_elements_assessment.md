## Buzzword Question

A 67-year-old man with mild cognitive impairment is admitted for a hip fracture repair. When the surgeon explains the procedure, risks, and alternatives, the patient states: 'I understand there's a surgery needed, but I don't want it,  I'd rather go home and rest.' He cannot explain the consequences of refusing surgery. The surgeon is uncertain whether to proceed. Which of the following is the first step?

## Buzzwords Used

Mild cognitive impairment; hip fracture; refusing surgery; cannot explain consequences; surgeon uncertain; capacity assessment

## Answer Options

A) Formally assess decision-making capacity using the four elements: understanding, appreciation, reasoning, and expression of a consistent choice 
B) Override the patient's refusal because he has cognitive impairment 
C) Contact the patient's family to make the decision 
D) Proceed with surgery because hip fracture repair is in the patient's best interest


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Decision-making capacity (DMC) is a clinical assessment, not a legal determination (competency is legal). DMC must be assessed for every specific decision. The four elements are: (1) Understanding — can the patient understand the information provided? (2) Appreciation — does the patient recognize how the information applies to their own situation? (3) Reasoning — can the patient reason through the decision using the information? (4) Expression — can the patient communicate a consistent choice? This patient's inability to explain the consequences of refusal raises concerns about appreciation and reasoning. Cognitive impairment ALONE does not remove capacity — it triggers formal assessment. Only after determining lack of capacity should surrogates or best-interest decisions be made.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

DMC assessment = Understanding + Appreciation + Reasoning + Consistent expression; cognitive impairment alone ≠ lacks capacity; must formally assess first

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Override the patient's refusal because he has cognitive impairment


Cognitive impairment does not automatically negate decision-making capacity. A patient with mild dementia may retain capacity for simple decisions while lacking it for complex ones. Overriding refusal without a formal capacity assessment violates patient autonomy. The law requires capacity assessment before substituted decision-making

**Buzzword Combo:** Cognitive impairment ≠ automatic lack of capacity; formal DMC assessment required first; capacity is decision-specific

### 2: C) Contact the patient's family to make the decision

Family involvement is appropriate only after determining that the patient lacks decision-making capacity. Bypassing the patient's right to self-determination without a capacity assessment is ethically and legally inappropriate. If the patient has capacity, his refusal must be respected even if family disagrees.

**Buzzword Combo:** Surrogate/family decision = appropriate ONLY after confirmed lack of capacity; must assess capacity first

### 3: D) Proceed with surgery because hip fracture repair is in the patient's best interest

Best-interest standard applies when a patient lacks capacity AND has no advance directive or surrogate. Proceeding without capacity assessment and over the patient's expressed refusal constitutes battery. Best interest cannot override an autonomous patient's refusal.

**Buzzword Combo:** Best-interest standard = last resort when: lacks capacity + no surrogate + no advance directive; cannot override competent patient's refusal
