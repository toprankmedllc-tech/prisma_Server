## Buzzword Question

A 67-year-old man with type 2 diabetes and hypertension is brought to the ER after a 3-hour episode of septic shock requiring vasopressors. His blood pressure is now stabilized at 118/74 mmHg. Labs show creatinine 3.8 mg/dL (baseline 1.1), BUN 58 mg/dL, and urinalysis reveals muddy brown granular casts. Urine sodium is 65 mEq/L and FENa is 3.2%. Which region of the kidney sustained the most damage and why?

## Buzzwords Used

Septic shock ; muddy brown casts ; FENa >2%

## Answer Options

A. Glomerulus
B. Renal medulla 
C.Juxtaglomerular apparatus
D.Ureteropelvic junction

## Correct Answer

Option B

## Explanation

The muddy brown granular casts + elevated FENa (>2%) + rising creatinine after a hypotensive episode = acute tubular necrosis (ATN). The medulla is preferentially damaged because it receives significantly less blood flow than the cortex, making it exquisitely sensitive to ischemia. The straight portion of the proximal tubule (S3) and thick ascending limb in the outer medulla are the most vulnerable segments.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Muddy brown granular casts + hypotension + FENa>2% 
Medulla receives disproportionately low blood flow making it most susceptible to ischemia.

## Wrong Options

### Option A -Glomerulus

Incorrect — While glomeruli can be injured in other conditions (eg, glomerulonephritis), they are cortical and receive robust blood flow; not the primary target in ischemic ATN.

**Buzzword Combo:** Robust blood flow+ glomerulonephritis+ not primary site for ATN  

### Option c JGA

Vasopressors may affect renin release, but JGA injury does not explain ATN or muddy brown casts.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​

**Buzzword Combo:** NA

### Option D Uretropelvic Junction 

The UPJ is a common site of obstruction due to stones(struvite) , not ischemic tubular injury; unrelated to ATN pathophysiology.

**Buzzword Combo:** Stones (struvite) +not common ischemic tubular injury site
