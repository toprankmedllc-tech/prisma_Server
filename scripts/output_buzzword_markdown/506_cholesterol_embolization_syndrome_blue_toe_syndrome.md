## Buzzword Question

An elderly patient develops painful, dusky blue toes with intact distal pulses 2 weeks after cardiac catheterization. Which of the following best explains this finding?

## Buzzwords Used

Elderly post-catheterization; dusky blue toes; intact distal pulses; blue toe syndrome; cholesterol embolization

## Answer Options

A) Microvascular occlusion by cholesterol crystals embolizing from atherosclerotic plaque
B) Occlusive thrombus in a major peripheral artery
C) Vasospasm of digital arteries from cold exposure
D) Systemic vasculitis with palpable purpura

## Correct Answer

A) Microvascular occlusion by cholesterol crystals embolizing from atherosclerotic plaque

## Explanation

"Blue toe syndrome" — digital ischemia with intact distal pulses — reflects microvascular occlusion by cholesterol crystals embolizing from an atherosclerotic plaque. Distal pulses are preserved because the larger feeding arteries remain patent; only the small arteries supplying the digits are occluded.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)


Post-catheterization + blue toes + intact distal pulses = cholesterol microemboli (blue toe syndrome)

## Wrong Options

### B) Occlusive thrombus in a major peripheral artery

Large-artery thrombotic occlusion produces absent distal pulses (acute limb ischemia with the 6 P's), not blue toes with intact pulses.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acute limb ischemia + absent pulses + 6 P's = large-vessel arterial occlusion

### C) Vasospasm of digital arteries from cold exposure

Describes Raynaud phenomenon — triphasic color changes (white → blue → red) on cold exposure with normal warm baseline, not persistent blue toes after a vascular procedure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cold-induced triphasic color change (white-blue-red) = Raynaud phenomenon

### D) Systemic vasculitis with palpable purpura

Vasculitis (e.g., hypersensitivity, IgA, ANCA-associated) produces palpable purpura rather than blue toes with intact pulses, and has different inciting factors.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Palpable purpura + leukocytoclastic vasculitis on biopsy = hypersensitivity vasculitis
