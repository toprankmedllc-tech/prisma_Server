## Buzzword Question

A climber develops high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE). Which vascular mechanism is primarily responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

High altitude; pulmonary edema; hypoxemia; pulmonary vascular changes; V/Q mismatch; hypoxic environment

## Answer Options

A) Global pulmonary vasodilation due to hypoxia
B) Uneven hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction 
C) Decreased capillary hydrostatic pressure due to dehydration
D) Left ventricular systolic failure causing pulmonary congestion

## Correct Answer

B) Uneven hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction 

## Explanation

At high altitude, low alveolar oxygen triggers hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction. When this response is exaggerated and uneven across lung regions, it increases pulmonary artery pressure and causes capillary stress failure, leading to non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema (HAPE).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

High altitude + hypoxia + patchy lung perfusion → uneven hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction → pulmonary hypertension → HAPE

## Wrong Options

### A) Global pulmonary vasodilation due to hypoxia

Pulmonary arteries constrict in response to hypoxia, not dilate. Vasodilation would improve oxygenation rather than cause pulmonary edema.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypoxia + vasodilation concept + incorrect pulmonary physiology reversal

### C) Decreased capillary hydrostatic pressure due to dehydration

HAPE is caused by increased, not decreased, pulmonary capillary pressure due to vasoconstriction-induced hypertension.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dehydration + low volume state + irrelevant to pulmonary capillary pressure rise

### D) Left ventricular systolic failure causing pulmonary congestion

Cardiogenic pulmonary edema requires left ventricular dysfunction, which is not present in high-altitude pulmonary edema.

**Buzzword Combo:** LV failure + cardiogenic edema + mismatch with high-altitude setting
