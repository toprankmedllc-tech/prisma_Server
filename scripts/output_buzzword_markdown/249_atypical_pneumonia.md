## Buzzword Question

A 26-year-old medical student presents with 5 days of low-grade fever, dry cough, and malaise after attending a crowded dorm gathering. He reports headache and sore throat but no pleuritic chest pain. Chest X-ray shows diffuse interstitial infiltrates rather than lobar consolidation. Cold agglutinin test is positive. Which organism is most likely responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

Young adult; crowded dorm; dry cough; low-grade fever; headache; interstitial infiltrates; cold agglutinins positive

## Answer Options

A) Streptococcus pneumoniae
B) Mycoplasma pneumoniae
C) Klebsiella pneumoniae
D) Staphylococcus aureus

## Correct Answer

B) Mycoplasma pneumoniae

## Explanation

This is Mycoplasma pneumoniae, a classic cause of atypical “walking pneumonia” in young adults in crowded settings. It presents with dry cough, low-grade fever, and interstitial infiltrates, and is associated with positive cold agglutinins due to autoimmune hemolysis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Crowded dorm + young adult + dry cough + interstitial infiltrates + cold agglutinins = Mycoplasma pneumoniae

## Wrong Options

### A) Streptococcus pneumoniae

S. pneumoniae causes lobar pneumonia with productive cough and rust-colored sputum, not dry cough or interstitial infiltrates, and is more acute and severe.

**Buzzword Combo:** Rust sputum + lobar consolidation + lancet diplococci = pneumococcal CAP mismatch

### C) Klebsiella pneumoniae

Klebsiella occurs in alcoholics and hospitalized patients with mucoid sputum and upper lobe cavitation, not young healthy individuals with atypical interstitial pneumonia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alcoholic + mucoid sputum + cavitation + Gram-negative capsule = Klebsiella mismatch

### D) Staphylococcus aureus

S. aureus causes post-influenza necrotizing pneumonia with abscess formation and Gram-positive cocci in clusters, not atypical interstitial pneumonia or cold agglutinin positivity

**Buzzword Combo:** Post-influenza + abscess + Gram-positive clusters = Staph aureus mismatch
