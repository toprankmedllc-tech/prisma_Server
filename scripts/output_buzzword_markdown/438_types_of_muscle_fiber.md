## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old competitive sprinter participates in a muscle physiology study examining skeletal muscle fiber composition in elite athletes. A biopsy sample from his quadriceps muscle demonstrates a predominance of fast-twitch fibers specialized for explosive activity. On gross examination, these fibers appear pale or white compared with the darker red fibers seen in endurance athletes. The researchers explain that this appearance is related to the metabolic properties of the fibers.
Which of the following best explains the pale color of type II muscle fibers?

## Buzzwords Used

fibers appear pale or white compared with the darker red fibers seen in endurance athletes

## Answer Options

A. Low myoglobin content and reduced capillary density
B. Increased mitochondrial concentration within the sarcoplasm
C. High levels of oxidative phosphorylation enzymes
D. Abundant oxygen storage for prolonged aerobic activity

## Correct Answer

A. Low myoglobin content and reduced capillary density

## Explanation

Type II (fast-twitch glycolytic) muscle fibers appear pale or white because they contain: Low myoglobin concentration ,Fewer mitochondria ,Reduced capillary supply ,Predominantly anaerobic metabolism ,These fibers are specialized for rapid, powerful contractions but fatigue quickly.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

White muscle fibers + low myoglobin + anaerobic metabolism + explosive activity = Type II fibers

## Wrong Options

### B. Increased mitochondrial concentration within the sarcoplasm

Type II glycolytic fibers contain fewer mitochondria than oxidative type I fibers.

**Buzzword Combo:** Aerobic metabolism + many mitochondria + endurance = type I fibers

### C. High levels of oxidative phosphorylation enzymes

Oxidative enzymes are abundant in red slow oxidative fibers, not pale fast glycolytic 

**Buzzword Combo:** Oxidative metabolism + fatigue resistance + endurance activity = oxidative fibers

### D. Abundant oxygen storage for prolonged aerobic activity

High oxygen storage due to abundant myoglobin is characteristic of type I red fibers.

**Buzzword Combo:** High myoglobin + sustained activity + red muscle = type I fibers
