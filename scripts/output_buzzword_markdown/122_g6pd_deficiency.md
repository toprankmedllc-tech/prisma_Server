## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old man develops fatigue, jaundice, and dark urine 48 hours after starting trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, with a prior episode after eating legumes. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

22-year-old man; fatigue; jaundice; dark urine; trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole; legumes; episodic hemolysis; oxidative trigger

## Answer Options

A) Hereditary spherocytosis
B) Autoimmune hemolytic anemia
C) G6PD deficiency
D) Beta-thalassemia minor

## Correct Answer

C. G6PD deficiency

## Explanation

G6PD deficiency is an X-linked disorder causing reduced NADPH production, which impairs glutathione regeneration. Oxidative stress from drugs (like sulfonamides) or fava beans triggers episodic hemolysis with bite cells and Heinz bodies.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Sulfa drugs + fava beans + bite cells = G6PD deficiency

## Wrong Options

### A. Hereditary spherocytosis

Hereditary spherocytosis causes chronic hemolysis due to RBC membrane defects (spectrin/ankyrin), leading to spherocytes and increased osmotic fragility. It is not triggered by oxidative stress or drugs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Spherocytes + splenomegaly + osmotic fragility = Hereditary spherocytosis

### B. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

Autoimmune hemolytic anemia is mediated by antibodies against RBCs and is Coombs-positive, often showing spherocytes. It is not classically triggered by fava beans or oxidative drugs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Coombs positive + spherocytes + warm antibodies = AIHA

### D. Beta-thalassemia minor

Beta-thalassemia minor causes chronic microcytic anemia with target cells due to reduced beta-globin synthesis. It does not cause episodic hemolysis triggered by oxidative stress.

**Buzzword Combo:** Microcytosis + target cells + ↑HbA2 = Beta-thalassemia minor
