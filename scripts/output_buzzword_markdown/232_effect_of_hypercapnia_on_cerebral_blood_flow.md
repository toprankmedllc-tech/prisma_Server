## Buzzword Question

A 46-year-old man with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is brought to the emergency department due to worsening confusion and somnolence. Arterial blood gas reveals markedly elevated PaCO₂. Neurologic examination shows no focal deficits. The physician explains that the patient’s elevated carbon dioxide level is increasing cerebral blood flow and contributing to increased intracranial pressure.

Which of the following best explains this mechanism?

## Buzzwords Used

COPD; confusion; somnolence; elevated PaCO₂; increased intracranial pressure

## Answer Options

A) Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasodilation
B) Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasoconstriction
C) Hypoxemia decreases cerebral blood flow
D) Increased MAP decreases cerebral perfusion pressure

## Correct Answer

A) Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasodilation

## Explanation

Carbon dioxide is a major regulator of cerebral blood flow. Elevated PaCO₂ causes cerebral vasodilation, which increases cerebral blood flow and intracranial pressure

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

COPD + elevated PaCO₂ + increased ICP = cerebral vasodilation

## Wrong Options

### B) Hypercapnia causes cerebral vasoconstriction

Hypercapnia produces vasodilation, not vasoconstriction. Vasoconstriction occurs with hypocapnia during hyperventilation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyperventilation + low CO₂ + vasoconstriction = decreased cerebral blood flow

### C) Hypoxemia decreases cerebral blood flow

Severe hypoxemia actually increases cerebral blood flow through vasodilation in order to preserve oxygen delivery.

**Buzzword Combo:** PaO₂ below 50 mm Hg + vasodilation + increased perfusion = hypoxic response

### D) Increased MAP decreases cerebral perfusion pressure

CPP equals MAP minus ICP. Increasing MAP generally increases CPP unless ICP rises proportionally.

**Buzzword Combo:** MAP increase + CPP increase + improved perfusion = opposite relationship
