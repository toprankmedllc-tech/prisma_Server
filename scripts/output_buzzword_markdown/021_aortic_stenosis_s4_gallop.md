## Buzzword Question

A 75-year-old man with exertional syncope and a delayed carotid upstroke is found to have a low-frequency, late diastolic heart sound heard best at the cardiac apex. This extra heart sound is most directly generated by which of the following mechanisms? 

## Buzzwords Used

Exertional syncope; delayed carotid upstroke; late diastolic heart sound 

## Answer Options

A) Rapid ventricular filling into a dilated cavity 
B) Opening of a thickened mitral valve 
C) Atrial contraction against a stiff ventricular wall 
D) Prolapse of the mitral valve leaflets into the atrium 

## Correct Answer

C) Atrial contraction against a stiff ventricular wall 

## Explanation

The patient has an S4 heart sound, which occurs in late diastole. In the setting of aortic stenosis, the left ventricle undergoes concentric hypertrophy, making it stiff and noncompliant. The S4 sound is generated by the left atrium vigorously contracting to push blood into this stiff, noncompliant ventricle. 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Delayed carotid upstroke + late diastolic sound + stiff ventricle = S4 gallop (Atrial contraction) 

## Wrong Options

### A) Rapid ventricular filling into a dilated cavity 

Rapid ventricular filling into a dilated cavity generates an S3 heart sound, which occurs in early diastole and is associated with volume overload and systolic heart failure. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Dilated cavity + early diastolic sound + volume overload = S3 gallop 

### B) Opening of a thickened mitral valve 

The opening of a thickened mitral valve generates an opening snap, a high-frequency early diastolic sound characteristic of mitral stenosis. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Thickened mitral valve + early diastolic sound + rumbling murmur = Opening snap 

### D) Prolapse of the mitral valve leaflets into the atrium 

Prolapse of the mitral valve leaflets generates a mid-systolic click, not a diastolic sound. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Mid-systolic sound + prolapsing leaflets = Mid-systolic click
