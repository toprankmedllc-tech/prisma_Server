## Buzzword Question

A 52-year-old woman presents with progressive unilateral hearing loss, tinnitus, and difficulty maintaining balance. Physical examination shows decreased corneal reflex on the affected side. MRI reveals a cerebellopontine angle mass. Which cranial nerve is most likely primarily affected?

## Buzzwords Used

Unilateral hearing loss; tinnitus; balance difficulty; cerebellopontine angle mass; decreased corneal reflex

## Answer Options

A) Trigeminal nerve (CN V)
B) Facial nerve (CN VII)
C) Vestibulocochlear nerve (CN VIII)
D) Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)

## Correct Answer

C) Vestibulocochlear nerve (CN VIII)

## Explanation

Vestibular schwannoma classically presents with unilateral sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, and balance problems due to CN VIII compression. Large tumors in the cerebellopontine angle can also compress CN V, decreasing the corneal reflex.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Unilateral hearing loss + tinnitus + cerebellopontine angle tumor = vestibular schwannoma affecting CN VIII

## Wrong Options

### A) Trigeminal nerve (CN V)

CN V involvement can decrease the corneal reflex, but it does not explain the hearing loss and tinnitus. CN V compression occurs secondarily in large tumors.

**Buzzword Combo:** Facial sensation loss + weak corneal reflex + mastication defects = CN V lesion

### B) Facial nerve (CN VII)

CN VII lesions cause facial weakness, hyperacusis, and impaired taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue rather than isolated hearing loss.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bell palsy + hyperacusis + loss of taste = CN VII dysfunction

### D) Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)

CN IX lesions cause impaired gag reflex, dysphagia, and loss of taste from the posterior third of the tongue, not hearing abnormalities.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dysphagia + absent gag reflex + posterior tongue taste loss = CN IX lesion
