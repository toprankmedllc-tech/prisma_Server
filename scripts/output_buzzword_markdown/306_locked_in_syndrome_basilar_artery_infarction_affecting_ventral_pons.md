## Buzzword Question

A 58-year-old man suddenly collapses and is brought to the emergency department. He is awake and able to blink vertically on command but cannot move his arms, legs, or lower face. Sensation is intact. MRI shows infarction of the ventral pons.

Which structure is most likely damaged?

## Buzzwords Used

Awake but quadriplegic ; preserved consciousness ; vertical eye movements intact ; ventral pontine infarct ; basilar artery thrombosis

## Answer Options

A) Medial longitudinal fasciculus
B) Corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts
C) Vestibular nuclei
D) Spinothalamic tract

## Correct Answer

B) Corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts

## Explanation

Locked-in syndrome is caused by basilar artery infarction damaging the ventral pons. Bilateral corticospinal and corticobulbar tract injury causes quadriplegia and paralysis of lower cranial nerves while sparing consciousness and vertical eye movements.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Awake quadriplegic patient + preserved blinking + ventral pons lesion = Corticospinal and corticobulbar tract damage

## Wrong Options

### A) Medial longitudinal fasciculus

MLF lesions cause internuclear ophthalmoplegia with impaired adduction and nystagmus, not complete body paralysis with preserved consciousness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Failure of adduction + abducting nystagmus + multiple sclerosis = Medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion

### C) Vestibular nuclei

Vestibular nuclei lesions produce vertigo, nausea, vomiting, and nystagmus rather than quadriplegia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vertigo + vomiting + nystagmus = Vestibular nuclei lesion

### D) Spinothalamic tract

Spinothalamic tract lesions cause loss of pain and temperature sensation contralaterally but do not produce locked-in syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Contralateral pain/temp loss + preserved vibration + spinal cord lesion = Spinothalamic tract damage
