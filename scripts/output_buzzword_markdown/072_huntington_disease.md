## Buzzword Question

A 42-year-old man presents with progressive choreiform movements, irritability, depression, and memory decline. His father had similar symptoms and died in his 50s. MRI shows atrophy of the caudate nucleus. Degeneration of which of the following pathways is most responsible for this patient’s hyperkinetic movements?

## Buzzwords Used

Chorea; personality changes; autosomal dominant; caudate atrophy; hyperkinesia

## Answer Options

A) Degeneration of GABA neurons in the indirect pathway of the striatum

B) Loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta

C) Lesion of the subthalamic nucleus

D) Destruction of cerebellar Purkinje cells

## Correct Answer

A) Degeneration of GABA neurons in the indirect pathway of the striatum

## Explanation

Huntington disease causes degeneration of GABAergic neurons in the indirect pathway of the striatum, particularly the caudate nucleus. Loss of indirect pathway inhibition leads to excessive thalamic stimulation and hyperkinetic choreiform movements.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chorea + caudate atrophy + autosomal dominant = Huntington disease

## Wrong Options

### B) Loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta

Loss of substantia nigra dopamine neurons is seen in Parkinson disease and causes hypokinetic symptoms such as bradykinesia and rigidity rather than chorea.

**Buzzword Combo:** Resting tremor + rigidity + bradykinesia = Parkinson disease

### C) Lesion of the subthalamic nucleus

Subthalamic nucleus lesions cause hemiballismus with violent flinging movements, usually after a lacunar stroke, not progressive chorea with dementia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Flinging movements + lacunar stroke + STN lesion = Hemiballismus

### D) Destruction of cerebellar Purkinje cells

Purkinje cell degeneration causes cerebellar dysfunction such as ataxia, dysmetria, and intention tremor rather than choreiform hyperkinesia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ataxia + dysmetria + intention tremor = Cerebellar lesion
