## Buzzword Question

55-year-old woman comes to the physician due to worsening fatigue, constipation, and mild bone pain over the past 3 months. Laboratory testing demonstrates a corrected serum calcium level of 11.4 mg/dL and a serum phosphorus level of 2.2 mg/dL. Her serum parathyroid hormone (PTH) level is elevated. In the kidneys, PTH works to correct calcium homeostasis. Which of the following details the exact location and transport mechanism upregulated by PTH to maximize calcium reabsorption?

## Buzzwords Used

Fatigue, constipation, bone pain; serum calcium 11.4 mg/dL; serum phosphorus 2.2 mg/dL; elevated PTH

## Answer Options

A) Apical Ca2+ channels (TRPV5) in the distal convoluted tubule
B) Paracellular passive diffusion driven by the positive lumen potential in the thick ascending limb
C) Basolateral Na 
+/Ca 2+ exchangers in the proximal convoluted tubule
D) Apical Na +/K+–2Cl −
  cotransporter in the thick ascending limb

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

PTH directly upregulates transcellular calcium reabsorption in the distal convoluted tubule (DCT) by increasing the opening probability of apical TRPV5 calcium channels. Calcium then diffuses across the cell and is pumped into the blood via basolateral Ca2+-ATPase and Na + /Ca 2+ exchangers.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Primary hyperparathyroidism + Distal convoluted tubule + Active transcellular transport = TRPV5 upregulation

## Wrong Options

### B

The majority of renal calcium ( 60−70%) is reabsorbed paracellularly in the proximal tubule and thick ascending limb ( 20%), but this passive process is modulated by the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) and loop voltage, whereas PTH specifically regulates the active transcellular mechanism in the DCT.

**Buzzword Combo:** Thick ascending limb + Positive lumen potential + Paracellular transport = Loop-diuretic sensitive calcium reabsorption

### C

The proximal convoluted tubule handles bulk calcium reabsorption passively alongside sodium and water; it is not the site of PTH-stimulated active calcium reabsorption, where PTH instead inhibits phosphate reabsorption.

**Buzzword Combo:** Proximal tubule + PTH activation + Phosphate wasting = Inhibition of sodium-phosphate cotransport

### D

The Na+−K+−2Cl −
  cotransporter creates the positive lumen charge that drives calcium reabsorption in the thick ascending limb, but it is not directly upregulated by PTH to actively extract calcium from the filtrate.

**Buzzword Combo:** NKCC2 cotransporter + Potassium recycling + Paracellular calcium drive = Thick ascending limb passive clearance
