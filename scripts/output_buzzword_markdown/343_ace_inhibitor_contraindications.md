## Buzzword Question

A 29-year-old woman with hypertension and chronic kidney disease is started on lisinopril. She returns 6 weeks later pregnant at 8 weeks gestation. Which of the following best describes the fetal risk of continuing lisinopril?

A) Fetal cardiac septal defects
B) Fetal renal tubular dysgenesis
C) Neonatal hyperthyroidism
D) Neural tube defects 

## Buzzwords Used

Pregnant woman;  lisinopril

## Answer Options

A) Fetal cardiac septal defects
B) Fetal renal tubular dysgenesis
C) Neonatal hyperthyroidism
D) Neural tube defects 

## Correct Answer

B) Fetal renal tubular dysgenesis

## Explanation

ACE inhibitors are absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy. Fetal angiotensin II is essential for normal renal development and perfusion. ACEi blockade of fetal RAAS → fetal renal tubular dysgenesis → ↓ urine output → oligohydramnios → Potter sequence complications: pulmonary hypoplasia (from oligohydramnios), skull hypoplasia, limb contractures, intrauterine growth restriction, and renal failure. 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)


ACEi + pregnancy = Fetal renal failure 

## Wrong Options

### A) Fetal cardiac septal defects

While first-trimester ACEi exposure has been associated with increased cardiac defects in some studies, the most established and severe teratogenic effect is fetal renal tubular dysgenesis 

**Buzzword Combo:** Lithium + pregnancy = Ebstein anomaly and septal defects.

### C) Neonatal hyperthyroidism

ACE inhibitors have no effect on thyroid ACE receptors or thyroid function. Neonatal thyroid disease is associated with maternal antithyroid antibodies (Graves)

**Buzzword Combo:** Neonatal hyperthyroidism + maternal Graves = TSH receptor antibody crossing placenta 

### D) Neural tube defects 

Neural tube defects are associated with folate deficiency and antiepileptic drugs not ACE inhibitors. ACEi has no mechanism for folate depletion

**Buzzword Combo:** Valproate + pregnancy = Neural tube defects
