## Buzzword Question

A 23-year-old man with long extremities and pectus excavatum dies suddenly from rupture of the ascending aorta. Histopathologic examination of the aortic wall would most likely demonstrate ?

## Buzzwords Used

Long extremities; pectus excavatum; ascending aortic rupture;

## Answer Options

A) Intimal foam cell accumulation
B) Cystic medial degeneration with fragmentation of elastic fibers
C) Granulomatous inflammation with multinucleated giant cells
D) Fibrinoid necrosis of small vessels

## Correct Answer

B) Cystic medial degeneration with fragmentation of elastic fibers

## Explanation

Marfan syndrome weakens connective tissue within the aortic media because of defective fibrillin-1, predisposing patients to aortic aneurysm and dissection.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Aortic dissection + marfanoid habitus + cystic medial degeneration = Marfan syndrome

## Wrong Options

### A) Intimal foam cell accumulation

Foam cells are characteristic of atherosclerosis rather than connective tissue disorders affecting the aorta.

**Buzzword Combo:** Cholesterol plaques + foam cells = Atherosclerosis

### C) Granulomatous inflammation with multinucleated giant cells

Granulomatous inflammation with giant cells is characteristic of giant cell arteritis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Temporal headache + jaw claudication + giant cells = Giant cell arteritis

### D) Fibrinoid necrosis of small vessels

Fibrinoid necrosis is associated with vasculitis and malignant hypertension.

**Buzzword Combo:** Severe hypertension + vessel wall necrosis = Fibrinoid necrosis
