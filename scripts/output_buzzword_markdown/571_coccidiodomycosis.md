## Buzzword Question

A 35-year-old construction worker in Arizona develops fever, cough, and erythema nodosum after a dust storm. Microscopy demonstrates large spherules filled with endospores. Which organism is responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

Arizona; Dust storm exposure; Fever; Cough; Erythema nodosum; Spherules with endospores

## Answer Options

A) Histoplasma capsulatum
B) Blastomyces dermatitidis
C) Coccidioides immitis
D) Paracoccidioides brasiliensis

## Correct Answer

C) Coccidioides immitis

## Explanation

Coccidioides immitis is a dimorphic fungus endemic to the southwestern United States. Infection follows inhalation of arthroconidia from disturbed soil and is characterized by large spherules containing endospores in tissue. Erythema nodosum is a classic associated finding.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Arizona + Dust storm exposure + Spherules with endospores = Coccidioides immitis

## Wrong Options

### A) Histoplasma capsulatum

Histoplasma is associated with bat and bird droppings in the Ohio and Mississippi River Valleys and appears as intracellular yeasts within macrophages.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ohio River Valley + Bat droppings + Intracellular yeast in macrophages = Histoplasma capsulatum

### B) Blastomyces dermatitidis

Blastomyces is identified by broad-based budding yeast and is more commonly associated with the Great Lakes and Ohio River Valley regions.

**Buzzword Combo:** Great Lakes region + Verrucous skin lesions + Broad-based budding yeast = Blastomyces dermatitidis

### D) Paracoccidioides brasiliensis

Paracoccidioides is endemic to Latin America and demonstrates multiple budding yeast cells arranged in a "captain's wheel" pattern.

**Buzzword Combo:** Latin America + Mucocutaneous lesions + Captain's wheel budding yeast = Paracoccidioides brasiliensis
