## Buzzword Question

A 30-year-old woman develops sudden shortness of breath, chest tightness, and tingling in her fingers after an acute panic attack. ABG shows low PaCO₂ with mild hypoxemia. Oxygen saturation improves rapidly with supplemental oxygen. No cardiopulmonary disease is found. Which of the following best describes her A–a gradient?



## Buzzwords Used

panic attack; hyperventilation; low PaCO₂; anxiety; transient hypoxemia; normal lung exam; rapid oxygen response

## Answer Options

A) Increased A–a gradient due to V/Q mismatch
B) Increased A–a gradient due to shunt
C) Normal A–a gradient due to hypoventilation
D) Normal A–a gradient due to hyperventilation

## Correct Answer

D) Normal A–a gradient due to hyperventilation

## Explanation

In hyperventilation due to anxiety, both PAO₂ and PaO₂ change proportionally, and there is no intrinsic defect in gas exchange. Therefore, the A–a gradient remains normal because oxygen transfer across the alveoli is intact.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

panic attack + hyperventilation + intact lungs → normal A–a gradient

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased A–a gradient due to V/Q mismatch

V/Q mismatch occurs in lung diseases like COPD or pneumonia, not in isolated anxiety-induced hyperventilation with normal lung function.

**Buzzword Combo:** lung disease + ventilation-perfusion inequality + abnormal gas exchange

### B) Increased A–a gradient due to shunt

Shunt physiology requires perfused but non-ventilated alveoli (e.g., ARDS, pneumonia), which is absent in this patient.

**Buzzword Combo:** alveolar filling + refractory hypoxemia + true shunt physiology

### C) Normal A–a gradient due to hypoventilation

Hypoventilation is characterized by elevated PaCO₂, not low PaCO₂ as seen in hyperventilation; thus this option mismatches the clinical scenario.

**Buzzword Combo:** CO₂ retention + reduced ventilation + respiratory depression= opiod overdose
