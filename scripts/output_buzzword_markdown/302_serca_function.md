## Buzzword Question

A physiology student is studying skeletal muscle relaxation during a laboratory session. The professor explains that after muscle contraction, calcium ions must be rapidly transported from the cytoplasm back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum by the SERCA pump. During an experiment, a mutation causes defective SERCA function in skeletal muscle fibers.
Which of the following is the most likely consequence of this defect?

## Buzzwords Used

 defective SERCA function in skeletal muscle fibers

## Answer Options

A. Prolonged elevation of cytosolic calcium causing impaired muscle relaxation
B. Inability of calcium to bind troponin C during contraction
C. Failure of acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction
D. Decreased ATP hydrolysis by myosin heads during the power stroke

## Correct Answer

A. Prolonged elevation of cytosolic calcium causing impaired muscle relaxation

## Explanation

SERCA (Sarcoplasmic/Endoplasmic Reticulum Ca2+-ATPase) is responsible for actively transporting calcium ions from the cytoplasm back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum after muscle contraction. If SERCA is defective, calcium remains elevated in the cytoplasm, allowing continued actin-myosin interaction and impaired muscle relaxation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Calcium reuptake defect + prolonged contraction + impaired relaxation = SERCA dysfunction

## Wrong Options

### B. Inability of calcium to bind troponin C during contraction

Troponin C itself binds calcium normally; the problem is failure of calcium removal from the cytoplasm.

**Buzzword Combo:** Calcium binding + thin filament regulation + contraction initiation = troponin C


### C. Failure of acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction

Acetylcholine release is mediated by presynaptic calcium-dependent vesicle fusion, not SERCA.

**Buzzword Combo:** Neuromuscular transmission + synaptic vesicles + motor end plate = acetylcholine release

### D. Decreased ATP hydrolysis by myosin heads during the power stroke

ATP hydrolysis during contraction is performed by myosin ATPase, not SERCA.


**Buzzword Combo:** Power stroke + cross-bridge cycling + ATPase activity = myosin head
