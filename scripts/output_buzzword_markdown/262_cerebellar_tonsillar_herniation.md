## Buzzword Question

A 55-year-old woman with a large posterior fossa hemorrhage from a cerebellar arteriovenous malformation abruptly develops apnea, profound bradycardia, hypotension, and complete loss of brainstem reflexes within minutes. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Buzzwords Used

Posterior fossa mass; abrupt cardiorespiratory collapse; loss of brainstem reflexes; medullary compression

## Answer Options

A) Cerebellar tonsillar herniation through the foramen magnum
B) Uncal herniation through the tentorial notch
C) Subfalcine herniation under the falx cerebri
D) Upward cerebellar herniation through the tentorial notch

## Correct Answer

A) Cerebellar tonsillar herniation through the foramen magnum

## Explanation

A rapidly expanding posterior fossa mass pushes the cerebellar tonsils downward through the foramen magnum, compressing the medulla and producing acute apnea, bradycardia, hypotension, and loss of brainstem reflexes (Cushing triad and brain death physiology).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Posterior fossa mass + apnea + bradycardia + hypotension + loss of brainstem reflexes = cerebellar tonsillar herniation through foramen magnum

## Wrong Options

### Uncal herniation through the tentorial notch

Uncal herniation is caused by a supratentorial mass and produces an ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil with contralateral hemiparesis, not isolated cardiorespiratory collapse from a posterior fossa mass.

**Buzzword Combo:** Supratentorial mass + ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil + contralateral hemiparesis = uncal herniation

### Subfalcine herniation under the falx cerebri

Subfalcine herniation compresses the ACA and causes contralateral leg weakness from a frontal supratentorial mass; it does not cause acute cardiorespiratory collapse.

**Buzzword Combo:** Frontal mass + ACA compression + contralateral leg weakness more than arm = subfalcine herniation

### Upward cerebellar herniation through the tentorial notch

Upward cerebellar herniation can occur when a posterior fossa mass pushes the cerebellum upward through the tentorial notch (often precipitated by ventricular drainage in a patient with a posterior fossa mass), compressing the midbrain. It causes midbrain signs but is less common and would not be the textbook explanation for acute medullary failure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Posterior fossa mass + ventricular shunting precipitating midbrain compression = upward (reverse) transtentorial herniation

## Related Concepts

Chiari malformations (chronic congenital tonsillar herniation vs acute pressure-driven); Cushing triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations) of raised ICP; Posterior fossa mass effect and the dangers of ventricular drainage
