## Buzzword Question

A 4-year-old boy has recurrent infections caused by Serratia marcescens, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Aspergillus species. Neutrophils from this patient fail to generate reactive oxygen species after phagocytosis. Which of the following tests is classically used to diagnose this disorder?

## Buzzwords Used

4-year-old boy; recurrent Serratia marcescens infections; recurrent Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections; recurrent Aspergillus infections; failure to generate reactive oxygen species after phagocytosis

## Answer Options

A) Dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry
B) Serum immunoglobulin quantification
C) Delayed-type hypersensitivity skin testing
D) Lymphocyte subset flow cytometry

## Correct Answer

A) Dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry


## Explanation

Chronic granulomatous disease is diagnosed with dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry, which evaluates the neutrophil oxidative burst. NADPH oxidase deficiency prevents normal production of reactive oxygen species needed for intracellular killing.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

4-year-old boy + recurrent Serratia/Pseudomonas/Aspergillus infections + failed reactive oxygen species generation = Dihydrorhodamine flow cytometry

## Wrong Options

### B) Serum immunoglobulin quantification

Serum immunoglobulin quantification evaluates humoral immunodeficiency, not phagocyte oxidative burst dysfunction. CGD typically has defective intracellular killing despite phagocytosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent sinopulmonary infections + low immunoglobulins + humoral deficiency = Serum immunoglobulin quantification

### C) Delayed-type hypersensitivity skin testing

Delayed-type hypersensitivity skin testing assesses T-cell–mediated immunity. CGD is caused by a neutrophil oxidative burst defect rather than primary T-cell dysfunction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Poor delayed skin response + T-cell dysfunction + cell-mediated immunity defect = Delayed-type hypersensitivity testing

### D) Lymphocyte subset flow cytometry

Lymphocyte subset flow cytometry evaluates numbers of T cells, B cells, and NK cells, especially in suspected combined immunodeficiency. CGD requires testing neutrophil reactive oxygen species production.

**Buzzword Combo:** Early severe infections + low T cells + combined immunodeficiency = Lymphocyte subset flow cytometry
