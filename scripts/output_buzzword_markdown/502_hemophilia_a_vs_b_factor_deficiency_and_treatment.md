## Buzzword Question

An 8-year-old boy presents with recurrent hemarthroses, easy bruising, and prolonged bleeding after dental extraction. His CBC is normal. Labs: PT normal, aPTT prolonged, bleeding time normal, platelet count normal. He is found to have Factor VIII deficiency. His maternal uncle has a similar condition. Which of the following correctly identifies his diagnosis, inheritance pattern, and treatment?

## Buzzwords Used

Hemarthroses; easy bruising; prolonged post-extraction bleeding; normal PT; prolonged aPTT; normal bleeding time; Factor VIII deficiency; maternal uncle affected

## Answer Options

A) Hemophilia A (Factor VIII deficiency); X-linked recessive; treat with recombinant Factor VIII concentrate 
B) Hemophilia B (Factor IX deficiency); X-linked recessive; treat with recombinant Factor IX concentrate 
C) Von Willebrand disease; autosomal dominant; treat with DDAVP (desmopressin) 
D) Hemophilia A; autosomal recessive; treat with fresh frozen plasma


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

A) Hemophilia A (Factor VIII deficiency); X-linked recessive; treat with recombinant Factor VIII concentrate B) Hemophilia B (Factor IX deficiency); X-linked recessive; treat with recombinant Factor IX concentrate C) Von Willebrand disease; autosomal dominant; treat with DDAVP (desmopressin) D) Hemophilia A; autosomal recessive; treat with fresh frozen plasma


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hemophilia A = Factor VIII; X-linked recessive; hemarthroses + prolonged aPTT + normal PT/BT; treatment = recombinant Factor VIII; maternal uncle = X-linked pattern

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Hemophilia B (Factor IX deficiency); X-linked recessive; treat with recombinant Factor IX concentrate

Hemophilia B (Christmas disease) has an identical clinical presentation to Hemophilia A (hemarthroses, prolonged aPTT, normal PT, X-linked recessive) but results from Factor IX deficiency. The specific factor assay showing Factor VIII deficiency distinguishes them. Treatment for B is Factor IX, not Factor VIII.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemophilia B = Factor IX deficiency; identical presentation to A; distinguished by factor assay; treat with recombinant Factor IX

### 2: C) Von Willebrand disease; autosomal dominant; treat with DDAVP (desmopressin)


Von Willebrand disease (vWD) presents with mucocutaneous bleeding (epistaxis, menorrhagia, gum bleeding) and a prolonged bleeding time (platelet plug formation depends on vWF). The aPTT may be mildly elevated because vWF stabilizes Factor VIII. However, hemarthroses are not typical of vWD,  they indicate deep coagulation pathway defects (hemophilia). The normal bleeding time in this patient argues against vWD.

**Buzzword Combo:** vWD = mucocutaneous bleeding + prolonged bleeding time + vWF deficiency; hemarthroses = hemophilia not vWD

### 3: D) Hemophilia A; autosomal recessive; treat with fresh frozen plasma

Hemophilia A is X-linked recessive, not autosomal recessive. The affected maternal uncle pattern confirms X-linked inheritance. Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) contains all clotting factors but is non-specific, requires large volumes, and carries infection risk,  recombinant factor concentrates are the preferred targeted treatment.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hemophilia A = X-linked recessive (not autosomal recessive); treat with recombinant Factor VIII (not FFP)
