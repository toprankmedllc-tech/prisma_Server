## Buzzword Question

24-year-old woman presents to the clinic complaining of muscle weakness and intermittent cramps. She admits to a long history of purging behaviors and restrictive eating. Physical examination shows dental erosions and parotid gland enlargement. Laboratory studies reveal a serum potassium level of 2.8 mEq/L, a serum bicarbonate level of 32 mEq/L, and a blood pressure of 98/60 mmHg. Urinalysis shows a urine chloride concentration of 8 mEq/L (normal is greater than 20 mEq/L). Which of the following renal physiological responses is most directly driving the maintenance of her metabolic alkalosis and worsening her hypokalemia?


## Buzzwords Used

Purging behaviors; dental erosions; parotid gland enlargement; serum potassium 2.8 mEq/L; urine chloride 8 mEq/L

## Answer Options

A)Increased hydrogen ion secretion via apical H+/K +-ATPase in the intercalated cells
B) Aldosterone-mediated sodium reabsorption in exchange for potassium and hydrogen secretion in the collecting duct
C) Direct inhibition of the Na/K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb
D) Decreased proximal tubule reabsorption of bicarbonate due to volume expansion


## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

Vomiting causes a loss of gastric HCl, leading to volume depletion and activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS). High aldosterone levels promote sodium reabsorption in the principal cells, generating a negative lumen potential that drives potassium and hydrogen excretion, maintaining both the hypokalemia and metabolic alkalosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Vomiting + Low urine chloride + High aldosterone = RAAS-driven potassium/hydrogen wasting

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased hydrogen ion secretion via apical H+/K + -ATPase in alpha-intercalated cells

severe hypokalemia, the H+/K + -ATPase in alpha-intercalated cells is actually upregulated to reabsorb potassium at the expense of secreting hydrogen ions, which contributes to the alkalosis but acts as a compensatory attempt to save potassium, rather than driving the potassium wasting.

**Buzzword Combo:** Severe hypokalemia + Intercalated cell activation + Urinary acidification = H +/K +ATPase upregulation

### C) Direct inhibition of the Na+−K +−2Cl − cotransporter in the thick ascending limb

Inhibition of the Na+−K +−2Cl − cotransporter describes the mechanism of loop diuretics (or Bartter syndrome), which would present with an elevated urine chloride concentration (greater than 20 mEq/L).

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypokalemia + Metabolic alkalosis + High urine chloride = Loop diuretic abuse / Bartter syndrome

### D) Decreased proximal tubule reabsorption of bicarbonate due to volume expansion

The patient is volume-depleted, not volume-expanded; volume depletion actually increases proximal tubule reabsorption of bicarbonate via angiotensin II, which prevents the kidneys from excreting excess bicarbonate.

**Buzzword Combo:** Volume expansion + Bicarbonate wasting + Normalization of pH = Contraction alkalosis reversal
