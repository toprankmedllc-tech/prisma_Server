## Buzzword Question

A 70-year-old man with trigeminal neuralgia refractory to medical therapy undergoes high-resolution MRI of the brain. The imaging study is most likely to demonstrate which underlying anatomic cause?

## Buzzwords Used

Older adult; classic trigeminal neuralgia; medically refractory; high-resolution MRI showing aberrant arterial loop

## Answer Options

A) Acoustic neuroma compressing CN V
B)  Plaque at the pontine root entry zone
C) Trigeminal schwannoma in Meckel cave
D) Aberrant arterial loop compressing the CN V root at its pontine entry zone

## Correct Answer

D) Aberrant arterial loop compressing the CN V root at its pontine entry zone

## Explanation

In adults over 50 with classic trigeminal neuralgia, the most common cause is compression of the CN V root entry zone by a vascular loop, typically a redundant superior cerebellar artery. Pulsatile compression is thought to produce focal demyelination and ephaptic transmission. Surgical microvascular decompression (Jannetta procedure) can be curative.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Older adult + classic trigeminal neuralgia + MRI showing aberrant vessel at CN V root + treated by microvascular decompression = vascular loop compression

## Wrong Options

### Acoustic neuroma compressing CN V

Acoustic neuroma at the cerebellopontine angle classically presents with progressive unilateral sensorineural hearing loss and tinnitus, not isolated trigger-evoked face pain.

**Buzzword Combo:** Progressive unilateral sensorineural hearing loss + tinnitus + CPA mass = vestibular schwannoma

###  Plaque at the pontine root entry zone

MS-related trigeminal neuralgia occurs in YOUNGER patients with other CNS demyelinating features. It is uncommon as the cause in patients over 50 without prior MS history.

**Buzzword Combo:** Trigeminal neuralgia in young adult + optic neuritis history + INO + periventricular plaques = MS

### Trigeminal schwannoma in Meckel cave

A trigeminal schwannoma usually produces progressive face sensory loss and dull continuous facial pain with persistent interictal deficits, not paroxysmal trigger-evoked stabs with a normal interictal exam.

**Buzzword Combo:** Progressive face sensory loss + dull continuous pain + Meckel cave mass = trigeminal schwannoma
