## Buzzword Question

A 6-week-old boy has severe recurrent infections caused by viruses, fungi, bacteria, and protozoa. Flow cytometry shows markedly decreased T cells, and antibody responses to protein antigens are poor. The most common cause of this disorder is a mutation affecting which of the following?

## Buzzwords Used

6-week-old boy; severe recurrent infections caused by viruses, fungi, bacteria, and protozoa; markedly decreased T cells; poor antibody responses to protein antigens

## Answer Options

A) Bruton tyrosine kinase
B) Common gamma chain of the IL-2 receptor
C) CD40 ligand on activated T cells
D) NADPH oxidase complex

## Correct Answer

B) Common gamma chain of the IL-2 receptor


## Explanation

The most common cause of SCID is an X-linked recessive mutation in the common gamma chain of the IL-2 receptor. The resulting profound T-cell defect causes loss of cell-mediated immunity and impaired T-cell–dependent humoral responses.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

6-week-old boy + recurrent infections of all types + low T cells = X-linked common gamma chain mutation causing SCID

## Wrong Options

### A) Bruton tyrosine kinase

BTK mutation causes Bruton agammaglobulinemia, which presents around 6 months of age with recurrent encapsulated bacterial infections. It does not cause severe infections of all organism types at 6 weeks of age.

**Buzzword Combo:** 6-month-old boy + recurrent encapsulated bacterial infections + low B cells = BTK mutation

### C) CD40 ligand on activated T cells

CD40 ligand deficiency causes hyper-IgM syndrome due to impaired class switching. B cells are present, but they cannot switch effectively from IgM to other immunoglobulin classes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent pyogenic infections + high IgM + low IgG/IgA/IgE = CD40L deficiency

### D) NADPH oxidase complex

NADPH oxidase deficiency causes chronic granulomatous disease, a phagocyte oxidative burst defect. It causes recurrent catalase-positive infections, not profound T-cell deficiency with impaired humoral responses.

**Buzzword Combo:** Catalase-positive infections + granuloma formation + abnormal oxidative burst = Chronic granulomatous disease
