## Buzzword Question

A young man with chronic distal weakness, pes cavus, stork-leg deformity, and uniformly slowed nerve conduction velocities is found to have the most common form of hereditary demyelinating peripheral neuropathy. Which genetic abnormality is most likely responsible?

## Buzzwords Used

Hereditary demyelinating polyneuropathy (CMT type 1A); most common form; chromosome 17

## Answer Options

A) SMN1 deletion
B) Dystrophin frameshift mutation
C) PMP22 duplication on chromosome 17p11.2
D) SOD1 missense mutation

## Correct Answer

C) PMP22 duplication on chromosome 17p11.2

## Explanation

CMT type 1A is caused by a 1.4 megabase duplication of PMP22 on chromosome 17p11.2. Overexpression of peripheral myelin protein 22 destabilizes peripheral myelin and produces repeated demyelination and remyelination, the substrate of the onion-bulb pathology.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hereditary demyelinating polyneuropathy + uniformly slowed nerve conduction + chromosome 17 + onion-bulb biopsy = PMP22 duplication (CMT1A)

## Wrong Options

### SMN1 deletion

SMN1 deletion causes spinal muscular atrophy, a pure lower motor neuron disease of infants and children with hypotonia and tongue fasciculations, not an adult demyelinating peripheral neuropathy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Floppy infant + tongue fasciculations + pure LMN signs + autosomal recessive = SMN1 deletion (spinal muscular atrophy)

### Dystrophin frameshift mutation

Dystrophin frameshift causes Duchenne muscular dystrophy, an X-linked recessive disease of boys with proximal weakness, calf pseudohypertrophy, and markedly elevated CK, not a peripheral neuropathy.

**Buzzword Combo:** Boy + proximal weakness + Gowers sign + calf pseudohypertrophy + elevated CK = dystrophin frameshift (Duchenne)

### SOD1 missense mutation

SOD1 mutation is the most commonly identified gene in familial ALS, a motor neuron disease with mixed UMN and LMN signs, not a hereditary peripheral demyelinating neuropathy.

**Buzzword Combo:** UMN + LMN signs + midlife onset + autosomal dominant family history = SOD1 mutation (familial ALS)
