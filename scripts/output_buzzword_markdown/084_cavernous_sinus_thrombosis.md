## Buzzword Question

A 34-year-old man presents with fever, severe headache, and progressive pain and swelling around his right eye for 3 days. He reports a recent boil on his upper lip that he tried to squeeze. On examination, he has periorbital edema, proptosis, and complete inability to abduct the right eye. There is also decreased sensation over the right forehead. Fundoscopy shows papilledema. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Fever ; facial skin infection ; upper lip boil ; periorbital edema ; proptosis ; ophthalmoplegia ; inability to abduct eye ; V1 sensory loss ; papilledema

## Answer Options

A) Orbital cellulitis
B) Cavernous sinus thrombosis
C) Brain abscess
D) Subarachnoid hemorrhage

## Correct Answer

B) Cavernous sinus thrombosis

## Explanation

Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis results from spread of facial infections (especially from the “danger triangle” of the face). It causes fever, painful ophthalmoplegia (CN III, IV, VI), proptosis, and V1/V2 sensory loss due to involvement of cranial nerves in the cavernous sinus.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Facial infection + ophthalmoplegia + proptosis + V1 loss = Cavernous sinus thrombosis

## Wrong Options

### A) Orbital cellulitis

Orbital Cellulitis causes painful eye movement and swelling but typically does not cause multiple cranial nerve palsies or V1/V2 sensory loss.

**Buzzword Combo:** Eyelid swelling + painful eye movement + fever = Orbital cellulitis

### C) Brain abscess

Brain Abscess presents with focal neurologic deficits and fever but does not classically cause proptosis or multiple ocular cranial nerve palsies.

**Buzzword Combo:** Fever + ring enhancing lesion + focal deficit = Brain abscess

### D) Subarachnoid hemorrhage

Subarachnoid Hemorrhage presents with sudden thunderclap headache and neck stiffness, not fever or orbital signs

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden worst headache + neck stiffness + xanthochromia = Subarachnoid hemorrhage
