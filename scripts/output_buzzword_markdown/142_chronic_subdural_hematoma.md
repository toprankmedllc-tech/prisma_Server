## Buzzword Question

An 82-year-old man on warfarin for atrial fibrillation has a low-impact fall 3 weeks ago. He now has progressive confusion and gait unsteadiness. CT shows a hyperdense crescent-shaped collection along the convexity of the right hemisphere that crosses the cranial suture lines. Which underlying mechanism best explains his hemorrhage?

## Buzzwords Used

Elderly patient; anticoagulation; low-impact trauma; crescent-shaped collection; crosses suture lines; subacute progressive cognitive decline

## Answer Options

A) Laceration of the middle meningeal artery
B) Tearing of bridging cortical veins
C) Rupture of a saccular aneurysm 
D) Rupture of a Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysm 

## Correct Answer

B) Tearing of bridging cortical veins

## Explanation

Subdural hematomas result from tearing of the bridging cortical veins that traverse the subdural space from the cerebral cortex to the dural venous sinuses. In elderly patients, age-related cerebral atrophy stretches these veins, making them vulnerable to rupture even with minor head trauma. Anticoagulation further raises the risk and slows clot formation, explaining the subacute course. Blood accumulates between the dura and arachnoid in a CRESCENT shape that CROSSES suture lines, since the subdural space is not constrained by dural attachments at sutures.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Elderly + cerebral atrophy + anticoagulation + low-impact fall + crescent shape + crosses sutures = bridging cortical vein tear → subdural hematoma

## Wrong Options

### Laceration of the middle meningeal artery

This mechanism causes an EPIDURAL hematoma: a biconvex/lens-shaped hyperdensity that does NOT cross suture lines, classically with a lucid interval after temple trauma in younger patients. The shape and patient profile here are wrong.

**Buzzword Combo:** Young patient + temple trauma + lucid interval + lens-shaped + does NOT cross sutures = middle meningeal artery → epidural hematoma

### Rupture of a saccular aneurysm

Anterior communicating artery aneurysm rupture causes SUBARACHNOID hemorrhage with a thunderclap headache and blood layered in the basal cisterns and sulci, not a crescent over the cortical convexity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden worst-headache-of-life + meningismus + ADPKD + blood in basal cisterns = anterior communicating artery → subarachnoid hemorrhage

### Rupture of a Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysm in the basal ganglia

Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms rupture in chronic hypertension and produce INTRAPARENCHYMAL hemorrhage in the basal ganglia (especially putamen), thalamus, pons, or cerebellum, not a subdural convexity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic uncontrolled hypertension + basal ganglia / putaminal bleed + Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysm = lenticulostriate artery → intraparenchymal hemorrhage

## Related Concepts

Epidural hematoma (middle meningeal artery, biconvex shape); Subarachnoid hemorrhage (anterior communicating artery aneurysm); Hypertensive intraparenchymal hemorrhage (Charcot-Bouchard / lenticulostriate)
