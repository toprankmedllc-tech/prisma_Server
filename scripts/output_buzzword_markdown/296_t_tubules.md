## Buzzword Question

A physiology student is studying excitation-contraction coupling in skeletal muscle. A toxin selectively damages the structure responsible for transmitting depolarization from the sarcolemma deep into the muscle fiber. Following stimulation, acetylcholine release and motor end-plate depolarization occur normally, but muscle contraction is weak due to reduced calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
Which of the following structures was most likely damaged?

## Buzzwords Used

muscle contraction is weak due to reduced calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum

## Answer Options

A. Sarcoplasmic reticulum
B. T tubules
C. Myosin thick filaments
D. Synaptic vesicles

## Correct Answer

B. T tubules

## Explanation

Sarcolemmal depolarization→ T tubule depolarization→ Ca2+ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum
T tubules are deep invaginations of the sarcolemma that conduct depolarization into the interior of skeletal muscle fibers, triggering calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum during excitation-contraction coupling.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Deep sarcolemmal invaginations + intracellular depolarization + excitation-contraction coupling = T tubules

## Wrong Options

### A. Sarcoplasmic reticulum

Stores calcium and releases it during muscle contraction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Intracellular Ca²⁺ storage + ryanodine receptors + muscle contraction = sarcoplasmic reticulum

### C. Myosin thick filaments

**Buzzword Combo:** ATPase activity + thick filament + power stroke = myosin

### D. Synaptic vesicles

Store and release acetylcholine from the presynaptic terminal.


**Buzzword Combo:** Presynaptic terminal + neurotransmitter storage + exocytosis = synaptic vesicles
