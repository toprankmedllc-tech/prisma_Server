## Buzzword Question

A patient with severe alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency continues to have progressive COPD symptoms despite smoking cessation and bronchodilator therapy. Which treatment directly replaces the deficient protein?

## Buzzwords Used

Severe alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency; COPD; replacement therapy


## Answer Options

 A) IV augmentation therapy
 B) Inhaled corticosteroids
 C) Lung volume reduction surgery
 D) Oral azithromycin


## Correct Answer

A. IV augmentation therapy


## Explanation

IV augmentation therapy provides alpha-1 antitrypsin replacement and is indicated in individuals with severe deficiency.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Severe AAT deficiency + replacement treatment + COPD = IV augmentation therapy

## Wrong Options

### B. Inhaled corticosteroids


Inhaled corticosteroids may improve COPD symptoms but do not replace alpha-1 antitrypsin.


**Buzzword Combo:** Airway inflammation + asthma/COPD + inhaled anti-inflammatory = Inhaled corticosteroids


### C. Lung volume reduction surgery


Surgery may help selected emphysema patients but does not correct the underlying deficiency.


**Buzzword Combo:** Advanced emphysema + hyperinflation + surgical management = Lung volume reduction surgery


### D. Oral azithromycin


Azithromycin may reduce COPD exacerbations but is not replacement therapy.

**Buzzword Combo:** COPD exacerbation prevention + anti-inflammatory antibiotic + chronic therapy = Azithromycin
