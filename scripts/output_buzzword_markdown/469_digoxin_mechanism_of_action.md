## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man with heart failure and atrial fibrillation is started on digoxin for symptom control and ventricular rate control. Which of the following best describes the mechanism by which digoxin improves cardiac contractility?

A) Beta-1 receptor agonism 
B) Na/K-ATPase inhibition
C) Phosphodiesterase-3 inhibition 
D) Direct L-type calcium channel activation


## Buzzwords Used

B) Na/K-ATPase inhibition

## Answer Options

A) Beta-1 receptor agonism 
B) Na/K-ATPase inhibition
C) Phosphodiesterase-3 inhibition 
D) Direct L-type calcium channel activation

## Correct Answer

B) Na/K-ATPase inhibition

## Explanation

Digoxin inhibits Na/K-ATPase, causing intracellular sodium to accumulate. Elevated intracellular sodium reduces the activity of the sodium-calcium exchanger, impairing calcium efflux from cardiomyocytes. The resulting increase in intracellular calcium enhances contractile force.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Na/K-ATPase inhibition + HF = Digoxin 

## Wrong Options

### A) Beta-1 receptor agonism 

Beta-1 agonism is the mechanism of dobutamine. Digoxin does not interact with adrenergic receptors. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Positive Inotropy + Positive Chronotropy + Increased Renin = Beta-1 agonism

### C) Phosphodiesterase-3 inhibition 

PDE-3 inhibition is the mechanism of milrinone. Digoxin does not affect phosphodiesterases. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Inotropy + vasodilation = PDE-3 inhibition (Milrinone)

### D) Direct L-type calcium channel activation 

Digoxin raises intracellular calcium indirectly through NCX after Na/K-ATPase inhibition. It does not directly activate L-type calcium channels. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Vasoconstriction + Inotropy =  L-type calcium channel activation
