## Buzzword Question

During a rapid IV saline infusion, a patient's stroke volume increases. This response is best explained by which of the following principles?

## Buzzwords Used

IV saline infusion; increased preload; increased stroke volume; Frank-Starling law

## Answer Options

A) Increased ventricular end-diastolic volume increases stroke volume via optimized sarcomere length-tension overlap
B) Increased afterload decreases stroke volume
C) Wall tension is proportional to pressure and radius and inversely proportional to wall thickness
D) Cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume

## Correct Answer

A) Increased ventricular end-diastolic volume increases stroke volume 

## Explanation

The Frank-Starling law states that increased preload (LVEDV) stretches sarcomeres to a more favorable length-tension position, optimizing actin-myosin overlap and increasing stroke volume. IV fluid increases venous return → ↑ preload → ↑ SV.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

IV fluid + ↑ venous return + ↑ LVEDV → ↑ stroke volume = Frank-Starling law

## Wrong Options

### 
B) Increased afterload decreases stroke volume

A true statement, but it describes the afterload-SV relationship, not the preload-driven Frank-Starling response.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
↑ Afterload + ↓ stroke volume = afterload-SV relationship

### C) Wall tension is proportional to pressure and radius and inversely proportional to wall thickness

This is Laplace's law, which explains wall tension in dilated ventricles and aneurysm expansion.

**Buzzword Combo:** 

T = (P × r) / 2h = Laplace's law

### D) Cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume

This is the cardiac output formula (CO = HR × SV), not the Frank-Starling law.

**Buzzword Combo:** CO = HR × SV = cardiac output formula
