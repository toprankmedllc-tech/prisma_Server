## Buzzword Question

A 69-year-old man is admitted following an acute inferior STEMI managed with thrombolysis. On day 3, he develops sudden-onset severe dyspnea and a new loud holosystolic murmur at the cardiac apex with radiation to the axilla. Echocardiography reveals a flail posterior mitral valve leaflet with severe mitral regurgitation.

Which of the following arteries is most likely responsible for the papillary muscle ischemia leading to this complication?

A) Left anterior descending artery 
B) Left circumflex artery 
C) Posterior descending artery 
D) Obtuse marginal artery


## Buzzwords Used

 Inferior STEMI; holosystolic murmur at the cardiac apex; Mitral regurgitation.

## Answer Options

A) Left anterior descending artery 
B) Left circumflex artery 
C) Posterior descending artery 
D) Obtuse marginal artery

## Correct Answer

C) Posterior descending artery 

## Explanation

The posteromedial papillary muscle is vulnerable to ischemia in inferior MIs because it relies solely on the posterior descending artery (PDA). Unlike the anterolateral muscle's dual supply, even mild PDA ischemia can cause muscle rupture, leading to acute, life-threatening mitral regurgitation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Inferior MI + acute severe MR + flail posterior mitral leaflet = posteromedial papillary muscle rupture

## Wrong Options

### A) Left anterior descending artery 

The LAD branches contribute to the blood supply of the anterolateral papillary muscle, which has dual supply and is therefore more resistant to ischemia and rupture. LAD territory disease produces anterior MI, not inferior MI with posterior leaflet pathology. 

**Buzzword Combo:** LAD + LCX = anterolateral papillary muscle (dual supply)

### B) Left circumflex artery 

The anterolateral papillary muscle receives a dual blood supply from the left anterior descending (LAD) and left circumflex (LCX) arteries. The left circumflex does not supply the posteromedial papillary muscle in right-dominant circulation

**Buzzword Combo:** LAD + LCX = anterolateral papillary muscle (dual supply)

### D) Obtuse marginal artery

The obtuse marginal artery is a branch of the LCX that supplies the lateral wall and contributes to anterolateral papillary muscle supply. It does not supply the posteromedial papillary muscle. 

**Buzzword Combo:**  lateral wall + anterolateral papillary muscle = Obtuse marginal
