## Buzzword Question

A 2-day-old male neonate is brought to the NICU due to progressive cyanosis and respiratory distress. He was born at term to a mother with poorly controlled gestational diabetes. Pulse oximetry shows severe hypoxemia .Physical examination reveals tachypnea and a single loud S2 without any significant murmur. Chest radiograph demonstrates an “egg-on-a-string” cardiac silhouette. Which of the following is the most important initial management to ensure survival?

## Buzzwords Used

Cyanosis; Maternal diabetes; egg on a string appearance 


## Answer Options

A) Administration of Indomethacin
B) Administration of Prostaglandin E1
C) Immediate surgical closure of the ductus arteriosus
D) High-dose oxygen therapy

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

The diagnosis is Transposition of the Great Vessels (TGV), which is "egg-on-a-string" appearance on X-ray and cyanosis that is independent of oxygen. Survival depends on maintaining a shunt (PDA or VSD) to allow mixing of the separate systemic and pulmonary circuits.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

TGV + Separate Circuits = PGE1 (Keep PDA open)

## Wrong Options

### A) Administration of Indomethacin

Indomethacin is a COX inhibitor used to close a Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA), which would be fatal in a patient with TGV.

**Buzzword Combo:** Indomethacin + NSAID = PDA Closure

### C) Immediate surgical closure of the ductus arteriosus

Closing the ductus would prevent the mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, leading to immediate clinical deterioration.

**Buzzword Combo:** PDA closure + TGV = Death

### D) High-dose oxygen therapy

In cyanotic heart disease caused by structural "right-to-left" shunts or separate circuits, supplemental oxygen does little to increase systemic O2 saturation and may actually promote ductal closure.

**Buzzword Combo:** Oxygen + PDA = Vasoconstriction/Closure

## Related Concepts

Tetralogy of Fallot; Persistent Truncus Arteriosus; Prostaglandin vs. NSAID effects on ductus
