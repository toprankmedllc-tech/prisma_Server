## Buzzword Question

A 7-month-old boy develops recurrent sinopulmonary infections with encapsulated bacteria after several months of normal health. Physical examination shows very small tonsillar tissue and few palpable lymph nodes. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

7-month-old boy after several months of normal health; recurrent sinopulmonary infections with encapsulated bacteria; very small tonsillar tissue and few palpable lymph nodes

## Answer Options

A) Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia
B) Severe combined immunodeficiency
C) DiGeorge syndrome
D) Common variable immunodeficiency

## Correct Answer

A) Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia


## Explanation

Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia is caused by a BTK mutation that blocks B-cell maturation, leading to absent mature B cells and low immunoglobulins. It classically presents in boys around 6 months of age with recurrent encapsulated bacterial infections and scant lymphoid tissue.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Male infant after maternal IgG wanes + recurrent encapsulated bacterial infections + scant tonsils/lymph nodes = Bruton X-linked agammaglobulinemia

## Wrong Options

### B) Severe combined immunodeficiency

SCID causes combined cellular and humoral immune deficiency with recurrent bacterial, viral, fungal, and protozoal infections. This question points to isolated humoral deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent bacterial, viral, fungal, and protozoal infections + severe early infancy presentation + T-cell dysfunction = Severe combined immunodeficiency

### C) DiGeorge syndrome

DiGeorge syndrome causes T-cell deficiency due to thymic aplasia, usually with hypocalcemia and conotruncal heart defects. The clue pattern here is absent mature B-cell tissue and encapsulated bacterial infections.

**Buzzword Combo:** Conotruncal defect + hypocalcemia + absent thymic shadow = DiGeorge syndrome

### D) Common variable immunodeficiency

Common variable immunodeficiency presents later with recurrent infections and low immunoglobulins. The early male infant presentation strongly favors Bruton agammaglobulinemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Recurrent sinopulmonary infections + low immunoglobulins + later onset = Common variable immunodeficiency
