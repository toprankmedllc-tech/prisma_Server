## Buzzword Question

A 24-year-old medical student volunteers for a clinical physiology study assessing renal hemodynamic autoregulation. Baseline parameters, including blood pressure, renal blood flow, and glomerular filtration rate (GFR), are recorded as within normal limits. The student is then given an acute intravenous infusion of a selective β1 -adrenergic receptor agonist. After the infusion reaches a steady-state plasma concentration, which of the following mechanical and chemical changes is most likely occurring within this volunteer's juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)?

## Buzzwords Used

Juxtaglomerular apparatus; β 1 -adrenergic receptor agonist; Hemodynamic autoregulation; Renin release; Efferent arteriole vasoconstriction

## Answer Options

A) Direct stimulation of juxtaglomerular (JG) cells leading to increased renin secretion, resulting in downstream efferent arteriolar vasoconstriction and an increased filtration fraction (FF).
B) Increased stretching of the afferent arteriolar smooth muscle cells, triggering a reflex suppression of macula densa-mediated nitric oxide synthesis.
C) Enhanced NaCl delivery to the early distal convoluted tubule (DCT), causing a profound intracellular calcium influx into the specialized macular sensor cells.
D) Absolute stabilization of long-term GFR driven by an immediate, paradoxical down-regulation of pulmonary angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) expression.

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Activating β 1receptors directly stimulates the JG cells (modified smooth muscle of the afferent arteriole) to secrete renin. The resulting increase in systemic and local Angiotensin II preferentially targets the efferent arteriole, causing vasoconstriction; this preserves or increases GFR while decreasing renal plasma flow (RPF), thereby raising the filtration fraction (FF=GFR/RPF).

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Beta-1 agonist activation + Juxtaglomerular cells + Renin hypersecretion = Efferent arteriolar tone acceleration

## Wrong Options

### B

An acute increase in renin and subsequent vasoconstriction increases downstream systemic pressure, but it does not cause localized afferent stretching that suppresses macula densa nitric oxide pathways as its primary JGA driving mechanism.

**Buzzword Combo:** Afferent arteriolar stretch + Myogenic reflex + Nitric oxide suppression = Autoregulation loop mismatch

### C

Renin release and subsequent efferent vasoconstriction act to optimize or maintain GFR under lower pressure states, but a β 1agonist does not cause high NaCl delivery to trigger an inhibitory calcium influx in the macula densa; it bypasses the macula densa entirely via direct sympathetic tracking.

**Buzzword Combo:** Macula densa NaCl sensor + Intracellular calcium influx + Distal convoluted tubule delivery = Tubuloglomerular feedback inversion trap

### D

While the text notes that the JGA maintains long-term GFR via the RAAS system, acute or chronic JGA sympathetic stimulation has no direct or indirect impact on the constitutive expression of ACE within the pulmonary vascular endothelium.

**Buzzword Combo:** Long-term GFR stabilization + Pulmonary ACE modulation + Endothelial enzyme shift = Extrarenal regulatory fallacy
