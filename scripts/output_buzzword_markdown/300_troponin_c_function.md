## Buzzword Question

A physiology student is studying the process of excitation-contraction coupling in skeletal muscle. During the lecture, the professor explains that muscle contraction begins when calcium ions released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum bind to a specific regulatory protein on the thin filament. This binding produces a conformational change that moves tropomyosin away from the myosin-binding sites on actin, allowing cross-bridge formation to occur.
Which of the following proteins directly binds calcium ions during skeletal muscle contraction?

## Buzzwords Used

 proteins directly binds calcium ions during skeletal muscle contraction

## Answer Options

A. Tropomyosin
B. Titin
C. Troponin C
D. Myosin light chain

## Correct Answer

C. Troponin C

## Explanation

Troponin C is a component of the troponin complex located on the thin filament of skeletal muscle. During excitation-contraction coupling, calcium ions released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum bind directly to troponin C. This interaction causes a conformational change that shifts tropomyosin away from the myosin-binding sites on actin, allowing actin-myosin cross-bridge formation and muscle contraction.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Calcium binding + thin filament regulation + conformational change = troponin C

## Wrong Options

### A. Tropomyosin

Blocks the myosin-binding sites on actin during resting conditions.


**Buzzword Combo:** Actin site blockade + thin filament regulation + resting muscle = tropomyosin


### B. Titin

**Buzzword Combo:** Elastic recoil + sarcomere stabilization + thick filament support = titin

### D. Myosin light chain

Participates in regulation of myosin head activity but does not directly bind calcium in skeletal muscle contraction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Myosin regulation + thick filament component + motor activity = myosin light chain
