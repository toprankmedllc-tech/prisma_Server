## Buzzword Question

A 16-year-old girl is brought to the physician for evaluation of primary amenorrhea. She is at the 5th percentile for height and has a broad chest with widely spaced nipples. On cardiovascular examination, a bicuspid aortic valve is suspected. A cytogenetic study is performed. Which of the following is the most likely mechanism responsible for this patient’s chromosomal abnormality?

## Buzzwords Used

Primary amenorrhea; Short stature; Shield chest; Bicuspid aortic valve.

## Answer Options

A) Maternal meiotic non-disjunction
B) Paternal meiotic non-disjunction
C) Robertsonian translocation
D) Uniparental disomy

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

This patient has Turner Syndrome (45,X). The most common mechanism for the loss of the second X chromosome (in approximately 80% of cases) is the loss of the sex chromosome in the paternal sperm during meiosis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

45,X + Paternal non-disjunction = Turner Syndrome

## Wrong Options

### A) Maternal meiotic non-disjunction

While maternal non-disjunction is the primary cause of Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome), it is less commonly the cause of the single-X genotype in Turner Syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Maternal non-disjunction + Advanced maternal age = Trisomy 21

### C) Robertsonian translocation

Robertsonian translocations involve acrocentric chromosomes (13, 14, 15, 21, 22) and are a common cause of Familial Down Syndrome, but not Turner Syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acrocentric chromosomes + Translocation = Familial Down Syndrome


### D) Uniparental disomy

Uniparental disomy (two copies from one parent) causes imprinting disorders like Prader-Willi or Angelman, but not a monosomy like Turner Syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two copies from one parent + Imprinting = Prader-Willi/Angelman
