## Buzzword Question

A 61-year-old vegetarian man presents with progressive difficulty walking and numbness in his feet. Examination shows impaired vibration and position sense in both lower extremities, spasticity, hyperreflexia, and a positive Babinski sign. Romberg test is positive. Laboratory testing reveals macrocytic anemia.

Which spinal cord tracts are most likely involved?

## Buzzwords Used

Loss of vibration ; positive Romberg ; spasticity ; macrocytic anemia ; bilateral Babinski sign

## Answer Options

A) Spinothalamic tract only
B) Dorsal columns and lateral corticospinal tracts
C) Spinocerebellar tracts only
D) Anterior horn cells

## Correct Answer

B) Dorsal columns and lateral corticospinal tracts

## Explanation

Vitamin B12 deficiency causes subacute combined degeneration involving the dorsal columns and lateral corticospinal tracts. This leads to loss of vibration/proprioception with concurrent upper motor neuron signs such as spasticity and Babinski reflexes.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Macrocytic anemia + vibration loss + UMN signs = Dorsal columns and corticospinal tract degeneration

## Wrong Options

### A) Spinothalamic tract only

Spinothalamic tract lesions primarily cause pain and temperature deficits, which are not the dominant findings here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pain/temp loss + contralateral sensory deficit + anterior white commissure = Spinothalamic tract lesion

### C) Spinocerebellar tracts only

Spinocerebellar tract lesions cause ataxia without the marked vibration loss and UMN findings seen in B12 deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ataxic gait + dysmetria + cerebellar dysfunction = Spinocerebellar tract lesion

### D) Anterior horn cells

Anterior horn cell lesions cause lower motor neuron findings such as fasciculations and hyporeflexia, not spasticity and dorsal column deficits.

**Buzzword Combo:** Muscle atrophy + fasciculations + hyporeflexia = Anterior horn cell lesion
