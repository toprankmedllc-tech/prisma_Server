## Buzzword Question

A 28-year-old pregnant woman at 32 weeks gestation is found to have glucose in her urine on routine dipstick. Her fasting plasma glucose is 85 mg/dL and HbA1c is 5.1%. Which of the following best explains this finding?

## Buzzwords Used

Pregnancy; glucosuria; normal plasma glucose; increased GFR

## Answer Options

A) Decreased renal glucose reabsorption due to tubular damage
B) Gestational diabetes mellitus causing hyperglycemia
C) Increased GFR lowers the plasma glucose threshold for glucosuria
D) SGLT2 receptor downregulation in pregnancy

## Correct Answer

C

## Explanation

Normal pregnancy increases GFR, which increases filtered glucose load. This causes the renal threshold for glucosuria to occur at lower plasma glucose concentrations, resulting in glucosuria despite normal blood glucose levels.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Pregnancy + ↑GFR + normal glucose = glucosuria at lower threshold

## Wrong Options

### B) Gestational diabetes mellitus

This is the classic premature closure trap — glucosuria in pregnancy immediately suggests GDM, but her fasting glucose and HbA1c are completely normal, ruling out diabetes as the cause.

**Buzzword Combo:** Glucosuria + pregnancy = GDM (incorrect without hyperglycemia)

### A) Tubular damage

There is no clinical history suggesting tubular injury (no toxin exposure, no Fanconi features). Pregnancy-associated glucosuria is physiologic, not pathologic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Glucosuria + pregnancy = tubular damage (incorrect — physiologic mechanism)

### D) SGLT2 downregulation

SGLT2 inhibitors pharmacologically block reabsorption, but pregnancy does not downregulate SGLT2 receptors. The mechanism is hemodynamic (↑GFR), not transporter-based.

**Buzzword Combo:** Glucosuria + pregnancy = SGLT2 dysfunction (incorrect mechanism)
