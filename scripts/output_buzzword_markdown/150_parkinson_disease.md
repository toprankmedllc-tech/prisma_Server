## Buzzword Question

A 70-year-old man develops an asymmetric resting tremor, ratchet-like rigidity, and slow shuffling gait. Loss of which neuronal projection is most directly responsible for his motor symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

Asymmetric resting tremor; cogwheel rigidity; bradykinesia; shuffling gait

## Answer Options

A) Dopaminergic projection from substantia nigra pars compacta to the striatum
B) Cholinergic projection from the nucleus basalis of Meynert to the cortex
C) GABAergic projection from the caudate nucleus to the globus pallidus externus
D) Serotonergic projection from the raphe nuclei to the cortex

## Correct Answer

A) Dopaminergic projection from substantia nigra pars compacta to the striatum

## Explanation

Parkinson disease results from degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta that project to the striatum (the nigrostriatal pathway). Loss of striatal dopamine reduces facilitation of the direct pathway and disinhibits the indirect pathway, producing bradykinesia, rigidity, and resting tremor.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Asymmetric resting tremor + cogwheel rigidity + bradykinesia + shuffling gait = loss of nigrostriatal dopamine → Parkinson disease

## Wrong Options

### Cholinergic projection from the nucleus basalis of Meynert to the cortex

Loss of cholinergic neurons in the nucleus basalis of Meynert underlies the memory loss of Alzheimer disease, not parkinsonian motor signs.

**Buzzword Combo:** Progressive memory loss + cholinergic deficit + amyloid plaques + tau tangles = Alzheimer disease

### GABAergic projection from the caudate nucleus to the globus pallidus externus

Loss of these GABAergic medium spiny neurons causes Huntington disease, presenting with chorea and caudate atrophy on imaging, not bradykinesia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chorea + caudate atrophy + autosomal dominant family history + CAG repeats = Huntington disease

### Serotonergic projection from the raphe nuclei to the cortex

Serotonergic dysfunction from raphe nuclei is implicated in mood disorders such as major depressive disorder, not parkinsonism.

**Buzzword Combo:** Depressed mood + anhedonia + sleep/appetite disturbance = serotonergic deficit → major depression

## Related Concepts

Alzheimer disease (nucleus basalis of Meynert, cholinergic loss); Huntington disease (caudate GABAergic loss); Major depression (serotonin pathway)
