## Buzzword Question

A 30-year-old mountain climber is rescued after being trapped at very high altitude. He is confused and cyanotic with an arterial PaO₂ of 42 mm Hg. The physician explains that cerebral blood flow will increase despite otherwise normal autoregulation. Which of the following best explains this physiologic response?

## Buzzwords Used

High altitude; confusion; cyanosis; PaO₂ 42 mm Hg; increased cerebral blood flow

## Answer Options

A) Severe hypoxemia causes cerebral vasodilation
B) Decreased PaCO₂ causes cerebral vasoconstriction
C) Increased intracranial pressure increases CPP
D) Elevated MAP directly suppresses autoregulation

## Correct Answer

A) Severe hypoxemia causes cerebral vasodilation

## Explanation

Cerebral blood flow is primarily regulated by PaCO₂, but severe hypoxemia (PaO₂ < 50 mm Hg) also causes cerebral vasodilation. This increases cerebral blood flow in an attempt to preserve oxygen delivery to the brain.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

High altitude + PaO₂ below 50 mm Hg + confusion = hypoxia-induced cerebral vasodilation

## Wrong Options

### B) Decreased PaCO₂ causes cerebral vasoconstriction

Hypocapnia causes cerebral vasoconstriction and decreased cerebral blood flow. The question specifically focuses on severe hypoxemia as the trigger for increased perfusion.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyperventilation + low CO₂ + vasoconstriction = reduced cerebral blood flow

### C) Increased intracranial pressure increases CPP

Increased intracranial pressure lowers cerebral perfusion pressure because CPP = MAP − ICP. Elevated ICP impairs cerebral perfusion.

**Buzzword Combo:** Elevated ICP + decreased CPP + cerebral ischemia = impaired perfusion

### D) Elevated MAP directly suppresses autoregulation

Cerebral autoregulation maintains relatively constant blood flow across a range of MAP values. Severe hypoxemia, not MAP elevation, is responsible for vasodilation here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal autoregulation + MAP changes + stable flow = not hypoxia-mediated vasodilation
