## Buzzword Question

A newborn develops profound cyanosis shortly after birth. Chest radiograph demonstrates an “egg-on-a-string” appearance. Which embryologic defect most likely caused this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Egg-on-a-string ; Cyanosis at birth ; Conotruncal defect

## Answer Options

A) Failure of spiraling of the aorticopulmonary septum
B) Abnormal neural crest migration causing persistent truncus arteriosus
C) Defective endocardial cushion formation
D) Leftward displacement of the infundibular septum

## Correct Answer

A) Failure of spiraling of the aorticopulmonary septum

## Explanation

The “egg-on-a-string” appearance is classic for transposition of the great arteries. This condition results from failure of the aorticopulmonary septum to spiral during embryologic cardiac development.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Egg-on-a-string + Severe neonatal cyanosis + Parallel circulation = TGA

## Wrong Options

### B) Abnormal neural crest migration causing persistent truncus arteriosus

Persistent truncus arteriosus results from failed septation of the truncus arteriosus and presents with a single arterial trunk.

**Buzzword Combo:** Single arterial trunk + Cyanosis + Neural crest defect = Persistent truncus arteriosus

### C) Defective endocardial cushion formation

Endocardial cushion defects produce atrioventricular septal defects and are strongly associated with Down syndrome.

**Buzzword Combo:** Down syndrome + AV septal defect + Common AV valve = Endocardial cushion defect

### D) Leftward displacement of the infundibular septum

This defect causes Tetralogy of Fallot, which classically presents with boot-shaped heart and cyanotic spells.

**Buzzword Combo:** Boot-shaped heart + RV outflow obstruction + Cyanotic spells = Tetralogy of Fallot
