## Buzzword Question

A 24-year-old woman with severe intermittent abdominal pain, anxiety, confusion, dark urine during attacks, and elevated urinary porphobilinogen most likely has which condition?

## Buzzwords Used

intermittent abdominal pain; anxiety; confusion; dark urine; elevated porphobilinogen; no photosensitivity

## Answer Options

A) Acute intermittent porphyria
B) Porphyria cutanea tarda
C) Hemochromatosis
D) Lead poisoning

## Correct Answer

A. Acute intermittent porphyria

## Explanation

Acute intermittent porphyria is caused by porphobilinogen deaminase deficiency, leading to accumulation of porphyrin precursors such as ALA and PBG. It presents with neurovisceral symptoms including abdominal pain, psychiatric manifestations, dark urine, and no photosensitivity.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Abdominal pain + psychiatric symptoms + elevated PBG + no photosensitivity = AIP

## Wrong Options

### B. Porphyria cutanea tarda

PCT presents with photosensitivity, blistering skin lesions, and hypertrichosis due to uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase deficiency. Neurovisceral symptoms are absent.

**Buzzword Combo:** Photosensitivity + blistering skin lesions + hypertrichosis = PCT

### C. Hemochromatosis

Hemochromatosis is an iron overload disorder causing bronze skin pigmentation, cirrhosis, diabetes, and elevated ferritin, not elevated porphobilinogen or neurovisceral attacks.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bronze diabetes + cirrhosis + elevated ferritin = Hemochromatosis

### D. Lead poisoning

Lead poisoning causes abdominal pain and neuropathy with elevated ALA but normal PBG. Peripheral smear typically shows basophilic stippling.

**Buzzword Combo:** Basophilic stippling + wrist drop + elevated ALA = Lead poisoning
