## Buzzword Question

A 4-year-old girl presents with 5 days of fever, bilateral non-purulent conjunctivitis, polymorphous truncal rash, unilateral cervical lymphadenopathy, strawberry tongue with cracked lips, and palmar/plantar erythema with edema. These features fulfill the diagnostic criteria for which of the following?

## Buzzwords Used

Fever ≥5 days; bilateral non-purulent conjunctivitis; polymorphous rash; cervical adenopathy; strawberry tongue; palmar/plantar changes; CRASH-and-Burn; Kawasaki disease

## Answer Options


A) Kawasaki disease
B) Scarlet fever
C) Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome
D) Measles

## Correct Answer

A) Kawasaki disease

## Explanation

Kawasaki disease is a clinical diagnosis based on fever ≥5 days plus at least 4 of the 5 CRASH features: Conjunctivitis (bilateral, non-purulent), Rash (polymorphous), Adenopathy (cervical, usually unilateral), Strawberry tongue with cracked lips, and Hand/foot changes — with "Burn" representing the high persistent fever.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Fever ≥5 days + CRASH features (conjunctivitis, rash, adenopathy, strawberry tongue, hand/foot changes) = Kawasaki disease

## Wrong Options

### B) Scarlet fever


Caused by group A strep with a sandpaper rash, strawberry tongue, and circumoral pallor — but no conjunctivitis, no extremity changes, and no coronary artery involvement.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sandpaper rash + strawberry tongue + circumoral pallor + GAS pharyngitis = scarlet fever

### C) Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome

S. aureus exfoliative toxin causes diffuse desquamation with positive Nikolsky sign — without conjunctivitis, strawberry tongue, or cervical adenopathy.

**Buzzword Combo:** 
Diffuse exfoliation + positive Nikolsky + exfoliative toxin = SSSS

### D) Measles

Measles presents with Koplik spots, cephalocaudal maculopapular rash, the 3 C's (cough, coryza, conjunctivitis) — but lacks strawberry tongue and extremity changes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Koplik spots + cephalocaudal rash + 3 C's = measles (rubeola)
