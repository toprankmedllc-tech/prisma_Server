## Buzzword Question

A 45-year-old man with type 2 diabetes is started on empagliflozin. Two weeks later, his fasting plasma glucose is 210 mg/dL and repeat urine dipstick shows 3+ glucose. His physician notes that prior to starting empagliflozin, at the same plasma glucose of 210 mg/dL, his urine dipstick showed only trace glucose. Which of the following best accounts for the increased glucosuria on empagliflozin compared to before treatment at the same plasma glucose level?

## Buzzwords Used

Type 2 DM; empagliflozin; same plasma glucose; more glucosuria than before; SGLT2 inhibitor

## Answer Options

A) Empagliflozin increases GFR, raising the filtered glucose load
B) Empagliflozin blocks SGLT2, lowering the renal glucose threshold below 210 mg/dL
C) Empagliflozin saturates SGLT1 in the PCT, eliminating secondary glucose reabsorption
D) Empagliflozin causes splay phenomenon by creating heterogeneity among SGLT2 transporters

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

Empagliflozin inhibits SGLT2, which is responsible for ~90% of renal glucose reabsorption. By blocking SGLT2, the renal glucose threshold is pharmacologically lowered to below 210 mg/dL, so glucose that was previously reabsorbed is now excreted — even though plasma glucose is unchanged. This is a transporter-level shift in threshold, not a GFR or filtration change.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Empagliflozin + SGLT2 block + same plasma glucose + more glucosuria = lowered renal threshold

## Wrong Options

### A) Empagliflozin increases GFR

SGLT2 inhibitors actually have a modest effect of slightly reducing intraglomerular pressure via tubuloglomerular feedback, but this is not the mechanism of glucosuria. More importantly, increased GFR would increase filtered load but not necessarily overcome reabsorption unless the threshold is also changed.

**Buzzword Combo:** SGLT2i + ↑GFR = glucosuria (incorrect primary mechanism)

### C) SGLT1 saturation

This is a wrong option choice as SGLT1 handles ~10% of glucose reabsorption in the S3 segment and is NOT blocked by empagliflozin. SGLT1 remains functional and continues reabsorbing residual glucose. The primary mechanism is SGLT2 blockade in S1/S2, not SGLT1 saturation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Empagliflozin + SGLT1 blocked = incorrect (empagliflozin is SGLT2-selective)

### D) Splay phenomenon from SGLT2 heterogeneity

Splay is an intrinsic anatomical phenomenon due to natural nephron-to-nephron variability in Tₘ — it exists in all individuals regardless of drug therapy. Empagliflozin does not create new heterogeneity among transporters; it uniformly inhibits SGLT2 across nephrons, shifting the entire threshold curve leftward.

**Buzzword Combo:** Empagliflozin + splay = incorrect (splay is pre-existing, not drug-induced)
