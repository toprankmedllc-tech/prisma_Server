## Buzzword Question

Patient reports chest pain, dysphagia to solids and liquids, and nighttime coughing episodes due to regurgitation. Imaging reveals a markedly dilated esophagus with tapering near the gastroesophageal junction. The underlying pathology most likely involves degeneration of neurons in the Myenteric Plexus leading to deficiency of which substances?

## Buzzwords Used

chest pain, dysphagia, regurgitation, degeneration of the myenteric plexus 

## Answer Options

A. Acetylcholine and Substance P
B. Nitric oxide and vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)
C. Histamine and gastrin
D. Serotonin and dopamine

## Correct Answer

B. Nitric oxide and vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)

## Explanation

The primary pathology is degeneration of the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus, especially the inhibitory neurons responsible for relaxing the lower esophageal sphincter (LES).

Normally:

Nitric oxide (NO) → relaxes smooth muscle
VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide) → relaxes smooth muscle
Acetylcholine (ACh) and Substance P → promote contraction

So in achalasia there is loss of inhibitory signaling:

Achalasia: ↓NO+↓VIP⇒failure of LES relaxation

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Dysphagia + chest pain + degeneration of the myeneteric plexus = Achalasia 

## Wrong Options

### A. Acetylcholine and Substance P

Acetylcholine (ACh) and Substance P → promote contraction whereas Achalasia is degeneration of inhibitory neurons of the myenteric plexus 

**Buzzword Combo:** ACH + Substance P = contraction of the LES 

### C. Histamine and gastrin

Gastrin and histamine work in a sequential, cooperative manner to regulate gastric acid secretion, with gastrin acting as the primary initiator that triggers histamine release. Gastrin is released from G cells, stimulating ECL cells to release histamine, which then acts on parietal cells (via H2 receptors) to produce acid.

**Buzzword Combo:** Histamine + Gastrin = Increase acidity

### D. Serotonin and dopamine

Dopamine and serotonin are essential neurotransmitters regulating mood, sleep, and behavior. Dopamine drives motivation, reward, and pleasure ("wanting"), while serotonin regulates mood stability, happiness, and contentment ("liking"). Imbalances in these chemicals are linked to depression, anxiety, and sleep disorders.

**Buzzword Combo:** Serotonin + dopamine = Regulation of mood and sleep
