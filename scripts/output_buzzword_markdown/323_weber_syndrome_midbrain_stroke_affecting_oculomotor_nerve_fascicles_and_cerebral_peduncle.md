## Buzzword Question

A 64-year-old man suddenly develops drooping of the right eyelid and double vision. On examination, the right eye is positioned “down and out” with a dilated pupil. He also has left-sided spastic hemiparesis involving the arm and leg. MRI reveals an infarct in the right midbrain.

## Buzzwords Used

Down and out eye ; ptosis ; blown pupil ; contralateral hemiparesis ; midbrain infarct

## Answer Options

A) Facial motor nucleus
B) Oculomotor nerve fascicles
C) Vestibular nuclei
D) Nucleus ambiguus

## Correct Answer

B) Oculomotor nerve fascicles

## Explanation

Weber syndrome is caused by a midbrain infarction involving the oculomotor nerve fascicles and cerebral peduncle. CN III involvement causes ptosis, mydriasis, and a “down and out” eye, while corticospinal tract involvement causes contralateral hemiparesis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Down and out eye + blown pupil + contralateral hemiparesis = Oculomotor nerve fascicles

## Wrong Options

### A) Facial motor nucleus

The facial motor nucleus is located in the pons and lesions cause ipsilateral facial paralysis, not oculomotor deficits.

**Buzzword Combo:** Bell palsy + hyperacusis + loss of forehead movement = Facial motor nucleus

### C) Vestibular nuclei

Vestibular nuclei lesions cause vertigo, nystagmus, nausea, and vomiting rather than eye deviation with pupillary dilation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vertigo + vomiting + nystagmus = Vestibular nuclei

### D) Nucleus ambiguus

The nucleus ambiguus controls swallowing and phonation through CN IX and X. Lesions cause dysphagia and hoarseness.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dysphagia + hoarseness + absent gag reflex = Nucleus ambiguus
