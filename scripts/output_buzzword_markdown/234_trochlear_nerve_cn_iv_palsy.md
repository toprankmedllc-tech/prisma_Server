## Buzzword Question

A 34-year-old man presents after head trauma with vertical diplopia that worsens when walking downstairs. He reports that tilting his head away from the affected side improves his symptoms. Physical examination reveals impaired depression of the adducted eye. Which cranial nerve is most likely injured?

## Buzzwords Used

Head trauma; vertical diplopia; difficulty walking downstairs; head tilt; impaired depression of adducted eye

## Answer Options

A) Oculomotor nerve (CN III)
B) Trochlear nerve (CN IV)
C) Abducens nerve (CN VI)
D) Optic nerve (CN II)

## Correct Answer

B) Trochlear nerve (CN IV)

## Explanation

The trochlear nerve innervates the superior oblique muscle, which depresses the adducted eye. CN IV palsy causes vertical diplopia that worsens when looking down, especially while descending stairs.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Vertical diplopia + difficulty walking downstairs + head tilt = CN IV palsy

## Wrong Options

### A) Oculomotor nerve (CN III)

CN III palsy causes a down-and-out eye with ptosis and pupillary dilation. These findings are absent here.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ptosis + blown pupil + down-and-out eye = CN III lesion

### C) Abducens nerve (CN VI)

CN VI palsy causes inability to abduct the eye and horizontal diplopia, not vertical diplopia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Medial eye deviation + impaired abduction + horizontal diplopia = CN VI lesion

### D) Optic nerve (CN II)

CN II lesions cause visual loss or visual field defects rather than impaired extraocular movement.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vision loss + afferent pupillary defect + visual field cut = CN II lesion
