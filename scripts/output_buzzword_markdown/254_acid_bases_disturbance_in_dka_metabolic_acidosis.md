## Buzzword Question

A 22-year-old man with type 1 diabetes is brought to the ED with nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain. He has not taken insulin for 2 days. ABG: pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 26 mmHg, HCO₃ 11 mEq/L. Urine dipstick is positive for ketones. What is the primary acid-base disturbance?

## Buzzwords Used

Type 1 diabetes; Insulin noncompliance; Nausea; Vomiting; Abdominal pain; pH 7.25; PaCO₂ 26; HCO₃ 11; Ketones; Metabolic acidosis

## Answer Options

A) Respiratory alkalosis with metabolic compensation
B) Metabolic acidosis with appropriate respiratory compensation
C) Metabolic acidosis with superimposed respiratory acidosis
D) Mixed metabolic and respiratory alkalosis

## Correct Answer

B

## Explanation

pH is acidemic and HCO₃ is low — confirming primary metabolic acidosis. The low PaCO₂ of 26 represents Kussmaul respirations (compensatory hyperventilation). At Step 1 level, the key recognition is: low pH + low HCO₃ = metabolic acidosis; low PaCO₂ = appropriate compensation blowing off CO₂.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

DKA + insulin noncompliance + ketones + low HCO₃ + low PaCO₂ = Metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation (Kussmaul breathing)

## Wrong Options

### A) Respiratory alkalosis with metabolic compensation

This is the premature closure trap — the low PaCO₂ catches the student’s eye first, leading them to label it as primary respiratory alkalosis. However, the pH is 7.25 (acidemic, not alkalemic), so the primary process cannot be alkalosis. Always determine the pH direction before assigning the primary disorder.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low PaCO₂ seen first + alkalosis association = Premature label of respiratory alkalosis before checking pH direction

### C) Metabolic acidosis with superimposed respiratory acidosis

A superimposed respiratory acidosis would show an elevated PaCO₂, not a low one. The PaCO₂ of 26 is low, indicating hyperventilation — the appropriate compensatory response — not a second acidotic process.

**Buzzword Combo:** Two acidoses suspected + acidemic pH = Incorrect addition of respiratory acidosis when PaCO₂ is actually low

### D) Mixed metabolic and respiratory alkalosis

The pH is 7.25 — clearly acidemic. An alkalosis of any kind is impossible when the pH is this low. This answer reflects failure to anchor on the pH as the first step in ABG interpretation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mixed” terminology + multiple abnormal values = Alkalosis incorrectly assigned despite unambiguously acidemic pH
