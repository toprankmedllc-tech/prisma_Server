## Buzzword Question

A 30-year-old man develops sudden onset dyspnea, chest pain, and dizziness immediately after rapidly ascending to the surface while scuba diving. He becomes confused and hypotensive within minutes. On examination, a “mill wheel” murmur is heard. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

scuba diving; rapid ascent; sudden dyspnea; chest pain; dizziness; hypotension; confusion; mill wheel murmur

## Answer Options

A) Air embolism
B) Decompression sickness
C) Fat embolism
D) Pulmonary embolism

## Correct Answer

A) Air embolism

## Explanation

Rapid ascent during scuba diving can force air bubbles into the venous circulation, leading to obstruction of right ventricular outflow and acute cardiovascular collapse. A classic clue is the “mill wheel” murmur.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

rapid ascent + sudden dyspnea + hypotension + confusion + mill wheel murmur

## Wrong Options

### B) Decompression sickness

Caused by nitrogen bubble formation in tissues leading to joint pain and neurologic symptoms, not acute right heart obstruction or mill wheel murmur.

**Buzzword Combo:** rapid ascent + joint pain + neurologic deficits + nitrogen bubbles

### C) Fat embolism

Occurs after long bone fractures with petechiae, hypoxemia, and neurologic symptoms, not diving-related acute collapse.

**Buzzword Combo:** long bone fracture + petechiae + ARDS + delayed respiratory distress

### D) Pulmonary embolism

PE causes sudden dyspnea and pleuritic chest pain but is due to DVT embolization, not air entry or diving-related rapid ascent.

**Buzzword Combo:** post-op immobilization + pleuritic chest pain + hypoxia + clear lungs
