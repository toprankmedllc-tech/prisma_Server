## Buzzword Question

A child with recurrent epistaxis, easy bruising, prolonged bleeding from minor cuts since infancy, thrombocytopenia, normal PT and aPTT, impaired platelet adhesion, and defective vWF binding most likely has deficiency of which platelet receptor?

## Buzzwords Used

epistaxis; easy bruising; thrombocytopenia; normal PT; normal aPTT; impaired platelet adhesion; defective vWF binding

## Answer Options

A. GPIIb/IIIa receptor deficiency
B. GPIb receptor deficiency
C. Factor VIII deficiency
D. ADAMTS13 deficiency

## Correct Answer

B. GPIb receptor deficiency

## Explanation

Bernard–Soulier syndrome is caused by deficiency of the GPIb-IX-V receptor complex, which is responsible for platelet binding to von Willebrand factor. This leads to defective platelet adhesion and mucocutaneous bleeding with giant platelets.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

GPIb deficiency + vWF binding defect + giant platelets = Bernard–Soulier syndrome

## Wrong Options

### A. GPIIb/IIIa receptor deficiency

GPIIb/IIIa deficiency causes Glanzmann thrombasthenia, characterized by defective platelet aggregation with normal adhesion and normal ristocetin response.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal ristocetin + absent aggregation to ADP = Glanzmann thrombasthenia

### C. Factor VIII deficiency

Factor VIII deficiency causes hemophilia A with prolonged aPTT and deep tissue bleeding such as hemarthrosis, not platelet adhesion defects.

**Buzzword Combo:** Prolonged aPTT + hemarthrosis + X-linked inheritance = Hemophilia A

### D. ADAMTS13 deficiency

ADAMTS13 deficiency causes TTP due to large vWF multimers leading to microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and organ ischemia, not isolated platelet receptor dysfunction.

**Buzzword Combo:** Schistocytes + thrombocytopenia + neurologic symptoms = TTP
