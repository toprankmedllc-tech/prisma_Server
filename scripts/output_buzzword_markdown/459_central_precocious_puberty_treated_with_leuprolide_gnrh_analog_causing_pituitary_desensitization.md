## Buzzword Question

A 7-year-old girl is brought to the pediatric clinic by her parents due to early breast development and the onset of pubic hair over the past 6 months. She has had a recent growth spurt and is taller than most of her classmates.

On examination, she has Tanner stage III breast development and pubic hair. Bone age is advanced compared to her chronological age. MRI brain is normal.

She is started on a medication that initially increases LH and FSH secretion but, with continuous use, suppresses gonadotropin release and halts further pubertal progression.

Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of action of this drug?

## Buzzwords Used

precocious puberty ; early breast development ; pubic hair ; advanced bone age ; Tanner stage III ; initial LH surge ; later suppression of gonadotropins ; GnRH analog ; growth spurt

## Answer Options

A) Competitive GnRH receptor blockade at pituitary
B) Continuous stimulation of GnRH receptors leading to downregulation
C) Estrogen receptor blockade in hypothalamus
D) Aromatase inhibition in peripheral tissues

## Correct Answer

B) Continuous stimulation of GnRH receptors leading to downregulation

## Explanation

Leuprolide is a GnRH analog used in central precocious puberty. Continuous stimulation of GnRH receptors causes receptor downregulation in the anterior pituitary, leading to decreased LH and FSH and suppression of puberty progression.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Precocious puberty + initial LH surge + later suppression = GnRH agonist (leuprolide) downregulation

## Wrong Options

### A) Competitive GnRH receptor blockade at pituitary

This describes Degarelix, which causes immediate suppression of LH/FSH without an initial hormone flare.

**Buzzword Combo:** Immediate LH suppression + no flare = GnRH antagonist

### C) Estrogen receptor blockade in hypothalamus

This describes Clomiphene, which is used to induce ovulation, not suppress puberty.

**Buzzword Combo:** Infertility + ovulation induction + estrogen feedback removal = clomiphene

### D) Aromatase inhibition in peripheral tissues

This describes Letrozole, which decreases estrogen synthesis but does not act via pituitary GnRH receptor downregulation.

**Buzzword Combo:** Breast cancer + low estrogen + no LH flare = aromatase inhibitor
