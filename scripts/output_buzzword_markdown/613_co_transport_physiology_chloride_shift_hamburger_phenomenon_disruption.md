## Buzzword Question

A patient has impaired chloride exchange in RBCs. Which acid-base disturbance is most directly affected?

## Buzzwords Used

RBC chloride exchange; chloride shift; Hamburger phenomenon; CO₂ transport; bicarbonate buffering; carbonic anhydrase; acid-base balance

## Answer Options

A) Metabolic acidosis compensation
B) Respiratory acidosis compensation
C) Metabolic alkalosis development
D) Respiratory alkalosis development

## Correct Answer

A) Metabolic acidosis compensation

## Explanation

The chloride shift allows bicarbonate generated in RBCs to exit into plasma in exchange for chloride, enabling effective CO₂ transport and buffering. Impairment disrupts bicarbonate handling, directly affecting the body’s ability to compensate for metabolic acid loads.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

RBC chloride shift failure + impaired HCO₃⁻ transport + buffering defect → impaired metabolic acidosis compensation

## Wrong Options

### B) Respiratory acidosis compensation

Respiratory acidosis is primarily compensated by renal bicarbonate retention, not the chloride shift mechanism in RBCs.

**Buzzword Combo:** CO₂ retention + renal compensation + unrelated RBC transport pathway

### C) Metabolic alkalosis development

Metabolic alkalosis is not directly caused by impaired chloride shift; it is usually due to acid loss (vomiting, diuretics), not RBC transport defects.

**Buzzword Combo:** Alkali gain + H⁺ loss + unrelated chloride transport mechanism

### D) Respiratory alkalosis development

Respiratory alkalosis results from hyperventilation and excessive CO₂ loss, which is unrelated to intracellular chloride-bicarbonate exchange.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyperventilation + CO₂ washout + ventilatory control mechanism
