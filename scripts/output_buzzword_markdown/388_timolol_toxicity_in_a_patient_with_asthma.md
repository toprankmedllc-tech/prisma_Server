## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man with chronic open-angle glaucoma is started on topical therapy to reduce intraocular pressure. Two weeks later, he develops worsening wheezing, shortness of breath, and decreased exercise tolerance. His medical history is significant for asthma and hypertension. Which medication most likely caused this patient’s respiratory symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

Open-angle glaucoma ; topical eye drops ; asthma ; wheezing ; nonselective beta blocker ; decreased aqueous humor synthesis

## Answer Options

A) Latanoprost
B) Pilocarpine
C) Timolol
D) Acetazolamide

## Correct Answer

C) Timolol

## Explanation

Timolol is a nonselective beta-blocker used to decrease aqueous humor production in glaucoma. Systemic absorption can cause bronchoconstriction and worsen asthma symptoms.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Glaucoma eye drops + asthma exacerbation + nonselective beta blocker = Timolol

## Wrong Options

### A) Latanoprost

Latanoprost increases uveoscleral outflow and commonly causes iris darkening and eyelash growth, not bronchospasm.

**Buzzword Combo:** Iris browning + eyelash growth + prostaglandin analog = Latanoprost

### B) Pilocarpine

Pilocarpine increases trabecular outflow and causes miosis and cyclospasm rather than beta-blocker–induced bronchoconstriction

**Buzzword Combo:** Miosis + acute angle closure treatment + trabecular meshwork opening = Pilocarpine

### D) Acetazolamide

Acetazolamide decreases aqueous humor synthesis and may cause metabolic acidosis or paresthesias, not bronchospasm.

**Buzzword Combo:** Carbonic anhydrase inhibition + decreased aqueous humor + paresthesias = Acetazolamide
