## Buzzword Question

A patient with a long-standing uncorrected congenital heart defect develops Eisenmenger syndrome. Which of the following best describes the underlying mechanism?

## Buzzwords Used


Long-standing uncorrected congenital heart defect; Eisenmenger syndrome; pulmonary vascular remodeling; increased pulmonary vascular resistance; shunt reversal

## Answer Options

A) Pulmonary vascular remodeling with shunt reversal
B) Acute massive pulmonary embolism
C) Progressive left ventricular failure
D) Vasculitic destruction of pulmonary vessels

## Correct Answer

A) Pulmonary vascular remodeling with shunt reversal

## Explanation

Chronic left-to-right shunting causes pulmonary overcirculation, triggering intimal proliferation and smooth muscle hypertrophy of pulmonary arterioles. The resulting rise in pulmonary vascular resistance drives right ventricular hypertrophy, and once right-sided pressures exceed left-sided pressures, the shunt reverses direction to right-to-left.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)


Chronic L→R shunt + pulmonary vascular remodeling + ↑ PVR + RVH + shunt reversal = Eisenmenger syndrome

## Wrong Options

### B) Acute massive pulmonary embolism

Produces acute right ventricular dilation and strain, not the chronic remodeling of Eisenmenger.

**Buzzword Combo:** Acute hypoxia + RV dilation + McConnell sign + S1Q3T3 = massive pulmonary embolism

### C) Progressive left ventricular failure

Causes pulmonary venous hypertension (group 2 PH) without reversing intracardiac shunts.

**Buzzword Combo:** LV failure + ↑ LV end-diastolic pressure + pulmonary venous HTN = group 2 pulmonary HTN

### D) Vasculitic destruction of pulmonary vessels

Vasculitis destroys vessels but does not produce the high-flow-driven hypertrophy and intimal proliferation of Eisenmenger.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sinopulmonary + renal + c-ANCA + saddle nose = granulomatosis with polyangiitis
