## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old man with a long-standing history of hypertension and Type 2 diabetes mellitus presents for a routine follow-up. His current medications include metformin and a thiazide diuretic. Laboratory studies show a serum creatinine of 1.4 mg/dL and a serum potassium of 3.3 mEq/L. Urinalysis reveals microalbuminuria. To optimize blood pressure control, provide renal protection, and address his hypokalemia, he is switched from his thiazide diuretic to an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor. Which of the following statements best describes the mechanism by which this new medication provides renal protection in diabetic nephropathy?

## Buzzwords Used

Type 2 diabetes; Microalbuminuria; ACE inhibitor; Renal protection; Diabetic nephropathy

## Answer Options

A) Dilates the afferent arteriole, increasing the glomerular filtration rate (GFR)
B) Constricts the efferent arteriole, increasing the filtration fraction (FF)
C) Dilates the efferent arteriole, decreasing intraglomerular pressure
D) Inhibits NaCl reabsorption in the early distal convoluted tubule

## Correct Answer

C) Dilates the efferent arteriole, decreasing intraglomerular pressure

## Explanation

ACE inhibitors prevent the synthesis of Angiotensin II, which preferentially constricts the efferent arteriole. By blocking this constriction, ACE inhibitors cause efferent arteriole dilation, which lowers intraglomerular pressure and reduces protein excretion (microalbuminuria), slowing the progression of diabetic nephropathy and renal remodeling.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Diabetes + Microalbuminuria + ACE inhibitor = Loss of Angiotensin II vasoconstriction → Dilates the efferent arteriole, decreasing intraglomerular pressure.

## Wrong Options

### A) Dilates the afferent arteriole, increasing the glomerular filtration rate (GFR)

Dilating the afferent arteriole would increase intraglomerular pressure and hyperfiltration, which worsens protein crashing across the glomerular basement membrane and accelerates diabetic kidney disease.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diabetes + Microalbuminuria + ACE inhibitor = Misidentifying the site of arteriolar action as afferent → Dilates the afferent arteriole, increasing GFR.

### B) Constricts the efferent arteriole, increasing the filtration fraction (FF)

This describes the physiological action of endogenous Angiotensin II during states of low blood volume to maintain GFR; an ACE inhibitor explicitly prevents this effect.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diabetes + Microalbuminuria + ACE inhibitor = Confusing the drug's mechanism with the action of the hormone it inhibits → Constricts the efferent arteriole, increasing the filtration fraction.

### D) Inhibits NaCl reabsorption in the early distal convoluted tubule

This is the mechanism of action of thiazide diuretics, which the patient was taking previously, not the mechanism of ACE inhibitors.

**Buzzword Combo:** Diabetes + Microalbuminuria + ACE inhibitor = Confusing the mechanism of the old discontinued drug with the new one → Inhibits NaCl reabsorption in the early distal convoluted tubule.
