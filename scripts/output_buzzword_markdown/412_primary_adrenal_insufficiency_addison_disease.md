## Buzzword Question

A patient with fatigue, hypotension, hyperpigmentation, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia is diagnosed with adrenal insufficiency caused by autoimmune destruction of the adrenal cortex. What is the diagnosis?


## Buzzwords Used

Hyperpigmentation ; hypotension ; hyponatremia ; hyperkalemia ; autoimmune destruction 


## Answer Options

 A) Primary adrenal insufficiency
 B) Secondary adrenal insufficiency
 C) SIADH
 D) Hyperaldosteronism


## Correct Answer

A) Primary adrenal insufficiency


## Explanation

Primary adrenal insufficiency results from destruction of the adrenal cortex, causing cortisol and aldosterone deficiency. Elevated ACTH causes characteristic hyperpigmentation.


## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

 Hyperpigmentation + hyperkalemia + hypotension = Primary adrenal insufficiency

## Wrong Options

###  B) Secondary adrenal insufficiency


Secondary adrenal insufficiency does not cause hyperpigmentation or significant hyperkalemia because aldosterone production is preserved.


**Buzzword Combo:**  Low ACTH + steroid withdrawal + no hyperpigmentation = Secondary adrenal insufficiency


### C) SIADH


SIADH causes euvolemic hyponatremia without hyperpigmentation or hyperkalemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Hyponatremia + concentrated urine + euvolemia = SIADH


###  D) Hyperaldosteronism


Hyperaldosteronism causes hypertension and hypokalemia due to excess aldosterone activity.


**Buzzword Combo:** Hypertension + hypokalemia + metabolic alkalosis = Hyperaldosteronism
