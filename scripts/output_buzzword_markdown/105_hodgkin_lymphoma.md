## Buzzword Question

A 26-year-old man with painless cervical lymphadenopathy and intermittent fever has lymph node biopsy showing large binucleated "owl-eye" cells. This finding is most characteristic of which cell type?

## Buzzwords Used

painless lymphadenopathy; owl-eye cells; Reed-Sternberg cells; intermittent fever; Hodgkin lymphoma

## Answer Options

A) Reed-Sternberg cells
B) Smudge cells
C) Auer rods
D) Rouleaux formation

## Correct Answer

A. Reed-Sternberg cells

## Explanation

Reed-Sternberg cells are large binucleated B cells with prominent eosinophilic nucleoli that create an owl-eye appearance. They are characteristic of Hodgkin lymphoma and are typically CD15+ and CD30+.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Owl-eye cells + painless lymphadenopathy = Hodgkin lymphoma

## Wrong Options

### B. Smudge cells

Smudge cells are fragile mature lymphocytes commonly seen in chronic lymphocytic leukemia, especially in elderly patients.

**Buzzword Combo:** Smudge cells + elderly + lymphocytosis = CLL

### C. Auer rods

Auer rods are needle-shaped azurophilic granules found in myeloblasts in acute myeloid leukemia, especially acute promyelocytic leukemia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Auer rods + myeloblasts + DIC = APL

### D. Rouleaux formation

Rouleaux formation refers to stacked RBCs due to elevated serum proteins, commonly seen in multiple myeloma.

**Buzzword Combo:** Rouleaux + M spike + lytic lesions = Multiple myeloma
