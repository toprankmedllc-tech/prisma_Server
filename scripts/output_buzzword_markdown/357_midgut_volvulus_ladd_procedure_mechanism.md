## Buzzword Question

A neonate with midgut volvulus undergoes a Ladd procedure. The bowel is left in a non-rotated position. Which mechanism best explains prevention of recurrence?

## Buzzwords Used

Ladd procedure; non-rotation; midgut volvulus; mesenteric base; Ladd bands

## Answer Options

A) Normal 270° counterclockwise rotation around SMA is restored
B) Mesenteric base is broadened after division of Ladd bands
C) Ischemic bowel is routinely resected
D) Ladd bands are used to fix bowel in correct anatomical position

## Correct Answer

B) Mesenteric base is broadened after division of Ladd bands

## Explanation

The Ladd procedure prevents recurrence by dividing Ladd bands and broadening the mesenteric base, reducing the risk of bowel twisting around the SMA. It does not restore normal embryologic rotation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Broad mesenteric base = prevents recurrent volvulus

## Wrong Options

### A) Normal 270° counterclockwise rotation around SMA is restored

The procedure does not restore normal 270° embryologic rotation; instead, it intentionally leaves the bowel in a non-rotated configuration to reduce volvulus risk.

**Buzzword Combo:** Restored rotation ≠ Ladd procedure outcome

### C) Ischemic bowel is routinely resected

Bowel resection may be performed if ischemia is present but is not the mechanism for preventing recurrence of volvulus.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ischemia → resection = complication management, not prevention

### D) Ladd bands are used to fix bowel in correct anatomical position

Ladd bands are fibrous peritoneal bands causing obstruction and are divided during surgery; they are not used for fixation of bowel.

**Buzzword Combo:** Ladd bands = divided, not structural support
