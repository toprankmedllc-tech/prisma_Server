## Buzzword Question

A 68-year-old man is hospitalized for several weeks after a severe hip fracture. During rehabilitation, he notices marked thinning and weakness of the muscles in his immobilized leg. A muscle biopsy demonstrates increased degradation of contractile proteins within skeletal muscle fibers through a pathway involving protein tagging and intracellular proteolysis.
A medical student asks which cellular mechanism is primarily responsible for this patient’s muscle atrophy.
Which of the following is the most likely mechanism underlying this patient’s muscle loss?

## Buzzwords Used

increased degradation of contractile proteins within skeletal muscle fibers through a pathway involving protein tagging and intracellular proteolysis.

## Answer Options

A. Addition of sarcomeres in parallel
B. Increased calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum
C. Removal of muscle proteins through the ubiquitin-proteasome system
D. Increased number of skeletal muscle fibers through hyperplasia

## Correct Answer

C. Removal of muscle proteins through the ubiquitin-proteasome system

## Explanation

Muscle atrophy occurs when protein degradation exceeds protein synthesis. In disuse atrophy, skeletal muscle proteins are tagged with ubiquitin and degraded by proteasomes, leading to loss of myofibrils and decreased muscle size.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Disuse atrophy + protein degradation + ubiquitin tagging = muscle atrophy


## Wrong Options

### A.Addition of sarcomeres in parallel

This occurs in skeletal muscle hypertrophy during resistance training, not in muscle wasting.

**Buzzword Combo:** Resistance training + muscle hypertrophy + increased fiber diameter = hypertrophy

### B.Increased calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum

Calcium release is necessary for muscle contraction but does not explain progressive muscle loss during immobilization.

**Buzzword Combo:** Excitation-contraction coupling + calcium release + muscle contraction = sarcoplasmic reticulum function

### D. Increased number of skeletal muscle fibers through hyperplasia

Adult skeletal muscle growth occurs mainly through hypertrophy rather than significant hyperplasia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Formation of new muscle fibers + minimal role in adult humans = hyperplasia
