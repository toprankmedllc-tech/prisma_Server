## Buzzword Question

A 30-year-old man presents after a high-speed motor vehicle accident with worsening shortness of breath and hypoxemia developing over a few hours. Chest imaging shows patchy, non-segmental opacities in one lung field without pneumothorax or mediastinal shift. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Blunt chest trauma; Motor vehicle accident; Delayed hypoxemia; Worsening dyspnea; Patchy opacities; Non-segmental infiltrates; No pneumothorax; No mediastinal shift

## Answer Options

A) Pulmonary contusion
B) Tension pneumothorax
C) Flail chest
D) Pulmonary embolism

## Correct Answer

A) Pulmonary contusion

## Explanation

Pulmonary contusion is lung parenchymal injury following blunt chest trauma, leading to alveolar hemorrhage and edema. It classically presents with delayed hypoxemia and patchy, non-segmental infiltrates on imaging without pleural air or mediastinal shift.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Blunt trauma + delayed hypoxemia + patchy infiltrates + no pneumothorax = Pulmonary contusion

## Wrong Options

### B) Tension pneumothorax

Tension pneumothorax would show absent breath sounds, hyperresonance, and mediastinal shift rather than patchy infiltrates.

**Buzzword Combo:** Trauma + hypotension + tracheal deviation + hyperresonance = Tension pneumothorax

### C) Flail chest

Flail chest presents with paradoxical chest wall movement due to multiple rib fractures, often associated with underlying contusion but not defined by imaging infiltrates alone.

**Buzzword Combo:** Multiple rib fractures + paradoxical movement + respiratory distress = Flail chest

### D) Pulmonary embolism

PE presents with sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, and risk factors like immobility, not patchy post-traumatic lung infiltrates.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sudden dyspnea + risk factors + clear chest imaging early = Pulmonary embolism
