## Buzzword Question

29-year-old man presents to the clinic with numbness and burning discomfort in the upper anterior thigh and scrotal region several weeks after undergoing inguinal hernia repair surgery. He reports increased sensitivity in the groin area but denies weakness of the lower extremities or difficulty walking. Physical examination reveals decreased sensation over the superior medial thigh and anterior scrotum. Motor strength and deep tendon reflexes are normal.
Injury to which of the following nerves is the most likely cause of this patient’s symptoms?

## Buzzwords Used

decreased sensation over the superior medial thigh and anterior scrotum.

## Answer Options

A. Femoral nerve
B. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve
C. Genitofemoral nerve
D. Obturator nerve

## Correct Answer

C. Genitofemoral nerve

## Explanation

This patient most likely has injury to the genitofemoral nerve, which can occur during procedures involving the inguinal region such as hernia repair surgery. Patients may develop: Groin pain or numbness ,Loss of sensation over the anterior scrotum , Sensory changes over the superior anterior thigh 

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Post-hernia repair + anterior scrotal numbness + upper anterior thigh sensory loss = genitofemoral nerve injury

## Wrong Options

### A. Femoral nerve

Incorrect — Femoral nerve injury causes weakness of knee extension and decreased patellar reflex.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak quadriceps + reduced patellar reflex = femoral neuropathy

### B. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve

Incorrect — Compression of this nerve causes meralgia paresthetica with sensory symptoms over the anterolateral thigh only.

**Buzzword Combo:** Obesity + tight belt + lateral thigh burning pain = meralgia paresthetica

### D. Obturator nerve

Incorrect — Obturator nerve injury causes weakness with thigh adduction and sensory loss over the medial thigh.

**Buzzword Combo:** Weak thigh adduction + medial thigh numbness = obturator nerve injury
