## Buzzword Question

A patient with chronic metabolic acidosis is found to have an inability to acidify the urine below pH 5.5 despite systemic acidemia. Which of the following additional findings is most likely present?

## Buzzwords Used

Metabolic acidosis; Urine pH >5.5

## Answer Options

A) Generalized aminoaciduria
B) Recurrent nephrolithiasis
C) Hyperkalemia due to decreased aldosterone
D) Elevated urine specific gravity

## Correct Answer

B) Recurrent nephrolithiasis

## Explanation

Distal (type 1) renal tubular acidosis results from impaired hydrogen ion secretion in the distal nephron, preventing normal urine acidification. Patients commonly develop calcium phosphate kidney stones and nephrocalcinosis due to persistently alkaline urine.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Hypokalemia + alkaline urine + nephrolithiasis = Type 1 RTA

## Wrong Options

### Generalized aminoaciduria

Generalized aminoaciduria is characteristic of Fanconi syndrome due to proximal tubular dysfunction. It is commonly associated with proximal (type 2) renal tubular acidosis rather than distal RTA

**Buzzword Combo:** Fanconi syndrome + aminoaciduria + proximal tubular defect = Type 2 RTA

### Hyperkalemia due to decreased aldosterone

Hyperkalemia is characteristic of type 4 renal tubular acidosis caused by hypoaldosteronism or aldosterone resistance. These patients typically have impaired potassium and hydrogen ion secretion

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypoaldosteronism + hyperkalemia = Type 4 RTA

### Elevated urine specific gravity

Elevated urine specific gravity reflects concentrated urine and intact renal concentrating ability rather than a specific type of renal tubular acidosis. It is not a defining feature of distal RTA

**Buzzword Combo:** Concentrated urine + increased urine osmolality = Water conservation
