## Buzzword Question

A 27-year-old man with schizophrenia is seen in follow-up. His auditory hallucinations and paranoid delusions have resolved on antipsychotic therapy, but he remains socially isolated, rarely speaks, shows little emotional expression, and has difficulty initiating tasks. His roommate states he spends most of the day lying in bed and has stopped pursuing hobbies. Which of the following symptom clusters best characterizes his current presentation?

## Buzzwords Used

Hallucinations resolved; paranoid delusions resolved; social isolation; poverty of speech; flat affect; avolition; anhedonia; difficulty initiating


## Answer Options

A) Negative symptoms (avolition, alogia, affective flattening, anhedonia)
 B) Positive symptoms (auditory hallucinations, paranoid delusions, disorganized speech) 
C) Cognitive symptoms (impaired working memory, executive dysfunction) 
D) Mood symptoms (dysphoria, psychomotor retardation, suicidal ideation)

## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Negative symptoms in schizophrenia represent a diminution or absence of normal functions and include the “5 As”: Avolition (inability to initiate/persist in goal-directed activity), Alogia (poverty of speech), Affective flattening (reduced emotional expression), Anhedonia (inability to experience pleasure), and Asociality (social withdrawal). These symptoms often persist after positive symptoms remit with antipsychotic treatment and are the primary driver of long-term functional disability. This patient displays all five features with complete resolution of positive symptoms.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Flat affect + alogia + avolition + anhedonia + asociality + resolved hallucinations/delusions = Negative symptoms of schizophrenia

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Positive symptoms (auditory hallucinations, paranoid delusions, disorganized speech)

1 Explanation: Positive symptoms represent an excess or distortion of normal functions and include hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech, and grossly disorganized behavior. These symptoms have explicitly resolved with antipsychotic therapy in this patient. His current presentation — flat affect, poverty of speech, avolition — reflects negative symptoms, not positive ones.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wrong 1: Hallucinations + delusions + disorganized speech/behavior = Positive symptoms of schizophrenia

### 2: C) Cognitive symptoms (impaired working memory, executive dysfunction)

2 Explanation: Cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia include deficits in working memory, processing speed, executive function, and attention. While common and functionally significant, they are not the primary characterization of the symptom cluster described (flat affect, alogia, avolition, anhedonia, social isolation). The vignette emphasizes motivational and affective deficits consistent with the negative symptom domain.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wrong 2: Impaired working memory + poor executive function + processing speed deficits = Cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia

### 3: D) Mood symptoms (dysphoria, psychomotor retardation, suicidal ideation)


Mood symptoms such as dysphoria, guilt, and suicidal ideation point toward a depressive episode. While negative symptoms and depression share surface similarities (e.g., psychomotor slowing, low energy), negative symptoms are specifically tied to schizophrenia’s core psychopathology. No depressive cognitions or suicidal ideation are described; the features are consistent with the schizophrenia negative symptom cluster.

**Buzzword Combo:** Wrong 3: Dysphoria + guilt + suicidal ideation + neurovegetative features = Major depressive episode (not schizophrenia negative symptoms)
