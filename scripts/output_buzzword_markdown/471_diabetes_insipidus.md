## Buzzword Question

A patient develops polyuria, polydipsia, hypernatremia, and persistently dilute urine despite elevated antidiuretic hormone levels. Which of the following medications is most strongly associated with this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Polyuria; Dilute urine; ADH resistance

## Answer Options

A) Furosemide
B) Spironolactone
C) Desmopressin
D) Lithium

## Correct Answer

D) Lithium

## Explanation

Lithium can damage collecting duct cells and impair responsiveness to antidiuretic hormone, leading to nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Patients develop polyuria, polydipsia, hypernatremia, and inability to concentrate urine

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Lithium use + ADH resistance + dilute urine = Nephrogenic DI

## Wrong Options

### Furosemide

Furosemide inhibits the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the thick ascending limb and is commonly associated with hypokalemia and metabolic alkalosis.

**Buzzword Combo:** Loop diuretic + hypokalemia + metabolic alkalosis = Furosemide

### Spironolactone

Spironolactone antagonizes aldosterone receptors and may cause hyperkalemia and gynecomastia.

**Buzzword Combo:** Aldosterone blockade + hyperkalemia + gynecomastia = Spironolactone

### Desmopressin

Desmopressin is used to treat central diabetes insipidus by replacing deficient antidiuretic hormone activity.

**Buzzword Combo:** Synthetic ADH + central DI treatment = Desmopressin
