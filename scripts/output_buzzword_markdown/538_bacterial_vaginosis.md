## Buzzword Question

A 26-year-old woman presents with thin gray-white vaginal discharge and a strong fishy odor that worsens after sexual intercourse. Vaginal microscopy demonstrates epithelial cells coated with bacteria. Vaginal pH is 5.5. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Buzzwords Used

Thin gray-white discharge; Fishy odor; Positive whiff test; Clue cells; Vaginal pH >4.5; No inflammation

## Answer Options

A) Candida vulvovaginitis
B) Trichomonas vaginitis
C) Bacterial vaginosis
D) Gonococcal cervicitis

## Correct Answer

C) Bacterial vaginosis

## Explanation

Bacterial vaginosis is caused by replacement of normal lactobacilli with anaerobic bacteria such as Gardnerella vaginalis. Patients present with thin gray-white discharge, fishy odor, clue cells, and elevated vaginal pH without significant inflammation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Clue cells + Fishy odor + pH >4.5 = Bacterial vaginosis

## Wrong Options

### A) Candida vulvovaginitis

Candida infection causes intense itching with thick white “cottage cheese” discharge and normal vaginal pH. Pseudohyphae are seen on KOH prep rather than clue cells.

**Buzzword Combo:** Pseudohyphae + Thick white discharge + Normal pH = Candida vulvovaginitis

### B) Trichomonas vaginitis

Trichomoniasis presents with frothy yellow-green discharge, strawberry cervix, and motile trichomonads on wet mount. Significant inflammation is usually present.

**Buzzword Combo:** Strawberry cervix + Motile trichomonads + Frothy discharge = Trichomonas vaginitis

### D) Gonococcal cervicitis

Gonococcal cervicitis usually presents with mucopurulent cervical discharge and pelvic pain rather than clue cells or fishy odor. Gram-negative intracellular diplococci are characteristic.

**Buzzword Combo:** Mucopurulent cervicitis + Intracellular diplococci + Pelvic pain = Gonococcal cervicitis
