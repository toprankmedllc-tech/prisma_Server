## Buzzword Question

A 38-year-old accountant with recently diagnosed rheumatoid arthritis tells her primary care physician that she has taken a leave of absence from work, is excused from her household responsibilities by her spouse, expects her physician to 'fix her completely,' and believes she has no obligation to adhere to her prescribed methotrexate regimen because 'that's the doctor's job, not mine.' According to Talcott Parsons' sociological model of illness, which of the following rights and obligations is this patient incorrectly applying?

## Buzzwords Used

Leave of absence from work; excused from responsibilities; expects physician to fix her; refuses medication adherence; Parsons' sick role; rights and obligations

## Answer Options

A) The patient is correctly exempt from normal role obligations but incorrectly believes she has no obligation to cooperate with treatment and seek competent medical care
B) The patient is correctly exempt from work obligations but incorrectly retains the right to be considered morally responsible for her illness
C) The patient incorrectly claims exemption from work because Parsons' model only applies to acute, not chronic, illness
D) The patient is exercising all four components of the sick role correctly; her refusal of medication is protected under patient autonomy rights


## Correct Answer

A

## Explanation

Talcott Parsons (1951) described the sick role as a sociological construct with two rights and two obligations. The two rights are: (1) exemption from normal social role responsibilities (work, household duties), proportional to the severity of illness; and (2) not being held morally responsible for the illness (sickness is involuntary). The two obligations are: (1) the sick person must want to get well and work toward recovery, illness is not a desirable permanent state; and (2) the sick person must seek technically competent medical help and comply with treatment. This patient correctly exercises her rights: she takes leave (right 1) and is not blamed for having RA (right 2). However, she violates both obligations: she refuses to see medication adherence as her responsibility (obligation 2 violated) and implicitly suggests her recovery is entirely the physician's domain (obligation 1 undermined). The answer identifies the specific violation: exemption is correctly applied, but the obligation to cooperate with treatment is not.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Exempt from normal duties (RIGHT) + not morally blamed (RIGHT) + must seek to recover (OBLIGATION violated) + must comply with treatment (OBLIGATION violated) = Sick Role, Parsons

## Wrong Options

### 1: B) Correctly exempt from work but incorrectly retains moral responsibility

Parsons' sick role explicitly exempts the ill person from moral responsibility for their illness, being sick is considered an involuntary state. This patient is NOT incorrectly claiming moral responsibility; she never does so. The answer conflates the rights with the obligations and misidentifies which component is violated.

**Buzzword Combo:** Sick person blamed for illness + moral culpability assigned = Violation of sick role right 2 (moral exemption)

### 2: C) Exemption only applies to acute illness

Parsons' original model was critiqued for applying most cleanly to acute illness, but the model itself does not restrict sick role rights to acute conditions. In practice, chronic illness patients may exercise modified versions of sick role rights. The work exemption for RA (a disabling autoimmune condition) is clinically and sociologically appropriate and is not the error being identified.

**Buzzword Combo:** Chronic illness + sick role exemption disputed = Critique of Parsons' model (not a patient violation)

### 3: D) Patient is exercising all four components correctly

This is clearly incorrect. Patient autonomy does not include the right to refuse one's own treatment obligations under the sick role. While patients may refuse treatment legally, Parsons' sociological model specifically identifies cooperation with treatment as an obligation of the sick role. The patient who demands the rights without fulfilling the obligations is misapplying the model.

**Buzzword Combo:** Rights exercised + obligations refused + patient autonomy invoked = Misapplication of sick role obligations
