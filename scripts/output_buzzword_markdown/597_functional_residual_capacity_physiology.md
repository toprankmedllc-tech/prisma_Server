## Buzzword Question

A 35-year-old healthy patient undergoes pulmonary physiology testing. When moved from standing to supine position under anesthesia, oxygenation worsens due to reduced lung volumes at rest. What change occurs in FRC in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

Supine position; anesthesia; healthy patient; decreased oxygenation; reduced lung volumes; diaphragm displacement; physiology of FRC

## Answer Options

A) Increased due to diaphragm descent
B) Decreased due to reduced chest wall expansion
C) Unchanged due to stable compliance
D) Increased due to increased perfusion

## Correct Answer

B) Decreased due to reduced chest wall expansion

## Explanation

Functional residual capacity (FRC) decreases in the supine position because the abdominal contents push the diaphragm upward, reducing lung expansion. Anesthesia further decreases chest wall tone, worsening lung volume reduction and contributing to lower oxygenation.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Supine position; diaphragm elevation; ↓ chest wall expansion; ↓ FRC

## Wrong Options

### A) Increased due to diaphragm descent

In the supine position, the diaphragm moves upward due to abdominal pressure, not downward, leading to reduced lung volumes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Standing position; diaphragm descent; increased lung volume

### C) Unchanged due to stable compliance

FRC is highly position-dependent and decreases significantly in supine position even in healthy individuals, so it is not unchanged.

**Buzzword Combo:** Normal compliance; static lung volume; no positional effect

### D) Increased due to increased perfusion

Although perfusion may increase in supine position, it does not increase lung volume; instead, mechanical restriction leads to decreased FRC.

**Buzzword Combo:** Perfusion changes; gas exchange; not lung volume physiology
