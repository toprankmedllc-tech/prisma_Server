## Buzzword Question

A 19-year-old with known HOCM presents with palpitations and exertional chest pain. His physician plans to initiate medical therapy to reduce symptoms and LVOT obstruction. Which of the following is the most appropriate first-line pharmacologic agent?

A) Nitroglycerin
B) Metoprolol 
C) Digoxin 
D) Hydralazine

## Buzzwords Used

HOCM

## Answer Options

A) Nitroglycerin
B) Metoprolol 
C) Digoxin 
D) Hydralazine

## Correct Answer

B) Metoprolol 

## Explanation

Beta-blockers (e.g., metoprolol) are first-line medical treatment in HOCM. They reduce heart rate (allowing more complete LV filling, increasing LV cavity size), decrease myocardial contractility, and decrease the dynamic obstruction. Calcium channel blockers (verapamil) are second-line.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

HOCM + symptoms + First-line medical treatment = Beta-blocker (decreased LVOT obstruction) 

## Wrong Options

### A) Nitroglycerin

Nitroglycerin is a venodilator that reduces preload, which would worsen LVOT obstruction in HOCM and is contraindicated. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Exertional Chest Pain + ST Depression = Sublingual Nitroglycerin (Venodilation) Decreased LVEDV/LV Wall Stress

### C) Digoxin 

Digoxin is a positive inotrope that would increase myocardial contractility and worsen dynamic LVOT obstruction; it is contraindicated in HOCM. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Symptomatic HFrEF (systolic HF) + Digoxin = Increased Contractility (Positive Inotropy)

### D) Hydralazine

Hydralazine is an arterial vasodilator that reduces afterload, causing reflex tachycardia and decreasing LV cavity size, both of which worsen LVOT obstruction in HOCM. 

**Buzzword Combo:** Hypertension + Pregnancy = Hydralazine (safe blood pressure reduction in pregnancy)
