## Buzzword Question

A 62-year-old man with chronic kidney disease has fatigue. Laboratory studies show low hemoglobin, low serum iron, normal ferritin, and low reticulocyte count. Which of the following best explains the iron findings in this patient?

## Buzzwords Used

62-year-old man; chronic kidney disease; fatigue; low hemoglobin; low serum iron; normal ferritin; low reticulocyte count

## Answer Options

A) Iron is trapped in macrophages and storage sites

B) Iron is lost from chronic gastrointestinal bleeding

C) Iron cannot be incorporated into heme

D) Iron absorption is increased in the duodenum


## Correct Answer

A) Iron is trapped in macrophages and storage sites


## Explanation

Anemia of chronic disease causes low serum iron because hepcidin decreases ferroportin activity. This traps iron inside macrophages and storage sites, so ferritin is normal or increased. The body has iron, but the bone marrow cannot access it efficiently for erythropoiesis.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Chronic kidney disease + low iron + normal ferritin + low reticulocytes = anemia of chronic disease

## Wrong Options

### B. Iron is lost from chronic gastrointestinal bleeding


This describes iron deficiency anemia, which causes low iron, high TIBC, and low ferritin.

**Buzzword Combo:** Low iron + high TIBC + low ferritin = iron deficiency anemia

### C. Iron cannot be incorporated into heme


This describes sideroblastic anemia, in which iron is available but cannot be properly used to make heme.

**Buzzword Combo:** Iron overload + ring sideroblasts + microcytic anemia = sideroblastic anemia

### D. Iron absorption is increased in the duodenum


This is the opposite of anemia of chronic disease. Hepcidin decreases intestinal iron absorption by inhibiting ferroportin.

**Buzzword Combo:** High hepcidin + decreased ferroportin + decreased iron absorption = anemia of chronic disease
