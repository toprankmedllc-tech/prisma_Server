## Buzzword Question

A premature newborn develops severe respiratory distress shortly after birth. Chest X-ray shows ground-glass opacities. Histology reveals collapsed alveoli with absent surfactant and poorly developed lamellar bodies in alveolar epithelial cells. Which cell type is dysfunctional in this condition?

## Buzzwords Used

Premature newborn; respiratory distress; ground-glass opacities; collapsed alveoli; absent surfactant; lamellar bodies; alveolar epithelial cells; neonatal RDS

## Answer Options

A) Type I pneumocytes
B) Type II pneumocytes
C) Alveolar macrophages
D) Endothelial cells

## Correct Answer

B) Type II pneumocytes

## Explanation

Type II pneumocytes produce pulmonary surfactant stored in lamellar bodies, which prevents alveolar collapse by reducing surface tension. In premature infants, deficiency leads to neonatal respiratory distress syndrome with atelectasis and ground-glass lungs.

## Buzzword Combination (Correct)

Premature newborn + absent surfactant + lamellar bodies ↓ = Type II pneumocyte dysfunction

## Wrong Options

### A) Type I pneumocytes

Type I pneumocytes are responsible for gas exchange but do not produce surfactant. They are primarily affected in ARDS rather than neonatal surfactant deficiency.

**Buzzword Combo:** Gas exchange epithelium + hyaline membranes + ARDS = Type I pneumocyte injury

### C) Alveolar macrophages

Alveolar macrophages are immune scavenger cells involved in particle clearance, not surfactant production. Dysfunction here does not cause neonatal alveolar collapse.

**Buzzword Combo:** Dust cells + phagocytosis + inflammation = Alveolar macrophages

### D) Endothelial cells

Pulmonary endothelial cells maintain vascular integrity but do not synthesize surfactant. Injury is more relevant in ARDS or vascular leak syndromes.

**Buzzword Combo:** Vascular injury + edema + capillary leak = Endothelial cell dysfunction
