## Chief Complaint

6 months of progressive intermittent cyanosis and exercise intolerance

## Vignette

A 25-year-old man is referred to a cardiologist for evaluation of intermittent cyanosis and exercise intolerance that began approximately 6 months ago and have progressively worsened. His past medical history is notable for a congenital heart defect diagnosed shortly after birth; he was subsequently lost to follow-up and was never treated. He smokes half a pack of cigarettes per day. On physical examination, cyanosis of the lips and clubbing of the fingers are noted. Cardiac catheterization demonstrates the following oxygen saturations: superior vena cava 70%; inferior vena cava 70%; right atrium 70%; right ventricle 80%; pulmonary artery 85%; left atrium 97%; left ventricle 85%; aorta 85%.Which of the following is the most likely explanation of these findings?

## Key Symptoms

Intermittent cyanosis; progressive exercise intolerance; longstanding untreated congenital heart defect

## Labs

Cardiac catheterization oxygen saturations: SVC 70%; IVC 70%; right atrium 70%; right ventricle 80%; pulmonary artery 85%; left atrium 97%; left ventricle 85%; aorta 85%

## Physical Exam

 Cyanosis of the lips; clubbing of the fingers

## Main Clue

Young adult with untreated congenital heart defect + new cyanosis and clubbing + step-up in O₂ saturation between RA (70%) and RV (80%) indicating a residual left-to-right shunt at the ventricular level + desaturation of the left ventricle (85%) and aorta (85%) indicating reversed right-to-left shunting

## Supporting Clue

The mixed picture — both a residual L→R step-up in the right ventricle and desaturation of left-sided chambers — is characteristic of bidirectional shunting in evolving Eisenmenger syndrome from a long-standing uncorrected VSD

## Question

Which of the following is the most likely explanation of these findings?

## Correct Answer

C) Reversal of long-standing left-to-right shunt
