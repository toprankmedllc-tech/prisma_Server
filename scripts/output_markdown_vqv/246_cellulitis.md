## Chief Complaint

Swelling and redness of rt leg

## Vignette

A 72-year-old man presents to the emergency department with progressive redness and pain of his right lower leg for 3 days. He reports chills and increasing difficulty ambulating. His medical history is significant for poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity (BMI 36), chronic venous insufficiency, and chronic tinea pedis between the toes. Medications include metformin, lisinopril, and furosemide.
He states that 5 days ago he noticed “cracked skin” between his toes after prolonged walking.
Temperature is 38.5°C (101.3°F), blood pressure 132/78 mmHg, pulse 104/min, respirations 18/min.
Physical examination demonstrates an erythematous, warm, tender plaque extending from the dorsum of the right foot to the mid-calf.

The lesion is poorly demarcated and mildly edematous. No fluctuance is appreciated. No crepitus is present. Interdigital scaling and fissuring are noted between the 4th and 5th toes.

Labs showed : WBCs : 16000, CRP :150 
Blood cultures are pending.

Which of the following best explains the most likely pathogenesis of this patient’s condition?
