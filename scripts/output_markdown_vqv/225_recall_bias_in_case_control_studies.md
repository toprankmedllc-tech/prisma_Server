## Vignette

An epidemiologist at a children's hospital designs a case-control study to investigate the association between prenatal acetaminophen use and the development of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children. Cases are 250 children aged 6–12 years with newly diagnosed ADHD confirmed by a child psychiatrist using DSM-5 criteria. Controls are 250 children aged 6–12 years without ADHD, matched for age, sex, and socioeconomic status, recruited from the same pediatric practices.  Mothers of both groups are contacted by telephone and asked to recall their use of acetaminophen during each trimester of pregnancy. The telephone interview is conducted by research assistants who are aware of each child's case/control status. Mothers of children with ADHD are significantly more likely to recall using acetaminophen regularly during the second trimester compared to mothers of controls (OR = 2.4, 95% CI: 1.6–3.5).  Before publishing, the research team convenes to critically appraise their methodology. One team member argues that the central limitation of this study is not the choice of controls or the statistical analysis, but rather a specific systematic error arising from the fundamental nature of retrospective exposure assessment in the context of a known outcome.  Which of the following most accurately identifies the primary systematic error in this study's design?

## Key Symptoms

Retrospective maternal recall of prenatal medication use; cases (ADHD) mothers vs control mothers; mothers aware of their child's diagnosis; interviewers aware of case status; differential recall by disease status

## Main Clue

Mothers of ADHD children (cases) are motivated to search their memory for potential explanations and may systematically over-recall acetaminophen use vs mothers of healthy controls → differential recall = recall bias


## Supporting Clue

 Interviewers knowing case status adds potential for interviewer bias (differential probing); retrospective design inherently susceptible; case-control studies are specifically prone to recall bias because exposure is assessed AFTER outcome is known
