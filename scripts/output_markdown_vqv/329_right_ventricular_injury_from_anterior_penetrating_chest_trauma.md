## Chief Complaint

Single stab wound to anterior chest; hypotension; muffled heart sounds

## Vignette

A 24-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after a single stab wound to the anterior chest just left of the midsternal line at the fourth intercostal space. On arrival, he is agitated with blood pressure 76/52 mmHg, pulse 124/min, and oxygen saturation 91% on room air. Neck veins are visibly distended and heart sounds are muffled. Emergency bedside pericardiocentesis yields 50 mL of frank blood and blood pressure improves to 106/68 mmHg. He is taken urgently to the operating room. Through a median sternotomy, the surgeon visualizes the pericardium, opens it, and immediately encounters active hemorrhage from the anterior cardiac surface.
From which of the following cardiac chambers is the bleeding most likely originating?

A) Left atrium 
B) Right atrium 
C) Coronary sinus 
D) Left ventricle 
E) Right ventricle
