## Vignette

A 61-year-old woman with a 3-cm solitary pulmonary nodule found incidentally on chest CT is scheduled for a CT-guided percutaneous transthoracic needle biopsy. Her oncologist meets with her in clinic 5 days before the procedure for the informed consent discussion. During the 25-minute encounter, the oncologist explains: the nature of the procedure (CT-guided needle insertion through the chest wall under imaging guidance); the purpose (tissue diagnosis to determine benign vs malignant); the material risks (pneumothorax 15–25%, hemoptysis 5–10%, bleeding, infection, small risk of air embolism); the expected benefits (definitive histological diagnosis to guide treatment planning); and the alternatives available (surgical VATS wedge resection more invasive, higher yield; PET/CT  non-invasive but metabolic not histological; watchful waiting with serial CT appropriate only for low-risk nodules); and the option to decline entirely.  The patient asks several clarifying questions about the pneumothorax risk and recovery expectations, then correctly restates the procedure, its risks, and alternatives back to the physician in her own words, and states she understands and agrees to proceed. The physician documents the consent discussion in the medical record and the patient signs the written consent form.  One week later, on the morning of the scheduled biopsy, the patient calls the procedure suite and states she has changed her mind and no longer wishes to proceed with the biopsy at this time.  Which of the following is NOT a required element of valid informed consent, as evaluated at the time of the original consent discussion in the clinic?

## Key Symptoms

Disclosure: procedure, purpose, risks (pneumothorax, hemoptysis, bleeding), benefits, alternatives, option to decline; Understanding: patient restates information correctly (teach-back); Capacity: patient reasons, asks questions, communicates stable decision; Voluntariness: autonomous decision without coercion; Patient withdrawal: exercises right to decline before procedure

## Main Clue

Four required elements of valid informed consent: (1) Disclosure, (2) Understanding, (3) Decision-making capacity, (4) Voluntariness. Guarantee of outcome is NEVER required,  medicine cannot guarantee procedural results.

## Supporting Clue

Consent is a PROCESS (discussion, understanding, decision) not just a signature; patients may withdraw consent at any time before procedure, this right is an expression of autonomy and does not invalidate the original consent process; written consent form is documentation, not consent itself
