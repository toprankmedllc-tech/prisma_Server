## Chief Complaint

Sudden onset of severe, tearing chest pain radiating between the shoulder blades

## Vignette

A 64-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after the sudden onset of severe, tearing chest pain that radiates between his shoulder blades. His past medical history is notable for poorly controlled hypertension for over 20 years, hyperlipidemia, and a 30 pack-year smoking history. On examination, his blood pressure is 198/112 mm Hg in the right arm and 154/88 mm Hg in the left arm; pulse is 104/min. He is diaphoretic and anxious. Cardiac auscultation reveals an early diastolic murmur at the right upper sternal border. Chest radiograph shows a widened mediastinum, and CT angiography of the chest demonstrates an intimal flap involving the ascending aorta.
Which of the following is the most significant risk factor in this patient's history for his condition?

## Physical Exam

 Diaphoresis; anxious appearance; significant inter-arm blood pressure differential (>20 mm Hg); early diastolic murmur at the right upper sternal border (aortic regurgitation from proximal extension)

## Main Clue

Longstanding uncontrolled hypertension + sudden tearing interscapular chest pain + inter-arm BP differential + widened mediastinum + intimal flap on CT angiography

## Supporting Clue

Aortic regurgitation murmur and ascending aortic involvement indicate a Stanford Type A dissection — most strongly associated with chronic hypertension
