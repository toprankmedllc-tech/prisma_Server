## Chief Complaint

Palpitations and weight loss

## Vignette

A 31-year-old woman comes to the clinic because of 3 months of palpitations, heat intolerance, anxiety, and unintentional weight loss despite increased appetite. She delivered her first child approximately 11 months ago and says her symptoms began several months after stopping breastfeeding. She denies neck pain, recent upper respiratory illness, or visual disturbances.

Physical examination shows a pulse of 110/min, warm moist skin, and a fine tremor. Thyroid examination reveals a single 2-cm nodule in the left thyroid lobe without diffuse enlargement. No ophthalmopathy is present.

Laboratory studies:

TSH: ↓
Free T4: ↑

Radioactive iodine uptake scan demonstrates a focal area of increased uptake in the left thyroid lobe with suppression of the remaining thyroid tissue.

Which of the following is the most likely explanation for this patient’s manifestations?

A. Autoantibody-mediated diffuse activation of TSH receptors throughout the thyroid gland

B. Transient lymphocytic destruction of thyroid follicles occurring after pregnancy

C. Activating mutation causing constitutive stimulation of the TSH receptor–cAMP pathway in thyroid follicular cells

D. Granulomatous inflammation with multinucleated giant cells causing release of preformed thyroid hormone

E. Autonomous hormone secretion from multiple hyperplastic thyroid nodules throughout the gland

F. T-cell–mediated destruction of thyroid epithelium with Hurthle cell formation

G. Excess thyroid stimulation by hCG due to trophoblastic tissue proliferation
