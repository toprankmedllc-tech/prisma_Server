## Vignette

A 28-year-old woman comes to the fertility clinic because she has been unable to conceive for the past 18 months despite regular unprotected intercourse. She reports menstrual periods occurring every 2–4 months since adolescence. The patient also notes progressive facial hair growth and acne over the past several years. She has no history of pelvic inflammatory disease or prior pregnancies.

Physical examination shows central obesity and coarse hair on the chin and upper lip. Laboratory studies demonstrate elevated serum testosterone and an increased LH-to-FSH ratio. Pelvic ultrasound reveals bilaterally enlarged ovaries containing multiple small peripheral cysts.

The patient is started on a medication that acts as an estrogen receptor antagonist in the hypothalamus, thereby preventing estrogen-mediated negative feedback and increasing release of pituitary gonadotropins.

Two months later, she develops hot flashes and intermittent visual disturbances but successfully ovulates.

Which of the following drugs was most likely prescribed?
