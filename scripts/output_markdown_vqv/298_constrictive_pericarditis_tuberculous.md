## Chief Complaint

6 months of progressive abdominal distension, bilateral lower extremity swelling, and exertional dyspnea

## Vignette

A 44-year-old man who immigrated to the United States from rural India 3 years ago presents to the emergency department with 6 months of progressive abdominal distension, bilateral lower extremity swelling, and exertional dyspnea. He has lost 5 kg over the same interval and reports early satiety. He was treated several years ago for a chronic cough and night sweats but stopped his medications partway through the course. On examination, his blood pressure is 108/72 mm Hg, pulse is 92/min, and respirations are 18/min. Jugular venous pressure is markedly elevated and rises further with inspiration. There is hepatomegaly with mild ascites and bilateral pitting lower extremity edema. Lung auscultation is clear. Cardiac auscultation reveals an early diastolic sound following S2 at the left sternal border.
Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Physical Exam

Markedly elevated jugular venous pressure that rises further with inspiration (Kussmaul sign); hepatomegaly with mild ascites; bilateral pitting lower extremity edema; clear lungs; early diastolic sound following S2 at the left sternal border (pericardial knock)

## Main Clue


Immigrant from a TB-endemic region with incompletely treated chronic cough and night sweats + chronic progressive right-sided heart failure picture + Kussmaul sign + early diastolic pericardial knock

## Supporting Clue


Clear lungs in the setting of marked right-sided congestion (JVD, hepatomegaly, ascites, edema) indicates impaired ventricular filling from a constrictive process rather than left-sided pulmonary congestion
