## Chief Complaint

Severe abdominal pain

## Vignette

A 7-year-old boy is brought to the emergency department because of severe abdominal pain that began this morning. The pain is mostly in the right lower quadrant and has been associated with nausea but no vomiting or diarrhea. He recently immigrated from Libya. His mother says he has had several episodes of painful swelling of the fingers over the past year, usually after playing outside in hot weather. Physical examination shows mild scleral icterus and diffuse abdominal tenderness without rebound tenderness. Laboratory studies show a hemoglobin concentration of 7.9 g/dL, increased reticulocyte count, increased indirect bilirubin, and increased LDH. A peripheral blood smear shows crescent-shaped erythrocytes.

## Key Symptoms

Right lower quadrant abdominal pain; nausea; painful finger swelling

## Labs

Hemoglobin 7.9 g/dL; increased reticulocyte count; increased indirect bilirubin; increased LDH; crescent-shaped erythrocytes on peripheral smear

## Physical Exam

Mild scleral icterus; diffuse abdominal tenderness without rebound tenderness

## Main Clue

Crescent-shaped erythrocytes with episodic abdominal pain and hemolytic anemia

## Supporting Clue

History of painful finger swelling after hot-weather activity suggests dactylitis and dehydration-triggered vaso-occlusive crises.
