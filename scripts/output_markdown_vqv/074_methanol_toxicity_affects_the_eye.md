## Chief Complaint

Unresponsiveness and visual disturbance

## Vignette

A 45-year-old homeless man is brought to the emergency department after being found unresponsive in an alley. Bystanders report seeing him drinking from an unlabeled bottle earlier that afternoon. On arrival he is confused and agitated, complaining that his vision has become blurry and that “everything looks white.” He has no known medical history. His temperature is 36.8°C, blood pressure is 100/60 mmHg, heart rate is 110 bpm, and pulse oximetry is 94% on room air. He is breathing deeply and laboriously. Arterial blood gas reveals pH 7.18, PaCO₂ 24 mmHg, and HCO₃⁻ 10 mEq/L. Serum anion gap is 24. Serum osmolality is 320 mOsm/kg against a calculated osmolality of 295 mOsm/kg. Formate level is elevated. Pupils are dilated bilaterally and visual acuity is markedly reduced in both eyes. Which of the following toxic metabolites is most responsible for the visual symptoms in this patient?

## Key Symptoms

Confusion; visual disturbance (“blurry vision, everything looks white”); high anion gap metabolic acidosis; elevated osmolar gap

## Labs

pH 7.18; HCO₃⁻ 10 mEq/L; PaCO₂ 24 mmHg; Anion gap 24; Serum osmolality 320; Calculated osmolality 295 (osmolar gap = 25); Formate level elevated

## Physical Exam

Dilated pupils; decreased visual acuity bilaterally; Kussmaul respirations

## Main Clue

Visual disturbance + high anion gap metabolic acidosis + elevated osmolar gap = methanol toxicity; formate (toxic metabolite) causes optic nerve damage

## Supporting Clue

Kussmaul respirations represent renal compensation — the kidney retains HCO₃⁻ while the lungs blow off CO₂; ADH (vasopressin) acting on the collecting duct regulates water balance during this acidotic state
