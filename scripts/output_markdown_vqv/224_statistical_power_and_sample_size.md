## Vignette

A nephrology fellow is designing a randomized controlled trial to evaluate whether a new ACE inhibitor formulation reduces the rate of decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) compared to the current standard ACE inhibitor in patients with stage 3 chronic kidney disease and proteinuria. Based on pilot data, the investigators anticipate a clinically meaningful difference of 3 mL/min/1.73m² per year between the two groups, with a standard deviation of 8 mL/min/1.73m² in the rate of eGFR decline.  The fellow's mentor initially suggests enrolling only 25 patients per group due to budget constraints. The fellow conducts a formal power calculation and determines that with 25 patients per group, the study would achieve only 35% statistical power to detect the anticipated difference at a two-sided alpha of 0.05. She argues that this is insufficient and proposes increasing the sample size.  At the end of the study, if it were conducted with only 25 patients per group and the result came back as p = 0.18 (non-significant), the fellow is concerned that this represents a failure of study design rather than a true null result  that the study may have failed to detect a real difference.  Which of the following changes to the study design would most directly and substantially increase the statistical power to detect the anticipated true difference in eGFR decline?

## Key Symptoms

Power = 35% (inadequate; standard ≥80%); small sample; anticipated true difference missed; p = 0.18 non-significant; suspected Type II error; power = 1 − beta; increases with larger n, larger effect size, lower variability

## Main Clue

Power = 1  beta; most direct way to increase power is to increase sample size; power also increased by increasing effect size, decreasing variability, or increasing alpha

## Supporting Clue

n=25 gives 35% power far below conventional 80%; non-significant result with low-powered study likely represents Type II error; increasing n to 225/group would achieve 80% power for this scenario
