## Vignette

The state immunization division is evaluating its measles vaccination program following a cluster of 23 measles cases in a community of 12,000 children and adults associated with a school that has a documented vaccination rate of 78%. A pediatric infectious disease specialist is asked to present to the state health board on the theoretical requirements for herd immunity in measles and why 78% vaccination coverage is insufficient.  The specialist explains that measles, caused by a paramyxovirus transmitted via respiratory droplets and airborne particles, has one of the highest basic reproduction numbers (R₀) of any known infectious disease. The R₀ for measles in a fully susceptible population is approximately 15, meaning that in the absence of any immunity, a single infected individual would on average directly infect 15 others. She emphasizes that achieving herd immunity in which the proportion of immune individuals is sufficient to interrupt sustained community transmission even without vaccinating every person  requires a mathematically defined minimum immune fraction of the population.  The specialist also notes that the 2-dose MMR vaccine provides approximately 97% effectiveness against measles infection and that even with highly efficacious vaccines, the theoretical herd immunity threshold must account for vaccine failure rates when calculating the required vaccination coverage.  What proportion of the population must be immune (through vaccination or prior infection) to achieve herd immunity for measles, and why is 78% vaccination coverage insufficient?

## Key Symptoms

R₀ = 15; HIT = 1 − (1/R₀) = 1 − 1/15 = 1 − 0.067 = 0.933 ≈ 93%; 78% < 93% → insufficient for herd immunity; higher R₀ = more transmissible = higher HIT required

## Main Clue

Herd immunity threshold (HIT) = 1 − (1/R₀) = 1 − (1/15) ≈ 93%; 78% < 93% → sustained transmission possible; highest-R₀ diseases require near-universal immunity

## Supporting Clue

: Measles R₀ ≈ 12-18 (one of highest known); COVID-19 R₀ ≈ 2-3 → HIT ≈ 50-67%; poliovirus R₀ ≈ 5-7 → HIT ≈ 80-86%; required vaccination coverage = HIT / vaccine effectiveness
