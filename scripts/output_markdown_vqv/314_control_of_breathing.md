## Chief Complaint

Somnolence and respiratory depression

## Vignette

A 29-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department after an accidental opioid overdose. She is somnolent, has shallow breathing, and requires stimulation to remain awake. Her respiratory rate is 6 breaths/min, blood pressure is 110/70 mmHg, and oxygen saturation is 84% on room air. Arterial blood gas shows: pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 68 mmHg, PaO₂ 58 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 26 mEq/L. She is given naloxone and her respiratory drive improves rapidly. Which of the following best describes the primary site responsible for sensing the increased CO₂ levels that normally stimulate ventilation in this patient?

## Key Symptoms

Somnolence; shallow breathing; decreased respiratory drive; opioid intoxication; hypoventilation

## Labs

ABG: pH 7.25; PaCO₂ 68 mmHg; PaO₂ 58 mmHg; HCO₃⁻ 26 mEq/L

## Physical Exam

Somnolent; shallow respirations; reduced responsiveness

## Main Clue

Opioid overdose causing hypoventilation → CO₂ retention → central chemoreceptor-driven ventilatory control

## Supporting Clue

Rapid improvement after naloxone (reversal of medullary respiratory depression)

## Question

Which of the following best describes the primary site responsible for sensing the increased CO₂ levels that normally stimulate ventilation?

## Correct Answer

C) Central chemoreceptors in the medulla sensing increased H⁺ in CSF
