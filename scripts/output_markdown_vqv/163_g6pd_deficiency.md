## Chief Complaint

Yellowing of the eyes and dark urine

## Vignette

A 12-year-old boy is brought to the clinic because of yellowing of his eyes and dark urine for 1 day. Three days ago, he developed fever, sore throat, and nasal congestion. His mother gave him a leftover antibiotic from home. He has no history of easy bruising, prolonged bleeding, or chronic illness. His maternal uncle had a similar episode of jaundice as a child after taking medication for malaria. Temperature is 37.1°C, blood pressure is 106/68 mm Hg, pulse is 104/min, and oxygen saturation is 99% on room air. Physical examination shows scleral icterus and mild jaundice. There is no hepatosplenomegaly. Laboratory studies show hemoglobin of 9.1 g/dL, MCV of 88 fL, reticulocyte count of 8.2%, total bilirubin of 3.6 mg/dL, direct bilirubin of 0.5 mg/dL, LDH of 740 U/L, haptoglobin of 18 mg/dL, and platelets of 248,000/mm³. Peripheral blood smear shows irregularly shaped erythrocytes with semicircular membrane defects. Supravital staining shows inclusions within erythrocytes.
