## Vignette

A 67-year-old male with a history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hypertension is brought to the emergency department by his wife after she found him increasingly difficult to arouse over the past several hours. She reports that he has had a productive cough with yellowish sputum, worsening shortness of breath, and decreased oral intake for the past four days. He takes metformin, lisinopril, and uses an albuterol inhaler as needed. He has a 45 pack-year smoking history and is not on home oxygen. His blood pressure is 94/62 mmHg, heart rate is 118 bpm, respiratory rate is 28 breaths per minute, pulse oximetry is 81% on room air, and temperature is 38.9°C. On physical examination he is obtunded and responsive only to sternal rub, he has prolonged expiratory phase with diffuse expiratory wheezes bilaterally, and mild right lower lobe crackles. His extremities are warm with delayed capillary refill of 4 seconds. Laboratory studies reveal the following: pH 7.18, PaCO₂ 68 mmHg, PaO₂ 44 mmHg, bicarbonate 20 mEq/L, sodium 138 mEq/L, potassium 5.4 mEq/L, chloride 104 mEq/L, glucose 310 mg/dL, lactate 5.8 mmol/L, creatinine 2.4 mg/dL, and BUN 38 mg/dL. Serum ketones are negative. A chest radiograph shows right lower lobe consolidation with hyperinflated lung fields bilaterally. Which of the following most accurately describes this patient’s acid-base disturbance?
