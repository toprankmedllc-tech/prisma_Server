## Chief Complaint

## Vignette

A 34-year-old woman presents to the neurology clinic for evaluation of progressive symptoms over the past 5 months. She describes drooping of both eyelids that is barely noticeable in the morning but becomes severe by the evening, and horizontal double vision that worsens with prolonged reading. Over the past 4 weeks she has developed difficulty chewing tougher foods, occasional nasal regurgitation of liquids, and a hoarse voice toward the end of the day. She reports that her symptoms briefly improve after a short rest. She has no muscle pain, no sensory disturbance, and no autonomic complaints such as dry mouth or constipation. On examination, blood pressure is 118/72 mm Hg and pulse is 76/min. Resting facial appearance is normal, but after 60 seconds of sustained upgaze, both eyelids show marked ptosis. Extraocular movements show fatigable diplopia. Pupils are equal and reactive. Deep tendon reflexes are 2+ and symmetric throughout, and there is no postexercise augmentation of reflexes. Strength testing reveals progressive weakness of the deltoids with repetitive shoulder abduction that recovers after rest. Repetitive nerve stimulation at low frequency (3 Hz) shows a decremental response of the compound muscle action potential. CT of the chest reveals a 4-cm anterior mediastinal mass.

## Main Clue

Fatigable ptosis and diplopia + bulbar weakness + decremental EMG response + thymoma = AChR-antibody-positive generalized myasthenia gravis

## Supporting Clue

Normal reflexes, no autonomic features, no improvement with brief exercise: these rule out Lambert-Eaton, the most important mimic
