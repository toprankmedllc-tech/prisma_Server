## Chief Complaint

90 minutes of substernal chest pressure and diaphoresis

## Vignette

A 64-year-old man presents to the emergency department with 90 minutes of substernal chest pressure and diaphoresis. He has a history of hypertension and hyperlipidemia and a 30 pack-year smoking history. On examination, his blood pressure is 96/58 mm Hg, pulse is 42/min and regular, and respirations are 18/min. He appears diaphoretic but is alert and well-perfused. Jugular venous pressure is mildly elevated; lungs are clear. ECG shows ST-segment elevations in leads II, III, and aVF with reciprocal ST depression in leads I and aVL. Initial troponin is markedly elevated.
The branch of the artery involved in this patient's infarct is most likely which of the following?

## Key Symptoms

Substernal chest pressure; diaphoresis

## Labs

Markedly elevated troponin

## Physical Exam

Diaphoretic; alert and well-perfused; mildly elevated JVP; clear lungs; ECG with ST elevations in II, III, aVF and reciprocal ST depression in I, aVL

## Main Clue

Acute chest pain + inferior ST elevations (II, III, aVF) with reciprocal changes (I, aVL) + bradycardia (HR 42/min) → inferior STEMI with AV nodal involvement

## Supporting Clue

The combination of an inferior STEMI and bradycardia points to occlusion of the RCA at a point that compromises blood flow to the AV node, which is supplied by the AV nodal artery — a branch of the PDA (in right-dominant circulations, ~85% of people)
