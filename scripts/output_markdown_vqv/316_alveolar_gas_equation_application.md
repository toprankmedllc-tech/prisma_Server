## Chief Complaint

Worsening shortness of breath

## Vignette

A 58-year-old man with a long history of smoking presents to the emergency department with worsening shortness of breath over 3 days. He is afebrile but tachypneic and using accessory muscles. Arterial blood gas on room air shows: pH 7.32, PaCO₂ 60 mmHg, PaO₂ 52 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 30 mEq/L. A chest X-ray shows hyperinflated lungs and flattened diaphragms consistent with emphysema. The clinician suspects impaired gas exchange and calculates the expected alveolar oxygen tension (PAO₂) using the alveolar gas equation. The calculated PAO₂ is 80 mmHg. Despite supplemental oxygen, the patient’s hypoxemia persists. Which of the following best explains the increased A–a gradient in this patient?

## Key Symptoms

## Main Clue

Increased A–a gradient with hypoxemia despite oxygen in emphysema

## Supporting Clue

PAO₂–PaO₂ mismatch (80 vs 52) indicating impaired oxygen transfer
