## Chief Complaint

Anterior STEMI as end-stage complication of uncontrolled DM2 and HTN driven by structural SDOH barriers

## Vignette

A 58-year-old African American man with a 12-year history of type 2 diabetes and hypertension presents to the emergency department with crushing substernal chest pain, diaphoresis, and dyspnea of 2 hours' duration. ECG shows ST elevations in V1–V4. Troponin I is markedly elevated. He is diagnosed with an anterior STEMI and taken emergently for percutaneous coronary intervention, which reveals 100% occlusion of the proximal left anterior descending artery.  Review of his medical record shows that despite 14 documented primary care visits over 4 years, his hemoglobin A1c has never been below 9.8% (most recently 11.2%) and his blood pressure has never been at goal (most recent 164/98 mmHg). He has been prescribed metformin, lisinopril, amlodipine, and atorvastatin, but pharmacy refill records show he has filled only his metformin, no refills of the other three medications in 18 months.  During a post-MI social work interview, the patient discloses: he works two jobs (construction days, night security) with no sick leave or flexibility for daytime clinic appointments; the nearest pharmacy closed 14 months ago and he must take two buses requiring 3+ hours round trip to reach the next closest one; his annual income is approximately $28,000 supporting a household of four; his lisinopril costs $47/month and amlodipine $52/month out-of-pocket because his employer does not offer health insurance; and he has never been asked about any of these barriers at any of his 14 prior clinic visits.  Which of the following best identifies the primary systems-based barrier contributing to this patient's failure to achieve chronic disease control?

## Key Symptoms

Medication unaffordability ($99/month for two medications on $28,000 income); no health insurance; pharmacy closure requiring 3+ hour round trip; work schedule preventing daytime appointments; SDOH barriers never screened across 14 visits; metformin the only affordable/accessible medication → only one filled

## Labs

## Imaging Findings

## Main Clue

Social determinants of health (medication cost, geographic pharmacy inaccessibility, work schedule inflexibility, poverty, lack of insurance) are structural systems-level barriers driving medication non-adherence,  not patient unwillingness; SDOH never screened = systems failure across 14 visits

## Supporting Clue

SDOH account for 30-55% of health outcomes; framing as 'non-adherence' without structural context misplaces responsibility; health disparities in CVD disproportionately affect Black men via SDOH; systems interventions: medication assistance programs, telehealth, mail pharmacy, social work referral
