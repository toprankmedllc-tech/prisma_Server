## Chief Complaint

Worsening shortness of breath

## Vignette

 A 64-year-old man is brought to the intensive care unit because of worsening shortness of breath for the past 12 hours. Three days ago, he was admitted for septic shock secondary to perforated diverticulitis and underwent emergent surgery. Despite broad-spectrum antibiotics and fluid resuscitation, he develops progressive respiratory distress requiring supplemental oxygen. On examination, he appears tachypneic and cyanotic. Diffuse crackles are heard bilaterally. Chest x-ray demonstrates diffuse bilateral pulmonary infiltrates without cardiomegaly or pleural effusions. Arterial blood gas analysis reveals severe hypoxemia despite oxygen therapy. Echocardiography shows normal left ventricular systolic function. He is intubated and mechanically ventilated. Which of the following is the most appropriate ventilatory strategy to reduce mortality and prevent further lung injury in this patient?
