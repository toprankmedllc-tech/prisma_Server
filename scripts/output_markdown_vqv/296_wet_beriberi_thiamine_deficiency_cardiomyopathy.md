## Chief Complaint

 6 weeks of progressive dyspnea on exertion with bilateral lower extremity swelling

## Vignette

A 47-year-old man presents to the emergency department with 6 weeks of progressive dyspnea on exertion and bilateral lower extremity swelling. He has a long history of heavy alcohol use, drinking approximately a fifth of vodka daily for the past 15 years. He reports a poor and irregular diet. His past medical history is significant for a 20-year history of poorly controlled hypertension despite being on three antihypertensive medications. He mentions in passing that several months ago he had a few weeks of tingling and numbness in both feet that gradually resolved on its own. He denies chest pain, cough, or hemoptysis. On examination, his blood pressure is 122/58 mm Hg, pulse is 108/min, and respirations are 22/min. His extremities feel warm and have bounding peripheral pulses. Jugular venous pressure is elevated, and there is bilateral pitting edema to the mid-calves. ## Physical Exam

Warm extremities with bounding peripheral pulses; elevated jugular venous pressure; bilateral pitting lower extremity edema to the mid-calves; scattered bibasilar pulmonary crackles

## Main Clue

Chronic alcoholic with poor diet + high-output heart failure features (warm extremities, bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure, tachycardia) + signs of fluid overload (JVD, edema, crackles)

## Supporting Clue


The remote episode of bilateral foot paresthesias suggests an earlier subclinical episode of dry beriberi (peripheral polyneuropathy from thiamine deficiency); combined with current high-output failure, the pattern points to evolving thiamine deficiency rather than alcoholic cardiomyopathy (low-output dilated CM with narrow pulse pressure) or hypertensive heart disease (pressure-overload LVH with S4 and narrow pulse pressure)
