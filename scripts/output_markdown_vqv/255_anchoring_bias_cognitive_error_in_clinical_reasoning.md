## Chief Complaint

Progressive exertional dyspnea, productive cough, low-grade fever

## Vignette

A 58-year-old man with a 35 pack-year smoking history presents to the emergency department with a 3-week history of progressive exertional dyspnea, a productive cough, and low-grade fever. His wife reports that he has had similar episodes twice in the past two years, each diagnosed as "COPD exacerbation" and treated successfully with bronchodilators and a prednisone taper. The triage note prominently documents "known COPD exacerbation" based on the prior medical records. The on-call resident reviews the triage note before entering the room and immediately orders nebulized albuterol, systemic corticosteroids, and supplemental oxygen. On examination, the patient is tachycardic at 108 bpm, tachypneic at 22 breaths/min, and has oxygen saturation of 91% on room air. Lung auscultation reveals diminished breath sounds at the right base with dullness to percussion in that region. The resident documents "COPD exacerbation, responding to bronchodilators" after noting mild symptomatic improvement and plans for discharge. The attending physician later reviews the case and orders a chest radiograph, which reveals a large right-sided pleural effusion with a nodular density at the right hilum. CT chest with contrast demonstrates a 3.2 cm spiculated right hilar mass with ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, findings highly concerning for primary lung malignancy with malignant pleural effusion. The patient is admitted and bronchoscopy with biopsy confirms non-small cell lung carcinoma. Which of the following best describes the primary cognitive error made by the resident in this clinical encounter?

## Key Symptoms

Unilateral diminished breath sounds; dullness to percussion right base; tachycardia; hypoxia 91%; prior COPD label in triage note; right hilar mass on imaging; malignant pleural effusion; NSCLC confirmed

## Labs

WBC 11,200/µL (mild leukocytosis); LDH elevated at 342 U/L; pleural fluid LDH > 2/3 serum LDH (exudative by Light's criteria)

## Imaging Findings

CXR: right pleural effusion + right hilar nodular density. CT chest: 3.2 cm spiculated right hilar mass; ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy; large right pleural effusion

## Physical Exam

Diminished breath sounds right base; dullness to percussion right lower lobe; no wheezing; no prolonged expiratory phase; tachycardia; no peripheral edema

## Main Clue

Resident pre-committed to diagnosis of COPD exacerbation based on triage note and prior records before performing an independent examination; failed to integrate unilateral physical findings (asymmetric dullness, absent breath sounds) inconsistent with COPD, which is typically a diffuse obstructive process

## Supporting Clue

Anchoring bias = over-reliance on first information encountered (triage label, prior records) when updating diagnostic probability in light of new clinical data. Classic COPD exacerbation produces bilateral hyperinflation, not unilateral pleural dullness.
