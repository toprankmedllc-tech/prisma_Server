## Chief Complaint

Respiratory distress and declining mental status after motor vehicle collision

## Vignette

A 24-year-old man is brought to the emergency department after a motor vehicle collision. Due to declining mental status and respiratory distress, rapid-sequence endotracheal intubation is planned. After administration of intravenous anesthetic agents, a paralytic medication is given. Seconds later, the patient develops brief generalized muscle fasciculations followed by complete skeletal muscle paralysis, allowing successful intubation.

During surgery, the anesthesiologist notices increasing end-tidal CO₂, generalized muscle rigidity, tachycardia, and a rapidly rising body temperature. Laboratory studies later reveal markedly elevated creatine kinase levels and hyperkalemia. The patient is treated appropriately, and surgery is completed.

Several hours after discontinuation of anesthesia, the patient remains apneic and unable to move his extremities despite being fully conscious. His wife reports that the patient’s father once had “difficulty waking up after anesthesia.”

Which of the following best explains this patient’s prolonged paralysis?
