## Chief Complaint

14 months of persistent grief, daily intense crying, social withdrawal, functional impairment, passive death wish, inability to accept husband's death

## Vignette

A 58-year-old woman is brought to her primary care physician by her daughter 14 months after the unexpected death of her husband of 31 years from a myocardial infarction. Her daughter reports that her mother has been unable to return to any of her prior activities, refuses to visit the couple's friends, has removed all photographs from the walls of the home because viewing them is 'unbearable,' and cries daily with the same intensity as in the first week after the loss. The patient states she has no interest in 'moving forward' and believes her own life has 'lost all meaning.' She endorses passive wishes to die so she can 'be with him,' though she denies active suicidal ideation, plan, or intent. She describes persistent difficulty accepting that the death was real, intense longing for her husband that occupies most of her waking hours, and a pervasive sense of bitterness about the circumstances of his death. She has not returned to work as a librarian despite being medically cleared to do so and states she is unable to 'function around other people.' She sleeps approximately 4 hours per night and has lost 18 pounds over the past year. She denies hallucinations but reports occasionally 'hearing' her husband's voice calling her name when she is falling asleep. She has no prior psychiatric history, no medical conditions, and takes no medications. Urine toxicology is negative. TSH, CBC, and CMP are all within normal limits. On mental status examination, she is tearful and subdued, with a constricted affect. She is alert and oriented. Thought process is coherent and goal-directed. No psychotic features are identified on full structured evaluation. Which of the following most accurately characterizes this patient's presentation?

## Main Clue

Grief symptoms that are clinically indistinguishable in character from normal grief but markedly abnormal in duration (14 months), intensity (unchanged from week 1), and functional impact (unable to work, complete social withdrawal),  plus the core diagnostic features: persistent yearning, difficulty accepting the death as real, bitterness, and identity disruption

## Supporting Clue

Normal grief involves gradual softening of acute distress over weeks to months with preservation or eventual restoration of function. Passive death wish ('wanting to join him') is common in normal grief and is not equivalent to suicidal ideation. Hypnagogic hallucinations (hearing the deceased's voice while falling asleep) are normal grief phenomena, not psychosis. The absence of a mood episode with anhedonia, excessive guilt, neurovegetative symptoms, and the presence of grief-specific content (longing, yearning, denial) distinguish this from MDD.
