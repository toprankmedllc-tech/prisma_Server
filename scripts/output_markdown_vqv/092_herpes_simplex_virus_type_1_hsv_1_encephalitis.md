## Chief Complaint

Acute febrile encephalopathy with personality change and seizure

## Vignette

A 48-year-old previously healthy man is brought to the emergency department by his wife because of 3 days of high fever, severe headache, and bizarre behavior. She reports that yesterday he became uncharacteristically irritable, yelled at family members for no reason, and was unable to recall a conversation they had just had. This morning he had a witnessed generalized convulsion lasting 90 seconds. He has no history of HIV, immunosuppression, or recent travel. On examination, temperature is 39.0°C (102.2°F), heart rate 108/min, blood pressure 138/86 mm Hg. He is disoriented to place and time and produces fragmented, incoherent speech. There is mild right-sided face and arm weakness. Meningismus is absent. Noncontrast CT of the head is unremarkable. MRI of the brain shows asymmetric T2/FLAIR hyperintensity with foci of petechial hemorrhage in the left medial temporal lobe and adjacent insular cortex. Lumbar puncture shows 220 white blood cells/µL (90% lymphocytes), 60 red blood cells/µL (with no contamination from the procedure), protein 95 mg/dL, glucose 62 mg/dL (serum 100 mg/dL).

## Key Symptoms

3-day febrile illness; behavioral / personality change; impaired short-term memory; aphasic, fragmented speech; new-onset generalized seizure; focal right-sided weakness; absence of meningismus

## Labs

CBC: WBC 11,500/µL with lymphocytic predominance. CSF: 220 WBC/µL (90% lymphocytes), 60 RBC/µL (no traumatic tap), protein 95 mg/dL, glucose 62 mg/dL (serum 100 mg/dL).

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT head: unremarkable. MRI brain: asymmetric T2/FLAIR hyperintensity with petechial hemorrhage in the left medial temporal lobe and adjacent insular cortex

## Physical Exam

Febrile, disoriented to place and time; fragmented incoherent speech; mild right face and arm weakness; no meningismus; pupils equal and reactive; no rash; cranial nerves otherwise intact

## Main Clue

Acute febrile encephalopathy with prominent BEHAVIORAL/MEMORY changes, focal deficit, seizure, and MRI showing temporal lobe inflammation with petechial hemorrhage: classic for HSV-1 hemorrhagic encephalitis

## Supporting Clue

CSF lymphocytic pleocytosis with RBCs and normal glucose supports HSV-1 (hemorrhagic necrosis releases RBCs into CSF); HSV PCR is the confirmatory test, but mortality without prompt empiric treatment is >70%, so therapy must NOT wait for PCR
