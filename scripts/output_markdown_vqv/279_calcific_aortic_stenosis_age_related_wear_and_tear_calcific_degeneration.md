## Chief Complaint

6 months of progressive exertional dyspnea with two episodes of presyncope and exertional chest discomfort

## Vignette

A 78-year-old man presents to clinic with 6 months of progressively worsening exertional dyspnea and two episodes of presyncope while climbing stairs. He also reports occasional chest discomfort with exertion that resolves with rest. His past medical history is significant for hypertension and hyperlipidemia. On examination, his blood pressure is 142/78 mm Hg, pulse is 78/min, and respirations are 16/min. Cardiac auscultation reveals a harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic ejection murmur at the right upper sternal border that radiates to the carotids, with a delayed and diminished carotid upstroke.
Which of the following is the most likely underlying mechanism of this patient's condition?

## Physical Exam

Harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic ejection murmur at the right upper sternal border radiating to the carotids; delayed and diminished carotid upstroke (pulsus parvus et tardus)

## Main Clue

Elderly patient + classic AS triad (syncope, angina, exertional heart failure) + harsh crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur at RUSB radiating to carotids + pulsus parvus et tardus

## Supporting Clue

Advanced age (78 years) and absence of childhood rheumatic fever or known congenital bicuspid valve point to age-related calcific degeneration as the most common etiology in patients >70
