## Chief Complaint

Two weeks of progressive headache, fever, and mental status changes in an HIV positive man off ART

## Vignette

A 38-year-old man with a 6-year history of HIV infection presents to the emergency department because of 2 weeks of progressively worsening dull headache, intermittent low-grade fevers, malaise, and mild nausea. Over the past 3 days his partner has noted that he is more confused and forgetful than usual. He stopped his antiretroviral therapy 18 months ago and has missed several follow-up visits. He denies neck stiffness, photophobia, focal weakness, seizures, or rash. On examination he is thin and chronically ill-appearing. He is oriented to person and place but not to date and is slow to respond. The neck is supple. There is no rash. Cranial nerves and motor strength are normal; reflexes are 2+ and symmetric. Fundoscopic examination shows mild bilateral papilledema. A noncontrast CT of the head shows no mass or hemorrhage; the ventricles are mildly enlarged. Lumbar puncture is performed with the patient in the lateral decubitus position.

## Key Symptoms

Subacute headache with fever; confusion and slowed mentation; bilateral papilledema; supple neck (notably lacking meningismus despite meningeal infection, typical of cryptococcal disease); preserved focal neurologic exam

## Labs

CD4 count 38 cells/mm³ (normal 500 to 1500); HIV viral load 248,000 copies/mL. CBC: WBC 4.0 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 11.4 g/dL, Plt 178 × 10⁹/L. BMP: Na 134 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L, Cr 1.0 mg/dL, glucose 92 mg/dL. CSF: opening pressure 32 cm H₂O (normal less than 20); WBC 28/µL (88% lymphocytes); RBC 0/µL; protein 92 mg/dL (normal less than 45); glucose 38 mg/dL (serum 92 mg/dL).

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT head: no mass or hemorrhage; mildly enlarged ventricles

## Physical Exam

Chronically ill appearing; oriented to person and place, not to date; SUPPLE neck; no rash; normal cranial nerves and strength; symmetric 2+ reflexes; bilateral papilledema

## Main Clue

Subacute meningitic illness in a profoundly immunocompromised HIV patient (CD4 less than 100) with elevated CSF opening pressure, lymphocytic pleocytosis, low CSF glucose, and elevated protein; pattern most consistent with cryptococcal meningitis

## Supporting Clue

Notable LACK of meningismus despite documented meningeal infection (cryptococcal disease often shows minimal neck stiffness because the indolent yeast triggers little neutrophilic inflammation), and dramatically elevated opening pressure (greater than or equal to 25 cm H₂O); both are characteristic and clinically important findings
