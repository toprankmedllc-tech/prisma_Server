## Chief Complaint

2 days of progressively worsening sharp central chest pain

## Vignette

A 58-year-old man is brought to the emergency department by his wife because of 2 days of worsening chest pain. He has a history of end-stage renal disease on hemodialysis, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hypertension. His wife notes he has missed several recent appointments due to transportation difficulties. The pain is sharp, central, and worsens with deep inspiration; he is unable to lie flat. His temperature is 37.4°C (99.3°F), blood pressure is 162/96 mm Hg, pulse is 102/min, and respirations are 22/min. SpO₂ is 96% on room air. Cardiac examination reveals distant heart sounds and a soft, transient scratching sound at the left lower sternal border. Jugular venous pressure is elevated to 10 cm H₂O. Lungs are clear bilaterally. ECG shows low-voltage QRS complexes diffusely without ST-segment elevation or PR-segment depression. Laboratory studies show:

Blood urea nitrogen: 98 mg/dL
Creatinine: 9.1 mg/dL
Potassium: 5.6 mEq/L
Troponin I: 0.04 ng/mL (normal <0.04)
WBC: 11,200/μL

Lead-in: Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?
