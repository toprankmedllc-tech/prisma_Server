## Vignette

A 78-year-old man comes to the physician for a routine health maintenance examination. He has no acute complaints and feels well. The patient is functionally active and walks 2 miles daily without chest pain, dyspnea, palpitations, or lower extremity claudication. His medical history is significant only for osteoarthritis of the knees. He uses acetaminophen occasionally for joint pain and takes no other medications. He is a lifelong nonsmoker. Blood pressure is 168/74 mm Hg in the right arm and 165/72 mm Hg in the left arm, pulse is 64/min and regular, and respirations are 14/min. BMI is 24 kg/m². Cardiopulmonary examination reveals clear lungs bilaterally to auscultation and percussion. The apical impulse is non-displaced. Heart sounds reveal a regular rate and rhythm; a soft S4 is heard at the apex, but there are no murmurs, rubs, or S3 gallops. There is no jugular venous distention or lower extremity edema. Laboratory studies are within normal limits. An electrocardiogram shows normal sinus rhythm without ST-segment or T-wave abnormalities. Which of the following is the most likely underlying cause of this patient's vital sign findings?

## Physical Exam

Clear lungs; non-displaced apical impulse; soft S4 gallop; no murmurs/rubs; 2+ symmetric pulses; no edema or JVD

## Main Clue

Elevated systolic blood pressure with a normal diastolic blood pressure (wide pulse pressure) in an asymptomatic elderly patient

## Supporting Clue

Soft S4 gallop (indicating mild age-related decreased left ventricular compliance); completely normal labs and ECG
