## Chief Complaint

32 years old; Male; Long-term fluoxetine (SSRI) use; Tramadol started 3 days ago (a weak opioid that also inhibits serotonin reuptake and is a mild serotonin releaser); No antipsychotic exposure; No general anesthesia or succinylcholine; No illicit drug use

## Vignette

A 32-year-old man with a history of major depressive disorder and chronic low back pain has been taking fluoxetine 40 mg daily for the past year. Three days ago his primary care physician started tramadol 50 mg every 6 hours for a pain flare. Today his wife found him agitated and confused in their kitchen, drenched in sweat, and brought him to the emergency department. On arrival he is restless and pacing in the bed. He alternately laughs and shouts. His pupils are 6 mm and reactive bilaterally. His skin is hot and profusely diaphoretic. The hand muscles show a fine rapid tremor at rest. When the examiner rapidly dorsiflexes the right ankle, the foot shows multiple sustained, repetitive, rhythmic flexion-extension beats that fade only after the examiner releases. Reflexes are brisk at the patella and Achilles bilaterally. Tone is increased in the lower more than upper extremities. Bowel sounds are hyperactive. He has not been started on any new psychiatric medication, has not used illicit drugs, and has not had general anesthesia.

## Key Symptoms

Altered mental status (agitation, confusion); marked autonomic hyperactivity (fever, sweating, mydriasis, hyperactive bowel sounds, tachycardia, hypertension); neuromuscular hyperactivity (fine rapid tremor, brisk reflexes, INDUCIBLE SUSTAINED CLONUS in the lower extremities, increased tone greater in lower than upper extremities); recent addition of a serotonergic drug to an existing SSRI

## Labs

CBC: WBC 13.4 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 14.8 g/dL, Plt 250 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 138 mEq/L, K 4.0 mEq/L, Cr 1.0 mg/dL, glucose 110 mg/dL, bicarbonate 19 mEq/L; CK 480 U/L; LFTs within normal limits; TSH 1.6 mIU/L; Urine and serum toxicology negative for cocaine, amphetamines, and PCP; Serum ethanol negative.

## Imaging Findings

Noncontrast CT head: no acute hemorrhage, mass, or hydrocephalus

## Physical Exam

Agitated, restless, alternating laughing and shouting; pupils 6 mm reactive bilaterally; hot and profusely diaphoretic skin; fine rapid resting tremor; INDUCIBLE SUSTAINED ANKLE CLONUS bilaterally (foot shows multiple rhythmic flexion-extension beats after rapid dorsiflexion); brisk patellar and Achilles reflexes throughout; increased tone in lower more than upper extremities; hyperactive bowel sounds

## Main Clue

The triad of (1) MENTAL STATUS CHANGES, (2) AUTONOMIC HYPERACTIVITY, and (3) NEUROMUSCULAR HYPERACTIVITY (especially INDUCIBLE CLONUS, HYPERREFLEXIA, and lower-greater-than-upper hypertonia) in the setting of TWO SEROTONERGIC DRUGS started together (chronic fluoxetine plus newly added tramadol) is diagnostic for serotonin syndrome 

## Supporting Clue

Distinguishing this from neuroleptic malignant syndrome: NMS develops over DAYS TO WEEKS after starting a DOPAMINE D2 ANTAGONIST (antipsychotic or antiemetic), with markedly slower bradyreflexia / bradykinesia, UNIFORM LEAD-PIPE RIGIDITY without clonus, and markedly elevated CK. Serotonin syndrome develops over HOURS TO 24 HOURS after a serotonergic drug change, with HYPERREFLEXIA, clonus, and only modestly elevated CK
