## Vignette

A research team at a large academic medical center is designing a prospective investigation to evaluate whether a novel direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) reduces the incidence of ischemic stroke compared to warfarin in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation. The investigators randomly assign 2,000 eligible patients with documented non-valvular atrial fibrillation and a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of ≥2 to receive either the investigational DOAC (n=1,000) or dose-adjusted warfarin targeting an INR of 2.0–3.0 (n=1,000). The assignment sequence is generated by a computer algorithm, and the randomization is stratified by CHA₂DS₂-VASc score and prior stroke history to ensure balanced baseline characteristics.  All enrolled patients, their treating physicians, and the outcome adjudicators reviewing suspected stroke events are masked to treatment assignment throughout the 2-year follow-up period. Patients are followed with quarterly clinic visits, serial INR monitoring for the warfarin group, and standardized neurological assessments. The primary outcome is a composite of ischemic stroke and systemic arterial embolism confirmed by imaging. Secondary outcomes include major bleeding events, all-cause mortality, and quality of life assessed by validated patient-reported outcome measures.  An independent data safety monitoring board reviews unblinded interim analyses at prespecified intervals and has the authority to halt the trial early for overwhelming benefit, harm, or futility. All patients provide written informed consent, and the study protocol has been approved by the institutional review board. A planned enrollment of 2,000 patients provides 80% statistical power to detect a 25% relative risk reduction in the primary outcome at a two-sided alpha of 0.05. ## Main Clue

Computer-generated random allocation + intervention + prospective follow-up + double-blind (patients and physicians masked) + active comparator = Double-blind RCT; gold standard for establishing causality

## Supporting Clue
