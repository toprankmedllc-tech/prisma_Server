## Chief Complaint

Bilious vomiting 

## Vignette

A 2-day-old male newborn is brought to the emergency department with bilious vomiting and increasing abdominal distension. His mother reports poor feeding and episodes of crying whenever his abdomen is touched. He was born at term with no complications. On exam, his abdomen is tender and mildly distended, and bowel sounds are diminished. A plain abdominal radiograph is nonspecific. An upper GI contrast study shows a corkscrew appearance of the duodenum and proximal jejunum, with the ligament of Treitz sitting in an abnormal position.
