## Chief Complaint

4 months of progressive dyspnea on exertion, lower extremity edema, and abdominal distension

## Vignette

A 68-year-old man with a 10-year history of multiple myeloma presents with 4 months of progressive dyspnea on exertion, lower extremity swelling, and increasing abdominal girth. He has lost 6 kg over the same interval. On examination, his blood pressure is 102/68 mm Hg, pulse is 88/min, and respirations are 18/min. Jugular venous pressure is elevated, there is bilateral pitting edema to the knees, and he has hepatomegaly with mild ascites. ECG demonstrates low-voltage QRS complexes diffusely. Echocardiography shows concentric thickening of both ventricular walls with biatrial enlargement and preserved ejection fraction, with marked impairment of diastolic relaxation. Endomyocardial biopsy reveals extracellular eosinophilic deposits that exhibit apple-green birefringence under polarized light after Congo red staining.
Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Physical Exam

Elevated jugular venous pressure; bilateral pitting lower extremity edema to the knees; hepatomegaly with mild ascites

## Main Clue

Multiple myeloma + right-heart-failure presentation + paradoxical low-voltage ECG with thickened LV on echo + apple-green birefringence on Congo red staining

## Supporting Clue

Preserved EF with severe diastolic dysfunction defines restrictive cardiomyopathy; multiple myeloma is the classic source of AL (light-chain) amyloid
