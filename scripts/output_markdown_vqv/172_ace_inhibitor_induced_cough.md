## Chief Complaint

Persistent dry nonproductive cough × 2 weeks; New after starting lisinopril; Resolves with switch to losartan

## Vignette

A 56-year-old African American man with hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and proteinuria is started on lisinopril 10 mg daily. Four weeks later, he returns complaining of a persistent dry, nonproductive cough for the past 2 weeks that awakens him at night. He has no fever, rhinorrhea, wheezing, or dyspnea. His blood pressure is well-controlled at 128/78 mmHg. Lung examination is completely clear. Chest X-ray is normal. He is switched to losartan 50 mg daily. One week later, his cough resolves completely.
Which of the following best explains why he developed a cough on lisinopril but not on losartan?

A) Losartan directly inhibits tracheal cough receptors
B) Lisinopril increase airway bradykinin accumulation
C) Lisinopril causes direct mast cell degranulation in the airways
D) Losartan has greater selectivity for the pulmonary ACE isoform
E) Lisinopril leads to a decrease in pulmonary prostaglandin levels
