## Chief Complaint

Progressive instability and "clumsy" gait over the last two years.

## Vignette

A 15-year-old male is brought to the clinic by his parents due to a two-year history of progressive instability and a "clumsy" gait. His parents note that he frequently stumbles, particularly in low-light settings, and his speech has become increasingly difficult to understand. He has also developed progressive weakness in his lower extremities. The family history is notable for a paternal uncle who developed similar "walking problems" in his early teens.

On physical examination, the patient has a high-arched appearance to his feet (pes cavus) and hammer toes. There is marked kyphoscoliosis of the spine. Neurological examination reveals a dysarthric speech pattern and a significant decrease in vibratory sensation and proprioception in the feet and ankles bilaterally. Deep tendon reflexes are absent at the patella and Achilles. However, plantar reflex testing elicites an upward movement of the great toes bilaterally.

## Key Symptoms

Dysarthria; frequent stumbles; loss of balance in low-light settings; progressive weakness in the lower extremities.

## Labs

Complete blood count shows a hemoglobin of 13.8 g/dL; mean corpuscular volume (MCV) of 90 fL; normal serum Vitamin E levels; normal serum B12 and methylmalonic acid.

## Imaging Findings

MRI of the brain and spine reveals atrophy of the cervical spinal cord and thinning of the dorsal roots. Transthoracic echocardiogram shows concentric left ventricular hypertrophy with an ejection fraction of 65%.

## Physical Exam

High-arched feet (pes cavus) and hammer toes; marked kyphoscoliosis; decreased vibratory sensation and proprioception in the feet and ankles; absent patellar and Achilles reflexes; upgoing toes on Babinski testing.

## Main Clue

The "paradoxical" neurologic exam: absent deep tendon reflexes (lower motor neuron sign) combined with extensor plantar responses (upper motor neuron sign).

## Supporting Clue

Concentric left ventricular hypertrophy in a teenager with skeletal deformities and normal Vitamin E/B12 levels.
