## Chief Complaint

Cluster of recurring severe periorbital headaches with ipsilateral autonomic features for 5 weeks

## Vignette

A 42-year-old man presents with 5 weeks of recurring severe right-sided headaches. The pain is intense and located behind the right eye, lasts about 45 minutes, and occurs 2 to 3 times each day, often waking him from sleep at approximately 2 AM. During an attack he is unable to sit still and paces around the room rubbing his eye. With each attack his right eye becomes red and waters profusely, his right nostril runs, and his wife has noted slight drooping of the right eyelid with a small right pupil that resolve between attacks. He has had similar 4 to 8 week episodes of headache in alternating years, separated by long periods of complete freedom from pain. He has no nausea, photophobia, or phonophobia. He drinks 2 to 3 beers in the evening and has noticed that even small amounts of alcohol immediately trigger an attack during an episode. He takes no daily medications.

## Key Symptoms

Severe strictly unilateral periorbital pain; brief duration (about 45 minutes); multiple attacks daily often at the same nocturnal hour; circa-annual cluster pattern with intervals of remission; alcohol immediately triggers attacks during a cluster; ipsilateral conjunctival injection, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, partial ptosis, miosis; marked restlessness during attacks

## Labs

CBC: WBC 7.4 × 10⁹/L, Hgb 14.8 g/dL, Plt 250 × 10⁹/L; BMP: Na 140 mEq/L, K 4.1 mEq/L, Cr 0.9 mg/dL, glucose 92 mg/dL; TSH 1.5 mIU/L (0.4 to 4.0); LFTs normal.

## Imaging Findings

MRI brain with and without contrast unremarkable; no pituitary mass, cavernous sinus lesion, or arteriovenous malformation

## Physical Exam

Between attacks the examination is unremarkable. During the observed attack: right conjunctival injection, right lacrimation, right rhinorrhea, mild right upper-eyelid droop, miosis of the right pupil, marked agitation with pacing. Neurologic examination is otherwise normal

## Main Clue

Strictly unilateral periorbital pain lasting under 3 hours, multiple times per day in clusters, with ipsilateral cranial autonomic features (conjunctival injection, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, ptosis, miosis), prominent restlessness, alcohol trigger, and circa-annual cluster pattern. Together these define cluster headache, a trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia

## Supporting Clue

Migraineurs typically lie still in a dark, quiet room (movement worsens their pain), whereas cluster patients PACE because the pain is so severe and the disorder activates autonomic restlessness. The ipsilateral ptosis and miosis (a partial Horner syndrome from carotid sympathetic irritation) is another distinguishing feature
