## Chief Complaint

 6 days of high fever unresponsive to antipyretics, with rash and irritability

## Vignette

A 3-year-old boy is brought to the pediatrician because of 6 days of high fever that has not responded to acetaminophen or ibuprofen. His mother also reports a rash that began on his trunk, marked irritability, and reluctance to walk over the past 2 days. On examination, his temperature is 39.4°C (102.9°F), pulse is 138/min, and respirations are 26/min. He has bilateral non-purulent conjunctival injection, dry and erythematous lips with a deeply erythematous tongue, a polymorphous erythematous rash on his trunk and extremities, and indurated edema of his hands and feet with redness of the palms and soles. A tender enlarged anterior cervical lymph node is palpable on the right side. Laboratory studies show a leukocytosis with a left shift and an elevated C-reactive protein.
Which of the following is the most common complication of this patient's condition?

## Key Symptoms

High fever for 6 days unresponsive to antipyretics; diffuse polymorphous truncal rash; marked irritability; reluctance to walk; bilateral non-purulent conjunctivitis; dry erythematous lips with erythematous tongue; indurated edema of hands and feet with palmar/plantar erythema; tender unilateral cervical lymphadenopathy

## Labs

Leukocytosis with left shift; elevated C-reactive protein

## Physical Exam

 Bilateral non-purulent conjunctival injection; dry and erythematous lips with a deeply erythematous ("strawberry") tongue; polymorphous erythematous rash on trunk and extremities; indurated edema of the hands and feet with redness of the palms and soles; tender enlarged anterior cervical lymph node on the right

## Main Clue

Young child + fever ≥5 days + bilateral non-purulent conjunctivitis + strawberry tongue + polymorphous truncal rash + extremity changes (palmar/plantar erythema, edema) + unilateral cervical lymphadenopathy

## Supporting Clue

Markedly elevated inflammatory markers (CRP, leukocytosis with left shift) and acute systemic illness in a young child are consistent with a medium-vessel vasculitis affecting the coronary arteries
