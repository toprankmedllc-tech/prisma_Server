## Chief Complaint

Severe shortness of breath

## Vignette

A 52-year-old man is admitted to the intensive care unit after severe acute pancreatitis due to gallstones. On hospital day 3, he develops rapidly worsening shortness of breath and agitation. He is tachypneic and using accessory muscles. Oxygen saturation is 82% despite 100% FiO₂ via a non-rebreather mask.
Arterial blood gas on 100% oxygen shows:
pH: 7.48
PaCO₂: 30 mmHg
PaO₂: 52 mmHg
HCO₃⁻: 22 mEq/L
Chest radiograph shows bilateral diffuse “ground-glass” opacities without cardiomegaly. Pulmonary artery wedge pressure is 10 mmHg. Despite intubation and mechanical ventilation, oxygenation remains poor with markedly decreased lung compliance. The underlying pathophysiology of this condition is most likely due to which of the following?

## Key Symptoms

Rapidly worsening dyspnea; Hypoxemia refractory to oxygen; Tachypnea; Agitation; Respiratory distress; ICU deterioration after pancreatitis

## Labs

ABG: pH 7.48; PaCO₂ 30 mmHg; PaO₂ 52 mmHg on 100% FiO₂; HCO₃⁻ 22 mEq/L; Normal pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (10 mmHg)

## Imaging Findings

Bilateral diffuse ground-glass opacities; No cardiomegaly

## Physical Exam

Diffuse bilateral crackles; Use of accessory muscles; Severe respiratory distress

## Main Clue

Severe hypoxemia (PaO₂ 52 on 100% oxygen) with normal wedge pressure after pancreatitis

## Supporting Clue

Bilateral ground-glass opacities + decreased lung compliance + ICU trigger (pancreatitis)
