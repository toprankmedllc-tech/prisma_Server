## Vignette

An epidemiology research team at a state health department is conducting annual surveillance of active tuberculosis (TB) in a defined geographic catchment area with a stable registered population of 100,000 adults. The team begins its surveillance period on January 1 of the surveillance year. At midnight on January 1, a review of all active TB registrations reveals 400 confirmed active TB cases currently under treatment or follow-up in the population. These 400 cases were diagnosed at varying points over the preceding 2 years and represent patients still within their treatment course.  During the calendar surveillance year (January 1 through December 31), 200 individuals in the catchment area are newly diagnosed with active TB based on confirmed positive sputum culture results. Of the 400 cases present at the start of the year: 80 patients complete treatment and are declared cured, 60 patients die from TB-related complications, and the remaining 260 continue treatment into the following year. All 200 newly diagnosed cases are still under treatment at year's end.  The team prepares a comprehensive annual TB surveillance report for submission to the state health commissioner.  Which of the following correctly calculates the period prevalence of active TB in this population for the surveillance year?


## Key Symptoms

Jan 1 point prevalence: 400/100,000 = 0.4%; New cases (incidence) during year: 200; Period prevalence = (400+200)/100,000 = 600/100,000 = 0.6%; Deaths and recoveries do NOT reduce period prevalence because those individuals had the disease during the period

## Labs

Point prevalence Jan 1 = 400/100,000 = 0.4%; Incidence = 200/100,000 = 0.2% per year; Period prevalence = 600/100,000 = 0.6%; Point prevalence Dec 31 = (260+200)/100,000 = 460/100,000 = 0.46%

## Main Clue

 Period prevalence = (all cases existing at start of period + all new cases during period) / population = (400 + 200)/100,000 = 600/100,000 = 0.6%; deaths and cures do NOT subtract those individuals had disease during the period

## Supporting Clue

Period prevalence > point prevalence at either endpoint; captures everyone who had disease at any point during the defined interval; point prevalence Dec 31 = 460/100,000 (different measure)
