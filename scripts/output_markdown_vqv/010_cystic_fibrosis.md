## Vignette

A 17-year-old white male with a known history of cystic fibrosis presents to the pulmonology clinic with a 3-week history of worsening productive cough with green sputum and progressive dyspnea on exertion. He has been hospitalized three times since age 5 for pneumonia and has had oily, foul-smelling stools since childhood. His mother mentions he has been significantly delayed in puberty compared to his peers. Over the past 4 months, he has lost 6 kg despite maintaining his usual diet. On examination, he appears thin and mildly dyspneic. His chest is barrel-shaped with digital clubbing bilaterally, diffuse coarse crackles, and expiratory wheezing. Nasal polyps are noted on anterior rhinoscopy. He is at Tanner stage II. 
Vitals show a blood pressure of 112/70 mmHg, heart rate of 98 bpm, temperature of 38.3°C, and oxygen saturation of 91% on room air. 
Labs reveal a WBC of 14,200/µL with 82% neutrophils, markedly decreased fecal elastase, low fat-soluble vitamin levels (A, D, E, and K), a fasting blood glucose of 187 mg/dL, and an HbA1c of 7.4% — both newly elevated compared to labs drawn 8 months ago. Sweat chloride is 68 mEq/L. Sputum culture grows mucoid Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Chest X-ray demonstrates bilateral upper lobe bronchiectasis, hyperinflation, and peribronchial cuffing without lobar consolidation. He is admitted and started on IV tobramycin and piperacillin-tazobactam for his pulmonary exacerbation. Which of the following is the most appropriate long-term management for his newly discovered glucose abnormality?​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​

## Key Symptoms

Chronic productive cough with green sputum; progressive dyspnea on exertion; 6 kg weight loss over 4 months; 3 episodes of “pneumonia” since age 5; oily, foul-smelling stools since childhood; delayed puberty

## Labs

Sweat chloride: 68 mEq/L; WBC: 14,200/µL with 82% neutrophils; Sputum culture: Pseudomonas aeruginosa (mucoid); Fecal elastase: markedly decreased; Fat-soluble vitamin levels (A, D, E, K): all low; Blood glucose: 187 mg/dL (fasting); HbA1c: 7.4%

## Imaging Findings

CXR: bilateral upper lobe bronchiectasis, hyperinflation, peribronchial cuffing; No lobar consolidation

## Physical Exam

Barrel-shaped chest; digital clubbing bilaterally; coarse crackles and expiratory wheeze diffusely; nasal polyps on anterior rhinoscopy; Tanner stage II (delayed for age)

## Main Clue

Chronic Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection + bronchiectasis + pancreatic exocrine insufficiency in a young white male = Cystic Fibrosis

## Supporting Clue

New fasting hyperglycemia (BG 187, HbA1c 7.4%) in the setting of CF = CF-related diabetes (CFRD), caused by progressive destruction of islet cells from pancreatic fibrosis — distinct from Type 1 or Type 2 DM
