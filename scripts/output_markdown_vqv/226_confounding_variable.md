## Vignette

: A research team at a public health institute publishes findings from a large cross-sectional study of 15,000 adults across a metropolitan area. They report a statistically robust positive association between daily consumption of energy drinks and the development of hypertension (OR = 3.8, 95% CI: 2.9–4.9, p < 0.001). The investigators control for age, sex, income, and physical activity level in a multivariable logistic regression model, but they do not measure cigarette smoking status or caffeine intake from other sources in their survey.  A hypertension specialist reviewing the manuscript for peer review argues that the reported association may be entirely or substantially explained by a third variable that the investigators failed to account for. She notes that cigarette smoking is strongly associated with energy drink consumption (heavy energy drink users smoke at nearly twice the rate of non-users in the available demographic data) and is itself an established independent risk factor for hypertension, and that smoking is not on the causal pathway between energy drink consumption and hypertension physiology.  Which of the following best explains the specialist's concern about the validity of the reported association?

## Key Symptoms

Energy drink exposure; hypertension outcome; cigarette smoking associated with both energy drinks AND hypertension; smoking not on causal pathway between energy drinks and hypertension; classic confounding triad

## Main Clue

Cigarette smoking is: (1) associated with the exposure (energy drink use), (2) independently associated with the outcome (hypertension), and (3) NOT on the causal pathway between exposure and outcome → satisfies all 3 criteria for confounding

## Supporting Clue

Uncontrolled confounding inflates OR; controlling for smoking would attenuate the association; randomization (absent in observational studies) is the most complete confounder control; stratification and multivariate adjustment are analytic approaches
