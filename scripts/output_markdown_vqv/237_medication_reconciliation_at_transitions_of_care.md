## Chief Complaint

 Readmission: lacunar infarct from unanticoagulated AF (omitted warfarin) + metformin toxicity (duplicate metformin) — both from discharge medication reconciliation failure

## Vignette

A 74-year-old woman with HFrEF (EF 30%), type 2 diabetes, CKD stage 3b, and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation is discharged after a 6-day hospitalization for acute decompensated heart failure. During the admission: her home metformin 1000 mg BID was held for contrast exposure and AKI; a new spironolactone 25 mg daily was added on day 2; her warfarin was held for a transesophageal echocardiogram on day 3 and never restarted in the inpatient chart; and her lisinopril was uptitrated from 5 mg to 10 mg daily.  The discharging resident, simultaneously managing three other discharges, generates the discharge prescription by copying the most recent inpatient medication list from the EHR without cross-referencing the home medication list from the admission note. The resulting discharge prescription omits warfarin entirely, includes both the held metformin 1000 mg BID (automatically carried forward despite being held) and a new metformin 500 mg BID added by the hospitalist on day 4 (creating a duplicate), correctly lists the uptitrated lisinopril, and correctly includes the new spironolactone.  Eight days later, the patient is readmitted. Her INR is 0.9 (subtherapeutic  off warfarin for 15 total days), she has bilateral leg cramps and nausea (metformin toxicity from dual dosing), and brain MRI reveals a new lacunar infarct consistent with cardioembolic stroke from unanticoagulated atrial fibrillation.  Which of the following systems-based interventions would most directly prevent this specific category of patient harm?

## Physical Exam

New mild left hemiparesis; INR 0.9; bilateral lower extremity edema

## Main Clue

Standardized medication reconciliation (systematic comparison of home vs inpatient medication lists at every care transition) directly prevents omitted and duplicated medications at discharge, the specific root cause of both the stroke and metformin toxicity

## Supporting Clue

Transitions of care (admission, transfer, discharge) = highest-risk periods for medication errors; pharmacist-led reconciliation most effective; high-risk medications (anticoagulants, insulin, narrow-therapeutic-index drugs) require extra vigilance; Joint Commission National Patient Safety Goal
