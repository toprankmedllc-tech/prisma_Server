## Chief Complaint

Fever, cough, poor feeding, and watery diarrhea

## Vignette

A 2-month-old boy is brought to the emergency department because of fever, cough, poor feeding, and watery diarrhea. He has had persistent oral white plaques since the first month of life despite antifungal therapy. His mother reports that he was recently treated for pneumonia and has had several episodes of prolonged diarrhea since birth. A maternal uncle died during infancy after repeated severe infections. Physical examination shows oral plaques that scrape off with mild bleeding, diffuse crackles over both lung fields, small tonsillar tissue, and few palpable cervical lymph nodes. Temperature is 38.7°C, heart rate is 164/min, blood pressure is 82/48 mm Hg, and pulse oximetry is 91% on room air. Laboratory studies show leukocytes 3,100/mm³, absolute lymphocyte count 420/mm³, IgG 105 mg/dL, IgA 4 mg/dL, IgM 11 mg/dL, CD3+ T cells 1% of lymphocytes, and CD19+ B cells 3% of lymphocytes. Chest x-ray shows bilateral interstitial infiltrates and no visible thymic shadow.
