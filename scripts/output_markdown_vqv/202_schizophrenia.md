## Chief Complaint

Paranoid delusions, auditory hallucinations, social withdrawal, self-neglect × 8 months

## Vignette

A 24-year-old man is brought to the emergency department by his mother and older brother after they found him barricading the door of his apartment with furniture and screaming that government agents had installed surveillance equipment in his walls. His family reports that over the past 8 months, he has gradually stopped attending his college courses, quit his part-time job at a grocery store, and rarely leaves his apartment. He stopped bathing approximately 3 months ago and has been surviving on canned food that he refused to let his family purchase for him out of fear that it was "contaminated." His mother tearfully reports that "he used to be the most social kid in the room."  On arrival to the emergency department, the patient is agitated and disheveled. He refuses to make eye contact and insists the treating physician is "in on it." He reports that he has been hearing two male voices that speak to each other about his movements throughout the day, commenting on everything he does. He denies any symptoms of depression or elevated mood. He denies using alcohol, tobacco, or recreational drugs and states that substances are part of the government's plan to "weaken people." Urine toxicology is negative for all substances. Basic metabolic panel, complete blood count, thyroid-stimulating hormone, and B12 levels are all within normal limits.  Physical examination reveals a young man appearing older than his stated age, with poor hygiene and body odor. He is alert and oriented to person, place, and time but exhibits markedly disorganized thought process with loose associations. His affect is flat throughout the interview. Neurological examination is non-focal.  Which of the following best describes the minimum symptom duration criteria required to establish this patient's diagnosis?
