## Chief Complaint

Hip fracture; patient refusing ORIF; healthcare proxy overriding stated patient refusal; team uncertain whether to honor patient or proxy

## Vignette

A 78-year-old woman with moderate Alzheimer's dementia, type 2 diabetes, and osteoporosis is admitted to the orthopedic service after sustaining an intertrochanteric hip fracture from a ground-level fall at her assisted living facility. The orthopedic surgeon recommends surgical fixation with open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) under spinal anesthesia, explaining that without surgery she will be immobile, experience severe chronic pain, and face substantially elevated risks of deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, pressure ulcers, aspiration pneumonia, and mortality within 12 months.  When the surgeon explains the recommendation, the patient says: "No. I've lived a good long life. I do not want any more operations. I want to go home to my cats and be comfortable." Her daughter, who was designated as her healthcare proxy in a durable power of attorney for healthcare signed 6 years ago when the patient had mild cognitive impairment, strongly disagrees: "She doesn't understand what she's saying, the dementia is talking. I am her healthcare proxy and I am authorizing this surgery."  On brief bedside mental status examination, the patient is alert, knows she is in a hospital, knows her name, states she has a broken hip, and consistently and repeatedly says she does not want surgery because she wants to be comfortable and return home. She is partially oriented (knows person and place, confused about date). The medical team is uncertain how to proceed given the conflict between the patient's expressed wishes and the proxy's insistence.  Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

## Key Symptoms

Moderate Alzheimer's; patient verbally refusing surgery consistently; patient provides reasoned basis for refusal ('lived good life; want comfort; go home to cats'); partial orientation; healthcare proxy designated by valid DPOA; proxy insisting on surgery; capacity not yet formally assessed; dementia ≠ automatic lack of capacity

## Labs

Hip X-ray: Displaced intertrochanteric fracture; CBC/BMP: WNL

## Imaging Findings

Right hip X-ray: Displaced intertrochanteric fracture with varus angulation

## Physical Exam

Alert; knows name and that she is in hospital with broken hip; consistently refuses surgery; partial orientation; right lower extremity shortened and externally rotated; pain on passive movement

## Main Clue

Moderate Alzheimer's dementia does NOT automatically eliminate decision-making capacity. Capacity is decision-specific and must be FORMALLY ASSESSED before the proxy's authorization can override the patient's expressed refusal. Assess capacity FIRST.

## Supporting Clue

If capacity INTACT → patient's refusal must be respected (autonomy prevails over beneficence); if capacity ABSENT → healthcare proxy makes decision; proxy may ONLY decide when patient lacks capacity; dementia diagnosis alone insufficient to bypass patient's expressed wishes without capacity assessment
