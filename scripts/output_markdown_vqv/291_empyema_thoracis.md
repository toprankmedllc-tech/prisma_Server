## Chief Complaint

Fever with worsening shortness of breath and pleuritic chest pain

## Vignette

A 62-year-old man presents with fever, productive cough, and worsening right-sided chest pain for 2 weeks. He was treated with oral antibiotics for presumed community-acquired pneumonia 10 days ago, with partial improvement followed by clinical deterioration. He now reports increasing shortness of breath and pleuritic chest pain. On examination, temperature is 104F, pulse is 118/min, respiratory rate is 28/min, and blood pressure is 105/68 mmHg. Breath sounds are markedly decreased over the right lower lung field with dullness to percussion. Tactile fremitus is reduced. Chest X-ray shows a large right-sided pleural effusion with multiple air-fluid levels and evidence of loculations. Ultrasound-guided thoracentesis reveals thick, foul-smelling fluid. Pleural fluid analysis shows pH 7.08, glucose 25 mg/dL, LDH is markedly elevated, and Gram stain shows mixed anaerobic organisms. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Key Symptoms

Fever; productive cough; pleuritic chest pain; progressive dyspnea; post-pneumonia clinical worsening; foul-smelling sputum/pleural fluid symptoms

## Labs

Pleural fluid pH 7.08; pleural fluid glucose 25 mg/dL; elevated LDH; Gram stain positive for mixed anaerobic bacteria

## Imaging Findings

Chest X-ray: large right-sided pleural effusion with loculations; air-fluid levels; ultrasound: complex septated pleural fluid collection

## Physical Exam

Decreased breath sounds over right lower lung field; dullness to percussion; reduced tactile fremitus; decreased chest expansion

## Main Clue

Low pleural fluid pH (<7.2) with low glucose + loculated purulent pleural fluid after pneumonia

## Supporting Clue

Foul-smelling anaerobic organisms + clinical deterioration after initial pneumonia improvement

## Question

Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?
