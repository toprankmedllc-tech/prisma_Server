## Chief Complaint

Progressive chest pressure × 5 hours; Radiation to right jaw; Diaphoresis

## Vignette

A 64-year-old man with type 2 diabetes and dyslipidemia presents to the emergency department with 5 hours of progressively worsening pressure-like chest discomfort radiating to his right jaw. He is diaphoretic and mildly short of breath. Blood pressure is 98/66 mmHg and heart rate is 82/min. The 12-lead ECG on arrival shows normal sinus rhythm with ST-segment depression and T-wave inversions in leads II, III, and aVF. Troponin I returns significantly elevated. He is diagnosed with a non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction and admitted for medical stabilization with coronary angiography planned for the following morning. Approximately 9 hours after admission, new 12-lead ECG shows progressive prolongation of the PR interval followed by a dropped QRS complex in a repeating pattern.

Which of the following coronary arteries is most likely to be the culprit vessel in this patient?

A) Left anterior descending artery 
B) Left circumflex artery 
C) Left main coronary artery 
D) Diagonal branch of the LAD 
E) Right coronary artery
