## Vignette

A 58-year-old male with a history of chronic heart failure presents for a routine follow-up appointment. He reports fatigue, mild muscle weakness, and occasional palpitations over the past two weeks. He denies chest pain, shortness of breath at rest, or syncope. His current medications include digoxin, metoprolol, furosemide, and lisinopril. He is adherent to his medications and has made no recent dietary changes. His blood pressure is 138/86 mmHg, heart rate is 88 bpm, pulse oximetry is 97% on room air, and temperature is 37.0°C. Physical examination reveals mild bilateral lower extremity edema and an irregularly irregular rhythm on cardiac auscultation. Laboratory studies show serum potassium of 6.2 mEq/L, sodium of 139 mEq/L, pH of 7.38, bicarbonate of 24 mEq/L, glucose of 92 mg/dL, and a therapeutic digoxin level. Which of the following mechanisms is most likely responsible for this patient’s hyperkalemia?
