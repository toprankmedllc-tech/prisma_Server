## Chief Complaint

Progressive proximal muscle weakness, frequent falls, Gowers' sign, calf pseudohypertrophy, CK 18,400

## Vignette

A 6-year-old boy is brought to the pediatric neurology clinic by his parents because of progressive difficulty keeping up with peers in physical activities over the past 18 months. His mother reports he walked independently at 14 months but began falling frequently at age 4, stopped climbing playground equipment, and can no longer keep pace with his twin brother during play. More recently he has had significant difficulty rising from the floor: when asked to stand from a seated position, he places his hands on his thighs, then his knees, and uses his arms to push himself upright by walking his hands up his legs to assume a standing posture. His mother notes that his calf muscles appear "bigger and more muscular" than his twin's, despite his overall weakness.  His past medical history is notable for two episodes of markedly elevated liver enzymes on routine laboratory screening at ages 3 and 5, attributed to unknown causes by previous providers. There is no relevant paternal family history. His maternal uncle died at age 22 from respiratory failure, and another maternal uncle currently uses a wheelchair for mobility.  Physical examination reveals: bilateral calf pseudohypertrophy (firm, rubber-like consistency on palpation); proximal lower extremity weakness (hip flexors 3+/5, hip extensors 3+/5, knee extensors 4/5); proximal upper extremity weakness (shoulder abductors 4/5); distal strength preserved (4+/5); deep tendon reflexes diffusely reduced bilaterally; positive Gowers' sign on floor-to-stand testing; waddling gait with lumbar hyperlordosis; bilateral toe-walking. Cardiopulmonary examination is unremarkable at this age.  Laboratory studies are obtained. Serum creatine kinase (CK) returns as 18,400 U/L (reference range 30–200 U/L).  Which of the following genetic defects most specifically and commonly underlies this patient's condition?

## Key Symptoms

Positive Gowers' sign; bilateral calf pseudohypertrophy (firm); proximal > distal weakness; CK 18,400 U/L (>90× upper limit); waddling gait; hyperlordosis; maternal uncle died at 22 of respiratory failure (DMD complication); another uncle in wheelchair; X-linked inheritance; prior unexplained transaminase elevations (= CK elevation from muscle, not hepatic origin)

## Labs

CK: 18,400 U/L (markedly elevated; NR 30-200); ALT: 68 U/L (elevated from muscle origin, not hepatic); AST: 72 U/L; LDH: elevated; Aldolase: elevated

## Physical Exam

Bilateral calf pseudohypertrophy (firm); proximal weakness hip/shoulder girdle; positive Gowers' sign; waddling gait; lumbar hyperlordosis; bilateral toe-walking; reduced DTRs; distal strength preserved

## Main Clue

Gowers' sign + calf pseudohypertrophy + CK 18,400 U/L + X-linked maternal family history (uncle died respiratory failure at 22 = DMD natural history) = Duchenne MD; caused by FRAMESHIFT deletions/duplications in DMD gene (dystrophin, Xp21) → absent functional dystrophin

## Supporting Clue

DMD = largest human gene (2.4 Mb, Xp21); prior unexplained ALT/AST elevations = CK elevation from muscle spilling into transaminase assays; Duchenne (frameshift → no dystrophin → severe, onset 3-5) vs Becker (in-frame → reduced dystrophin → milder, later onset); loss of ambulation by early teens in Duchenne
