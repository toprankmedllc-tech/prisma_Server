## Chief Complaint

Acute confusion in a chronic alcoholic with hypoglycemia

## Vignette

A 52-year-old homeless man with a long history of heavy alcohol use is brought to the emergency department after being found confused at a bus stop. He is disheveled and his clothing is soiled. He cannot give a coherent history and does not know the date or where he is. On examination, his temperature is 36.4°C (97.5°F), blood pressure 108/68 mm Hg, heart rate 102/min, oxygen saturation 96% on room air. He smells strongly of alcohol. When asked to follow a moving target with his eyes, the right eye does not abduct fully and he develops horizontal jerking eye movements that beat away from the direction of gaze. His gait is wide-based and unsteady; he sways and nearly falls when asked to stand with feet together. Strength is preserved in all extremities. Cranial nerves are otherwise intact. Fingerstick glucose is 56 mg/dL.

## Key Symptoms

Acute confusion / encephalopathy; impaired lateral eye movement and horizontal nystagmus; wide-based unsteady gait; chronic alcohol use; likely malnutrition; hypoglycemia

## Labs

Fingerstick glucose 56 mg/dL; further labs pending

## Physical Exam

Confused, disoriented to time and place; right eye fails to abduct; horizontal nystagmus on lateral gaze in both directions; wide-based unsteady gait with truncal swaying; intact strength and otherwise intact cranial nerves

## Main Clue

Chronic alcoholic / malnourished patient with the triad of acute confusion + ophthalmoplegia + ataxia = Wernicke encephalopathy from thiamine (B1) deficiency

## Supporting Clue

Administering intravenous DEXTROSE before THIAMINE drives the remaining thiamine into glucose metabolism and can precipitate or worsen Wernicke encephalopathy and lead to permanent Korsakoff amnestic syndrome
