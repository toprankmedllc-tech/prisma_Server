## Chief Complaint

Progressive tremor and slowing of movement

## Vignette

A 64-year-old man is brought to the clinic by his wife because of progressive slowing of movement and tremors over the past 3 years. He reports difficulty initiating movement, taking longer to button his shirt, and frequently shuffling while walking. His wife has noticed decreased facial expression and softer speech. Physical examination reveals a resting “pill-rolling” tremor, cogwheel rigidity, and bradykinesia. The patient is started on a medication that is converted into dopamine in the central nervous system along with another medication that inhibits peripheral conversion of the drug. Several years later, he develops involuntary choreiform movements during therapy. A second medication is added that increases dopamine release and decreases dopamine reuptake to help control these abnormal movements.

Which of the following best describes the mechanism of the medication added to reduce this patient’s involuntary movements?
