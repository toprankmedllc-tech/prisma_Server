## Chief Complaint

Difficulty walking and slurred speech

## Vignette

A 61-year-old man is brought to the emergency department due to progressive difficulty walking and slurred speech for the past 2 days. His wife reports that he has been “walking like he is drunk” and keeps missing objects when trying to grab them with his right hand. He also complains of nausea and intermittent vertigo. The patient has a history of poorly controlled hypertension and hyperlipidemia. Neurologic examination reveals a wide-based gait, right-sided intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia, and impaired finger-to-nose testing on the right. Horizontal nystagmus and scanning speech are also present. Muscle strength and sensory examination are normal. MRI of the brain demonstrates an infarct involving the lateral hemisphere of the right cerebellum affecting a deep cerebellar nucleus that receives Purkinje cell input from the cerebellar hemispheres and projects to the contralateral red nucleus and thalamus. Which of the following cerebellar nuclei is most likely affected?
