## Vignette

A 14-month-old male is brought in by his parents for “another chest infection” and poor weight gain. He was born at 38 weeks gestation via cesarean section due to fetal bradycardia. Maternal history is unremarkable and there is no known family history of genetic or immune disorders.
This is his third respiratory infection in 6 months. He was hospitalized at 10 months for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and has had persistent oral thrush despite two courses of oral fluconazole. His parents report episodes of unexplained irritability and muscle twitching since age 3 months and note he “never seemed to catch up” after cardiac surgery. His weight is at the 3rd percentile.

## Key Symptoms

Third respiratory infection in 6 months (two prior episodes of pneumonia); one prior hospitalization for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) at 10 months; persistent oral thrush despite two courses of oral fluconazole; episodes of unexplained irritability and muscle twitching since age 3 months; poor weight gain (weight at 3rd percentile); parents note child “never seemed to catch up” after cardiac surgery

## Labs

Serum calcium: 7.1 mg/dL (low); phosphorus: 5.8 mg/dL (elevated); CBC: WBC 6,200 cells/µL with lymphocyte count 950 cells/µL (low); hemoglobin: 10.4 g/dL; flow cytometry: CD3+ T cells markedly reduced, CD19+ B cells normal, NK cells normal; IgG: 480 mg/dL (low-normal for age); IgM: 98 mg/dL (normal); IgA: 42 mg/dL (normal)

## Imaging Findings

Chest X-ray: right lower lobe consolidation; mediastinum appears narrow with no visible thymic shadow; Echocardiogram (from records at age 2 weeks): interrupted aortic arch, repaired surgically at 6 weeks of life

## Physical Exam

Weight and length below 5th percentile; subtle facial findings: slightly low-set posteriorly rotated ears, small jaw, wide-set eyes, thin upper lip; well-healed midline sternotomy scar; diffuse crackles over right lower lobe on auscultation; white plaques on tongue and buccal mucosa; no peripheral lymphadenopathy; deep tendon reflexes intact; mild hand stiffening noted during exam when blood pressure cuff inflated (Trousseau sign)

## Main Clue

Absent thymic shadow + conotruncal cardiac defect (interrupted aortic arch) + low CD3+ T cells with intact B cells + hypocalcemia with elevated phosphorus and low PTH pattern

## Supporting Clue

Recurrent opportunistic infections (PCP, refractory oral candidiasis) in a child with subtle dysmorphic features and a repaired cardiac anomaly points to a combined structural and immune developmental defect rooted in pharyngeal pouch maldevelopment — not a pure antibody or combined lymphocyte deficiency

## Question

Which of the following most accurately describes the underlying mechanism responsible for this patient’s susceptibility to opportunistic infections?

## Correct Answer

C) Defective embryologic development of pharyngeal pouches resulting in absent thymic tissue and failure of T-cell maturation
